### Question 2(b) — Schedule I & composite vs mixed supply

**(i) Any four activities under Schedule I (supply even without consideration):**
1. **Permanent transfer or disposal of business assets** where input tax credit has been availed on those assets.
2. **Supply of goods or services between related persons, or between distinct persons** (u/s 25), when made in the course or furtherance of business (gifts by an employer to an employee exceeding ₹50,000 in a year are included).
3. **Supply of goods** by a principal to his agent (or by an agent to his principal) where the agent undertakes to supply/receive such goods on behalf of the principal.
4. **Import of services** by a person from a related person or from any of his own establishments outside India, in the course or furtherance of business.

**(ii) Composite vs mixed supply:**
| Basis | Composite supply | Mixed supply |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Two or more taxable supplies **naturally bundled** and supplied together in the ordinary course of business, one of which is a **principal supply** | Two or more individual supplies made together for a **single price**, which are **not naturally bundled** and can be supplied separately |
| Example | Supply of goods **with transport and insurance** (goods = principal supply) | A **gift hamper** of chocolates, sweets and a candle sold for one price |
| Rate of tax | Rate of the **principal supply** | **Highest** rate applicable to any of the individual supplies |

**OR — Question 3(b) (Concept of "supply", Schedules II & III):**
- **Supply (section 7):** "Supply" includes all forms of supply of goods or services — sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease, disposal — made or agreed to be made **for a consideration** by a person **in the course or furtherance of business**; it also includes activities specified in **Schedule I** made without consideration, and import of services for consideration (whether or not in the course of business).

### Question 6(b) — Section 9(3) vs 9(4) (reverse charge)

| Basis | Section 9(3) | Section 9(4) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Tax on **notified categories of goods/services** is payable by the **recipient**, regardless of whether the supplier is registered | Tax on supply of **notified goods/services** by an **unregistered supplier** to a **registered recipient** is payable by the recipient |

**OR — Question 6(b) (Dual GST structure):**
- **Intra-State supply** (supplier and place of supply in the same State/UT): **CGST** (levied and collected by the **Central Government**) + **SGST** (by the **State Government**) / **UTGST** (in a Union Territory without a legislature, by the **Central Government** under the UTGST Act). Both together equal the total GST rate (e.g., 18% = 9% + 9%).
- **Inter-State supply** (supplier and place of supply in different States/UTs, and imports): **IGST** (levied and collected by the **Central Government**), broadly equal to CGST + SGST; the IGST is later apportioned between the Centre and the destination State.
- **Any four levies subsumed into GST:** Central Excise Duty (on most goods), Service Tax, State VAT / Sales Tax (on most goods), Central Sales Tax, Entry Tax / Octroi, Luxury Tax, Entertainment Tax (except that levied by local bodies), and Purchase Tax. *(Any four acceptable.)* **Not subsumed:** Basic Customs Duty, stamp duty, State excise on alcohol for human consumption, and (currently) tax on petroleum products and electricity.
