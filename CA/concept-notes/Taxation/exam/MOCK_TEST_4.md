## Division B — Model Solutions

### Question 1(a) — Total income & tax, old regime (Mr. Arjun, AY 2026-27)

**Step 1 — Clubbing of minor daughter's income (sec 64(1A)):**
Interest ₹35,000 clubbed, less exemption u/s 10(32) ₹1,500 = **₹33,500** included under Income from Other Sources.

**Step 2 — Head-wise income:**

| Head | ₹ |
|---|---|
| **Profits & gains of business** | 9,00,000 |
| **Capital gains** | |
| — STCG on listed shares (sec 111A) | 1,00,000 |
| — LTCG on plot of land (long-term, indexed) | 3,00,000 |
| **Income from Other Sources** | |
| — FD interest | 60,000 |
| — Dividend (Indian company) | 20,000 |
| — Clubbed minor's income (net) | 33,500 |
| IOS total | 1,13,500 |
| **Gross Total Income** | **14,13,500** |

**Step 3 — Deductions under Chapter VI-A (allowed only against normal-rate income, not against 111A/LTCG):**
- **80C** — PPF ₹1,50,000 (within ceiling) = **₹1,50,000**
- **80D** — self & family (all < 60): premium ₹21,000 + preventive check-up ₹6,000 (capped at ₹5,000) = ₹26,000, but overall limit ₹25,000 → **₹25,000**
- **80G** — donation ₹40,000, eligible for 50% subject to qualifying limit (see working) → **₹20,000**

*80G qualifying-limit working:*
Adjusted GTI = GTI − LTCG − STCG u/s 111A − deductions u/s 80C to 80U (except 80G)
= 14,13,500 − 3,00,000 − 1,00,000 − (1,50,000 + 25,000) = **₹8,38,500.**
Qualifying limit = 10% × 8,38,500 = ₹83,850. Donation ₹40,000 < ₹83,850 → whole ₹40,000 qualifies; 50% = **₹20,000.**

Total Chapter VI-A = 1,50,000 + 25,000 + 20,000 = **₹1,95,000.**

**Step 4 — Total income:**
14,13,500 − 1,95,000 = **₹12,18,500**, of which special-rate income = LTCG 3,00,000 + STCG 111A 1,00,000 = ₹4,00,000; **normal-rate income = ₹8,18,500.**

**Step 5 — Tax liability (old regime, age 52 — normal slabs; basic exemption ₹2,50,000):**

| Component | Working | Tax (₹) |

**OR — Question 3(b) — Credit and debit notes (Sec 34):**
- **Credit note** — issued **by the supplier** where, after issuing a tax invoice, the **taxable value or tax charged is found to exceed** the actual (e.g., goods returned, deficiency in supply, post-supply discount meeting Sec 15(3) conditions, or price reduction). It **reduces** the supplier's output tax liability.
- **Debit note** — issued **by the supplier** where the **taxable value or tax charged is found to be less** than the actual (e.g., upward price revision). It **increases** the output tax liability. (A "supplementary invoice" is included in a debit note.)
- **Time limit (credit note):** must be declared in a return **on or before 30 November** following the end of the FY in which the supply was made, or the date of furnishing the relevant annual return, **whichever is earlier**. *(No such outer time limit for debit notes; but ITC on a debit note is available up to the same 30 November/annual-return limit under Sec 16(4).)*
- **ITC reversal condition:** the reduction in the supplier's output tax on account of a credit note is allowed **only if** the recipient has **reversed the corresponding ITC**; the tax/interest passed on must not have been retained by the recipient (i.e., no unjust enrichment).

### Question 5(b) — Conditions for ITC u/s 16(2) (main alternative)

A registered person is entitled to ITC only if **all four** conditions of section 16(2) are satisfied:
1. He is in **possession of a tax invoice / debit note** (or prescribed document), and the details have been furnished by the supplier and **communicated to the recipient** (Sec 16(2)(a)/(aa) — supplier reported it in GSTR-1, appearing in GSTR-2B).
2. He has **received the goods and/or services** (including "bill-to-ship-to" deliveries deemed received).
3. The **tax charged has actually been paid to the Government** by the supplier (Sec 16(2)(c)), in cash or through admissible ITC.
4. He has **furnished the return** under section 39 (Sec 16(2)(d)); additionally, he must **pay the supplier** the value of supply plus tax **within 180 days** of the invoice, else the credit availed is added back with interest (Sec 16(2), second proviso).

**Time limit (Sec 16(4)):** ITC on an invoice/debit note for a financial year may be availed **up to 30 November** following the end of that FY, or the date of filing the relevant **annual return**, whichever is **earlier**.

**OR — Question 5(b) — Composite vs mixed supply:**
- **Composite supply** (Sec 2(30)) — two or more **naturally bundled** supplies made together in the ordinary course of business, one of which is the **principal supply**; taxed at the **rate of the principal supply.**
- **Mixed supply** (Sec 2(74)) — two or more supplies made together for a **single price** that are **not naturally bundled**; taxed at the **rate of that supply which attracts the highest rate of tax.**

| Case | Classification | Reason & rate |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Goods packed, transported, insured for a single price | **Composite supply** | Naturally bundled; principal supply = the goods → taxed at the goods' rate |
| (ii) Gift pack of chocolates + sweets + candle for a single price | **Mixed supply** | Not naturally bundled; can be sold separately → taxed at the **highest** applicable rate among the items |
| (iii) Works contract (construction of a building) | **Composite supply** (specifically deemed a supply of **service** under Sch II) | Treated as a service; taxed at the works-contract service rate |
