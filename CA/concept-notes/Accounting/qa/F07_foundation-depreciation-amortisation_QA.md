## Section A — Theory

### Q1. Define depreciation and explain why it is called a process of allocation, not of valuation. (5 marks)

**Model answer.**
Depreciation is the **systematic allocation of the depreciable amount** (cost less residual value) of a fixed asset over its **useful life**. It reflects the gradual consumption of the asset's economic benefits through use, passage of time, and obsolescence.

It is a process of **allocation, not valuation**, because:

1. It starts from a **known, verifiable figure** — historical cost — and merely spreads it across years by a stated rule; it does **not** try to estimate the asset's current market price each year.
2. The resulting book value (WDV) is simply *cost minus depreciation charged so far* — a **leftover of the allocation exercise**, not an appraisal of what the asset would fetch if sold.
3. Depreciation is charged **even when the market value rises**, because the objective is to match the asset's cost to the periods that use it, not to track its price.

*Presentation tip: give the one-line definition first, then the three reasons as numbered points. Examiners award a mark for the "cost − residual over useful life" phrasing and a mark for the "allocation not valuation" distinction.*

### Q2. State any five causes of depreciation. (5 marks)

**Model answer.**

| Cause | Nature |
|-------|--------|
| Wear and tear | Physical deterioration from actual use (internal) |
| Efflux (passage) of time | Loss of value simply with time, e.g. a lease running down |
| Obsolescence | Newer/better technology makes the asset economically outdated |
| Depletion | Exhaustion of a wasting asset (coal, oil, ore) as it is extracted |
| Accident / permanent fall in value | Sudden damage or a permanent decline in usefulness |

*Key sentence: "Depreciation may arise from internal causes (wear and tear, depletion) or external causes (obsolescence, efflux of time, permanent fall in value)."*

### Q3. Distinguish between the Straight-Line Method and the Written Down Value Method. (6 marks)

**Model answer.** Present as a table.

| Basis | Straight-Line Method | Written Down Value Method |
|-------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Base for charge | Original cost (fixed) | Opening book value (declining) |
| Annual charge | Equal every year | Decreases every year |
| Book value at end of life | Reaches residual value exactly | Approaches but never reaches zero |
| Dep + repairs pattern | Total rises over time | Total stays roughly even |
| Suitability | Assets with even use, low repairs (building, furniture) | Machinery, vehicles with rising repairs |
| Income-tax recognition | Generally not | Yes — tax uses WDV on blocks of assets |

*Closing line for full marks: "WDV front-loads depreciation so that as repairs rise with age, the falling depreciation charge offsets them, keeping the total yearly burden on the P&L roughly constant."*

### Q4. Why is depreciation charged even in a year in which the business makes a loss? (3 marks)

**Model answer.** Because the asset is **consumed by use and time regardless of the firm's profitability**. The matching principle requires the asset's cost to be spread over the periods that use it, not only over profitable ones. Omitting depreciation in a loss year would understate the loss, overstate assets, and break consistency — it is a form of profit manipulation the systematic-charge rule exists to prevent.

### Q5. "Depreciation provides funds for replacement of the asset." Do you agree? (4 marks)

**Model answer.** **Only partly.** Depreciation is a **non-cash charge** — no money physically leaves or is set aside when it is recorded. Its true effect is to **reduce reported profit**, which in turn **limits the profit that can be withdrawn as dividends or drawings**. By retaining that profit inside the business, cash *tends* to be preserved and is *available* for replacement. But depreciation itself does **not create a dedicated cash fund**; a fund exists only if the firm separately invests the retained amounts (e.g. a sinking fund / depreciation fund investment). So the correct statement is: depreciation helps *retain* funds, it does not *create* a fund.

### Q6. How is a change in the method of depreciation treated? Contrast the classic exam approach with the current AS position. (5 marks)

**Model answer.**

- **Current position (AS 10 revised, 2024 scheme):** the method of depreciation is an **estimate**, so a change is a **change in accounting estimate** applied **prospectively** — the remaining book value is depreciated under the new method over the remaining useful life; past years are **not** disturbed.
- **Classic textbook approach (still examined):** a change "**with retrospective effect**" means recomputing depreciation for all past years under the new method, finding the difference from what was actually charged, and adjusting the **arrears of depreciation through the P&L** in the year of change — charging a **shortfall** as extra depreciation, or **writing back an excess** as income.

*Presentation tip: state both, and add "the treatment followed depends on the exact wording of the problem." That sentence protects the mark whichever version the examiner expects.*

### Q7. What is amortisation? How does the useful life of an intangible asset differ from that of a tangible asset for this purpose? (4 marks)

**Model answer.** **Amortisation** is the systematic write-off of the cost of an **intangible asset** (patent, copyright, licence) over its useful life — the intangible twin of depreciation, usually computed straight-line with nil residual value. For an intangible, the useful life is taken as the **shorter of its legal/contractual life and its economic (commercial) life**: a patent with a 20-year legal life but only 5 years of commercial usefulness is amortised over **5** years. **Goodwill** is not systematically amortised under AS; it is tested for impairment instead.

### Q8. State the journal entries under (a) the direct method and (b) the provision for depreciation method, and explain the Balance Sheet difference. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**

| Method | Depreciation entry | Balance Sheet shows |
|--------|--------------------|---------------------|
| Direct (charge to asset) | Depreciation A/c Dr → Asset A/c | Asset at **written-down value** (cost lost) |
| Provision method | Depreciation A/c Dr → Provision for Depreciation A/c | Asset at **cost**, *less* Provision for Depreciation |

In both, Depreciation A/c is then closed to P&L (Profit & Loss A/c Dr → Depreciation A/c). The provision method is preferred because it **preserves both the original cost and the total accumulated depreciation** as separate, visible figures.

### Q9. What is an Asset Disposal Account and what does its balancing figure represent? (3 marks)

**Model answer.** When an asset is sold, an **Asset Disposal A/c** is opened. It is **debited with the cost** of the asset sold and **credited with (i) the accumulated depreciation** on it (transferred from the Provision A/c) **and (ii) the sale proceeds**. The **balancing figure is the profit or loss on sale**: a balance on the **credit** side is a **loss** (transferred to P&L debit), and a balance on the **debit** side is a **profit** (transferred to P&L credit). Effectively it computes *Sale proceeds − WDV at date of sale*.

### Q10. Give two reasons why freehold land is not depreciated but a leasehold property is amortised. (3 marks)

**Model answer.**
1. **Freehold land has an unlimited useful life** — it does not wear out or become obsolete with use, so there is no cost to allocate over time.
2. **Leasehold property is held for a fixed lease term**; its usefulness expires when the lease ends, so its cost is **amortised over the lease period** to match the benefit consumed each year.

## Section B — Numerical Problems

### Q11. SLM depreciation and rate. (4 marks)

A firm buys equipment for **Rs 5,50,000**, incurs **Rs 30,000** on installation, expects a residual value of **Rs 40,000** and a useful life of **8 years**. Compute the annual depreciation under SLM and the rate of depreciation on cost.

**Solution.**
- Cost = 5,50,000 + 30,000 (installation is capitalised) = **Rs 5,80,000.**
- Depreciable amount = 5,80,000 − 40,000 = **Rs 5,40,000.**
- Annual depreciation = 5,40,000 ÷ 8 = **Rs 67,500.**
- Rate on cost = (67,500 ÷ 5,80,000) × 100 = **11.64% p.a.**

Check: 8 × 67,500 = 5,40,000 = depreciable amount; closing book value after 8 yrs = 5,80,000 − 5,40,000 = 40,000 = residual. ✔

### Q12. WDV schedule. (4 marks)

A machine costing **Rs 2,50,000** is depreciated at **15% p.a. on WDV**. Show depreciation and closing WDV for the first three years.

**Solution.**

| Year | Opening WDV | Dep @ 15% | Closing WDV |
|------|------------:|----------:|------------:|
| 1 | 2,50,000 | 37,500 | 2,12,500 |
| 2 | 2,12,500 | 31,875 | 1,80,625 |
| 3 | 1,80,625 | 27,093.75 | 1,53,531.25 |

Check: 2,50,000 − (37,500 + 31,875 + 27,093.75) = 2,50,000 − 96,468.75 = 1,53,531.25 = closing WDV. ✔

### Q13. Depreciation up to date of sale + profit/loss. (5 marks)

A machine bought on 1 April 2021 for **Rs 3,00,000** is depreciated at **10% p.a. on cost (SLM)**. On **1 October 2023** it is sold for **Rs 2,10,000**. Year-end is 31 March. Compute the WDV at the date of sale and the profit or loss.

**Solution.**
Depreciation @ 10% on cost = 30,000 per full year.
- 2021-22: 30,000
- 2022-23: 30,000
- 2023-24 (1 Apr – 1 Oct = 6 months): 30,000 × 6/12 = 15,000

Accumulated depreciation to date of sale = 30,000 + 30,000 + 15,000 = **Rs 75,000.**
WDV at sale = 3,00,000 − 75,000 = **Rs 2,25,000.**
Sale proceeds 2,10,000 − WDV 2,25,000 = **Loss on sale Rs 15,000.**

### Q15. Change of method — WDV to SLM, retrospective. (8 marks)

A machine bought on 1 April 2020 for **Rs 6,00,000** was depreciated at **15% p.a. on WDV**. On 31 March 2023 (after 3 years) the firm switches to **SLM @ 10% p.a. on cost with retrospective effect**. Compute the adjustment and pass the entry.

**Solution.**

**Step 1 — WDV charged.**

| Year | Opening WDV | Dep @ 15% | Closing WDV |
|------|------------:|----------:|------------:|
| 2020-21 | 6,00,000 | 90,000 | 5,10,000 |
| 2021-22 | 5,10,000 | 76,500 | 4,33,500 |
| 2022-23 | 4,33,500 | 65,025 | 3,68,475 |
| **Total (WDV)** | | **2,31,525** | |

**Step 2 — SLM required @ 10% on cost = 60,000 p.a. × 3 = Rs 1,80,000.** Book value under SLM = 6,00,000 − 1,80,000 = **4,20,000.**

**Step 3 — Adjustment.**

| | Rs |
|---|---:|
| Depreciation charged under WDV | 2,31,525 |
| Depreciation required under SLM | 1,80,000 |
| **Excess already charged → write back** | **51,525** |

Here WDV charged **more** than SLM, so there is an **excess of Rs 51,525** to be **written back** (income). Book value is raised from 3,68,475 to **4,20,000**.

**Journal entry, 31 March 2023:**

| Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|-------------|--------:|--------:|
| Machinery A/c ... Dr | 51,525 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To Profit & Loss A/c (excess depreciation written back) | | 51,525 |

From 2023-24 onwards, charge SLM @ 60,000 per year.

*Note: under the current AS 10 prospective approach, no write-back would occur; the WDV of 3,68,475 would simply be depreciated on SLM over the remaining life. Follow the wording of the question — here "retrospective" is specified.*

### Q16. Amortisation of a patent with mid-year purchase. (5 marks)

A patent is acquired on **1 July 2023** for **Rs 1,20,000**; useful life **5 years**, nil residual. Year-end 31 March. Show amortisation for 2023-24 and 2024-25 and the carrying amount at 31 March 2025.

**Solution.**
Full-year amortisation = 1,20,000 ÷ 5 = 24,000.
- 2023-24 (1 Jul – 31 Mar = 9 months): 24,000 × 9/12 = **Rs 18,000.** Carrying amount = 1,20,000 − 18,000 = 1,02,000.
- 2024-25 (full year): **Rs 24,000.** Carrying amount = 1,02,000 − 24,000 = **Rs 78,000.**

**Entry (2023-24):** Amortisation A/c Dr 18,000 → Patent A/c 18,000; then P&L A/c Dr 18,000 → Amortisation A/c 18,000. ✔

### Q17. Asset bought and part sold — Machinery A/c under direct method. (8 marks)

On 1 April 2022 a firm's Machinery A/c stood at **Rs 8,00,000** (this represents two machines of Rs 4,00,000 each, bought 1 April 2021). Depreciation **10% p.a. on WDV**, charged directly to the Machinery A/c. On **30 September 2023** one machine is sold for **Rs 2,90,000**. Prepare the Machinery A/c for 2022-23 and 2023-24.

**Solution.**

**WDV of each machine at 1 Apr 2022** (after 1 year's dep): 4,00,000 − 40,000 = **3,60,000 each** (total 7,20,000). But the account shows 8,00,000 as at 1 Apr 2022 — meaning the Rs 8,00,000 is *cost*, and only the *opening balance* is stated. Re-reading: the account "stood at Rs 8,00,000" on 1 Apr 2022, i.e. this is the **opening book value** carried forward.

To keep it unambiguous, treat **1 Apr 2022 opening WDV = 8,00,000 for the two machines together (4,00,000 each)**, and depreciate on WDV going forward.

*Working per machine from 1 Apr 2022:*
- Each machine opening WDV (1 Apr 2022) = 4,00,000.
- 2022-23 dep @ 10% = 40,000 each → closing WDV 3,60,000 each.
- Machine sold 30 Sep 2023: 2023-24 dep for 6 months = 3,60,000 × 10% × 6/12 = 18,000 → WDV at sale = 3,60,000 − 18,000 = **3,42,000.**
- Proceeds 2,90,000 − 3,42,000 = **Loss Rs 52,000.**
- Remaining machine: 2023-24 full-year dep = 3,60,000 × 10% = 36,000 → closing WDV 3,24,000.

**Machinery Account**

| Date | Particulars | Rs | Date | Particulars | Rs |
|------|-------------|---:|------|-------------|---:|
| 01-04-22 | To Balance b/d | 8,00,000 | 31-03-23 | By Depreciation A/c (40,000×2) | 80,000 |
| | | | 31-03-23 | By Balance c/d | 7,20,000 |
| | | **8,00,000** | | | **8,00,000** |
| 01-04-23 | To Balance b/d | 7,20,000 | 30-09-23 | By Depreciation A/c (on sold machine, 6 mths) | 18,000 |
| | | | 30-09-23 | By Bank A/c (proceeds) | 2,90,000 |
| | | | 30-09-23 | By Profit & Loss A/c (loss) | 52,000 |
| | | | 31-03-24 | By Depreciation A/c (remaining machine) | 36,000 |
| | | | 31-03-24 | By Balance c/d | 3,24,000 |

Check the sold machine: opening WDV 3,60,000 = dep 18,000 + proceeds 2,90,000 + loss 52,000 = 3,60,000. ✔ Remaining machine closing WDV = 3,60,000 − 36,000 = 3,24,000 = balance c/d. ✔

### Q18. Profit on sale. (4 marks)

A vehicle costing **Rs 8,00,000** (bought 1 April 2020, depreciated **25% p.a. WDV**) is sold on **1 April 2023** for **Rs 3,80,000**. Compute the profit or loss on sale.

**Solution.**

| Year | Opening WDV | Dep @ 25% | Closing WDV |
|------|------------:|----------:|------------:|
| 2020-21 | 8,00,000 | 2,00,000 | 6,00,000 |
| 2021-22 | 6,00,000 | 1,50,000 | 4,50,000 |
| 2022-23 | 4,50,000 | 1,12,500 | 3,37,500 |

WDV on 1 Apr 2023 = **Rs 3,37,500.** Sold for 3,80,000 → **Profit on sale = 3,80,000 − 3,37,500 = Rs 42,500.** (No part-year depreciation, as sale is on the first day of the new year.)

### Q19. Choosing the SLM rate to hit a target residual. (4 marks)

An asset costs **Rs 4,50,000**, expected residual **Rs 90,000**, life **6 years**. Under SLM, what annual rupee charge and what rate on cost are needed? Verify that book value reaches residual.

**Solution.**
- Depreciable amount = 4,50,000 − 90,000 = 3,60,000.
- Annual charge = 3,60,000 ÷ 6 = **Rs 60,000.**
- Rate on cost = (60,000 ÷ 4,50,000) × 100 = **13.33% p.a.**
- After 6 years: 4,50,000 − (6 × 60,000) = 4,50,000 − 3,60,000 = **90,000 = residual.** ✔

### Q20. Repairs vs capital — effect on depreciation. (5 marks)

On 1 April 2022 a machine costs **Rs 10,00,000** (life 10 years, SLM, nil residual). On 1 April 2023 the firm spends **Rs 2,00,000**: of this, **Rs 1,50,000** is a major upgrade that extends and enhances the machine, and **Rs 50,000** is routine repairs. Show the depreciation for 2022-23 and 2023-24.

**Solution.**
- 2022-23 depreciation = 10,00,000 ÷ 10 = **Rs 1,00,000.** WDV 1 Apr 2023 = 9,00,000.
- The **Rs 1,50,000 upgrade is capital** (enhances the asset) → add to the asset: revised carrying amount = 9,00,000 + 1,50,000 = 10,50,000, to be depreciated over the **remaining 9 years**.
- The **Rs 50,000 repairs is revenue** → charged straight to P&L as an expense, not capitalised.
- 2023-24 depreciation = 10,50,000 ÷ 9 = **Rs 1,16,667** (rounded). WDV end 2023-24 = 10,50,000 − 1,16,667 = **9,33,333.**

*Key point for marks: only expenditure that increases future benefits beyond the original assessment is capitalised; routine repairs that merely maintain the asset are expensed. Capitalising repairs, or expensing an upgrade, both misstate profit and depreciation.*

### Q22. Amortisation — legal vs economic life. (4 marks)

A copyright is bought for **Rs 3,60,000**. Its legal protection runs **12 years**, but the work is expected to generate revenue for only **8 years**. Compute the annual amortisation and state the reasoning.

**Solution.**
Useful life = **shorter of 12 (legal) and 8 (economic) = 8 years.**
Annual amortisation = 3,60,000 ÷ 8 = **Rs 45,000 per year** (nil residual assumed).
Reasoning: amortisation matches the cost to the periods that actually **benefit** from the asset; benefits cease after 8 years, so writing off over 12 would understate the expense in early years and leave an unrecoverable balance later.

### Q23. Theory — why WDV suits machinery. (4 marks)

Explain, from first principles, why the Written Down Value method is preferred for plant and machinery.

**Model answer.** Machinery is **most productive when new** and needs **progressively more repairs and maintenance as it ages**. The WDV method charges **high depreciation in early years** (when repairs are low) and **low depreciation in later years** (when repairs are high). As a result, the **combined burden of depreciation + repairs stays roughly constant** across the asset's life, giving a fairer, more stable charge against each year's revenue — which the flat SLM charge cannot achieve, since under SLM the total (flat depreciation + rising repairs) climbs every year.

### Q24. Comprehensive — SLM with residual, disposal, and balance sheet. (8 marks)

Machine bought 1 April 2021 for **Rs 7,20,000**; residual **Rs 20,000**; life **5 years**; SLM; provision method. On **31 March 2024** it is sold for **Rs 2,50,000**. Compute annual depreciation, accumulated depreciation at sale, WDV, and profit/loss.

**Solution.**
Annual depreciation = (7,20,000 − 20,000) ÷ 5 = 7,00,000 ÷ 5 = **Rs 1,40,000 per year.**
Accumulated depreciation to 31 Mar 2024 (3 full years) = 3 × 1,40,000 = **Rs 4,20,000.**
WDV at sale = 7,20,000 − 4,20,000 = **Rs 3,00,000.**
Proceeds 2,50,000 − WDV 3,00,000 = **Loss on sale Rs 50,000.**

**Disposal A/c check:** Dr cost 7,20,000 = Cr [provision 4,20,000 + bank 2,50,000 + P&L loss 50,000] = 7,20,000. ✔
