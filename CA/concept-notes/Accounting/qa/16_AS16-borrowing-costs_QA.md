# Q&A — AS 16 — Borrowing Costs

> Companion question bank to the concept chapter. Every question is followed immediately by a complete model answer. All figures in Rupees (₹). Based on ICAI AS 16.

---

## Section A — Concept-check Questions (test the WHY)

**A1. What is the core recognition principle of AS 16?**
Borrowing costs that are **directly attributable** to the acquisition, construction or production of a **qualifying asset** are **capitalised** as part of the cost of that asset. **All other** borrowing costs are recognised as an **expense** in the period in which they are incurred. The logic: interest on funds tied up in building an asset is as much a cost of getting that asset ready as bricks and labour, so it belongs in the asset's carrying amount.

**A2. Define a "qualifying asset". Give the decisive test.**
A qualifying asset is one that **necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale**. The decisive test is *time to get ready*, not size or cost. Examples: manufacturing plants, power stations, inventories requiring long maturing (e.g. wine, aged spirits), investment properties under construction. **Not** qualifying: assets ready for use when acquired, and inventories routinely mass-produced over short periods.

**A3. Why does AS 16 subtract income earned on the temporary investment of specific borrowings, but not for general borrowings?**
For a **specific** borrowing, the surplus funds not yet spent are, by definition, the borrowed money itself; any income earned on parking them is a direct offset to the actual cost of that borrowing, so it is **deducted**. For **general** borrowings, capitalisation is based on applying a **capitalisation rate** to expenditure — there is no identifiable idle balance of a specific loan, so no such deduction is prescribed.

**A4. Why is the amount capitalised on general borrowings capped at actual borrowing costs incurred?**
The capitalisation-rate method is an *estimate* of the cost of financing the asset from the general pool. You cannot capitalise more interest than the entity actually incurred in the period — that would create fictitious cost. Hence the ceiling: amount capitalised ≤ actual borrowing costs incurred during the period.

**A5. State the three conditions for COMMENCEMENT of capitalisation.**
All three must be met: (a) **expenditure** on the asset is being incurred; (b) **borrowing costs** are being incurred; and (c) **activities** necessary to prepare the asset for its intended use/sale are in progress (includes technical/administrative work such as obtaining permits, not merely physical construction).

**A6. When must capitalisation be SUSPENDED, and when is it NOT suspended?**
Capitalisation is **suspended** during **extended periods** in which active development is **interrupted**. It is **not** suspended when: (i) substantial technical/administrative work is being carried on, or (ii) a temporary delay is a **necessary part** of getting the asset ready (e.g. high water levels expected during construction of a bridge that delay construction in a region).

**A7. When does capitalisation CEASE?**
When **substantially all the activities** necessary to prepare the qualifying asset for its intended use or sale are **complete** — not when it is legally handed over or first used. If completed in parts and each part is usable while others continue (e.g. a business park of several buildings), capitalisation ceases for each part as it is completed.

**A8. Can exchange differences on foreign-currency borrowings be borrowing costs?**
Yes — to the extent they are regarded as an **adjustment to interest cost**. AS 16 includes "exchange differences arising from foreign currency borrowings to the extent that they are regarded as an adjustment to interest costs" within the definition of borrowing costs. (The precise measurement interacts with AS 11 / the para 4(e) benchmark.)

## Section B — Graded Computational Problems

### B1 (Easy) — Specific borrowing with temporary investment income

**Q.** On 1 April 2023, Vega Ltd. borrowed ₹50,00,000 at 10% p.a. **specifically** to construct a factory. Construction ran the whole year (complete 31 March 2024). Before spending it all, surplus funds were temporarily invested, earning ₹80,000. Compute the borrowing cost to be capitalised.

**Solution.**
- Actual borrowing cost = ₹50,00,000 × 10% × 12/12 = **₹5,00,000**
- Less: income on temporary investment of the specific borrowing = (₹80,000)
- **Borrowing cost capitalised = ₹5,00,000 − ₹80,000 = ₹4,20,000**

Journal entry:
```
Factory (Asset) A/c        Dr.   4,20,000
   To Interest / Borrowing Cost A/c   4,20,000
(Being borrowing cost, net of investment income, capitalised)
```

### B2 (Easy–Moderate) — Capitalisation rate on general borrowings

**Q.** Orion Ltd. finances its projects from a general pool: 12% loan ₹30,00,000 and 10% loan ₹20,00,000, both outstanding the whole year. Average expenditure on a qualifying asset during the year was ₹24,00,000, all funded from the general pool. Compute the borrowing cost to be capitalised.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — Total general borrowing cost:
- 12% × ₹30,00,000 = ₹3,60,000
- 10% × ₹20,00,000 = ₹2,00,000
- Total = ₹5,60,000 on total borrowings ₹50,00,000

Step 2 — Capitalisation rate = ₹5,60,000 / ₹50,00,000 = **11.2%**

Step 3 — Amount to capitalise = 11.2% × ₹24,00,000 = **₹2,68,800**

Step 4 — Ceiling check: ₹2,68,800 ≤ actual ₹5,60,000. ✔ OK.
**Capitalise ₹2,68,800**; balance ₹5,60,000 − ₹2,68,800 = ₹2,91,200 expensed.

### B3 (Moderate) — Weighted-average expenditure with dated payments

**Q.** Sirius Ltd. constructed a qualifying asset during FY 2023-24 (whole year). Payments: 1 April 2023 ₹18,00,000; 1 October 2023 ₹12,00,000. Financed entirely from general borrowings: 10% debentures ₹40,00,000 and 13% term loan ₹10,00,000, both outstanding all year. Compute the amount to be capitalised.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — Weighted-average expenditure (weight by months outstanding in the year):
- ₹18,00,000 × 12/12 = ₹18,00,000
- ₹12,00,000 × 6/12 = ₹6,00,000
- **Weighted-average expenditure = ₹24,00,000**

Step 2 — Capitalisation rate:
- Interest: (10% × 40,00,000) + (13% × 10,00,000) = ₹4,00,000 + ₹1,30,000 = ₹5,30,000
- Total general borrowings = ₹50,00,000
- Rate = ₹5,30,000 / ₹50,00,000 = **10.6%**

Step 3 — Capitalise = 10.6% × ₹24,00,000 = **₹2,54,400**

Step 4 — Ceiling: ₹2,54,400 ≤ ₹5,30,000. ✔
Total cost of asset = ₹30,00,000 (expenditure) + ₹2,54,400 = **₹32,54,400**.

### B4 (Exam-hard) — Specific + general borrowings combined

**Q.** Polaris Ltd. began constructing a plant on 1 April 2023; completed 31 March 2024. Expenditure incurred: 1 April 2023 ₹24,00,000; 1 October 2023 ₹12,00,000. Financing:
- **Specific loan** of ₹20,00,000 @ 8% p.a., drawn on 1 April 2023, wholly used for this plant.
- **General borrowings** outstanding all year: 10% loan ₹30,00,000; 12% loan ₹20,00,000.

Compute the borrowing cost to be capitalised and the total cost of the plant.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — Cost on the specific borrowing (full year):
₹20,00,000 × 8% × 12/12 = **₹1,60,000**

Step 2 — Weighted-average expenditure on the asset:
- ₹24,00,000 × 12/12 = ₹24,00,000
- ₹12,00,000 × 6/12 = ₹6,00,000
- Total weighted-average expenditure = **₹30,00,000**

Step 3 — Expenditure financed by the general pool = weighted-average expenditure − specific borrowing used = ₹30,00,000 − ₹20,00,000 = **₹10,00,000**

Step 4 — Capitalisation rate for general pool:
- Interest = (10% × 30,00,000) + (12% × 20,00,000) = ₹3,00,000 + ₹2,40,000 = ₹5,40,000
- Total general borrowings = ₹50,00,000
- Rate = ₹5,40,000 / ₹50,00,000 = **10.8%**

Step 5 — Cost on general borrowings = 10.8% × ₹10,00,000 = **₹1,08,000**
Ceiling check: ₹1,08,000 ≤ actual general interest ₹5,40,000. ✔

Step 6 — Total borrowing cost capitalised = ₹1,60,000 + ₹1,08,000 = **₹2,68,000**
Total cost of plant = ₹36,00,000 (expenditure) + ₹2,68,000 = **₹38,68,000**

Journal entry:
```
Plant A/c                  Dr.   2,68,000
   To Interest / Borrowing Cost A/c   2,68,000
(Being borrowing cost capitalised: ₹1,60,000 specific + ₹1,08,000 general)
```

### B5 (Exam-hard) — Suspension of capitalisation

**Q.** Antares Ltd. took a specific loan of ₹60,00,000 @ 11% p.a. on 1 April 2023 to construct a bridge. Active construction ran April–September and again January–March. From **1 October to 31 December 2023 (3 months)** construction was suspended because of a **labour strike** (not a necessary part of getting the asset ready). Determine the borrowing cost to be capitalised and expensed for FY 2023-24.

**Solution.**
Total interest for the year = ₹60,00,000 × 11% = ₹6,60,000 (₹55,000 per month).

- Active development: April–Sept (6 months) + Jan–Mar (3 months) = **9 months** → capitalise.
- Suspension due to strike: Oct–Dec (3 months) → **expense** (extended interruption of active development).

Capitalised = ₹55,000 × 9 = **₹4,95,000**
Expensed (charged to Statement of P&L) = ₹55,000 × 3 = **₹1,65,000**
Check: ₹4,95,000 + ₹1,65,000 = ₹6,60,000. ✔

*Note:* had the delay been a *necessary* part of construction (e.g. monsoon flooding expected for that terrain), the full ₹6,60,000 would be capitalised.

### B6 (Exam-hard) — Cessation part-way; non-qualifying asset trap

**Q.** Rigel Ltd. borrowed ₹40,00,000 @ 9% specifically on 1 April 2023. The qualifying asset was **substantially complete on 31 December 2023** but was actually put to use only on 1 March 2024. Surplus specific funds earned ₹30,000 (all before 31 December 2023). Compute the borrowing cost to capitalise.

**Solution.**
Capitalisation ceases when **substantially all activities are complete (31 Dec 2023)**, *not* when the asset is first used (1 Mar 2024). So capitalise for 9 months (Apr–Dec); interest from 1 Jan 2024 onward is expensed.

- Capitalisable interest = ₹40,00,000 × 9% × 9/12 = ₹2,70,000
- Less: temporary investment income = (₹30,000)
- **Capitalised = ₹2,40,000**
- Expensed (Jan–Mar) = ₹40,00,000 × 9% × 3/12 = **₹90,000**

## Section C — Past-paper-style Full Questions (ICAI pattern)

### C1

**Q.** Zenith Ltd. is constructing a fixed asset. Following are the details (₹):
- Expenditure on the asset: 1 April 2023 — ₹50,00,000; 1 January 2024 — ₹30,00,000. Construction complete 31 March 2024.
- Borrowings during 2023-24: a **specific** 8% loan of ₹30,00,000 (drawn 1 April 2023 for this asset), and a **general** 11% loan of ₹50,00,000 (outstanding all year).

Determine the total cost of the asset as per AS 16. (Assume general loan is the only general borrowing.)

**Model Answer.**
Step 1 — Specific borrowing cost: ₹30,00,000 × 8% = **₹2,40,000** (full year).

Step 2 — Weighted-average expenditure:
- ₹50,00,000 × 12/12 = ₹50,00,000
- ₹30,00,000 × 3/12 (Jan–Mar) = ₹7,50,000
- Total = **₹57,50,000**

Step 3 — Financed by general pool = ₹57,50,000 − ₹30,00,000 (specific) = **₹27,50,000**

Step 4 — Capitalisation rate (general) = ₹5,50,000 / ₹50,00,000 = 11% (i.e. the single 11% loan).

Step 5 — General cost capitalised = 11% × ₹27,50,000 = **₹3,02,500** (≤ actual ₹5,50,000 ✔)

Step 6 — Total borrowing cost capitalised = ₹2,40,000 + ₹3,02,500 = **₹5,42,500**
Total cost of asset = ₹80,00,000 + ₹5,42,500 = **₹85,42,500**.

### C2

**Q.** During FY 2023-24, Comet Ltd. incurred the following on a qualifying asset and claimed the *entire* interest of ₹9,00,000 on its general borrowings as capitalised. The company's general borrowings were ₹60,00,000 at a weighted rate of 15% (₹9,00,000). Weighted-average expenditure on the asset was ₹40,00,000. Two sub-issues: (i) Is the company correct? (ii) The company also earned ₹1,20,000 by investing general-pool surplus and deducted it — correct?

**Model Answer.**
(i) **Incorrect.** Capitalisation on general borrowings = capitalisation rate × expenditure on the asset = 15% × ₹40,00,000 = **₹6,00,000**, subject to a ceiling of actual cost ₹9,00,000. The company may capitalise only **₹6,00,000**; the balance ₹3,00,000 must be expensed. Capitalising the full ₹9,00,000 overstates the asset by ₹3,00,000.

(ii) **Incorrect.** The deduction of income earned on temporary investment is permitted **only for specific borrowings**, not for the general pool. AS 16 does not allow netting off income against general borrowing costs. The ₹1,20,000 should be recognised as income, and the capitalisation stays at ₹6,00,000.

### C3

**Q.** Meridian Ltd. is a jeweller that maintains a large inventory of gold ornaments produced continuously and sold within weeks, and separately holds a stock of maturing single-malt whisky that takes 8 years to be saleable. It borrowed generally at 12%. State, with reasons, the treatment of borrowing costs attributable to each inventory. Also state whether a ready-to-use imported machine (installed within a week) is a qualifying asset.

**Model Answer.**
- **Gold ornaments:** *Not* a qualifying asset — routinely manufactured/turned over in a short period; it does **not** take a substantial period to be ready for sale. Related borrowing costs are **expensed**.
- **Maturing whisky (8 years):** *Is* a qualifying asset — inventory that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for sale. Directly attributable borrowing costs are **capitalised** as part of inventory cost.
- **Ready-to-use machine (installed in a week):** *Not* a qualifying asset — it does not require a substantial period to get ready for use; borrowing costs are **expensed**.

### C4 — Disclosure

**Q.** State the disclosures required by AS 16.

**Model Answer.** An enterprise should disclose:
1. The **accounting policy** adopted for borrowing costs; and
2. The **amount of borrowing costs capitalised** during the period.

(There is no requirement under AS 16 to disclose the capitalisation rate; that requirement belongs to Ind AS 23, not AS 16 — a common trap.)

## Section D — MCQs / Case Scenarios

**D1.** A qualifying asset is best identified by:
(a) High cost (b) Being a fixed asset (c) Necessarily taking a **substantial period of time** to get ready for use/sale (d) Being financed by loans.
**Answer: (c)** — the sole test in AS 16 is time to get ready, not cost or nature.

**D2.** Income earned on temporary investment of borrowed funds is deducted from borrowing costs:
(a) Always (b) Only for **specific** borrowings (c) Only for general borrowings (d) Never.
**Answer: (b)** — deduction applies only to specific borrowings.

**D3.** Capitalisation of borrowing costs commences when:
(a) The loan is sanctioned (b) Expenditure and borrowing costs are incurred **and** activities to prepare the asset are in progress (c) Physical construction starts only (d) The asset is put to use.
**Answer: (b)** — all three conditions must be satisfied.

**D4.** Capitalisation ceases when:
(a) The loan is repaid (b) The asset is legally handed over (c) **Substantially all** activities to make the asset ready are complete (d) The asset is first sold.
**Answer: (c)** — completion of substantially all activities, regardless of actual use.

**D5.** General-borrowing capitalisation of ₹8,00,000 is computed by the rate method, but actual borrowing cost incurred is ₹7,00,000. Capitalise:
(a) ₹8,00,000 (b) ₹7,00,000 (c) ₹7,50,000 (d) Nil.
**Answer: (b)** — capped at actual borrowing cost incurred.

**D6.** A 3-month suspension of construction caused by a labour strike:
(a) Is capitalised (b) Requires **suspension** of capitalisation for that period (c) Extends the asset's life (d) Is a prior-period item.
**Answer: (b)** — active development interrupted for an extended period → suspend capitalisation (interest expensed).

**D7.** A monsoon delay that is a *necessary* part of constructing a dam in that region:
(a) Suspends capitalisation (b) Does **not** suspend capitalisation (c) Is expensed (d) Stops the project.
**Answer: (b)** — a delay that is a necessary part of getting the asset ready does not trigger suspension.

**D8. (Case)** Nova Ltd. capitalised the full ₹6,00,000 interest on a general loan even though only 40% of the loan funded the qualifying asset (rate-method figure ₹2,40,000). The auditor should require the asset to be reduced by:
(a) Nil (b) ₹2,40,000 (c) **₹3,60,000** (d) ₹6,00,000.
**Answer: (c)** — only ₹2,40,000 is capitalisable; excess ₹3,60,000 must be reversed and expensed.

**D9.** Exchange differences on foreign-currency borrowings are borrowing costs:
(a) In full (b) **To the extent regarded as an adjustment to interest cost** (c) Never (d) Only if favourable.
**Answer: (b)** — per the AS 16 definition.

**D10.** Under AS 16, disclosure includes:
(a) Capitalisation rate (b) Accounting policy and **amount capitalised** (c) Fair value of asset (d) Loan covenants.
**Answer: (b)** — AS 16 requires only the policy and the amount capitalised (capitalisation-rate disclosure is an Ind AS 23 requirement).
