## Section A — Theory (model answers)

### Q1. Define partnership under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, and list its essential elements.

**Model answer (present as a definition + point list):**
Section 4 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 defines partnership as *"the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all."*

Essential elements:
1. **Association of two or more persons** (maximum 50, per Rule 10 of the Companies Act, 2013).
2. **Agreement** — partnership arises from contract, not status.
3. **Lawful business** carried on.
4. **Sharing of profits** (and, by implication, losses).
5. **Mutual agency** — each partner is principal and agent of the others; this is the *conclusive test* of partnership.

*Presentation tip:* Always quote Section 4 verbatim for the definition mark, then bullet the five elements, and flag **mutual agency** as the real test.

### Q2. State the rules applicable in the absence of a partnership deed.

**Model answer:** In the absence of a deed, or where the deed is silent, Section 13 of the Act applies:
| Matter | Rule |
|---|---|
| Profit/loss sharing | **Equal** |
| Interest on capital | **Not allowed** |
| Interest on drawings | **Not charged** |
| Salary/commission to partner | **Not allowed** |
| Interest on partner's **loan** to firm | **Allowed @ 6% p.a.** |

*Full-marks tip:* Emphasise that interest on a partner's loan (6%) is a **charge against profit** (P&L Account), whereas the others are appropriations — this distinction earns the concept mark.

### Q3. Distinguish between Fixed and Fluctuating Capital Accounts.

**Model answer (present as a comparison table):**
| Basis | Fluctuating | Fixed |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts per partner | One (Capital) | Two (Capital + Current) |
| Capital balance | Changes yearly | Stays fixed |
| Appropriations recorded in | Capital A/c | Current A/c |
| Nature of balance | Normally credit | Current A/c may be Dr or Cr |
| Default method | Yes | Only if deed requires |

### Q4. Distinguish between "dissolution of partnership" and "dissolution of firm."

**Model answer:**
- **Dissolution of partnership** = a change in the relation among partners (admission, retirement, death) — the **business continues** under a reconstituted firm. It does *not* require closing the books.
- **Dissolution of firm** (Section 39) = the business itself ends and the relationship among **all** partners is dissolved; assets are realised and accounts settled via the Realisation Account.
Every dissolution of firm involves dissolution of partnership, but not vice versa.

### Q5. Explain sacrificing ratio and gaining ratio and when each is used.

**Model answer:**
- **Sacrificing ratio = Old ratio − New ratio.** Used on **admission** — it shows the share old partners give up to the incoming partner; goodwill premium is credited to sacrificers in this ratio.
- **Gaining ratio = New ratio − Old ratio.** Used on **retirement/death** — it shows the share continuing partners gain from the outgoer; they compensate the outgoer for goodwill in this ratio.

### Q6. What is goodwill? Why is self-generated goodwill not shown in the books?

**Model answer:** Goodwill is the value of a firm's reputation and earning capacity that enables it to earn **super profits** — a return above the normal return on its capital employed. Per **AS 26 (Intangible Assets)**, only **purchased** goodwill (paid for in a transaction) may be recorded; **self-generated** goodwill is not recognised because its cost cannot be reliably measured and it was never acquired in an exchange. Hence, at admission/retirement it must be *valued* and adjusted through partners' capital accounts rather than recorded as an asset.

### Q7. State the order of settlement of accounts on dissolution (Section 48).

**Model answer:** Section 48 prescribes:
1. Pay the firm's debts to **third parties (outside creditors)**.
2. Repay **partners' loans/advances** to the firm.
3. Repay **partners' capital**.
4. Distribute any **surplus** among partners in their **profit-sharing ratio**.
Losses are met first out of profits, then capital, then by partners individually in their PSR.

### Q8. List four key differences between a traditional partnership and an LLP.

**Model answer:**
| Basis | Partnership | LLP |
|---|---|---|
| Governing law | Partnership Act, 1932 | **LLP Act, 2008** |
| Legal entity | Not separate | **Separate legal entity** |
| Liability | Unlimited | **Limited to contribution** |
| Perpetual succession | No | **Yes** |
LLP audit is compulsory only if turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh; annual filings are Form 8 (Solvency) and Form 11 (Annual Return) with the MCA.

### Q9. When is the "insufficient profit" (proportionate) rule applied?

**Model answer:** When appropriations such as interest on capital and partner salary are treated as **appropriations** (payable only out of profit) and the available profit is **less** than the total of such claims, the profit is distributed among the entitled partners **in the ratio of their claims** — no partner's claim is met in full and no artificial loss is created. This rule does **not** apply where interest on capital is a **guaranteed charge** under the deed, in which case it is paid in full even if that produces a loss.

### Q10. On death of a partner, what additional item is credited that is not usually relevant on ordinary retirement, and how is it computed?

**Model answer:** The deceased partner's **share of profit from the last balance-sheet date to the date of death**. It is computed on either:
- **Time basis:** previous year's (or average) profit × (months from BS date to death ÷ 12) × deceased's share; or
- **Turnover/sales basis:** profit estimated in proportion to sales achieved up to the date of death.
The total amount due is transferred to the **Deceased Partner's Executor's Account**.

## Section B — Numerical problems (fully solved)

### Q11. Profit & Loss Appropriation Account with interest on capital, drawings and salary.

P and Q are partners sharing **2:1**. Capitals (fluctuating): P ₹4,00,000, Q ₹2,00,000. Deed: interest on capital @ 8% p.a.; P's salary ₹5,000 per month; interest on drawings @ 10% p.a.; each partner withdrew ₹6,000 at the **beginning of every month**. Net profit before adjustments ₹2,00,000. Prepare the Appropriation Account.

**Solution:**
- Interest on capital: P = 4,00,000 × 8% = ₹32,000; Q = 2,00,000 × 8% = ₹16,000. Total ₹48,000.
- P's salary = 5,000 × 12 = ₹60,000.
- Interest on drawings (beginning of month → average period 6.5 months): total drawings = 6,000 × 12 = ₹72,000; interest each = 72,000 × 10% × (6.5 ÷ 12) = 72,000 × 0.10 × 0.5417 = ₹3,900. P = ₹3,900, Q = ₹3,900. Total ₹7,800.
- Profit available = 2,00,000 + 7,800 = ₹2,07,800.
- Less appropriations 48,000 + 60,000 = 1,08,000 → Divisible profit = ₹99,800.
- P (2/3) = ₹66,533.33; Q (1/3) = ₹33,266.67.

| Dr — P&L Appropriation A/c | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Interest on Capital: P 32,000; Q 16,000 | 48,000 | By Net Profit b/d | 2,00,000 |
| To Salary — P | 60,000 | By Interest on Drawings: P 3,900; Q 3,900 | 7,800 |
| To Profit: P 66,533; Q 33,267 | 99,800 | | |
| **Total** | **2,07,800** | **Total** | **2,07,800** |

Tally ₹2,07,800. ✔ (Divisible profit rounded: 66,533 + 33,267 = 99,800.)

### Q12. Interest on drawings — product method (irregular drawings).

R withdrew during the year: ₹20,000 on 1 May, ₹10,000 on 1 August, ₹15,000 on 1 November, ₹5,000 on 1 February. Accounting year ends 31 March. Interest on drawings @ 12% p.a. Compute interest by the product method.

**Solution (months outstanding to 31 March):**
| Date | Amount | Months to 31 Mar | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 May | 20,000 | 11 | 2,20,000 |
| 1 Aug | 10,000 | 8 | 80,000 |
| 1 Nov | 15,000 | 5 | 75,000 |
| 1 Feb | 5,000 | 2 | 10,000 |
| | | **Total** | **3,85,000** |

Interest = Total product × rate ÷ 1200 = 3,85,000 × 12 ÷ 1200 = **₹3,850.** ✔

### Q13. Goodwill by four methods.

Profits: Year1 ₹80,000; Year2 ₹1,00,000; Year3 ₹1,20,000. Capital employed ₹5,00,000; normal rate of return 12%. Compute goodwill by (a) 2 years' purchase of average profit, (b) super profit at 2 years' purchase, (c) capitalisation of super profit, (d) capitalisation of average profit.

**Solution:**
- Average profit = (80,000 + 1,00,000 + 1,20,000) ÷ 3 = 3,00,000 ÷ 3 = ₹1,00,000.
- Normal profit = 5,00,000 × 12% = ₹60,000.
- Super profit = 1,00,000 − 60,000 = ₹40,000.

(a) Average profit method = 1,00,000 × 2 = **₹2,00,000.**
(b) Super profit method = 40,000 × 2 = **₹80,000.**
(c) Capitalisation of super profit = 40,000 × 100 ÷ 12 = **₹3,33,333.**
(d) Capitalisation of average profit: capitalised value = 1,00,000 × 100 ÷ 12 = ₹8,33,333; goodwill = 8,33,333 − 5,00,000 = **₹3,33,333.**

(Note (c) and (d) agree — both capitalise the super profit — a good internal check.) ✔

### Q14. Admission — new ratio, sacrificing ratio, goodwill distribution.

A and B share **5:3**. C is admitted for **1/4** share, which he acquires **entirely from A**. Goodwill of the firm ₹1,60,000; C brings his share of goodwill in cash. Compute the new ratio, sacrificing ratio and goodwill credited to each old partner.

**Solution:**
- C's share = 1/4, taken wholly from A.
- A's new share = 5/8 − 1/4 = 5/8 − 2/8 = 3/8. B unchanged = 3/8. C = 1/4 = 2/8.
- **New ratio A:B:C = 3:3:2.**
- Sacrificing ratio: A sacrifices 5/8 − 3/8 = 2/8 = 1/4; B sacrifices 3/8 − 3/8 = 0. **A alone sacrifices.**
- C's goodwill = 1,60,000 × 1/4 = ₹40,000, credited **entirely to A**.
Entries: Bank Dr 40,000 → Premium for Goodwill 40,000; Premium Dr 40,000 → A's Capital 40,000. ✔

Prepare the Revaluation Account and show the profit/loss shared.

**Solution:**

| Dr — Revaluation A/c | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Stock (decrease) | 8,000 | By Machinery (increase) | 40,000 |
| To Provision for Doubtful Debts | 4,000 | | |
| To Unrecorded Creditor | 6,000 | | |
| To Revaluation Profit: L 13,200; M 8,800 | 22,000 | | |
| **Total** | **40,000** | **Total** | **40,000** |

Revaluation profit = 40,000 − 18,000 = ₹22,000, shared **3:2** → L = 22,000 × 3/5 = ₹13,200; M = 22,000 × 2/5 = ₹8,800. ✔ (Credited to old partners in old ratio.)

### Q16. Admission — full capital accounts and balance sheet.

### Q17. Retirement — gaining ratio and goodwill adjustment.

G, H, K share **3:2:1**. H retires. G and K continue at **3:2**. Firm goodwill ₹1,20,000. Compute the gaining ratio and pass the goodwill entry.

**Solution:**
- Old: G 3/6, H 2/6, K 1/6. New: G 3/5, K 2/5.
- G gain = 3/5 − 3/6 = 18/30 − 15/30 = 3/30. K gain = 2/5 − 1/6 = 12/30 − 5/30 = 7/30.
- **Gaining ratio G:K = 3:7.** (Total gain 10/30 = 2/6 = H's old share ✔.)
- H's goodwill share = 1,20,000 × 2/6 = ₹40,000, borne by G and K in 3:7 → G = 40,000 × 3/10 = ₹12,000; K = 40,000 × 7/10 = ₹28,000.
Entry: G's Capital Dr 12,000; K's Capital Dr 28,000 → To H's Capital 40,000. ✔

### Q18. Retirement — amount due and transfer to loan account.

Using the firm in Q17 (G, H, K = 3:2:1), assume H's capital before retirement is ₹1,50,000. On retirement: revaluation profit ₹18,000 (shared old ratio 3:2:1); General Reserve ₹36,000 (old ratio). Compute the total due to H and show its transfer.

**Solution:**
- H's capital = ₹1,50,000.
- Add goodwill from gainers (Q17) = ₹40,000.
- Add revaluation profit share = 18,000 × 2/6 = ₹6,000.
- Add General Reserve share = 36,000 × 2/6 = ₹12,000.
- **Total due to H = 1,50,000 + 40,000 + 6,000 + 12,000 = ₹2,08,000**, transferred to **H's Loan A/c** (carries 6% p.a. under Section 37 if unpaid).
Entry: H's Capital A/c Dr 2,08,000 → To H's Loan A/c 2,08,000. ✔

### Q19. Death of a partner — profit share up to date of death.

X, Y, Z share **2:2:1**. Y dies on **30 June 2026** (year ends 31 March). Profit for the year ended 31 March 2026 was ₹3,60,000. As per deed, share of profit to date of death is on a **time basis** using last year's profit. Compute Y's share of profit up to death.

**Solution:**
- Period from 1 April to 30 June = **3 months.**
- Firm profit for 3 months (time basis) = 3,60,000 × 3/12 = ₹90,000.
- Y's share = 90,000 × 2/5 = **₹36,000**, credited to Y's Capital and charged to P&L Suspense A/c (or continuing partners in gaining ratio).
Entry: Profit & Loss Suspense A/c Dr 36,000 → To Y's Capital A/c 36,000. ✔

### Q20. Dissolution — Realisation Account.

Partners S and T share **3:2**. On dissolution the books show: Sundry Assets ₹6,00,000 (Debtors 2,00,000, Stock 1,50,000, Machinery 2,50,000); Creditors ₹1,50,000; Bank ₹40,000; Capitals S ₹3,00,000, T ₹1,90,000. Assets realised: Debtors ₹1,80,000, Stock ₹1,60,000, Machinery ₹2,30,000. Creditors paid ₹1,45,000 in full settlement. Realisation expenses ₹5,000. Prepare the Realisation Account.

**Solution:**

| Dr — Realisation A/c | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Debtors | 2,00,000 | By Creditors | 1,50,000 |
| To Stock | 1,50,000 | By Bank (Debtors) | 1,80,000 |
| To Machinery | 2,50,000 | By Bank (Stock) | 1,60,000 |
| To Bank (Creditors paid) | 1,45,000 | By Bank (Machinery) | 2,30,000 |
| To Bank (expenses) | 5,000 | By Loss transferred: S 18,000; T 12,000 | 30,000 |
| **Total** | **7,50,000** | **Total** | **7,50,000** |

Realisation loss: Dr side before loss = 2,00,000 + 1,50,000 + 2,50,000 + 1,45,000 + 5,000 = 7,50,000; Cr side before loss = 1,50,000 + 1,80,000 + 1,60,000 + 2,30,000 = 7,20,000; **Loss = ₹30,000**, shared 3:2 → S ₹18,000, T ₹12,000. ✔

**Verification of final cash & capitals:**
- Bank: opening 40,000 + realised (1,80,000 + 1,60,000 + 2,30,000 = 5,70,000) = 6,10,000; less creditors 1,45,000 and expenses 5,000 = **₹4,60,000** available for partners.
- S capital after loss = 3,00,000 − 18,000 = ₹2,82,000; T = 1,90,000 − 12,000 = ₹1,78,000; total = ₹4,60,000. ✔ Bank exactly settles both partners to nil.

### Q21. Commission to a partner — before vs after charging.

A firm's net profit before commission is ₹5,50,000. A partner is entitled to a commission of **10%**. Compute the commission (a) before charging such commission, (b) after charging such commission.

**Solution:**
(a) Before: 5,50,000 × 10% = **₹55,000.**
(b) After: 5,50,000 × 10 ÷ 110 = **₹50,000.**
Check (b): profit after commission = 5,50,000 − 50,000 = 5,00,000; 10% of 5,00,000 = 50,000 ✔.

### Q22. Interest on capital when fresh capital is introduced mid-year.

W's capital on 1 April 2025 was ₹3,00,000. He introduced ₹1,20,000 more on 1 October 2025. Interest on capital @ 10% p.a. Compute interest for the year ended 31 March 2026.

**Solution:**
- On ₹3,00,000 for full 12 months = 3,00,000 × 10% = ₹30,000.
- On ₹1,20,000 for 6 months (1 Oct–31 Mar) = 1,20,000 × 10% × 6/12 = ₹6,000.
- **Total interest on capital = ₹36,000.** ✔

### Q23. Insufficient profit — proportionate appropriation.

Partners J and M are entitled to interest on capital: J ₹40,000, M ₹20,000 (total ₹60,000). The firm's profit available for appropriation is only ₹45,000. Interest on capital is an **appropriation** (not a charge). How is the ₹45,000 distributed?

**Solution:** Since profit (₹45,000) is less than total interest claims (₹60,000), distribute in the **ratio of claims 40,000:20,000 = 2:1**:
- J = 45,000 × 2/3 = **₹30,000.**
- M = 45,000 × 1/3 = **₹15,000.**
No residual profit remains; no salary/profit share follows. ✔ (Had interest been a *charge*, full ₹60,000 would be allowed, creating a ₹15,000 loss shared in PSR.)

### Q24. LLP — contribution and profit sharing (short numerical + concept).

Two individuals form an LLP with contributions ₹6,00,000 and ₹4,00,000. The LLP Agreement is **silent** on profit sharing. First-year profit (after allowable partner remuneration) is ₹2,00,000. How is it shared, and which law governs?

**Solution:** An LLP is governed by the **LLP Act, 2008.** Where the LLP Agreement is silent, **Schedule I** of the Act applies, under which partners share profits and losses **equally**, irrespective of contribution (mirroring the Partnership Act's default).
- Therefore profit is shared **equally**: each partner ₹1,00,000.
- (Contribution ratio 6:4 is *irrelevant* when the agreement is silent — a common trap.) ✔
