# Q&A — AS 13 — Accounting for Investments

> Companion question bank to the concept chapter. Every question is followed immediately by its complete model answer. All figures in Rupees (₹). Standard referenced: AS 13 (ICAI), read with AS 9, AS 2, AS 10, AS 28.

## Section A — Concept-check Questions (test the WHY)

**A1. Why does AS 13 value current investments at *lower of cost and fair value* but long-term investments at *cost less permanent diminution*?**
Because the two are held for opposite reasons. A current investment is held to be sold in the near term (the "jeweller" holding stock to trade), so its realisable value matters *now* — prudence demands writing it down to fair value if that is lower, recognising the loss the market has already handed us, while unrealised gains are ignored (asymmetry). A long-term investment (the "grandmother's gold", held for years) will not be sold soon, so day-to-day market wobbles are irrelevant and are *not* recognised; only a diminution that is judged *permanent* (other than temporary) is provided for. The measurement rule follows the holding intention.

**A2. What is the accounting logic for excluding cum-interest and pre-acquisition dividend from the cost of an investment?**
Cost should reflect the price of the *asset* only. When a security is bought cum-interest, part of the price paid is really the seller's accrued interest up to the purchase date — that is a receivable, not part of the asset's cost. Similarly, a dividend/interest declared out of *pre-acquisition* profits is a recovery of capital, not income. Both are therefore stripped out of cost: interest accrued goes to the interest account (recovered on the next interest date) and pre-acquisition dividend is credited to the investment account (reducing cost), so post-acquisition income alone hits the P&L.

**A3. Bonus shares are received "free". Why does the total cost of the investment stay unchanged, and what *does* change?**
No consideration passes for bonus shares, so no cost is added — the total carrying amount is unchanged. What changes is the *number of shares* and hence the *average cost per share*, which falls. This matters on a subsequent partial sale, where cost of shares sold is computed on the reduced average.

**A4. When rights are offered and the investor *renounces (sells)* them instead of subscribing, how is the sale treated, and what is the exception under AS 13?**
Generally, the amount received on renouncing rights attached to shares already held is credited to the Profit & Loss Account (it is income, not a cost reduction). Exception (AS 13 para 13): where the investment was *acquired on a cum-right basis* and the market value immediately after it becomes *ex-right falls below the acquisition cost*, the sale proceeds of the rights may instead be applied to *reduce the carrying amount* to market value.

**A5. A long-term investment's market value has fallen 25% this year. Must it be written down?**
Not automatically. AS 13 requires a write-down only for a diminution that is *other than temporary (permanent)*. The assessment considers duration and extent of the fall, the investee's financial condition, dividend record, and prospects. If judged temporary, no provision is made; if permanent, the carrying amount is reduced and the loss charged to P&L. Each investment is assessed individually.

**A6. On what value is an investment transferred when reclassified between current and long-term, and why "lower of"?**
- Long-term → current: transfer at **lower of cost and carrying amount** at the date of transfer.
- Current → long-term: transfer at **lower of cost and fair value** at the date of transfer.
The "lower of" rule prevents an entity from dodging a loss by simply relabelling an investment — any embedded loss is crystallised on transfer, but latent gains are not booked (prudence).

**A7. How is investment property treated under AS 13, and where does it connect to AS 10 / AS 28?**
Investment property (land/building not held for own use or as inventory) is a *long-term investment*, carried at cost less permanent diminution — not depreciated as an operating asset under AS 10. Impairment thinking links to AS 28. (Under Ind AS 40 it would instead be measured at cost or fair value model — a key AS-vs-Ind-AS distinction.)

## Section B — Graded Computational Problems

### B1 (Easy) — Cost of acquisition
On 10-06-2024, Y Ltd bought 1,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at ₹50 per share and paid brokerage @2% and stamp duty ₹300. Compute the cost of the investment.

**Solution.** Purchase price = 1,000 × 50 = 50,000. Brokerage = 2% × 50,000 = 1,000. Stamp duty = 300. **Cost of investment = 50,000 + 1,000 + 300 = ₹51,300** (all directly attributable acquisition charges are capitalised — AS 13 para 9).

### B2 (Easy–Medium) — Cum-interest purchase
On 01-08-2024, Z bought ₹1,00,000 (nominal) 12% Debentures at ₹98 *cum-interest*. Interest is payable on 30 September and 31 March. Split the price into cost and interest.

**Solution.** Cash paid = 1,00,000 × 98/100 = 98,000. Interest last paid 31-03-2024; accrued 01-04-2024 to 01-08-2024 = 4 months: 1,00,000 × 12% × 4/12 = 4,000. **Cost of investment = 98,000 − 4,000 = ₹94,000**; ₹4,000 debited to Interest. On 30-09-2024 interest received = 1,00,000 × 12% × 6/12 = 6,000, of which 4,000 recovers the accrued amount and **₹2,000 (Aug–Sep) is post-acquisition income** to P&L.

### B3 (Medium) — Current investments at LCFV
A company holds three current investments at year-end:

| Scrip | Cost | Fair value |
|---|---|---|
| A | 50,000 | 45,000 |
| B | 30,000 | 38,000 |
| C | 60,000 | 55,000 |
| **Total** | **1,40,000** | **1,38,000** |

State the carrying amount (a) on an individual basis and (b) on a category (global) basis.

**Solution.**
(a) *Individual* lower of cost/FV: A 45,000 + B 30,000 + C 55,000 = **₹1,30,000** (unrealised gain on B ignored). Write-down to P&L = 1,40,000 − 1,30,000 = 10,000.
(b) *Category* basis: lower of total cost 1,40,000 and total FV 1,38,000 = **₹1,38,000**. Write-down = 2,000.
AS 13 permits either basis consistently; the individual (item-by-item) approach is more prudent.

### B4 (Exam-hard) — Bonus, rights and partial sale on average cost
Investment in equity shares of P Ltd (face value ₹10):
- 01-04-2024: Purchased 5,000 shares @ ₹30 = 1,50,000.
- 01-07-2024: Received bonus shares in ratio **1:2**.
- 01-10-2024: Rights **1:3** at ₹16 per share — fully subscribed.
- 01-01-2025: Sold 4,000 shares @ ₹26 per share.

Show the workings, the profit on sale and the closing carrying amount.

**Solution.**
1. Opening: 5,000 shares, cost 1,50,000.
2. Bonus 1:2 = 5,000/2 = **2,500 shares, cost NIL**. Running: 7,500 shares, cost 1,50,000.
3. Rights 1:3 = 7,500/3 = **2,500 shares × ₹16 = 40,000** added. Running: 10,000 shares, cost 1,90,000.
4. **Average cost = 1,90,000 / 10,000 = ₹19 per share.**
5. Sale of 4,000 shares: proceeds = 4,000 × 26 = 1,04,000; cost of shares sold = 4,000 × 19 = 76,000. **Profit on sale = 1,04,000 − 76,000 = ₹28,000** (to P&L).
6. **Closing: 6,000 shares, carrying amount = 1,90,000 − 76,000 = ₹1,14,000** (= 6,000 × ₹19). ✔ Reconciles.

Journal (sale):
```
Bank A/c                    Dr. 1,04,000
   To Investment in P Ltd A/c        76,000
   To Profit on Sale of Investment   28,000
```

### B5 (Medium) — Reclassification between categories
(i) A long-term investment costing 2,00,000 (carrying amount 2,00,000, no diminution) is reclassified as a current investment; its fair value on that date is 1,70,000.
(ii) A current investment costing 1,00,000, currently carried at fair value 90,000, is reclassified as long-term; fair value on transfer date is 90,000.

**Solution.**
(i) Long-term → current: transfer at **lower of cost (2,00,000) and carrying amount (2,00,000) = ₹2,00,000**. (Fair value is *not* the transfer rule here; the difference between 2,00,000 and 1,70,000 will be tested for write-down only when applying LCFV to the current portfolio afterwards, giving a 30,000 write-down to P&L.)
(ii) Current → long-term: transfer at **lower of cost (1,00,000) and fair value (90,000) = ₹90,000**; the embedded loss of ₹10,000 is charged to P&L on transfer.

## Section C — Past-paper-style Full Question (ICAI pattern)

**C1. Investment (three-column) Account with cum-interest and self-check.**
Q constructs the following in 12% Debentures of ₹100 each (interest payable 30 June and 31 December). Prepare the Investment Account for the year ended 31-12-2024 showing Nominal, Interest and Cost (Principal) columns.
- 01-04-2024: Bought 500 debentures at ₹96 **cum-interest**.
- 30-06-2024: Half-yearly interest received.
- 01-08-2024: Bought 300 debentures at ₹98 **ex-interest**.
- 31-12-2024: Half-yearly interest received; close the account.

**Solution — workings.**
Lot 1 (01-04): cash = 500 × 96 = 48,000. Accrued 01-Jan→01-Apr (3 m) on 50,000 nominal = 50,000 × 12% × 3/12 = **1,500**. Cost = 48,000 − 1,500 = **46,500**.
Interest 30-06 = 50,000 × 12% × 6/12 = **3,000**.
Lot 2 (01-08, ex-interest): cost = 300 × 98 = **29,400**; buyer separately pays accrued 01-Jul→01-Aug (1 m) on 30,000 = 30,000 × 12% × 1/12 = **300**.
Interest 31-12 = 80,000 × 12% × 6/12 = **4,800**.

**12% Debentures Investment Account**

| Date | Particulars | Nominal | Interest | Cost | Date | Particulars | Nominal | Interest | Cost |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 01-04 | To Bank | 50,000 | 1,500 | 46,500 | 30-06 | By Bank (int) | | 3,000 | |
| 01-08 | To Bank | 30,000 | 300 | 29,400 | 31-12 | By Bank (int) | | 4,800 | |
| 31-12 | To P&L (interest) | | 6,000 | | 31-12 | By Balance c/d | 80,000 | | 75,900 |
| | **Total** | **80,000** | **7,800** | **75,900** | | **Total** | **80,000** | **7,800** | **75,900** |

**Self-check (interest to P&L = 6,000):** post-acquisition interest earned = Lot 1 held 9 m (Apr–Dec): 50,000 × 12% × 9/12 = 4,500; Lot 2 held 5 m (Aug–Dec): 30,000 × 12% × 5/12 = 1,500; total = **6,000**. ✔ Cost column closes at 46,500 + 29,400 = **75,900** on 80,000 nominal. ✔

**C2. Diminution in value of a long-term investment.**
R Ltd holds 10,000 equity shares of S Ltd (long-term) at cost ₹8,00,000. At year-end, S Ltd's shares are quoted at ₹55 (value ₹5,50,000). S Ltd has incurred heavy losses for three consecutive years, its net worth has eroded and revival is unlikely. State the treatment.

**Solution.** The fall is *not* a temporary market fluctuation — three years of losses, eroded net worth and poor prospects indicate a **permanent (other than temporary) diminution**. AS 13 requires the carrying amount to be reduced and the loss charged to P&L:
```
Profit & Loss A/c                          Dr. 2,50,000
   To Provision for Diminution in Investments   2,50,000
```
Carried at ₹5,50,000. Had the fall been judged temporary, no provision would be made (long-term investments are otherwise carried at cost). Each investment is assessed individually — not netted against gains on other holdings.

## Section D — MCQs / Case Scenarios

**D1.** Interest accrued and included in the price of a cum-interest purchase is:
(a) added to cost of investment (b) charged to P&L (c) debited to interest and recovered on next interest date (d) ignored.
**Answer: (c).** It is the seller's pre-acquisition interest — a receivable, not part of asset cost.

**D2.** Current investments are carried at:
(a) cost (b) fair value (c) lower of cost and fair value (d) cost less permanent diminution.
**Answer: (c).** Prudence writes them down to fair value if lower; unrealised gains are ignored.

**D3.** Bonus shares received on a long-term equity investment result in:
(a) increase in total cost (b) income to P&L (c) no change in total cost but lower cost per share (d) reduction in total cost.
**Answer: (c).** No consideration passes; only quantity (and hence average cost) changes.

**D4.** On reclassification of a long-term investment as current, transfer is at:
(a) cost (b) fair value (c) lower of cost and carrying amount (d) higher of cost and fair value.
**Answer: (c).** AS 13 para 23.

**D5.** A dividend received out of *pre-acquisition* profits of the investee is:
(a) credited to P&L (b) credited to (reduces) the investment cost (c) added to cost (d) shown as a liability.
**Answer: (b).** It is a recovery of capital, not post-acquisition income.

**D6.** Investment property under AS 13 is classified as a:
(a) current investment (b) long-term investment (c) fixed asset under AS 10 (d) inventory under AS 2.
**Answer: (b).** Long-term investment, carried at cost less permanent diminution.

**D7. Case.** A company's current-investment portfolio has total cost 1,40,000 and total fair value 1,38,000 (individual lower-of figures sum to 1,30,000). It carries the portfolio at 1,38,000. This is:
(a) wrong — must be cost (b) acceptable — category (global) basis (c) wrong — must be fair value always (d) wrong — must be 1,30,000 only.
**Answer: (b).** AS 13 allows LCFV on either an individual *or* category basis, applied consistently.

**D8. Case.** An investor sells rights renounced on shares already held for ₹5,000; the shares were bought long ago and are well above cost (no ex-right fall below cost). The ₹5,000 is:
(a) reduced from cost (b) credited to P&L (c) a capital reserve (d) ignored.
**Answer: (b).** General rule — renunciation proceeds go to P&L; the cost-reduction exception applies only to cum-right purchases where the ex-right value falls below cost.

**D9.** Which cost is used to compute profit when part of a holding (built up via multiple purchases and bonus) is sold?
(a) FIFO cost (b) latest purchase cost (c) weighted-average cost (d) market value.
**Answer: (c).** AS 13 uses average carrying cost for partial disposals.
