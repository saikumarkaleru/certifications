## Section A — Theory (with model answers)

### Q1. What is a Not-for-Profit Organisation? Name the three final statements it prepares. *(3 marks)*

**Model answer.**
A **Not-for-Profit Organisation (NPO)** is an entity established to promote a service, charitable, cultural, educational, religious or social objective — not to earn profit for owners. Examples: clubs, schools, hospitals, charitable trusts, temples and professional bodies.

It prepares three statements:
(a) **Receipts and Payments Account** — a summarised cash book;
(b) **Income and Expenditure Account** — the revenue account showing Surplus or Deficit;
(c) **Balance Sheet** — showing assets, liabilities and the Capital/General Fund.

*Presentation tip:* name all three statements with a one-line purpose each; examiners award a mark per correctly identified and described statement.

---

### Q2. Distinguish between the Receipts and Payments Account and the Income and Expenditure Account. *(5 marks)*

**Model answer.**

| Basis | Receipts & Payments A/c | Income & Expenditure A/c |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Real account (summarised cash book) | Nominal account (like Profit & Loss) |
| Sides | Receipts = Debit; Payments = Credit | Expenditure = Debit; Income = Credit |
| Basis of accounting | Cash basis | Accrual basis |
| Capital items | Included | Excluded (only revenue items) |
| Period covered | Past, current and future | Current year only |
| Opening/closing balance | Begins & ends with cash/bank balance | No opening balance; ends with Surplus/Deficit |
| Non-cash items | Not recorded | Recorded (depreciation, loss on sale) |
| Objective | Shows cash movement | Shows performance (Surplus/Deficit) |

*Presentation tip:* a tabular answer earns full marks quickly; make sure to state the *sides* and the *basis* — the two most examinable distinctions.

---

### Q3. How is the treatment of a legacy different from a general donation? Give the reasoning. *(4 marks)*

**Model answer.**
A **legacy** is an amount received under the **will** of a deceased person. Being a **non-recurring windfall**, it is **capital in nature** and is added to the **Capital Fund** in the Balance Sheet — not credited to the Income and Expenditure Account. (If the legacy is small and of a recurring character, or the question so directs, it may be treated as revenue income.)

A **general donation** (small, no specific purpose) is a **recurring revenue receipt** and is **credited to the Income and Expenditure Account**. However, a donation received for a **specific purpose** (e.g., building) is capital in nature and is credited to the relevant **Special Fund**.

*Reasoning:* the classification follows economic substance — one-time windfalls and purpose-restricted amounts add to permanent resources (capital), whereas recurring, unrestricted amounts are income of the year (revenue).

*Presentation tip:* always give both the treatment AND the "recurring vs one-time / restricted vs unrestricted" reasoning — the reasoning mark is easy to miss.

---

### Q4. How are life membership fees treated, and why are they not credited to income? *(3 marks)*

**Model answer.**
**Life membership fees** are a lump sum paid once by a member in lieu of annual subscriptions for life. They are **capital in nature** and are credited to a **Life Membership Fund** (or added to the Capital Fund) shown on the liabilities side — **not** to the Income and Expenditure Account.

*Why:* the fee represents payment for **membership over many future years**, not just the current year. Recognising the entire amount as income in the year of receipt would overstate that year's Surplus and violate the matching concept. (Some organisations transfer a fixed annual amount from the fund to income each year to represent the subscription "consumed"; at Foundation the whole amount is capitalised.)

---

### Q5. Explain the treatment of a Special Fund (e.g., a Building Fund) and the income earned on its investments. *(4 marks)*

**Model answer.**
A **Special / Specific Fund** is created for a designated purpose from purpose-restricted donations, subscriptions or grants. Treatment:

- The fund is shown as a **separate liability** in the Balance Sheet (not merged into the Capital Fund).
- **Donations and income earned on the fund's investments** (e.g., interest on Building Fund investments) are **added to the fund**, NOT credited to the Income and Expenditure Account.
- **Expenses met out of the fund** (e.g., cost of the building, prizes awarded) are **deducted from the fund**, NOT charged to the Income and Expenditure Account.

*Reasoning:* the resources are legally/purpose-restricted, so both the fund's income and its outgo bypass the general revenue account. Only if the question directs the transfer of a fund's balance does it touch the Capital Fund or income.

---

### Q6. State the accounting treatment of the following: (i) entrance fees, (ii) sale of old newspapers, (iii) purchase of furniture, (iv) subscriptions received in advance. *(4 marks)*

**Model answer.**
(i) **Entrance fees** — generally treated as **revenue income** and credited to the Income and Expenditure Account (members join every year, so it is fairly regular), unless the question directs capitalisation.
(ii) **Sale of old newspapers/scrap** — a small recurring receipt → **revenue income**, credited to the Income and Expenditure Account.
(iii) **Purchase of furniture** — **capital expenditure** → shown as an asset in the Balance Sheet (only its depreciation goes to Income and Expenditure).
(iv) **Subscriptions received in advance** — a **liability** shown on the liabilities side of the Balance Sheet (income of a future year, not yet earned).

---

### Q7. Why must capital items be excluded from the Income and Expenditure Account? *(3 marks)*

**Model answer.**
The Income and Expenditure Account measures the **performance of a single year** by matching that year's revenue income against that year's revenue expenditure. **Capital items** — such as the purchase of a building or furniture, investments made, legacies received or the sale proceeds of an asset — provide benefits over **many years** or represent **additions to/withdrawals from permanent resources**. Charging them against one year's income would distort the Surplus/Deficit and misrepresent both performance and the asset position. Hence they are recorded only in the Balance Sheet (and in the Receipts and Payments Account on a cash basis). Only the *depreciation* of a capital asset — the portion consumed this year — enters the Income and Expenditure Account.

---

### Q8. Write the formula to compute subscription income for the Income and Expenditure Account, and state how each adjusting item appears in the Balance Sheet. *(4 marks)*

**Model answer.**

> **Subscription income = Subscriptions received during the year + Outstanding at the end − Outstanding at the beginning + Advance at the beginning − Advance at the end.**

Balance Sheet treatment of the adjusting items:
- **Outstanding subscriptions (closing)** → **asset** (subscriptions receivable).
- **Subscriptions received in advance (closing)** → **liability** (income received in advance).

*Presentation tip:* write the formula first, then map each term; a common mistake is subtracting the closing outstanding — remember, closing outstanding is *added* (earned but not yet received).

### Q9. Compute subscription income. *(4 marks)*

Subscriptions received during 2025-26 = Rs 88,000. Outstanding: opening Rs 7,000, closing Rs 11,000. Advance: opening Rs 3,000, closing Rs 4,500. Find the amount to be credited to the Income and Expenditure Account.

**Solution.**
Subscription income = 88,000 + closing outstanding 11,000 − opening outstanding 7,000 + opening advance 3,000 − closing advance 4,500 = **Rs 90,500.**

**Verification via Subscriptions Account:**

| Subscriptions A/c (Dr) | Rs | (Cr) | Rs |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Balance b/d (outstanding) | 7,000 | By Balance b/d (advance) | 3,000 |
| To Income & Expenditure (bal.) | 90,500 | By Bank (received) | 88,000 |
| To Balance c/d (advance) | 4,500 | By Balance c/d (outstanding) | 11,000 |
| | **1,02,000** | | **1,02,000** |

Dr total = 7,000 + 90,500 + 4,500 = 1,02,000; Cr total = 3,000 + 88,000 + 11,000 = 1,02,000. ✓ **Answer: Rs 90,500.**

### Q12. Find the opening Capital Fund. *(4 marks)*

On 1 April 2025, *Unity Club* had: Furniture Rs 40,000, Investments Rs 80,000, Subscriptions outstanding Rs 6,000, Cash Rs 14,000, Prize Fund Rs 30,000, Salaries outstanding Rs 4,000. Compute the opening Capital Fund.

| Salaries outstanding | 4,000 | Subscriptions outstanding | 6,000 |
| | | Cash | 14,000 |
| | **1,40,000** | | **1,40,000** |

Balanced. ✓ **Answer: Rs 1,06,000.**

### Q15. Subscriptions with both opening and closing arrears and advances. *(5 marks)*

For *Melody Club*, subscriptions received in 2025-26 were Rs 2,00,000. Opening outstanding Rs 18,000, closing outstanding Rs 22,000, opening advance Rs 9,000, closing advance Rs 6,000. Additionally, Rs 3,000 of the opening outstanding is now considered irrecoverable and is to be written off. Compute the subscription income for the Income and Expenditure Account and its net effect.

**Solution.**
Base subscription income = 2,00,000 + 22,000 − 18,000 + 9,000 − 6,000 = **2,07,000.**
The Rs 3,000 written off is a separate **expense** (bad debts / subscriptions written off) charged to the Income and Expenditure Account debit side; it does not alter the subscription income figure, which reflects amounts earned.

- **Subscription income (credit side):** Rs 2,07,000.
- **Subscriptions written off (debit side):** Rs 3,000.
- **Net effect on Surplus:** +2,07,000 − 3,000 = **+Rs 2,04,000.**

*Balance Sheet:* closing outstanding shown at 22,000 (already excludes the Rs 3,000 written off, assuming it was part of a different tranche; if the Rs 3,000 was within opening arrears now realised as bad, closing outstanding of new arrears stands at 22,000). Closing advance liability Rs 6,000.

### Q16. Treatment decision — donations. *(4 marks)*

*Hope Foundation* received during the year: (a) Rs 1,00,000 donation specifically "for construction of a new ward"; (b) Rs 25,000 general donations from various small donors; (c) Rs 5,00,000 donation from a philanthropist with instruction that it be "kept as a permanent endowment, only income to be used." Show the treatment of each.

**Solution.**

| Donation | Nature | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Rs 1,00,000 for a new ward | Specific-purpose (capital) | Credited to a **Ward Construction Fund** (Special Fund) on liabilities side; construction cost later met from this fund |
| (b) Rs 25,000 general | Revenue | Credited to the **Income and Expenditure Account** as income |

*Reasoning:* purpose-restricted and permanent amounts add to restricted/permanent resources (capital → funds); only the small, unrestricted recurring donation is free income of the year (revenue).

### Q18. Entrance fees — capitalise vs revenue. *(4 marks)*

A club received entrance fees of Rs 40,000 during the year. (a) Show the treatment if the club's policy is to capitalise entrance fees. (b) Show the treatment if the question is silent. (c) State the effect on Surplus in each case.

**Solution.**
(a) **Capitalise:** the entire Rs 40,000 is added to the **Capital Fund** in the Balance Sheet; it does NOT appear in the Income and Expenditure Account. **Effect on Surplus: nil.**
(b) **If silent:** the general/ICAI approach at Foundation is to treat entrance fees as **revenue income**, credited to the Income and Expenditure Account, stating the assumption. **Effect on Surplus: increases by Rs 40,000.**
(c) Summary:

| Policy | Where it goes | Effect on Surplus |
|---|---|---|
| Capitalise | Capital Fund | No effect |
| Revenue (if silent) | I&E credit | +Rs 40,000 |

*Presentation tip:* whenever the question is silent, state your assumption explicitly — examiners award the mark for a clearly reasoned, consistent treatment.

### Q21. Deficit scenario. *(5 marks)*

*Aid Society's* revenue incomes for the year were: Subscriptions Rs 80,000, Donations (general) Rs 10,000, Interest Rs 5,000. Revenue expenditures were: Salaries Rs 60,000, Rent Rs 24,000, Relief distributed Rs 20,000, Depreciation Rs 4,000. Opening Capital Fund Rs 1,50,000. Compute the Surplus/Deficit and the closing Capital Fund.

**Solution.**
Total income = 80,000 + 10,000 + 5,000 = **95,000.**
Total expenditure = 60,000 + 24,000 + 20,000 + 4,000 = **1,08,000.**
Since expenditure > income, there is a **Deficit = 1,08,000 − 95,000 = Rs 13,000.**

**Income and Expenditure Account:**

| Expenditure (Dr) | Rs | Income (Cr) | Rs |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Salaries | 60,000 | By Subscriptions | 80,000 |
| To Rent | 24,000 | By Donations | 10,000 |
| To Relief distributed | 20,000 | By Interest | 5,000 |
| To Depreciation | 4,000 | By Deficit (bal. fig.) | 13,000 |
| | **1,08,000** | | **1,08,000** |

**Closing Capital Fund = Opening 1,50,000 − Deficit 13,000 = Rs 1,37,000.**

*Check:* the Deficit appears on the *credit* (income) side as a balancing figure and is *deducted* from the Capital Fund. ✓

### Q22. Life membership with annual transfer. *(5 marks)*

*Elite Club* charges life membership fees of Rs 12,000 per member. During 2025-26, 10 new life members joined. ### Q23. Purchase and sale of a fixed asset. *(5 marks)*

*Vista Club* had sports equipment of Rs 80,000 (net) on 1 April 2025. During the year it sold old equipment (book value Rs 15,000) for Rs 18,000 and bought new equipment for Rs 40,000 on 1 October 2025. Depreciation is 10% p.a. on the closing balance of the year-end holding (charge full year on assets held at year-end; ignore depreciation on the item sold and pro-rate the new one by half-year). Show (a) profit/loss on sale, (b) depreciation, and (c) the closing equipment figure.

**Solution.**
(a) **Profit on sale** = Sale proceeds 18,000 − Book value 15,000 = **Rs 3,000** → credited to Income and Expenditure Account. Sale proceeds Rs 18,000 appear in Receipts and Payments only.

(b) **Depreciation:**
- On remaining old equipment = 80,000 − 15,000 (sold) = 65,000 → 10% × 65,000 = **6,500** (full year).
- On new equipment 40,000 (bought 1 Oct) → 10% × 40,000 × 6/12 = **2,000.**
- Total depreciation = 6,500 + 2,000 = **Rs 8,500** → Income and Expenditure debit.

(c) **Closing equipment figure:**

| Sports equipment | Rs |
|---|---:|
| Opening (net) | 80,000 |
| Less: Book value of item sold | (15,000) |
| Add: New equipment purchased | 40,000 |
| Less: Depreciation for the year | (8,500) |
| **Closing balance** | **96,500** |

*Check:* 80,000 − 15,000 + 40,000 − 8,500 = **96,500.** ✓
**Answers:** (a) profit Rs 3,000; (b) depreciation Rs 8,500; (c) closing equipment Rs 96,500.

### Q24. Quick MCQ-style set (state and justify). *(4 marks)*

State True or False with a one-line reason:
(a) The Receipts and Payments Account records only revenue items.
(b) A surplus increases the Capital Fund.
(c) Subscriptions received in advance are shown as an asset.
(d) Depreciation appears in the Receipts and Payments Account.

**Solution.**
(a) **False.** It records all cash items — capital and revenue, past, current and future.
(b) **True.** Surplus (excess of income over expenditure) is added to the Capital Fund.
(c) **False.** Subscriptions received in advance are a **liability** (income of a future year); outstanding subscriptions are the asset.
(d) **False.** Depreciation is a non-cash item; it appears only in the Income and Expenditure Account (and reduces the asset in the Balance Sheet), never in the Receipts and Payments Account.
