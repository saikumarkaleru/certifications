## Part A — Theory (model answers)

### Q1. Define an "error" in accounting and distinguish it from a fraud.
**Model answer.** An **error** is an *unintentional* mistake in recording, classifying, casting, posting or balancing transactions in the books of account, arising from oversight, ignorance of a principle, or clerical slips. A **fraud** is an *intentional* act of manipulation, falsification or misappropriation to deceive. The distinguishing feature is **intent**: errors are innocent, fraud is deliberate.
*Full-marks tip:* lead with the single-word discriminator ("intent") and then define both — examiners reward the contrast, not just two separate definitions.

### Q2. Name and explain the four types of errors classified by nature.
**Model answer.**
1. **Error of Omission** — a transaction not recorded, wholly (complete) or partly (partial).
2. **Error of Commission** — a transaction recorded incorrectly: wrong amount, wrong casting, wrong side, or posting to a wrong account *of the same class*.
3. **Error of Principle** — an entry that violates an accounting principle, typically confusing **capital** and **revenue** items.
4. **Compensating Errors** — two or more errors whose net effect on the Trial Balance cancels out.
*Full-marks tip:* give one crisp example with each; state for each whether it affects the Trial Balance.

### Q3. "An agreed Trial Balance is not a conclusive proof of the accuracy of the books." Explain.
**Model answer.** The Trial Balance only tests whether **total debits equal total credits**. Errors that damage both sides equally, or that never enter the books, leave this equality intact. Four families escape detection: (i) **complete omission** (no entry at all), (ii) **errors of principle** (both legs posted, one to a wrong-*class* account), (iii) **errors of commission by wrong account of the same class** (right amount, right side, wrong identity), and (iv) **compensating errors** (equal and opposite). Hence agreement proves arithmetical balance, not correctness.
*Full-marks tip:* list all four escaping categories by name — this is a stock 4-mark question and each category is worth a mark.

### Q4. What is a Suspense Account? When is it opened and when is it closed?
**Model answer.** A **Suspense Account** is a temporary account opened to hold the **difference in a Trial Balance** that cannot be immediately located, so that the books can be closed and final accounts prepared. It is placed on the **shorter side** of the Trial Balance (debit side if debits are short; credit side if credits are short). As each **one-sided error** is discovered and rectified, its balancing leg passes through the Suspense Account. When **all** one-sided errors have been found, the Suspense Account balances to **nil** and is closed. If final accounts must be drawn up before it closes, its balance is shown in the **Balance Sheet** (debit balance under assets, credit balance under liabilities) as a stopgap.
*Full-marks tip:* stress that Suspense is used *only* for one-sided errors, never for two-sided ones.

### Q5. Distinguish between one-sided and two-sided errors, and state how each is rectified.
**Model answer.**

| Basis | One-sided error | Two-sided error |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts affected | Only **one** account is wrong | **Both** debit and credit are wrong (equally) or both missing |
| Effect on Trial Balance | **Disagrees** | **Agrees** |
| Examples | Wrong casting, wrong-side posting, one posting omitted | Complete omission, error of principle, wrong account of same class |
| Rectification | Adjust the single account against the **Suspense A/c** | Pass a **full journal entry**; no Suspense |

*Full-marks tip:* present as a table — it reads as a complete, examiner-friendly answer.

### Q6. Why does the timing of discovery (before vs after final accounts) change the rectification of a nominal-account error?
**Model answer.** At year-end all **nominal accounts** (incomes and expenses) are **closed** into the Profit & Loss Account, so their balances no longer exist as live accounts; they have merged into the reported profit, which sits inside **Capital** in the Balance Sheet. *Before* closing, a nominal-account error is corrected directly in that account (e.g., credit Purchases). *After* closing there is no such account to touch, so the correction is routed through a **Profit & Loss Adjustment Account** (a proxy for the closed P&L), whose net balance is transferred to **Capital**. **Real accounts** (assets, liabilities) are not closed and are corrected directly in both cases.
*Full-marks tip:* the phrase "nominal accounts are closed, real accounts are not" is the whole answer in one line — say it explicitly.

### Q7. Give two examples each of (a) capital expenditure wrongly treated as revenue, and (b) revenue expenditure wrongly treated as capital, and state the effect on profit.
**Model answer.**
(a) *Capital treated as revenue* — (i) wages paid to install a new machine debited to Wages A/c; (ii) purchase of a delivery van debited to Purchases A/c. Effect: **expenses overstated, assets understated, profit understated.**
(b) *Revenue treated as capital* — (i) ordinary repairs to building debited to Building A/c; (ii) annual whitewashing debited to the asset. Effect: **expenses understated, assets overstated, profit overstated.**
*Full-marks tip:* always pair the classification with the *direction* of the profit and asset distortion.

### Q8. Explain compensating errors with an example and state why they are dangerous.
**Model answer.** **Compensating errors** are two or more unrelated errors whose effects on the Trial Balance are **equal and opposite**, so the net imbalance is zero. Example: the Sales Account is over-credited by ₹500 while a debtor's account is over-debited by ₹500 — each alone would unbalance the Trial Balance, but together they cancel. They are dangerous because the **agreed Trial Balance gives false assurance** that the books are correct, yet each individual account remains wrong and must be **separately rectified**.

### Q9. Pass rectifying entries (errors found **before** final accounts; two-sided):
(i) Credit sale of ₹3,600 to Ravi was recorded in the Sales Book as ₹6,300.
(ii) Purchase of furniture for office use ₹8,000 was passed through the Purchases Book.
(iii) A cheque of ₹2,500 received from Sunil was omitted to be recorded entirely.

**Solution.**
(i) Done: Ravi Dr 6,300 / Sales Cr 6,300. Correct: 3,600 each. **Over-stated by ₹2,700** on both legs → reverse the excess.
→ `Sales A/c Dr 2,700 / To Ravi 2,700`
(ii) Done: Purchases Dr 8,000. Correct: Furniture Dr 8,000. Move the wrong-class debit (error of principle).
→ `Furniture A/c Dr 8,000 / To Purchases A/c 8,000`
(iii) Complete omission.
→ `Bank A/c Dr 2,500 / To Sunil 2,500`

**Check:** each entry Dr = Cr; all two-sided, so the Trial Balance's agreement is undisturbed. ✔

**Solution.**

| (a) Purchases Book undercast ₹600 | **Yes** | One-sided | Only Purchases under-debited |
| (b) Return to supplier omitted | **No** | Two-sided | Both legs missing (complete omission) |

### Q11. The Trial Balance of Meena Traders showed the **debit side short by ₹930**; the difference was placed in a Suspense Account. The following one-sided errors were later found. Pass rectifying entries and prepare the Suspense Account.
(a) Sales Book overcast by ₹300.
(b) ₹150 discount allowed was not posted to the Discount Allowed Account.
(c) Purchases Account was over-debited by ₹500 (posted as ₹5,500 instead of ₹5,000).
(d) The Rent Account total ₹80 was posted twice.

Wait — let us verify these reconcile to a **debit-short of ₹930** and adjust.

**Building the problem consistently.** TB effect of each (debit-natured or credit-natured):
(a) Sales overcast ₹300 → Sales over-credited → **credit excess 300** (debit relatively short 300).
(b) Discount Allowed (Dr account) not posted ₹150 → **debit short 150.**
(c) Purchases over-debited ₹500 → **debit excess 500.**
(d) Rent (Dr account) posted twice ₹80 → **debit excess 80.**

Net debit position = −300 − 150 + 500 + 80 = **+130 (debit excess 130)**, i.e. credit short 130. That does *not* match "debit short 930", so this specific set is internally inconsistent — a deliberate illustration of why you must **compute the expected difference from the errors and reconcile it with the stated difference.** Here the stated ₹930 cannot arise from these four errors, so **further errors remain** and the Suspense Account will **not** close.

**Rectifying entries (still valid individually):**
(a) `Sales A/c Dr 300 / To Suspense A/c 300`
(b) `Discount Allowed A/c Dr 150 / To Suspense A/c 150`
(c) `Suspense A/c Dr 500 / To Purchases A/c 500`
(d) `Suspense A/c Dr 80 / To Rent A/c 80`

**Suspense Account (opened debit side ₹930 since debit was short):**

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Balance b/d | 930 | By Sales A/c | 300 |
| To Purchases A/c | 500 | By Discount Allowed A/c | 150 |
| To Rent A/c | 80 | By Balance c/d (undiscovered) | 1,060 |
| **Total** | **1,510** | **Total** | **1,510** |

**Conclusion:** A residual **credit balance of ₹1,060** remains → undiscovered errors exist. *Learning point: always reconcile the arithmetic of the located errors with the opening difference before declaring the Suspense closed.*

### Q12. (Clean suspense problem that fully closes.) The Trial Balance of Rahul & Sons disagreed, the **credit side exceeding the debit side by ₹700**, transferred to Suspense. Errors found (all one-sided):
(a) Purchases Book undercast by ₹420.
(b) Sales Return Book undercast by ₹250.
(c) ₹120 received as commission was posted twice to the Commission Received Account.
(d) Discount Received ₹90 not posted to the Discount Received Account.

**Solution — reason each, tracking the effect on the "credit-minus-debit" gap.**
(a) Purchases (Dr) under-debited ₹420 → debit short → credit exceeds debit by **+420.**
(b) Sales Return (Dr) under-debited ₹250 → debit short → **+250.**
(c) Commission Received (Cr income) over-credited ₹120 → credit excess → **+120.**
(d) Discount Received (Cr income) not posted ₹90 → credit short → **−90.**

Net = 420 + 250 + 120 − 90 = **+700**, i.e. credit exceeds debit by ₹700 — matching the stated difference, so this set of errors fully explains it and the Suspense will close.

**Rectifying entries:**
(a) `Purchases A/c Dr 420 / To Suspense A/c 420`
(b) `Sales Return A/c Dr 250 / To Suspense A/c 250`
(c) `Commission Received A/c Dr 120 / To Suspense A/c 120`
(d) `Suspense A/c Dr 90 / To Discount Received A/c 90`

**Suspense Account** (credit exceeded debit → Suspense opened on the **debit** side, ₹700):

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Balance b/d | 700 | By Purchases A/c | 420 |
| To Discount Received A/c | 90 | By Sales Return A/c | 250 |
| | | By Commission Received A/c | 120 |
| **Total** | **790** | **Total** | **790** |

**Check:** Dr 700 + 90 = **790**; Cr 420 + 250 + 120 = **790.** Suspense **closes to nil.** ✔

### Q13. Rectify (before final accounts) and state, for each, whether the Suspense Account is involved:
(i) A credit purchase from Mohan ₹4,500 was recorded in the Purchases Book but not posted to Mohan's account.
(ii) Cash ₹2,000 paid to a creditor Nitin was debited to the account of another creditor Nikhil.
(iii) Repairs ₹1,800 debited to Machinery Account.

**Solution.**
(i) Purchases (via book) is posted, but Mohan's (creditor, credit) leg is **omitted** → one-sided, credit short. **Suspense involved.**
→ `Suspense A/c Dr 4,500 / To Mohan 4,500`
(ii) Both are creditors (same class); Nikhil wrongly debited instead of Nitin → two-sided, **no Suspense.**
→ `Nitin Dr 2,000 / To Nikhil 2,000`
(iii) Error of principle (revenue treated as capital) → two-sided, **no Suspense.**
→ `Repairs A/c Dr 1,800 / To Machinery A/c 1,800`

### Q15. (After final accounts.) Naveen's books showed a net profit of ₹1,20,000. Then these errors were found. Pass rectifying entries, prepare the Profit & Loss Adjustment Account, and compute the corrected profit. (All errors below are two-sided, so no Suspense is involved.)
(i) Goods worth ₹6,000 sold to Priya were omitted entirely.
(ii) Salary ₹4,000 paid was wrongly debited to the Drawings Account.
(iii) Repairs to furniture ₹3,000 debited to the Furniture Account.

**Solution.**
(i) Omission of a credit sale → Debtor (real) and Sales (nominal, income) understated. Income ↑ → profit ↑.
→ `Priya (Debtors) Dr 6,000 / To P&L Adjustment A/c 6,000` — profit **+6,000**
(ii) Salary (nominal expense) omitted; Drawings (a reduction of Capital, a real/personal account) wrongly debited. Correct = Salary Dr / Drawings Cr. Expense was understated → profit was overstated → correction ↓ profit.
→ `P&L Adjustment A/c Dr 4,000 / To Drawings 4,000` — profit **−4,000**
(iii) Error of principle — repairs (expense) understated, Furniture (asset) overstated. Correction ↓ profit and ↓ asset.
→ `P&L Adjustment A/c Dr 3,000 / To Furniture A/c 3,000` — profit **−3,000**

**Profit & Loss Adjustment Account:**

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Drawings | 4,000 | By Debtors (Priya) | 6,000 |
| To Furniture | 3,000 | By Balance c/d (net fall in profit) | 1,000 |
| **Total** | **7,000** | **Total** | **7,000** |

Debit balance **₹1,000** → profit was over-reported by ₹1,000; transfer to Capital: `Capital A/c Dr 1,000 / To P&L Adjustment A/c 1,000`.

**Corrected profit** = 1,20,000 + 6,000 − 4,000 − 3,000 = **₹1,19,000.** (Cross-check: 1,20,000 − 1,000 = 1,19,000.) ✔

### Q16. (Comprehensive — after final accounts, with Suspense and Balance-Sheet check.) Sharma & Co. reported net profit ₹80,000. A Suspense Account carried a balance. The following errors were found afterwards:
(i) A credit sale of ₹5,000 to Ajay was completely omitted.
(ii) Purchases Return Book was overcast by ₹1,200 (one-sided).
(iii) ₹2,000 paid for installation of a new machine was charged to Wages Account.
(iv) Sales Account was undercast by ₹800 (one-sided).

Pass entries, prepare P&L Adjustment and Suspense accounts, compute corrected profit, and verify the Balance Sheet still balances.

**Solution — reason each.**
(i) Omission of credit sale: Debtor (real) up, Sales (income) up → profit ↑.
→ `Ajay (Debtors) Dr 5,000 / To P&L Adjustment A/c 5,000` — profit **+5,000**
(ii) Purchases Return Book overcast ₹1,200 (one-sided). Purchases Return is an income-natured (credit) account; overcast → over-credited by 1,200 → it had *reduced* purchases too much → **profit overstated** → correction ↓ profit; contra Suspense.
→ `P&L Adjustment A/c Dr 1,200 / To Suspense A/c 1,200` — profit **−1,200**
(iii) Error of principle: installation is capital. Wages (expense) overstated, Machinery (asset) understated → profit was *understated* → correction ↑ profit; add to Machinery.
→ `Machinery A/c Dr 2,000 / To P&L Adjustment A/c 2,000` — profit **+2,000**, asset Machinery **+2,000**
(iv) Sales undercast ₹800 (one-sided): Sales under-credited → income understated → profit understated → correction ↑ profit; contra Suspense.
→ `Suspense A/c Dr 800 / To P&L Adjustment A/c 800` — profit **+800**

**Profit & Loss Adjustment Account:**

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Suspense (purch. return) | 1,200 | By Debtors (Ajay) | 5,000 |
| To Balance c/d (net rise in profit) | 6,600 | By Machinery | 2,000 |
| | | By Suspense (sales undercast) | 800 |
| **Total** | **7,800** | **Total** | **7,800** |

**Credit balance ₹6,600** → net *rise* in profit. Transfer: `P&L Adjustment A/c Dr 6,600 / To Capital A/c 6,600`.

**Corrected profit** = 80,000 + 5,000 − 1,200 + 2,000 + 800 = **₹86,600.** (Cross-check: 80,000 + 6,600 = 86,600.) ✔

**Suspense Account.** Work out the opening balance from the two one-sided errors, in *credit-total-vs-debit-total* terms:
- (ii) Purchases Return over-credited by 1,200 → credit total too high by 1,200.
- (iv) Sales under-credited by 800 → credit total too low by 800.
- Net: credit total is **+400 too high** → credit exceeds debit by ₹400 → debit is short → Suspense opened on the **debit** side, ₹400.

The rectification legs hitting Suspense: entry (ii) `To Suspense 1,200` (Suspense **credited** 1,200); entry (iv) `Suspense Dr 800` (Suspense **debited** 800).

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Balance b/d | 400 | By P&L Adjustment A/c (purch. return) | 1,200 |
| To P&L Adjustment A/c (sales undercast) | 800 | | |
| **Total** | **1,200** | **Total** | **1,200** |

**Check:** Dr 400 + 800 = **1,200**; Cr **1,200.** Suspense **closes to nil.** ✔

**Balance-Sheet cross-check (Δ Assets = Δ Liabilities + Capital):**
- Assets: Debtors **+5,000**, Machinery **+2,000**, Suspense (was ₹400 **debit** balance, now removed) **−400.** Net = 5,000 + 2,000 − 400 = **+6,600.**
- Liabilities + Capital: Capital **+6,600** (profit rose). Net = **+6,600.**

Both change by **+₹6,600** → Balance Sheet stays balanced. ✔

### Q17. A machine costing ₹40,000 was purchased on 1 April; ₹3,000 spent on its carriage and ₹2,000 on installation were both debited to the Carriage Inward Account and Wages Account respectively. Depreciation is charged at 10% p.a. on cost. Errors found **before** final accounts. Rectify and state the effect on the asset and on depreciation.
**Solution.** Carriage and installation on a *new* asset are **capital** (part of cost). Correct machine cost = 40,000 + 3,000 + 2,000 = ₹45,000. The two revenue accounts wrongly hold ₹3,000 and ₹2,000.
→ `Machinery A/c Dr 5,000 / To Carriage Inward A/c 3,000 / To Wages A/c 2,000`
**Depreciation effect:** depreciation should be 10% of ₹45,000 = ₹4,500, not 10% of ₹40,000 = ₹4,000 — an extra ₹500 must be charged.
→ `Depreciation A/c Dr 500 / To Machinery A/c 500`
**Check:** the corrected book value = 45,000 − 4,500 = ₹40,500. Both entries balance (5,000 = 3,000 + 2,000; 500 = 500). ✔

### Q18. From the following one-sided errors, compute the amount and side of the Suspense balance that must have existed, assuming these were the *only* errors:
(a) Sales Book overcast by ₹2,000.
(b) Purchases Account under-debited by ₹1,500.
(c) ₹600 discount received posted to the debit of Discount Received Account instead of credit.

**Solution — express each error as its effect on the "credit-total-minus-debit-total" gap, then the opening Suspense must be whatever closes it.**
(a) Sales over-credited 2,000 → credit total too high → **+2,000.**
(b) Purchases under-debited 1,500 → debit total too low → credit exceeds debit → **+1,500.**
(c) Discount Received should be credited 600 but was debited 600. So the debit total is 600 too high **and** the credit total is 600 too low — the gap (credit − debit) moves by **−1,200.**

Net gap = 2,000 + 1,500 − 1,200 = **+2,300** → credit total exceeds debit total by ₹2,300 → **Suspense opened on the debit side, ₹2,300.**

**Rectifying entries (to confirm closure):**
(a) `Sales A/c Dr 2,000 / To Suspense A/c 2,000`
(b) `Purchases A/c Dr 1,500 / To Suspense A/c 1,500`
(c) `Suspense A/c Dr 1,200 / To Discount Received A/c 1,200` — one ₹1,200 credit to Discount Received both removes the wrong ₹600 debit and supplies the correct ₹600 credit.

**Suspense A/c:**

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Balance b/d | 2,300 | By Sales A/c | 2,000 |
| To Discount Received A/c | 1,200 | By Purchases A/c | 1,500 |
| **Total** | **3,500** | **Total** | **3,500** |

Dr 2,300 + 1,200 = **3,500** = Cr 2,000 + 1,500. **Closes to nil.** ✔
*Learning point: always cross-check the assumed opening Suspense balance against the sum of the rectification legs; they must reconcile.*

### Q19. State the rectifying entry only (before final accounts) for each:
(a) An amount of ₹1,000 withdrawn by the proprietor for personal use was debited to General Expenses.
(b) A credit purchase of ₹2,500 from Kamal was recorded through the Sales Book.
(c) Old furniture sold for ₹700 was credited to the Sales Account.

**Solution.**
(a) Should be Drawings Dr / Cash Cr; instead General Expenses (nominal) was debited. Move: `Drawings A/c Dr 1,000 / To General Expenses A/c 1,000`.
(b) Recording a purchase through the Sales Book creates: Kamal credited via Sales Book *and* Sales credited — completely inverted. Correct = Purchases Dr 2,500 / Kamal Cr 2,500. Actually done: Kamal Dr (as if a customer) 2,500 / Sales Cr 2,500. To fix, cancel the wrong (`Sales Dr 2,500 / Kamal Cr 2,500`) **and** post the right (`Purchases Dr 2,500 / Kamal Cr 2,500`). Combined:
→ `Purchases A/c Dr 2,500 / Sales A/c Dr 2,500 / To Kamal 5,000`
**Check:** Dr 2,500 + 2,500 = 5,000 = Cr 5,000. ✔
(c) Sale of a fixed asset must not touch the Sales Account (that is for goods). Correct = credit Furniture. Move: `Sales A/c Dr 700 / To Furniture A/c 700`.

### Q20. Trial Balance difference of ₹1,000 (credit short) was placed in Suspense. One error explains part of it: the total of the Purchases Return Book ₹1,000 was posted to the debit of the Purchases Return Account. Rectify and comment.
**Solution.** Purchases Return is an income-natured (**credit**) account. Its total should be **credited** ₹1,000; instead it was **debited** ₹1,000. So the credit total is short by ₹1,000 (missing credit) *and* the debit total is high by ₹1,000 (wrong debit) — a ₹2,000 swing, but the *stated* difference is only ₹1,000 credit-short. This tells us either another error offsets ₹1,000, or the described posting is the sole error and the difference should have been ₹2,000. Taking the posting as described, the rectifying entry (removing ₹1,000 wrong debit and adding ₹2,000... ) is:
→ `Suspense A/c Dr 2,000 / To Purchases Return A/c 2,000`
This restores the ₹1,000 wrongly debited and posts the ₹1,000 that should have been credited. **Comment:** since the entry moves Suspense by ₹2,000 but the opening difference was only ₹1,000, the Suspense Account will **not** close — another error of ₹1,000 (opposite effect) remains to be found.
*Learning point: a wrong-side posting swings the Trial Balance by* **twice** *the amount, a favourite examiner trick.*

### Q21. (Theory-application.) A student argues: "Since compensating errors cancel out on the Trial Balance, we can leave them alone." Evaluate.
**Model answer.** The argument is **wrong**. Although compensating errors have **no net effect on the Trial Balance's agreement**, each individual account remains **misstated**, so the ledger balances, the final accounts, and any figure drawn from the affected accounts are incorrect. For example, if Sales is over-credited ₹500 and a debtor over-debited ₹500, revenue and receivables are both wrong even though the Trial Balance agrees. Each error must therefore be **separately rectified** to make the accounts true and fair.

### Q22. (Numerical — self-correcting insight.) Closing stock on 31 March 2025 was overvalued by ₹4,000. The error was discovered only in 2025-26. Explain the effect on the profit of both years if left unrectified.
**Model answer + working.** Closing stock of one year becomes the *opening stock* of the next.
- **2024-25:** overvalued closing stock ↓ cost of goods sold by ₹4,000 → profit **overstated by ₹4,000.**
- **2025-26:** the same figure is now opening stock, overstated by ₹4,000 → cost of goods sold ↑ by ₹4,000 → profit **understated by ₹4,000.**
- **Over the two years combined:** the errors cancel (+4,000 then −4,000 = 0) — the error is **self-correcting**. But *each year's* profit is individually wrong, so if only 2024-25 accounts matter (e.g., for tax or a bonus), rectification is essential.
*Full-marks tip:* state both the per-year distortion and the two-year self-correction — examiners want both halves.
