### Solution 1(b) — Construction Contracts (AS 7)

**(i) Alpha Ltd.**

| Particulars | ₹ lakh |
|---|---|
| Contract price | 150 |
| Costs incurred to date | 60 |
| Estimated costs to complete | 40 |
| Total estimated cost | 100 |
| Total estimated profit (150 − 100) | 50 |
| Stage of completion = 60 ÷ 100 | 60% |

- **Contract revenue to be recognised** = 60% × 150 = **₹90 lakh**
- **Contract cost to be recognised** = **₹60 lakh** (costs incurred to date)
- **Profit to be recognised** = 60% × 50 = **₹30 lakh** (= 90 − 60)

**(ii) Beta Ltd.**

Total estimated cost = 70 + 50 = ₹120 lakh, which **exceeds** the contract price of ₹100 lakh. The contract is therefore expected to result in a **loss of ₹20 lakh**.

As per **AS 7**, when it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue, the **expected loss should be recognised as an expense immediately**, irrespective of the stage of completion of the contract. Hence Beta Ltd. must recognise the **entire foreseeable loss of ₹20 lakh** in the Statement of Profit and Loss for the year.

### Solution 3(b) — Accounting for Investments (AS 13)

**Principle:** Long-term investments are carried at cost; a provision is made only for a decline that is **other than temporary**, determined item by item. Current investments are carried at the **lower of cost and fair value**.

| # | Investment | Carrying value | P&L impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Long-term, cost ₹5,00,000; market ₹4,20,000 (temporary fall) | **₹5,00,000** (at cost) | **Nil** — a temporary decline is not provided for. |
| 2 | Long-term, cost ₹3,00,000; permanent decline to ₹2,00,000 | **₹2,00,000** | **₹1,00,000 charged** to P&L — a decline other than temporary is recognised. |
| 3 | Current, cost ₹2,00,000; fair value ₹1,85,000 | **₹1,85,000** (lower of cost and fair value) | **₹15,000 charged** to P&L. |

### Solution 4(b) — Segment Reporting (AS 17)

A segment is a **reportable segment** if it satisfies **any one** of the following 10% thresholds:

- **Revenue test:** segment revenue (external + inter-segment) ≥ 10% of total revenue of all segments. Total revenue = 220 + 70 + 30 + 20 + 10 = **350**; threshold = **35 lakh**.
- **Result test:** absolute segment result ≥ 10% of the greater, in absolute amount, of (i) combined profit of profit-making segments and (ii) combined loss of loss-making segments. Combined profit = 40 + 15 + 8 + 2 = **65**; combined loss = **25**; greater = 65; threshold = **6.5 lakh**.
- **Assets test:** segment assets ≥ 10% of total assets of all segments. Total assets = 300 + 120 + 90 + 40 + 30 = **580**; threshold = **58 lakh**.

| Segment | Revenue (≥35?) | \|Result\| (≥6.5?) | Assets (≥58?) | Reportable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 220 ✓ | 40 ✓ | 300 ✓ | **Yes** |
| B | 70 ✓ | 15 ✓ | 120 ✓ | **Yes** |
| C | 30 ✗ | 25 ✓ | 90 ✓ | **Yes** |
| D | 20 ✗ | 8 ✓ | 40 ✗ | **Yes** |
| E | 10 ✗ | 2 ✗ | 30 ✗ | **No** |

**Reportable segments: A, B, C and D.** Segment E does not meet any threshold.

**75% external-revenue test:** External revenue of reportable segments = 200 + 60 + 30 + 20 = **310**; total external revenue = 320; ratio = 310 ÷ 320 = **96.9% ≥ 75%**. The test is satisfied, so no additional segment need be designated as reportable. Segment E is shown in the reconciliation as "all other segments".

### Solution 6(b) OR — Related Party Disclosures (AS 18)

**(i) Parties deemed NOT to be related under AS 18** include:
1. Two companies simply because they have a **director in common** (unless the director is able to affect the policies of both in their mutual dealings).
2. A single **customer, supplier, franchiser, distributor or general agent** with whom the enterprise transacts a significant volume of business, merely by virtue of the resulting **economic dependence**.
3. **Providers of finance, trade unions, public utilities, and government departments/agencies** in the course of their normal dealings with the enterprise (by virtue only of those dealings).

**(ii) Examination:**
- **(a) Raw-material supplier (economic dependence only):** **Not a related party.** AS 18 specifically states that economic dependence on a single supplier, without control or significant influence, does not make the parties related. No disclosure required.
- **(b) Director and the firm in which the director is a partner:** **Related parties.** The director is a member of the **key management personnel (KMP)** of Orbit Ltd., and a firm in which a KMP (or a relative) has significant influence is a related party. Transactions with the firm require disclosure under AS 18.
