# Advanced Accounting — High-Yield / Weightage Map

> **Paper:** CA Intermediate, Group I, Paper 1 — *Advanced Accounting* (New Scheme 2023). 100 marks, 3 hours, **fully descriptive** (no MCQ section). Predominantly **practical/numerical** with a thin theory layer inside the Accounting Standards.
>
> **Honesty note:** Weightages below are *indicative*, built from ICAI's syllabus emphasis and the pattern across recent attempts (May 2024, Sep 2024, Jan 2025, May 2025) plus RTP/MTP question mix. ICAI does **not** publish fixed chapter weights and the split **shifts every attempt** — a "Medium" chapter can carry a full 20-mark question in any given paper. Treat this as a study-order guide, not a guarantee. **Verify against the latest ICAI RTP, MTP and Suggested Answers for your specific attempt before finalizing your plan.**

> **Coverage note:** The seven Accounting Standards groups above account for the *whole* ICAI AS component. Individually a given standard is worth ~5 marks, but **as a bloc the AS carry ~30–40 marks every paper** (the compulsory Q1 alone is usually four 5-mark AS parts). That is why every AS group is rated Must-master even though any single standard looks small.

## The 80/20 — study these first and deepest

These few chapters, mastered thoroughly, typically fetch the **majority of the paper**:

1. **The Accounting Standards bloc** (all groups — especially AS 3, AS 20, AS 16, AS 22, AS 11, AS 2, AS 10, AS 26, AS 28, AS 4, AS 9). ~30–40 marks and they anchor the compulsory Q1. Non-negotiable.
2. **Consolidated Financial Statements (AS 21)** — often a full 20-mark question; the single biggest practical.
3. **Amalgamation of Companies (AS 14)** — near-certain practical, high-scoring once the method is drilled.
4. **Internal Reconstruction** — recurring full-length practical.
5. **Financial Statements of Companies (Schedule III)** + **Cash Flow Statements (AS 3)** — presentation-heavy, frequently examined, quick marks once formats are memorised.

Master these five clusters and you have a realistic path to **65–75+ marks** before touching anything else.

## Safe to skim if genuinely short on time

Genuinely lower-yield — understand the concept and one worked example, but don't over-invest:

- **Framework for Preparation & Presentation of Financial Statements / Introduction to AS / Applicability of AS** — mostly 2–5 marks of theory; know the qualitative characteristics, elements and applicability tiers, then move on.
- **AS 24 (Discontinuing Operations) and AS 25 (Interim Financial Reporting)** — low-frequency, disclosure-oriented; a single read-through usually suffices.
- **AS 17 (Segment Reporting) and AS 18 (Related Party Disclosures)** — occasional short application/theory; skim the identification rules and disclosure lists.
- **AS 27 / AS 23 detail** — beyond the basic equity-method / proportionate ideas needed for CFS, deep detail is rarely required at Intermediate level.

Do **not** skim any of the practical/company-accounts chapters or the mainstream computational standards — the low-yield list is deliberately confined to disclosure-only or conceptual standards.

## One-line strategy

Treat it as a **~85% practical, ~15% theory** paper: secure the compulsory Q1 (four ~5-mark Accounting Standards parts) cold, then bank two full-length company-accounts practicals from {Consolidation, Amalgamation, Internal Reconstruction, Branch, Schedule III/Cash Flow} — presentation formats and journal-entry discipline win marks faster than any theory chapter.
