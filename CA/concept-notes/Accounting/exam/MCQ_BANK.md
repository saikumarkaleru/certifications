# Advanced Accounting — MCQ Bank (exam practice)

> **Marking reminder:** ICAI applies **+1 for each correct answer and −0.25 (one-fourth) for each wrong answer** in MCQ-based papers. There is **no** negative marking for un-attempted questions — so guess only when you can eliminate at least two options.
>
> Coverage: Framework & Applicability of AS, AS 1–29 (as applicable to CA Inter Advanced Accounting), plus Schedule III / Company Financial Statements, Buy-back, Amalgamation (AS 14), Internal Reconstruction, and Branch / Foreign Branch accounting. Questions are ordered roughly by topic. Mix of single-line and case-let style. Difficulty varies.

## A. Framework & Applicability of Accounting Standards

**Q1.** As per the Framework for Preparation and Presentation of Financial Statements, which qualitative characteristic requires that the substance of a transaction be reported rather than merely its legal form?

(a) Prudence
(b) Reliability (substance over form)
(c) Comparability
(d) Materiality

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** "Substance over form" is a component of **Reliability**. Prudence is also under Reliability but relates to caution under uncertainty, not to substance vs form — a common trap.

---

**Q2.** A Non-company entity is classified as a **Level I** enterprise (Non-company) under the ICAI classification if, among other things, its turnover in the immediately preceding accounting year exceeds:

(a) ₹10 crore
(b) ₹50 crore
(c) ₹250 crore
(d) ₹500 crore

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** For non-company entities, turnover exceeding **₹50 crore** (excluding other income) makes it Level I. The ₹250 cr / ₹500 cr figures relate to the Companies (Accounting Standards) turnover thresholds for SMC classification — not the non-company Level test.

---

**Q3.** Under the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, a company is **not** a Small and Medium Company (SMC), and hence cannot claim SMC exemptions, if it:

(a) Has borrowings exceeding ₹10 crore at any time during the year
(b) Is a listed company, or in the process of listing
(c) Is a bank / financial institution / insurance company
(d) All of the above

**Answer:** (d)
**Why:** Listing (or being in process), being a bank/FI/insurer, holding/subsidiary of a non-SMC, or borrowings above ₹10 crore — **any one** disqualifies SMC status. Choosing only (a) or (b) misses that these are cumulative disqualifiers.

## B. AS 1 — Disclosure of Accounting Policies

**Q4.** The three **fundamental accounting assumptions** under AS 1 are:

(a) Going Concern, Consistency, Accrual
(b) Going Concern, Prudence, Materiality
(c) Accrual, Substance over form, Consistency
(d) Prudence, Consistency, Accrual

**Answer:** (a)
**Why:** AS 1 recognises exactly three — **Going Concern, Consistency, Accrual**. Prudence, Substance over form and Materiality are the three **major considerations** governing selection of policies, not fundamental assumptions — the classic mix-up.

---

**Q5.** If a fundamental accounting assumption is followed in preparing financial statements:

(a) Specific disclosure is required
(b) No specific disclosure is required
(c) Disclosure is required only in the notes
(d) Disclosure is required only if turnover exceeds ₹50 crore

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 1 requires disclosure **only when a fundamental assumption is NOT followed**. Following it needs no disclosure (it is presumed).

## C. AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories

**Q6.** Inventories are valued at:

(a) Cost
(b) Net realisable value
(c) Lower of cost and net realisable value
(d) Lower of cost and market value

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** AS 2 mandates **lower of cost and NRV**. "Market value" (option d) is not the AS 2 term — NRV (estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell) is.

---

**Q7.** Which of the following costs is **excluded** from the cost of inventories under AS 2?

(a) Purchase price net of trade discount
(b) Non-refundable import duties
(c) Abnormal wastage of materials
(d) Fixed production overheads allocated on normal capacity

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** **Abnormal amounts of wasted materials, labour or other costs** are expensed, not inventoried. Refundable duties are excluded too, but trade-discount-net price and normal-capacity fixed overheads are properly included.

---

**Q8.** A trader's inventory of raw material X cost ₹100/kg; replacement cost is now ₹80/kg. The finished goods made from X are still expected to sell **at a profit**. At what value is raw material X carried?

(a) ₹80 (replacement cost)
(b) ₹100 (cost)
(c) ₹90 (average)
(d) NRV of finished goods

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 2: raw materials are **not written below cost if the finished products in which they will be used are expected to be sold at or above cost**. Replacement cost is used only when finished goods will sell at a loss — the trap in option (a).

## D. AS 3 — Cash Flow Statements

**Q9.** Under AS 3, interest **paid** by a **financial enterprise** is classified under which activity?

(a) Investing
(b) Financing
(c) Operating
(d) It may be operating or financing at option

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** For a financial enterprise, interest paid/received and dividends received are **operating** cash flows. For a non-financial (other) enterprise, interest paid is usually financing and interest/dividend received is investing — the distinction is frequently tested.

---

**Q10.** Under the **indirect method**, which adjustment is made to net profit before tax to arrive at operating cash flow?

(a) Add depreciation and loss on sale of asset
(b) Deduct depreciation
(c) Add dividend received
(d) Ignore non-cash items

**Answer:** (a)
**Why:** Non-cash/non-operating items are reversed: depreciation and loss on sale are **added back**; profit on sale and investment/interest income are deducted (shown under investing). Deducting depreciation (b) reverses the correct direction.

---

## E. AS 4 — Contingencies and Events Occurring After the Balance Sheet Date

**Q11.** A company declares dividend for the year **after** the balance sheet date but **before** approval of accounts. As per AS 4 (revised), the proposed dividend is:

(a) Provided as a liability in the current year's balance sheet
(b) Not recognised as a liability; disclosed in notes
(c) Adjusted against reserves
(d) Recognised only if approved by shareholders before year-end

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Post-revision, proposed dividend is a **non-adjusting event** — it is **not shown as a liability** at year-end and is only **disclosed**. Option (a) reflects the pre-revised treatment and is the trap.

---

**Q12.** Which of the following is an **adjusting event** occurring after the balance sheet date?

(a) Destruction of a major plant by fire after year-end
(b) Insolvency of a customer confirmed after year-end whose debt existed at year-end
(c) Announcement of a restructuring plan after year-end
(d) Decline in market value of investments after year-end

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Insolvency of a debtor **confirms a condition existing at the balance sheet date**, so the bad debt is adjusted. Fire, restructuring announcement and post-year fall in investment value reflect **new conditions** → non-adjusting (disclosure only).

## F. AS 5 — Net Profit/Loss, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies

**Q13.** Under AS 5, a **change in accounting estimate** (e.g., revised useful life of an asset) is:

(a) Applied retrospectively with restatement of prior years
(b) Applied prospectively
(c) Treated as a prior period item
(d) Charged to reserves

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Changes in **estimates are applied prospectively** (current and future periods). Retrospective restatement is not permitted for estimates — the confusion is with correction of errors (prior period items), which is different.

---

**Q14.** "Prior period items" under AS 5 arise from:

(a) Changes in estimates
(b) Errors or omissions in prior periods' financial statements
(c) Extraordinary transactions of the current year
(d) Changes in accounting policy

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Prior period items are **errors/omissions** in prior period statements, arising from mathematical mistakes, oversight or misinterpretation of facts. Estimate revisions are NOT prior period items — a heavily tested distinction.

---

## G. AS 7 — Construction Contracts

**Q15.** When the **outcome of a construction contract can be estimated reliably**, contract revenue and costs are recognised by reference to the:

(a) Completed contract method
(b) Stage (percentage) of completion at the reporting date
(c) Cash received from the customer
(d) Progress billings raised

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 7 uses **only the percentage-of-completion method**. The completed contract method is **not permitted** under AS 7 (a common wrong pick). Billings and cash received do not drive revenue recognition.

---

**Q16.** If it is probable that **total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue**, the expected loss is:

(a) Spread over the remaining period of the contract
(b) Recognised as an expense immediately
(c) Recognised only on completion
(d) Recognised in proportion to work certified

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 7 requires an **expected loss to be recognised immediately as an expense**, irrespective of stage of completion — the prudence override.

## H. AS 9 — Revenue Recognition

**Q17.** Under AS 9, revenue from **sale of goods** is recognised when:

(a) The goods are dispatched from the factory
(b) The buyer places a confirmed order
(c) Significant risks and rewards of ownership are transferred and no significant uncertainty exists as to collectability
(d) Cash is received

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** AS 9 conditions: transfer of **significant risks and rewards** + **no significant uncertainty on ultimate collection**. Dispatch, order or cash receipt alone don't determine timing.

---

**Q18.** Which of the following is recognised on a **time-proportion basis** under AS 9?

(a) Dividend income
(b) Interest income
(c) Revenue from sale of goods
(d) Royalty for a one-time lump-sum licence

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** **Interest** accrues on a time-proportion basis. **Dividends** are recognised when the **right to receive is established**; royalties on an accrual basis per the agreement. Mixing dividend with interest is the trap.

## I. AS 10 — Property, Plant and Equipment

**Q19.** Under AS 10 (revised), which cost is **capitalised** as part of an item of PPE?

(a) Costs of opening a new facility (inauguration)
(b) Costs of introducing a new product (advertising)
(c) Initial estimate of dismantling and site restoration obligation
(d) Administration and general overhead costs

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** The **decommissioning/restoration cost** initial estimate is included in cost. Inauguration, advertising for a new product, and general admin overheads are specifically **not** capitalised.

---

**Q20.** A company revalues a class of machinery upward. The revaluation surplus is:

(a) Credited to the statement of profit and loss
(b) Credited to revaluation reserve under owners' interest
(c) Adjusted against the cost of the asset
(d) Credited to general reserve directly

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** An upward revaluation is credited to **revaluation surplus (reserve)** in equity, not to P&L. It is routed to P&L only to the extent it reverses a previously recognised decrease of the **same** asset.

---

**Q21.** Under AS 10, if an item of PPE is acquired in exchange for a non-monetary asset, it is measured at:

(a) Book value of the asset given up, always
(b) Fair value, unless the exchange lacks commercial substance or fair value is not reliably measurable
(c) Fair value of the asset received only
(d) Original cost of the asset given up

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 10 measures the acquired asset at **fair value**; the exception (carrying amount of asset given up) applies when the exchange **lacks commercial substance** or FV is not reliably measurable.

## J. AS 11 — Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates

**Q22.** At the balance sheet date, a foreign-currency **monetary item** (e.g., trade payable) is reported using:

(a) The exchange rate on the transaction date
(b) The closing rate
(c) The average rate for the year
(d) A forward rate

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** **Monetary items** are restated at the **closing rate**; **non-monetary items** carried at historical cost stay at the transaction-date rate. Using the transaction rate for monetary items (a) is the trap.

---

**Q23.** Exchange differences on a foreign-currency monetary item that in substance forms part of an enterprise's **net investment in a non-integral foreign operation** are:

(a) Charged to P&L immediately
(b) Accumulated in a Foreign Currency Translation Reserve until disposal
(c) Adjusted to the cost of fixed assets
(d) Ignored

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Such differences accumulate in **FCTR** and are recognised in P&L only on **disposal** of the net investment. Immediate P&L recognition (a) applies to ordinary monetary items, not net investment in non-integral operations.

---

## K. AS 12 — Accounting for Government Grants

**Q24.** A government grant received specifically towards a **depreciable fixed asset** may be presented by:

(a) Crediting it directly to capital reserve in all cases
(b) Deducting from the gross value of the asset, or treating as deferred income
(c) Crediting the entire grant to the current year's P&L
(d) Adjusting against share capital

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 12 allows two methods for asset-related grants — **deduct from asset cost**, or treat as **deferred income** released over the asset's life. Direct credit to capital reserve is for **promoters' contribution / non-repayable, non-conditional** grants, not the general rule.

---

**Q25.** When a government grant becomes **refundable**, the amount refundable relating to a grant earlier credited to deferred income is:

(a) Adjusted against the deferred income balance first; any excess charged to P&L
(b) Charged fully to P&L in all cases
(c) Adjusted against capital reserve
(d) Ignored until actually paid

**Answer:** (a)
**Why:** AS 12 treats refund of a grant as a change in estimate — first applied against **unamortised deferred income**, and any **shortfall recognised immediately in P&L**.

## L. AS 13 — Accounting for Investments

**Q26.** A **current investment** under AS 13 is carried at:

(a) Cost
(b) Lower of cost and fair value
(c) Fair value
(d) Cost less amortisation

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Current investments → **lower of cost and fair value** (computed individually or by category). **Long-term** investments are carried at cost, reduced only for a **decline other than temporary** — do not swap the two rules.

---

**Q27.** Right shares subscribed for a long-term investment are carried at:

(a) Market value on subscription date
(b) The amount paid to acquire the rights
(c) Face value
(d) Nil

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Under AS 13, cost of right shares acquired is the **subscription amount paid**. If rights are **not subscribed but sold**, the sale proceeds go to P&L (special rule when investment acquired cum-right at premium — proceeds first reduce carrying cost).

## M. AS 14 — Accounting for Amalgamations

**Q28.** An amalgamation qualifies as an **"amalgamation in the nature of merger"** only if all five conditions are met. Which is one of them?

(a) At least 75% of the equity shareholders of the transferor become equity shareholders of the transferee
(b) At least 90% of the face value of equity shares of the transferor company (other than already held) is held by the transferee
(c) The business of the transferor is intended to be discontinued
(d) The consideration is discharged partly in cash to dissenting shareholders

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** One condition is that **≥ 90% of the face value of the transferor's equity shares** (other than those already held by transferee) become held by the transferee. Business must be **intended to be carried on** (not discontinued), and consideration must be discharged by **equity shares only** (except cash for fractional shares) — options (c)/(d) describe purchase-type features.

---

**Q29.** Under the **pooling of interests** method (merger), the difference between consideration and the share capital of the transferor is:

(a) Recognised as goodwill
(b) Recognised as capital reserve
(c) Adjusted in reserves (e.g., against/added to general reserve)
(d) Charged to P&L

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** In pooling of interests, no goodwill/capital reserve arises; the difference is **adjusted in reserves**. Goodwill or capital reserve arises only under the **purchase method** — the classic merger-vs-purchase trap.

## N. AS 16 — Borrowing Costs

**Q30.** Under AS 16, a **qualifying asset** is one that:

(a) Is expensive to acquire
(b) Necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale
(c) Is financed entirely by borrowings
(d) Is a fixed asset only

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Definition hinges on **"substantial period of time"** to get ready. Assets ready for use when acquired, and inventories routinely produced in large quantities on a repetitive basis, are **not** qualifying assets.

---

**Q31.** When funds are **borrowed generally** and used to obtain a qualifying asset, the borrowing cost eligible for capitalisation is determined by applying:

(a) The actual interest on that specific loan
(b) A capitalisation rate (weighted average of general borrowing costs) to expenditure on the asset
(c) The bank rate notified by RBI
(d) The prime lending rate

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** For **general borrowings**, apply the **weighted-average capitalisation rate** to outlays, capped at actual borrowing costs incurred. Specific-borrowing actual interest (a) applies only where the loan is specifically raised for that asset.

## O. AS 17 — Segment Reporting

**Q32.** A business/geographical segment is a **reportable segment** if it satisfies the 10% threshold on any of revenue, result or assets — but the total external revenue attributable to reportable segments must be at least what % of total enterprise revenue?

(a) 50%
(b) 60%
(c) 75%
(d) 90%

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** If reportable segments cover less than **75%** of total enterprise revenue, additional segments must be identified until the 75% threshold is met — even if they individually fail the 10% test.

## P. AS 18 — Related Party Disclosures

**Q33.** Which of the following relationships is **specifically excluded** from being a "related party" under AS 18?

(a) Key management personnel
(b) Holding and subsidiary companies
(c) A single customer with whom the enterprise transacts a significant volume of business, merely by that fact
(d) Associates

**Answer:** (c)
**Why:** AS 18 expressly states that a single customer/supplier/financier with mere economic dependence is **not** a related party by that fact alone. KMP, holding/subsidiary and associates are related parties.

---

**Q34.** Related party disclosures are **not** required in the financial statements of:

(a) A listed company
(b) A wholly-owned subsidiary whose holding (and ultimate holding) company is incorporated in India, in the consolidated statements of which it is included
(c) A company with turnover of ₹100 crore
(d) An associate

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 18 exempts a **wholly-owned Indian subsidiary** from disclosing transactions with other group entities where the parent presents CFS in India. The other entities enjoy no such blanket exemption.

## Q. AS 19 — Leases

**Q35.** A lease is classified as a **finance lease** if:

(a) The lease term is short
(b) It transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incident to ownership of the asset
(c) Legal title never passes
(d) Lease payments are fixed

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Classification depends on **transfer of risks and rewards**, not on transfer of legal title. Title need not pass for a lease to be a finance lease.

---

**Q36.** In the books of the **lessee**, a finance lease is recorded at the inception of the lease at:

(a) The total of minimum lease payments
(b) Lower of fair value of the leased asset and present value of minimum lease payments
(c) Fair value only
(d) Cash price plus interest

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 19: recognise the asset and liability at the **lower of fair value and PV of MLP**. Recording the undiscounted total of MLP (a) overstates both asset and liability by the finance charge.

## R. AS 20 — Earnings Per Share

**Q37.** In computing **basic EPS**, which is added back to net profit attributable to equity shareholders?

(a) Preference dividend
(b) Nothing — preference dividend and its tax are deducted
(c) Interest on debentures
(d) Proposed equity dividend

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Basic EPS = (Net profit after tax **less preference dividend and attributable tax**) ÷ weighted average equity shares. Preference dividend is **deducted**, not added back. Equity dividend is never adjusted.

---

**Q38.** For a **bonus issue** made during the year, the weighted average number of equity shares is adjusted:

(a) From the date of the bonus issue only
(b) As if the bonus shares existed at the beginning of the earliest period reported (retrospectively)
(c) On a time-proportion basis from issue date
(d) Only in the following year

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** A bonus issue involves **no consideration**, so shares are treated as outstanding for **all periods presented** (and prior EPS is restated). Time-apportioning a bonus issue (a)/(c) is wrong — that treatment is for a fresh issue for cash.

---

## S. AS 21 — Consolidated Financial Statements

**Q39.** Goodwill / capital reserve on consolidation is computed by comparing the **cost of the parent's investment** with:

(a) The parent's share in the equity of the subsidiary on the **date of acquisition**
(b) The parent's share in the equity of the subsidiary on the **reporting date**
(c) The face value of the shares acquired
(d) The fair value of the subsidiary's total assets

**Answer:** (a)
**Why:** Goodwill/capital reserve uses equity (net assets) at the **date of acquisition**. Post-acquisition changes go to consolidated reserves, split between parent and minority — using the reporting-date equity (b) is the classic error.

---

**Q40.** **Minority interest** in the consolidated balance sheet represents:

(a) The parent's share in the subsidiary
(b) The share of outsiders in the net assets (and results) of the subsidiary
(c) Only the minority's share of share capital
(d) Unrealised profit on inter-company stock

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Minority interest = outsiders' proportion of the **subsidiary's equity** — share capital **plus** their share of post-acquisition reserves and profits, not just capital (c).

## T. AS 22 — Accounting for Taxes on Income

**Q41.** A **deferred tax asset** arising from unabsorbed depreciation or carry-forward of losses is recognised only if there is:

(a) Reasonable certainty of future taxable income
(b) Virtual certainty supported by convincing evidence of future taxable income
(c) Any expectation of future profit
(d) Board approval

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** The stricter **"virtual certainty supported by convincing evidence"** test applies to DTA from unabsorbed depreciation/losses. Ordinary DTAs need only **reasonable certainty** — do not apply the lower test here (a).

---

**Q42.** Timing differences that arise because book depreciation exceeds tax depreciation in a year give rise to a:

(a) Deferred tax liability
(b) Deferred tax asset
(c) Permanent difference
(d) Current tax adjustment

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** When **book depreciation > tax depreciation**, book profit is lower now and higher later → future taxable amount higher → a **deferred tax asset**. (Tax dep > book dep gives a DTL.) Getting the direction reversed is the frequent slip.

---

**Q43.** Which of the following is a **permanent difference** (not giving rise to deferred tax)?

(a) Difference in depreciation rates under books vs Income Tax Act
(b) Donations disallowed under the Income Tax Act
(c) Provision for doubtful debts disallowed until write-off
(d) Expenditure allowed on payment basis (e.g., u/s 43B)

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** A **permanently disallowed** item (e.g., certain donations) never reverses → permanent difference → no deferred tax. Options (a),(c),(d) are **timing** differences that reverse.

## U. AS 23 — Investments in Associates in Consolidated Financial Statements

**Q44.** Under AS 23, investment in an **associate** in consolidated financial statements is accounted for using the:

(a) Cost method
(b) Equity method
(c) Proportionate consolidation method
(d) Fair value method

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Associates use the **equity method** in CFS. Proportionate consolidation (c) is for **joint ventures under AS 27**; cost method applies in the **separate** financial statements, not CFS.

## V. AS 24 — Discontinuing Operations

**Q45.** The **initial disclosure event** for a discontinuing operation under AS 24 is the **earlier** of:

(a) Board approval of the plan, and completion of the discontinuance
(b) Entering into a binding sale agreement, and the board approving and announcing a detailed formal plan
(c) The date the segment stops earning revenue
(d) The date of the annual general meeting

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** The trigger is the **earlier** of entering a **binding sale agreement** or the **board approving & announcing a detailed formal plan**. AS 24 concerns only **disclosure**, not measurement/impairment (which is AS 28).

## W. AS 25 — Interim Financial Reporting

**Q46.** For preparing interim financial statements, AS 25 requires an entity to use:

(a) Relaxed recognition and measurement principles
(b) The same accounting policies as in its annual financial statements
(c) Only the direct method for cash flows
(d) Cash basis of accounting

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 25's core principle: apply **the same recognition and measurement policies** as the annual statements ("integral/discrete" — measurement on a year-to-date basis). Interim reporting relaxes only the **volume of disclosure**, not the principles.

## X. AS 26 — Intangible Assets

**Q47.** Under AS 26, **internally generated goodwill**:

(a) Is capitalised at fair value
(b) Is not recognised as an asset
(c) Is amortised over 10 years
(d) Is recognised only on a business combination

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Internally generated goodwill is **never recognised** because it is not an identifiable resource controlled by the enterprise measurable at cost. Purchased goodwill in an amalgamation is a different matter (AS 14).

---

**Q48.** Expenditure on **research** and on **development** under AS 26 is treated as:

(a) Both capitalised
(b) Research expensed; development capitalised only if specified criteria (technical feasibility, intention, ability, future benefits, resources, reliable measurement) are met
(c) Both expensed
(d) Research capitalised; development expensed

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** **Research is always expensed**; **development** may be capitalised **only if all six criteria** are satisfied. Reversing the two (d) is the trap.

---

## Y. AS 27 — Financial Reporting of Interests in Joint Ventures

**Q49.** In consolidated financial statements, an interest in a **jointly controlled entity** is reported using:

(a) Equity method
(b) Proportionate consolidation
(c) Cost method
(d) Fair value method

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 27 uses **proportionate consolidation** for jointly controlled entities in CFS — line-by-line addition of the venturer's share. (Equity method (a) is for associates under AS 23.)

## Z. AS 28 — Impairment of Assets

**Q50.** Under AS 28, the **recoverable amount** of an asset is the:

(a) Higher of net selling price and value in use
(b) Lower of net selling price and value in use
(c) Carrying amount less depreciation
(d) Net selling price only

**Answer:** (a)
**Why:** Recoverable amount = **higher of net selling price and value in use**. Impairment loss arises only if **carrying amount > recoverable amount**. Picking "lower" (b) inverts the definition.

---

**Q51.** An impairment loss recognised for a **cash-generating unit** containing goodwill is allocated:

(a) Proportionately to all assets in the unit first
(b) First to goodwill, then pro-rata to other assets of the unit
(c) Entirely to goodwill only
(d) First to current assets

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 28 allocates the CGU impairment **first to goodwill**, then to the other assets **pro-rata** on carrying amounts (subject to floors — no asset below its own recoverable amount).

## AA. AS 29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets

**Q52.** A **provision** is recognised only when there is a present obligation, a probable outflow of resources, **and**:

(a) A reliable estimate of the amount can be made
(b) The board approves it
(c) It is virtually certain
(d) The obligation is legally enforceable

**Answer:** (a)
**Why:** All three — present obligation, probable outflow, **reliable estimate** — must exist. The obligation can be **legal or constructive** (need not be strictly legally enforceable), so (d) is too narrow.

---

**Q53.** A **contingent asset** under AS 29 is:

(a) Recognised when probable
(b) Not recognised in financial statements; disclosed in the report of the approving authority when the inflow of economic benefits is probable
(c) Disclosed always in notes
(d) Provided for when possible

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Contingent assets are **never recognised** (prudence) and are **disclosed** (in the approving authority's report) only when an inflow is **probable**. Recognising when merely probable (a) is wrong — that is the treatment for a virtually certain inflow, which ceases to be "contingent".

---

**Q54.** Which of the following can a provision be made for under AS 29?

(a) Future operating losses
(b) An onerous contract (unavoidable costs of meeting obligations exceed expected benefits)
(c) Repairs and maintenance of own assets expected next year
(d) General business risks

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** AS 29 permits a provision for the present obligation under an **onerous contract**. **Future operating losses** are expressly **not** provided for, and there is no present obligation for planned future repairs or general risks.

## BB. Financial Statements of Companies (Schedule III)

**Q55.** Under Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013, which of the following is shown under **"Other Current Liabilities"** rather than "Short-term Provisions"?

(a) Provision for tax
(b) Current maturities of long-term debt
(c) Provision for employee benefits
(d) Proposed dividend provision

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** **Current maturities of long-term debt** are "Other Current Liabilities". Provisions for tax/employee benefits are "Short-term Provisions". Schedule III presents a **vertical** balance sheet with Equity & Liabilities and Assets.

**Q56.** As per Schedule III, a liability is classified as **current** if, among other tests, it is expected to be settled within the company's normal operating cycle **or** is due to be settled within:

(a) 6 months from the reporting date
(b) 12 months from the reporting date
(c) 24 months from the reporting date
(d) The financial year only

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** The **12-month** criterion (or normal operating cycle) governs current classification for both assets and liabilities under Schedule III.

## CC. Buy-back of Securities

**Q57.** On buy-back of equity shares out of **free reserves**, a company must transfer a sum equal to the **nominal value** of the shares bought back to:

(a) Securities Premium Account
(b) Capital Redemption Reserve
(c) General Reserve
(d) Revaluation Reserve

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Sec. 69 requires transfer of the **nominal value** of shares bought back (out of free reserves / securities premium) to the **Capital Redemption Reserve**. This mirrors the CRR requirement on redemption of preference shares out of profits.

---

**Q58.** Which condition must be satisfied for a buy-back under the Companies Act, 2013?

(a) Buy-back can be up to 100% of paid-up capital and free reserves in any year
(b) The debt-equity ratio after buy-back must not exceed 2:1
(c) Buy-back requires no shareholder approval in any case
(d) Partly paid-up shares can be bought back

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Post buy-back **debt must not exceed twice** (2:1) equity (capital + free reserves). Buy-back is capped (25% of paid-up capital + free reserves; board can do up to 10% without special resolution), and **only fully paid** shares can be bought back — so (a),(c),(d) are wrong.

## DD. Internal Reconstruction

**Q59.** In an internal reconstruction, the balance left in the **Capital Reduction / Reconstruction Account** after writing off accumulated losses and fictitious assets is transferred to:

(a) Profit and Loss Account
(b) Capital Reserve
(c) Goodwill
(d) Securities Premium

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** Any **credit balance remaining** on the Capital Reduction Account is transferred to **Capital Reserve**. Internal reconstruction (unlike amalgamation/external reconstruction) does **not** liquidate the company — no new company is formed.

## EE. Branch Accounting (including Foreign Branches)

**Q60.** Under AS 11, when translating the financial statements of an **integral foreign operation** (dependent foreign branch), **monetary items** are translated at:

(a) Historical rate
(b) Closing rate
(c) Average rate
(d) Opening rate

**Answer:** (b)
**Why:** For an **integral** foreign operation, monetary items → **closing rate**; non-monetary items at historical (transaction-date) rate; income/expenses at rates on transaction dates (average as an approximation). The resulting exchange difference goes to **P&L**. (A **non-integral** operation translates all assets/liabilities at closing rate with the difference in **FCTR** — the key contrast.)

**Q61.** In the **Debtors System** of maintaining branch accounts (for a small dependent branch), the branch account is prepared in the books of the head office essentially as a:

(a) Nominal account to find branch profit or loss
(b) Real account showing branch assets only
(c) Memorandum account
(d) Personal account of the branch manager

**Answer:** (a)
**Why:** Under the Debtors (Synthetic) System the **Branch Account is a nominal account** whose balancing figure is the **branch profit/loss**. Goods sent at cost (or at invoice with a stock reserve adjustment) — but the account's purpose is profit determination, not merely listing assets.
