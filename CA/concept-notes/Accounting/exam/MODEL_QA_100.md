### Q1. Conceptual Framework — Qualitative Characteristics (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the **qualitative characteristics** of financial statements as laid down in the *Framework for Preparation and Presentation of Financial Statements* issued by the ICAI. Briefly explain any one constraint on relevant and reliable information.

**Model Answer:**
**Qualitative characteristics are the attributes that make the information provided in financial statements useful to users.** The Framework identifies **four principal qualitative characteristics**:

1. **Understandability** — Information must be readily understandable by users who have a reasonable knowledge of business, economic activities and accounting, and a willingness to study the information with reasonable diligence.
2. **Relevance** — Information is relevant when it influences the economic decisions of users by helping them evaluate past, present or future events or by confirming/correcting their past evaluations. Relevance is affected by the **nature** and **materiality** of the item.
3. **Reliability** — Information is reliable when it is free from material error and bias and can be depended upon to represent faithfully what it purports to represent. Reliability is supported by: **faithful representation, substance over form, neutrality, prudence and completeness.**
4. **Comparability** — Users must be able to compare the financial statements of an enterprise over time (to identify trends) and across different enterprises. This requires **consistency** of accounting policies and disclosure of policies followed.

**Constraint (any one):**
- **Timeliness** — Undue delay in reporting may make information more reliable but less relevant. A balance has to be struck between reliability and timeliness.
- **Balance between benefit and cost** — The benefit derived from information should exceed the cost of providing it.

**Conclusion:** True and fair financial statements are those that faithfully embody the above characteristics within these constraints.

**> Full-marks tip:** List all **four** characteristics as headings and mention the **sub-components of reliability** (substance over form, neutrality, prudence, completeness) — examiners award marks head-wise; a bare list without the reliability sub-points loses 1–2 marks.

### Q2. AS 1 — Fundamental Accounting Assumptions & Disclosure (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** With reference to **AS 1 "Disclosure of Accounting Policies"**, state the three **fundamental accounting assumptions**. How should the disclosure be made when (a) these assumptions are followed, and (b) any one of them is **not** followed? Also state the **major considerations** governing selection of accounting policies.

**Model Answer:**
**As per AS 1, three fundamental accounting assumptions underlie the preparation of financial statements** (Para 27):

1. **Going Concern** — The enterprise is normally viewed as continuing in operation for the foreseeable future, with neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or materially curtailing the scale of operations.
2. **Consistency** — Accounting policies are consistent from one period to another.
3. **Accrual** — Revenues and costs are recognised as they are earned or incurred (and not as money is received or paid) and recorded in the financial statements of the periods to which they relate.

**Disclosure requirement (Para 27):**
- **(a) If followed:** No specific disclosure is required — their acceptance and use is *assumed*.
- **(b) If not followed:** The fact **must be disclosed**.

**Major considerations governing selection of accounting policies (Para 17):**
- **Prudence** — Profits are not anticipated but recognised only when realised; provision is made for all known liabilities and losses even if the amount is a best estimate.
- **Substance over Form** — Transactions are accounted for in accordance with their commercial substance and reality, not merely their legal form.
- **Materiality** — Financial statements should disclose all "material" items, i.e., items the knowledge of which might influence the decisions of the user.

**Conclusion:** All significant accounting policies should be disclosed at **one place** as part of the financial statements, and any change having a material effect must be disclosed with its impact.

**> Full-marks tip:** Name the three assumptions **exactly** (Going Concern, Consistency, Accrual) and remember the rule reversal — disclosure is needed only when an assumption is **NOT** followed. Adding the three "major considerations" (Prudence, Substance over Form, Materiality) earns the last marks.

### Q3. AS 2 — Cost of Inventory (Inclusions/Exclusions) (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** From the following information, compute the **value of inventory** as per **AS 2 "Valuation of Inventories"**:

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Purchase price | 5,00,000 |
| Trade discount received | 20,000 |
| Import duty (non-refundable) | 30,000 |
| Freight inward | 15,000 |
| Administrative overheads | 25,000 |
| Selling & distribution costs | 10,000 |
| Storage cost (not necessary for further production) | 8,000 |
| Interest on borrowings | 12,000 |
| Abnormal wastage of material | 5,000 |

**Model Answer:**

**Working Note — Classification of costs (AS 2, Paras 6–13):**

*Costs of purchase that form part of inventory cost:*
- Purchase price ₹5,00,000
- Less: Trade discount ₹20,000 (deducted; AS 2 requires netting of trade discounts, rebates and duty drawbacks)
- Add: Import duty (non-refundable) ₹30,000
- Add: Freight inward ₹15,000

*Costs specifically EXCLUDED from inventory (Para 13):*
- Administrative overheads not contributing to bringing inventory to present location/condition — **excluded**
- Selling & distribution costs — **excluded**
- Storage costs not necessary in the production process before a further production stage — **excluded**
- Interest / borrowing costs (unless AS 16 conditions for a qualifying asset are met) — **excluded**
- Abnormal wastage of material — **excluded** (charged to P&L)

**Computation of Value of Inventory:**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Purchase price | 5,00,000 |
| Less: Trade discount | (20,000) |
| Add: Import duty | 30,000 |
| Add: Freight inward | 15,000 |
| **Value of Inventory** | **5,25,000** |

**Value of inventory as per AS 2 = ₹5,25,000.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Show the excluded items **explicitly** (with the reason "not part of cost") — writing them off silently loses the presentation marks. Remember trade discount is **deducted**, not added.

### Q4. AS 2 — Lower of Cost or NRV (Item-by-item + Raw Material Rule) (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** X Ltd. has the following items of inventory on 31.03.2026. Value the inventory as per **AS 2**.

| Item | Cost (₹) | Net Realisable Value (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Finished Good A | 2,00,000 | 2,40,000 |
| Finished Good B | 1,50,000 | 1,20,000 |
| Raw Material P (used in A) | 80,000 | 70,000 |
| Raw Material Q (used in B) | 60,000 | 55,000 |

Replacement cost of Raw Material P = ₹68,000 and of Raw Material Q = ₹52,000.

**Model Answer:**

**Principle (AS 2, Paras 5, 24):** Inventory is valued at **lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV)**, applied on an **item-by-item** basis. For **raw materials**, they are **not written down below cost if the finished products in which they will be incorporated are expected to be sold at or above cost**. Where a write-down of raw material is required, **replacement cost** is the best available measure of NRV.

**Working Note 1 — Finished Goods (lower of cost or NRV):**
- Good A: Cost ₹2,00,000 vs NRV ₹2,40,000 → **₹2,00,000** (finished good sold above cost)
- Good B: Cost ₹1,50,000 vs NRV ₹1,20,000 → **₹1,20,000** (write down by ₹30,000)

**Working Note 2 — Raw Materials:**
- **Raw Material P** (used in Good A): Good A is sold **at a profit** (NRV 2,40,000 > cost 2,00,000). Hence RM P is **not written down** → value at **cost ₹80,000**.
- **Raw Material Q** (used in Good B): Good B is sold **at a loss** (NRV 1,20,000 < cost 1,50,000). Hence RM Q **is** written down to its NRV, measured by replacement cost = **₹52,000**.

**Statement of Inventory Value:**

| Item | Value (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Finished Good A | 2,00,000 |
| Finished Good B | 1,20,000 |
| Raw Material P | 80,000 |
| Raw Material Q | 52,000 |
| **Total Inventory Value** | **4,52,000** |

**Total value of inventory = ₹4,52,000.**

**> Full-marks tip:** The trap is raw material: state the rule that RM is written down **only if the finished good is sold at a loss**, and then use **replacement cost** as NRV. Marking is lost if you blindly take the lower of cost/NRV for the raw materials without linking to the finished product.

### Q5. AS 3 — Cash Flow from Operating Activities (Indirect Method) (marks: 8) [Practical]
**Question:** From the following data of Sunrise Ltd. for the year ended 31.03.2026, compute **cash flow from operating activities** using the **indirect method** as per **AS 3**:

- Net profit before tax and extraordinary items: ₹4,00,000
- Depreciation on fixed assets: ₹70,000
- Goodwill amortised: ₹20,000
- Interest expense (financing): ₹30,000
- Interest income (investing): ₹15,000
- Profit on sale of machinery: ₹10,000
- Increase in trade receivables: ₹40,000
- Decrease in inventories: ₹25,000
- Increase in trade payables: ₹15,000
- Increase in prepaid expenses: ₹5,000
- Income tax paid during the year: ₹60,000

**Model Answer:**

**Cash Flow from Operating Activities (Indirect Method) — AS 3, Para 18(b):**

| Particulars | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Net profit before tax and extraordinary items** | | 4,00,000 |
| *Adjustments for non-cash / non-operating items:* | | |
| Add: Depreciation | 70,000 | |
| Add: Goodwill amortised | 20,000 | |
| Add: Interest expense (financing activity) | 30,000 | |
| Less: Interest income (investing activity) | (15,000) | |
| Less: Profit on sale of machinery (investing) | (10,000) | 95,000 |
| **Operating profit before working capital changes** | | **4,95,000** |
| *Changes in working capital:* | | |
| Less: Increase in trade receivables | (40,000) | |
| Add: Decrease in inventories | 25,000 | |
| Add: Increase in trade payables | 15,000 | |
| Less: Increase in prepaid expenses | (5,000) | (5,000) |
| **Cash generated from operations** | | **4,90,000** |
| Less: Income tax paid | | (60,000) |
| **Net Cash from Operating Activities** | | **4,30,000** |

**Net cash flow from operating activities = ₹4,30,000.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Add back interest EXPENSE and remove interest INCOME and profit on sale (these belong to financing/investing, not operating). Carry the **sign** correctly on each working-capital change: increase in a current asset is a **cash outflow (minus)**, increase in a current liability is a **cash inflow (plus)**.

### Q6. AS 3 — Classification of Cash Flows (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Classify the following into **Operating**, **Investing** or **Financing** activities as per **AS 3** for a **non-financial (manufacturing) enterprise**, giving reasons:
(i) Cash sales; (ii) Purchase of a patent; (iii) Dividend paid; (iv) Interest received on investments; (v) Income tax paid on operating profits; (vi) Proceeds from issue of debentures; (vii) Cash paid to employees; (viii) Purchase of shares of another company held as investment.

**Model Answer:**

**Basis (AS 3, Paras 5, 14–17):** Operating activities are the principal revenue-producing activities and other activities that are not investing or financing. Investing activities relate to acquisition/disposal of long-term assets and other investments. Financing activities result in changes in the size and composition of owners' capital and borrowings.

| Item | Classification | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Cash sales | **Operating** | Principal revenue-producing activity |
| (ii) Purchase of a patent | **Investing** | Acquisition of a long-term (intangible) asset |
| (iii) Dividend paid | **Financing** | Return to providers of capital |
| (iv) Interest received on investments | **Investing** | Return on investment held (non-financial entity) |
| (v) Income tax paid (on operating profits) | **Operating** | Tax on operations, unless specifically identifiable with investing/financing |
| (vi) Proceeds from issue of debentures | **Financing** | Change in composition of borrowings |
| (vii) Cash paid to employees | **Operating** | Operating cost of principal activities |
| (viii) Purchase of shares (investment) | **Investing** | Acquisition of an investment |

**Note:** For a **financial enterprise**, interest received/paid and dividends received would ordinarily be **operating** activities.

**> Full-marks tip:** State that the classification differs for a **financial enterprise**, and remember dividend PAID = financing while interest/dividend RECEIVED = investing (for non-financial entities). Wrong classification of tax (it defaults to operating) is a common error.

### Q7. AS 4 — Adjusting vs Non-Adjusting Events (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** The financial statements of Zenith Ltd. for the year ended 31.03.2026 were approved by the Board on 15.06.2026. State, with reasons, the treatment of the following events per **AS 4 "Contingencies and Events Occurring After the Balance Sheet Date"**:
(i) A debtor of ₹5,00,000 outstanding on 31.03.2026 became insolvent on 20.04.2026 and nothing is recoverable.
(ii) A major fire on 25.05.2026 destroyed a factory building of ₹80,00,000; operations are expected to be substantially curtailed.
(iii) The company declared a dividend of ₹10,00,000 on 10.06.2026 for FY 2025-26.
(iv) A customer filed a suit on 05.05.2026 for damages relating to goods **sold before** 31.03.2026; legal opinion indicates a probable liability of ₹3,00,000.

**Model Answer:**

**Principle (AS 4, Paras 8–8.6, 13–15):** Events occurring after the balance sheet date are of two types — **adjusting events** (provide additional evidence of conditions existing **at** the balance sheet date → adjust the financial statements) and **non-adjusting events** (concern conditions arising **after** the balance sheet date → do not adjust, but disclose if material). Assets and liabilities should also be adjusted for events indicating that the **going concern assumption** is no longer appropriate.

| Event | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **(i) Insolvency of debtor** | **Adjusting event.** The condition (doubtful recovery) existed at 31.03.2026; insolvency confirms it. **Write off ₹5,00,000** / make provision — adjust the financial statements. |
| **(ii) Fire destroying factory** | **Non-adjusting event** (condition arose after year-end). Ordinarily only **disclosure** is required. **However**, since operations are expected to be *substantially curtailed*, the **going concern** assumption is affected — hence the financial statements **must be adjusted** (assets restated) and the fact disclosed. |
| **(iii) Dividend declared after year-end** | Dividends **proposed/declared after the balance sheet date** but before approval are **not recognised as a liability** at the balance sheet date (per amended AS 4 read with Schedule III); they are **disclosed in the notes**. |
| **(iv) Suit for pre-year-end sale** | **Adjusting event** — the obligating event (sale) occurred before 31.03.2026 and a probable, estimable liability of ₹3,00,000 exists. **Provide ₹3,00,000** in the accounts. |

**> Full-marks tip:** For (ii), the examiner wants the **going-concern exception** — a normally non-adjusting event becomes adjusting if going concern is impaired. For (iii), state clearly that proposed dividend is **NOT** a liability (post-amendment) and is only **disclosed**.

### Q8. AS 4 — Proposed Dividend & Contingencies (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** "After the amendment to AS 4, proposed dividend is no longer a provision." Explain the current accounting treatment of **proposed dividend**. Also state how **contingencies** are dealt with under AS 4 today, given AS 29.

**Model Answer:**
**Proposed Dividend (AS 4, post-amendment):**
- **Dividends proposed or declared after the balance sheet date** but before approval of the financial statements are events occurring after the balance sheet date.
- As per the amended AS 4, such proposed dividend is **NOT recognised as a liability (provision)** as at the balance sheet date, because at that date there is **no present obligation** — the shareholders' approval is still awaited.
- It is **disclosed in the notes** to the financial statements (consistent with Schedule III).
- The liability is recognised **only in the year of declaration/approval** in the General Meeting.

**Contingencies under AS 4:**
- With the issuance of **AS 29 "Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets"**, the portion of AS 4 dealing with **contingencies stands withdrawn**.
- Therefore, **contingent liabilities and provisions are now governed by AS 29**, not AS 4.
- AS 4 today essentially deals **only with events occurring after the balance sheet date**.

**> Full-marks tip:** State the two-part answer clearly — (1) proposed dividend is a **note disclosure**, not a provision, and (2) contingencies have **moved to AS 29**. Mentioning "no present obligation exists at year-end" earns the conceptual mark.

### Q9. AS 5 — Change in Estimate / Prior Period / Extraordinary Items (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** State, with reasons, the treatment of the following in the financial statements of Orion Ltd. for FY 2025-26 as per **AS 5 "Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies"**:
(i) Depreciation for FY 2024-25 was under-charged by ₹2,00,000 due to an arithmetical error, discovered now.
(ii) The estimated useful life of a machine was revised, increasing current-year depreciation by ₹1,50,000.
(iii) Loss of ₹12,00,000 from an earthquake that destroyed a warehouse.
(iv) The company changed its method of inventory valuation from FIFO to weighted average, increasing current profit by ₹80,000.

**Model Answer:**

**Framework (AS 5, Paras 4, 8, 21, 23, 27, 33):**
- **Prior period items** — income/expenses arising in the current period as a result of **errors or omissions** in prior periods' financial statements. Shown **separately** so their impact on current profit/loss is perceived.
- **Extraordinary items** — income/expenses arising from events **clearly distinct from ordinary activities**, not expected to recur. Disclosed **separately** in the Statement of P&L.
- **Change in accounting estimate** — recognised **prospectively** in the period of change (and future periods).
- **Change in accounting policy** — the amount by which any item is affected is **disclosed** to the extent ascertainable.

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **(i) Under-charged depreciation (error)** | **Prior period item.** ₹2,00,000 is charged in FY 2025-26 and **disclosed separately** so that current profit is understood. |
| **(ii) Revision of useful life** | **Change in accounting estimate** — treated **prospectively**; the extra ₹1,50,000 is charged to the current year's P&L. **No** restatement of prior years. |
| **(iii) Earthquake loss** | **Extraordinary item** — ₹12,00,000 disclosed **separately** on the face of the Statement of P&L, as it is distinct from ordinary activities and non-recurring. |
| **(iv) FIFO → weighted average** | **Change in accounting policy.** The effect (₹80,000 increase in profit) must be **disclosed**. A change in policy is made only if required by statute/AS or for more appropriate presentation. |

**> Full-marks tip:** The classic exam trap is (i) vs (ii): an **error** is a *prior period item*; a genuine **revision of estimate** is *prospective*. Also distinguish **change in estimate** (prospective) from **change in policy** (disclose effect). Naming each category is worth the marks.

### Q10. AS 5 — Ordinary Activities & Disclosure (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Define **"ordinary activities"** and **"extraordinary items"** under AS 5. State the circumstances in which the **nature and amount of items of income or expense arising from ordinary activities** are required to be disclosed separately.

**Model Answer:**
**Definitions (AS 5, Para 4):**
- **Ordinary activities** — any activities undertaken by an enterprise as part of its business and such **related** activities in which the enterprise engages in furtherance of, incidental to, or arising from these activities.
- **Extraordinary items** — income or expenses that arise from events or transactions that are **clearly distinct from the ordinary activities** of the enterprise and, therefore, are **not expected to recur frequently or regularly** (e.g., loss from earthquake, attachment of property).

**Separate disclosure of items within ordinary activities (Para 12):**
When items of income and expense within profit/loss from ordinary activities are of **such size, nature or incidence** that their disclosure is **relevant to explain the performance** of the enterprise, their **nature and amount are disclosed separately.** Examples:
- Write-down of inventories to NRV and its reversal;
- Restructuring of activities and reversal of related provisions;
- Disposals of items of fixed assets and of long-term investments;
- Legislative changes with retrospective application;
- Litigation settlements; and
- Other reversals of provisions.

**Conclusion:** Such items remain part of ordinary activities (unlike extraordinary items) but their separate disclosure aids a proper understanding of performance.

**> Full-marks tip:** Do **not** confuse "separately disclosed ordinary items" with "extraordinary items" — the former are still ordinary. Listing 3–4 examples from Para 12 secures the illustration marks.

### Q11. AS 7 — Percentage of Completion Method (marks: 8) [Practical]
**Question:** Bharat Constructions Ltd. undertook a contract on 01.04.2025 for ₹100 lakhs. The following details are available as on 31.03.2026:
- Cost incurred to date: ₹30 lakhs
- Estimated further costs to complete: ₹60 lakhs
- Progress billings raised: ₹32 lakhs
- Amount received from customer: ₹28 lakhs

Compute (a) the **stage of completion**, (b) **contract revenue and contract cost** to be recognised in FY 2025-26, and (c) the **profit** to be recognised, as per **AS 7 "Construction Contracts"**. Show the relevant balance sheet figures.

**Model Answer:**

**Method (AS 7, Paras 21–34):** Contract revenue and costs are recognised by reference to the **stage of completion** (percentage of completion method). Stage of completion here is measured by the **cost-to-cost method** = costs incurred to date ÷ total estimated contract costs.

**Working Note 1 — Total estimated contract cost:**
= Cost incurred to date + Estimated further cost = ₹30 + ₹60 = **₹90 lakhs**

**Working Note 2 — Stage of completion:**
= Cost incurred to date ÷ Total estimated cost = 30 ÷ 90 = **33.33%**

**Working Note 3 — Total estimated profit:**
= Contract price − Total estimated cost = ₹100 − ₹90 = **₹10 lakhs**

**(a) Stage of completion = 33.33%**

**(b) & (c) Amounts recognised in FY 2025-26:**

| Particulars | ₹ (lakhs) |
|---|---:|
| Contract Revenue (33.33% × ₹100) | 33.33 |
| Contract Cost recognised (cost incurred to date) | 30.00 |
| **Profit recognised (33.33% × ₹10)** | **3.33** |

**Balance Sheet presentation (AS 7, Paras 27–29):**

| Particulars | ₹ (lakhs) |
|---|---:|
| Gross amount **due from** customer = (Costs incurred + Recognised profit) − Progress billings = (30 + 3.33) − 32 | **1.33 (asset)** |
| Trade receivable (Billings − Received = 32 − 28) | 4.00 |

**Profit to be recognised in FY 2025-26 = ₹3.33 lakhs.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Show stage of completion as a **separate working note**, and recognise cost = **actual cost incurred to date** (not % × total cost — they're equal here only by coincidence). The "amount due from/to customer" = Costs incurred + Profits − Billings — carry the sign to know asset vs liability.

### Q12. AS 7 — Expected Loss on Contract (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Surya Infra Ltd. has a contract of ₹90 lakhs. As on 31.03.2026: cost incurred to date ₹40 lakhs; estimated further cost to complete ₹65 lakhs. Progress billings ₹35 lakhs. Determine the amount of **loss** to be recognised in FY 2025-26 as per **AS 7**, and explain the principle.

**Model Answer:**

**Principle (AS 7, Paras 35–36):** When it is probable that **total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue**, the **expected loss** is recognised as an **expense immediately** (in full), irrespective of the stage of completion of the contract and the profit recognised on other contracts.

**Working Note 1 — Total estimated contract cost:**
= Cost incurred to date + Estimated further cost = ₹40 + ₹65 = **₹105 lakhs**

**Working Note 2 — Total expected outcome:**
= Contract price − Total estimated cost = ₹90 − ₹105 = **(₹15 lakhs) loss**

**Working Note 3 — Stage of completion (for revenue only):**
= 40 ÷ 105 = 38.10%

**Recognition in FY 2025-26:**
- Contract revenue recognised = 38.10% × ₹90 = ₹34.29 lakhs
- Contract cost to be recognised so that the **entire ₹15 lakhs loss** is booked now:
 = Revenue recognised + Total expected loss = ₹34.29 + ₹15 = **₹49.29 lakhs**

**Loss to be recognised immediately = ₹15 lakhs.**

**> Full-marks tip:** State the AS 7 rule that the **whole foreseeable loss** is recognised **immediately**, not proportionately. The single most important number is the **₹15 lakhs loss** — full loss regardless of the 38.1% completion.

### Q13. AS 9 — Revenue Recognition: Timing (Multiple Scenarios) (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** State, with reasons, when revenue should be recognised in each case per **AS 9 "Revenue Recognition"**:
(i) Goods sold on 28.03.2026 on "sale or return" basis; the buyer's approval was received on 10.04.2026.
(ii) Sale of ₹5,00,000 on 30.03.2026; significant uncertainty exists about collectability at the time of sale.
(iii) Interest of ₹40,000 on a fixed deposit for the quarter Jan–Mar 2026, received on 05.04.2026.
(iv) Dividend of ₹25,000 on shares, declared by the investee company on 15.04.2026.
(v) Advance of ₹1,00,000 received on 20.03.2026 against goods to be delivered in May 2026.

**Model Answer:**

**Framework (AS 9, Paras 6–14):**
- **Sale of goods** — revenue is recognised when the seller has transferred **property in the goods** (significant risks and rewards of ownership) to the buyer and no significant uncertainty exists regarding the **amount of consideration** and its **collectability**.
- **Rendering of services / use of resources** — Interest is recognised on a **time-proportion basis**; royalties on **accrual** as per agreement; **dividends** when the **right to receive payment is established**.
- Where **uncertainty of collection** exists, recognition is **postponed**.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **(i) Sale or return** | Revenue recognised only when the buyer **confirms/approves** the purchase (or time for rejection expires). Since approval came on **10.04.2026**, recognise revenue in **FY 2026-27**, not FY 2025-26. |
| **(ii) Uncertain collectability** | Revenue recognition is **postponed**; since significant uncertainty about collection exists at the time of sale, revenue is **not recognised** until the uncertainty is resolved. |
| **(iii) Interest Jan–Mar 2026** | Recognised on a **time-proportion basis** — it **accrues** in FY 2025-26. Hence ₹40,000 is recognised in **FY 2025-26**, though received later. |
| **(iv) Dividend declared 15.04.2026** | The **right to receive** payment is established only on **declaration (15.04.2026)** → recognise in **FY 2026-27**. |
| **(v) Advance received** | An advance is **not revenue** — no goods delivered. It is a **liability (advance from customer)** until delivery in May 2026, when revenue will be recognised. |

**> Full-marks tip:** Distinguish **accrual-based** items (interest → time-proportion, recognised even if unreceived) from **event-based** items (dividend → only on declaration; sale-or-return → only on approval). Marking a received advance as revenue is a guaranteed lost mark.

### Q14. AS 9 — Scope, Definitions & Disclosure (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the meaning of **"Revenue"** under **AS 9** and state the items **specifically excluded** from its scope. What are the **two conditions** for recognition of revenue from the **sale of goods**?

**Model Answer:**
**Meaning of Revenue (AS 9, Para 4.1):**
Revenue is the **gross inflow of cash, receivables or other consideration** arising in the course of the **ordinary activities** of an enterprise from:
- the **sale of goods**,
- the **rendering of services**, and
- the **use by others of enterprise resources** yielding interest, royalties and dividends.

Revenue is measured by the **charges made to customers or clients** for goods supplied and services rendered, and by the charges and rewards arising from the use of resources by them. In an **agency relationship**, revenue is only the **commission**, not the gross inflow.

**Items excluded from the scope of AS 9:**
- Revenue arising from **construction contracts** (AS 7);
- **Hire-purchase and lease agreements**;
- **Government grants and subsidies** (AS 12);
- **Realised/unrealised gains** from changes in foreign exchange rates and translation of foreign currency statements;
- Revenue of **insurance companies** arising from insurance contracts.

**Two conditions for recognising revenue from sale of goods (Para 11):**
1. The seller has **transferred to the buyer the property in the goods** (i.e., significant **risks and rewards** of ownership) for a price, or all significant risks and rewards have been transferred and the seller retains no effective control; **and**
2. **No significant uncertainty** exists regarding the **amount of consideration** that will be derived from the sale and its **collectability**.

**> Full-marks tip:** List the **excluded items** as a head-wise list (each worth a mark) and state the **two** sale-of-goods conditions in the AS's own words — "transfer of risks and rewards" + "no significant uncertainty of collection".

### Q15. AS 2 — Cost Formula (Weighted Average) (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** From the following, compute the **cost of closing inventory** using the **Weighted Average** cost formula as per **AS 2**:

| Date | Particulars | Units | Rate (₹) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 01.03.2026 | Opening stock | 200 | 40 |
| 05.03.2026 | Purchase | 300 | 44 |
| 12.03.2026 | Sale | 250 | — |
| 20.03.2026 | Purchase | 250 | 50 |
| 28.03.2026 | Sale | 300 | — |

(Use the **periodic weighted average** method.)

**Model Answer:**

**Principle (AS 2, Paras 14–16):** Under the **weighted average cost formula**, the cost of each item is determined from the weighted average of the cost of similar items at the beginning of a period and the cost of similar items purchased during the period.

**Working Note 1 — Total units and cost available for the period:**

| Particulars | Units | Rate (₹) | Value (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|

| Purchase 20.03 | 250 | 50 | 12,500 |

= Total value ÷ Total units = ₹33,700 ÷ 750 = **₹44.933 per unit**

**Working Note 3 — Closing units:**
= 750 − (250 + 300) sold = 750 − 550 = **200 units**

**Value of Closing Inventory:**
= 200 units × ₹44.933 = **₹8,986.67 ≈ ₹8,987**

**Cost of closing inventory (weighted average) = ₹8,987.**

### Q17. AS 4 / AS 29 — Contingencies (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** How should the following be dealt with in the financial statements of Pinnacle Ltd. for the year ended 31.03.2026? Refer to **AS 4** (events after balance sheet date) and **AS 29** (provisions/contingencies) as applicable.
(i) A claim of ₹6,00,000 was lodged against the company before 31.03.2026; legal advice on 05.05.2026 (before approval of accounts) confirms a probable liability of ₹4,00,000.
(ii) The company has given a guarantee of ₹10,00,000 for a loan taken by its subsidiary; the subsidiary is financially sound and default is not probable.
(iii) A machinery costing ₹15,00,000, in transit at year-end, was destroyed in an accident on 08.04.2026; the loss was not insured.

**Model Answer:**

**Principle:** Under **AS 29**, a **provision** is recognised when (a) there is a **present obligation** from a past event, (b) an outflow of resources is **probable**, and (c) the amount can be **reliably estimated**. A **contingent liability** (possible obligation, or present obligation where outflow is not probable/estimable) is **not provided** but **disclosed**. Under **AS 4**, an adjusting event provides evidence of conditions existing at the balance sheet date.

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **(i) Claim confirmed after year-end** | The claim existed **before** 31.03.2026 and post-year-end legal advice (before approval) is an **adjusting event** confirming a **present obligation** of ₹4,00,000, which is **probable and estimable**. **Recognise a provision of ₹4,00,000** in FY 2025-26. |
| **(ii) Guarantee for solvent subsidiary** | There is only a **possible obligation**; default is **not probable**. Hence **no provision**; disclose as a **contingent liability of ₹10,00,000** in the notes. |
| **(iii) Machinery destroyed after year-end** | The asset existed and the destruction occurred **after** the balance sheet date (condition arose after year-end) → **non-adjusting event**. Do **not** write down at 31.03.2026, but **disclose** the ₹15,00,000 loss in notes if material (and account for it in FY 2026-27). |
| | *If, however, the loss impairs going concern, the accounts would be adjusted.* |

**> Full-marks tip:** Apply the **AS 29 three-part test** for provisions and clearly separate **provision** (recognise) from **contingent liability** (only disclose). For (iii), the destruction is a *new condition* → **non-adjusting** → disclosure only.

### Q18. Applicability of Accounting Standards — Level Classification (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** For the purpose of applicability of Accounting Standards to **non-company entities**, entities are classified into levels. Briefly state the criteria for a **Level I** entity, and give **two examples** of standards / relaxations **not** available (i.e., exemptions) to lower-level entities.

**Model Answer:**
**Classification (ICAI scheme for non-company / non-corporate entities):** Entities are classified into **four levels** — Level I, II, III and IV — for determining the applicability of Accounting Standards and permitted exemptions/relaxations. **Level I entities** are required to comply with **all Accounting Standards in full.**

**A non-corporate entity is a Level I entity if it falls in any one of the following (any as applicable):**
1. Entities whose **equity or debt securities are listed** or are in the process of listing (in India or abroad);
2. **Banks** (including co-operative banks), **financial institutions** or entities carrying on **insurance** business;
3. Entities with **turnover** (excluding other income) exceeding **₹250 crore** in the immediately preceding accounting year;
4. Entities with **borrowings** (including public deposits) exceeding **₹50 crore** at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year;
5. **Holding and subsidiary** entities of any of the above.

**Examples of exemptions/relaxations available to lower-level entities (not to Level I):**
- **AS 3 (Cash Flow Statements)** — not applicable to Level II, III and IV entities.
- **AS 17 (Segment Reporting)** — not applicable to Level II, III and IV entities.
- Relaxations in disclosure requirements of **AS 15** (employee benefits), **AS 19** (leases), **AS 20** (EPS — presentation not mandatory for lower levels), **AS 29** (certain disclosures), etc.

**Conclusion:** Level I entities apply **all AS fully**; lower-level entities enjoy specified exemptions and disclosure relaxations.

**> Full-marks tip:** Quote the **quantitative thresholds** (turnover ₹250 crore, borrowings ₹50 crore) — these carry marks. Naming **AS 3 and AS 17** as non-applicable to lower levels is the standard illustration examiners expect.

### Q1. Revenue Recognition — AS 9 (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** For the year ended 31st March 2026, state with reasons the amount of revenue to be recognised by Suraksha Ltd in each of the following independent cases:
(i) Total sales of goods were ₹50,00,000. Of these, goods of sale value ₹4,00,000 were sent on a "sale or return" basis on 25th March 2026 (15-day approval period); no confirmation had been received up to 31st March 2026.
(ii) An advance of ₹2,00,000 was received on 28th March 2026 against goods to be delivered in April 2026.
(iii) A fixed deposit of ₹10,00,000 carries interest @9% p.a.; the bank had credited only ₹67,500 (for 9 months) up to 31st March 2026.
(iv) The investee company declared a dividend of ₹1,20,000 in its AGM held on 20th April 2026 for FY 2025-26.
(v) Royalty of ₹1,50,000 had accrued as per the agreement during the year.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 9):** Revenue from sale of goods is recognised when the seller has transferred property in the goods (significant risks and rewards) and no significant uncertainty exists regarding collection (para 6.1, 11). Revenue from interest, royalties and dividends is recognised as per para 13 — interest on a **time-proportion basis**, royalties on an **accrual basis** per the agreement, and dividends when the **right to receive is established**.

| Case | Treatment | Revenue recognised |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Normal sales ₹46,00,000; goods on sale-or-return ₹4,00,000 not yet approved → risks/rewards not transferred → **exclude** | Recognise ₹50,00,000 − ₹4,00,000 | **₹46,00,000** |
| (ii) Advance against future delivery — sale not yet made | Liability, not revenue | **Nil** |
| (iii) Interest for full year on time basis = 10,00,000 × 9% = 90,000; accrue balance ₹22,500 | Recognise full-year interest | **₹90,000** |
| (iv) Dividend declared 20.04.2026 — right to receive arises in FY 2026-27 | Not an event of FY 2025-26 | **Nil** |
| (v) Royalty on accrual per agreement | Recognise | **₹1,50,000** |

**Total revenue recognised for FY 2025-26 = ₹46,00,000 + ₹90,000 + ₹1,50,000 = ₹48,40,000** (plus interest already booked is subsumed in ₹90,000).

### Q2. Foreign Exchange — Import of Fixed Asset — AS 11 (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** Vishwas Ltd (reporting currency ₹) imported a machine on 1st January 2026 for USD 1,00,000 on credit. Exchange rates: 1.1.2026 — ₹82/USD; 31.3.2026 (year-end) — ₹84/USD; 15.4.2026 (date of payment) — ₹85/USD. Show the accounting treatment and the exchange differences in FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27. Depreciation may be ignored.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 11):** On initial recognition a foreign-currency transaction is recorded at the spot rate on the transaction date (para 9). At each balance-sheet date **monetary items** (here, the creditor) are restated at the closing rate, while **non-monetary items carried at cost** (the machine) are **not** restated (para 11). Exchange differences on monetary items are recognised in the **statement of profit and loss** (para 13).

**Working Notes / Entries:**

*1.1.2026 — initial recognition (USD 1,00,000 × ₹82):*
```
Machinery A/c            Dr  82,00,000
    To Sundry Creditor (USD)        82,00,000
```

*31.3.2026 — restate creditor (monetary) at ₹84 = ₹84,00,000; loss ₹2,00,000. Machinery is NOT restated:*
```
Exchange Loss (P&L) A/c  Dr   2,00,000
    To Sundry Creditor (USD)         2,00,000
```

*15.4.2026 — payment USD 1,00,000 × ₹85 = ₹85,00,000; creditor stood at ₹84,00,000; further loss ₹1,00,000 (FY 2026-27):*
```
Sundry Creditor A/c      Dr  84,00,000
Exchange Loss (P&L) A/c  Dr   1,00,000
    To Bank A/c                      85,00,000
```

**Answer:** Machinery is carried at **₹82,00,000** (unchanged). Exchange loss to P&L: **₹2,00,000 in FY 2025-26** and **₹1,00,000 in FY 2026-27**.

**> Full-marks tip:** The examiner tests whether you restate the **creditor but not the asset** — do NOT adjust the machine's cost for exchange difference (that was the old AS 11, 1994; current AS 11 routes it through P&L unless the para 46A option is invoked, which must be stated only if asked).

### Q3. Forward Exchange Contract — AS 11 (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** On 1st February 2026, Prasad Ltd (₹ reporting) entered into a 3-month forward contract to buy USD 1,00,000 to settle a genuine import payable (not for speculation). Spot rate on 1.2.2026 was ₹82/USD; the forward rate was ₹83.20/USD. The contract matures on 30th April 2026. Show how the premium is dealt with and the amount charged to P&L for the year ended 31st March 2026.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 11):** For a forward contract not intended for trading/speculation, the **premium or discount** (difference between the forward rate and the spot rate at inception) is **amortised as expense or income over the life of the contract** (para 36). Any exchange difference on the contract (from movement in spot rates) is recognised in P&L in the period in which the rate changes.

**Working Note — Premium computation:**
- Premium = (Forward rate − Spot rate at inception) × Foreign currency amount
- = (83.20 − 82.00) × 1,00,000 = **₹1,20,000**
- Life of contract = 3 months (Feb, Mar, Apr) → monthly amortisation = 1,20,000 ÷ 3 = **₹40,000/month**

**Allocation:**
- FY 2025-26 (February + March 2026 = 2 months): ₹40,000 × 2 = **₹80,000** → charged to P&L
- FY 2026-27 (April 2026 = 1 month): **₹40,000** → charged to P&L

**Answer:** Premium of ₹1,20,000 is amortised on a straight-line basis over the contract life; **₹80,000 is charged to the P&L for the year ended 31st March 2026** and the balance ₹40,000 in the next year.

**> Full-marks tip:** Premium = forward *minus spot at inception* (not the payable's original rate), and it is spread over the **contract's life**, apportioned to the reporting date — carry only 2 of the 3 months into FY 2025-26.

### Q4. Government Grant on Depreciable Asset — Two Methods — AS 12 (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** Ganga Ltd acquired a machine on 1st April 2025 for ₹40,00,000 and received a government grant of ₹10,00,000 towards it. The machine has a useful life of 8 years with nil residual value (SLM). Show, for the first year, the accounting under both permitted methods of AS 12 and the net charge to P&L.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 12):** A grant related to a depreciable asset may be accounted for by either **(a) deducting the grant from the gross value of the asset** (Method I), or **(b) treating it as deferred income** credited to P&L over the asset's useful life in proportion to depreciation (Method II) — para 8.3/8.4.

**Method I — Deduction from cost of asset:**
- Cost recorded = 40,00,000 − 10,00,000 = **₹30,00,000**
- Depreciation = 30,00,000 ÷ 8 = **₹3,75,000 p.a.**
- **Net charge to P&L (Yr 1) = ₹3,75,000**

**Method II — Deferred income:**
- Asset carried at ₹40,00,000; Depreciation = 40,00,000 ÷ 8 = ₹5,00,000
- Grant credited to P&L = 10,00,000 ÷ 8 = ₹1,25,000
- **Net charge to P&L (Yr 1) = 5,00,000 − 1,25,000 = ₹3,75,000**
- Deferred grant carried to Balance Sheet at year-end = 10,00,000 − 1,25,000 = **₹8,75,000** (₹1,25,000 current, ₹7,50,000 non-current)

**Answer:** Under both methods the **net charge to P&L is identical (₹3,75,000)**; the difference is only in presentation of the asset and the deferred-income liability.

**> Full-marks tip:** Show that the net P&L effect is the *same* under both methods; the marks for Method II are for disclosing the **unamortised deferred grant** split into current/non-current in the balance sheet.

### Q5. Government Grant — Promoters' Contribution & Refund — AS 12 (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** (a) Yamuna Ltd received a government grant of ₹20,00,000 by way of promoters' contribution for setting up a plant in a backward area; the grant is not linked to any specific asset. How should it be accounted? (b) Kaveri Ltd had credited a grant of ₹10,00,000 (received on a depreciable asset) to a "deferred grant" account under the deferred-income method; after 3 years the unamortised balance stood at ₹6,25,000 (annual credit ₹1,25,000). **Model Answer:**

**(a) Promoters' contribution:**
```
Bank A/c                Dr  20,00,000
    To Capital Reserve             20,00,000
```

**(b) Refund of grant — Working Note:**
- Unamortised deferred grant balance = ₹6,25,000
- Refund payable = ₹10,00,000
- Excess of refund over deferred credit = 10,00,000 − 6,25,000 = **₹3,75,000 → charged to P&L (extraordinary item)**

```
Deferred Government Grant A/c   Dr  6,25,000
Profit & Loss A/c (extraordinary) Dr 3,75,000
    To Bank A/c                          10,00,000
```

**> Full-marks tip:** Promoters' contribution → **Capital Reserve** (state it explicitly). On refund, first knock off the deferred-grant balance, then charge only the *excess* to P&L as an **extraordinary item** — quoting AS 5 earns the reasoning mark.

### Q6. Valuation of Investments (Portfolio) — AS 13 (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** As on 31st March 2026, Nivesh Ltd holds the following investments. Determine the carrying amount of each.
- Long-term: 10,000 equity shares of Alpha Ltd — cost ₹12,00,000, market value ₹9,00,000 (the decline is considered temporary).

- 1st July 2025: Sun Ltd made a bonus issue of 1 share for every 5 held.
- 1st October 2025: Sun Ltd made a rights issue of 1 share for every 4 held at ₹120 per share, which Dhan Ltd fully subscribed.

**Working Notes:**

### Q8. Borrowing Costs — Specific Borrowing — AS 16 (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** On 1st April 2025, Nirman Ltd borrowed ₹50,00,000 @10% p.a. specifically to construct a qualifying asset. Construction continued throughout the year. During the first 3 months, surplus funds of ₹10,00,000 out of this borrowing were temporarily invested and earned income @6% p.a. Compute the borrowing cost eligible for capitalisation for the year ended 31st March 2026.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 16):** When funds are **borrowed specifically** for obtaining a qualifying asset, the amount eligible for capitalisation is the **actual borrowing cost incurred less any income earned on the temporary investment** of those borrowings (para 10).

**Working Notes:**
- Actual interest = 50,00,000 × 10% = **₹5,00,000**
- Income on temporary investment = 10,00,000 × 6% × 3/12 = **₹15,000**
- Eligible borrowing cost = 5,00,000 − 15,000 = **₹4,85,000**

**Answer:** Borrowing cost to be **capitalised = ₹4,85,000**.

**> Full-marks tip:** For a *specific* borrowing, **deduct the temporary investment income** from the actual interest — forgetting the deduction (or pro-rating for the wrong period) loses the marks. Do NOT deduct such income under the general-borrowing route.

### Q9. Borrowing Costs — General Borrowings (Capitalisation Rate) — AS 16 (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** Setu Ltd finances the construction of a qualifying asset out of its general borrowings, which comprise a 12% loan of ₹40,00,000 and a 10% loan of ₹60,00,000 (both outstanding for the whole year). Expenditure on the asset was: ₹20,00,000 on 1st April 2025 and ₹30,00,000 on 1st October 2025. Compute the borrowing cost to be capitalised for the year ended 31st March 2026.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 16):** Where funds are borrowed **generally**, the capitalisation rate is the **weighted-average** of the borrowing costs of general borrowings outstanding during the period, applied to the **weighted-average expenditure** on the asset; the amount capitalised cannot exceed the **actual borrowing costs** incurred (para 12).

**Working Notes:**

*WN1 — Capitalisation rate:*
- Total interest = (40,00,000 × 12%) + (60,00,000 × 10%) = 4,80,000 + 6,00,000 = **₹10,80,000**
- Total borrowings = ₹1,00,00,000
- Capitalisation rate = 10,80,000 ÷ 1,00,00,000 = **10.8%**

*WN2 — Weighted-average expenditure on the asset:*
- 20,00,000 × 12/12 = 20,00,000
- 30,00,000 × 6/12 = 15,00,000
- Total = **₹35,00,000**

*WN3 — Borrowing cost to capitalise:*
- 35,00,000 × 10.8% = **₹3,78,000** (below actual interest ₹10,80,000, so fully capitalisable)

**Answer:** Borrowing cost to be **capitalised = ₹3,78,000**.

**> Full-marks tip:** Time-weight the *expenditure* (the ₹30 lakh spent on 1 Oct counts for only 6 months) and apply the **weighted-average capitalisation rate**; state the ceiling — capitalisation cannot exceed actual borrowing cost incurred.

### Q10. Purchase Consideration — AS 14 (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** B Ltd took over the business of A Ltd. Under the scheme, B Ltd agreed to:
- issue 5 equity shares of ₹10 each at ₹12 per share for every 4 equity shares held in A Ltd (A Ltd has 4,00,000 equity shares);
- issue 12% preference shares of ₹10 each at par to discharge A Ltd's 2,00,000 preference shares of ₹10 each, on the basis of 11 preference shares of B Ltd for every 10 held in A Ltd;
- meet A Ltd's liquidation expenses of ₹1,50,000.
Compute the purchase consideration as per AS 14.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 14):** Purchase consideration is the **aggregate of shares and other securities issued and payments made** by the transferee to the **shareholders** of the transferor (para 3(g)). Payments made to discharge liabilities/creditors and the transferor's **liquidation expenses are NOT part of purchase consideration**.

**Working Notes:**

*WN1 — To equity shareholders:*
- Shares issued = 4,00,000 × 5/4 = **5,00,000 equity shares** of B Ltd
- Value = 5,00,000 × ₹12 = **₹60,00,000**

*WN2 — To preference shareholders:*
- Shares issued = 2,00,000 × 11/10 = **2,20,000 preference shares** of B Ltd
- Value = 2,20,000 × ₹10 = **₹22,00,000**

*WN3 — Liquidation expenses ₹1,50,000 — excluded from purchase consideration.*

**Answer:** **Purchase Consideration = 60,00,000 + 22,00,000 = ₹82,00,000.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Purchase consideration is only what goes to the **shareholders** — value the equity shares at the *issue price* (₹12, including premium), and **exclude liquidation expenses / creditor payments**. Stating the exclusion explicitly secures a mark.

### Q11. Amalgamation — Journal Entries (Purchase Method) — AS 14 (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** B Ltd acquires the business of A Ltd (an amalgamation in the nature of purchase). The agreed values are: sundry assets ₹1,32,00,000 and liabilities taken over ₹40,00,000. The purchase consideration of ₹90,00,000 is discharged by issue of equity shares of ₹75,00,000 (face value) at a premium of ₹15,00,000. Pass the journal entries in the books of B Ltd and show the resulting goodwill/capital reserve.

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 14):** Under the **purchase method**, assets and liabilities of the transferor are incorporated at their **agreed (fair) values**; the excess of purchase consideration over the net assets acquired is **goodwill**, and any shortfall is **capital reserve** (para 12, 19).

**Working Note — Goodwill / Capital Reserve:**
- Net assets acquired = 1,32,00,000 − 40,00,000 = ₹92,00,000
- Purchase consideration = ₹90,00,000
- PC < Net assets → **Capital Reserve = 92,00,000 − 90,00,000 = ₹2,00,000**

**Journal Entries in the books of B Ltd:**
```
(1) Business Purchase A/c            Dr  90,00,000
        To Liquidator of A Ltd                90,00,000
    (Being purchase consideration agreed)

(2) Sundry Assets A/c                Dr 1,32,00,000
        To Sundry Liabilities A/c              40,00,000
        To Business Purchase A/c               90,00,000
        To Capital Reserve A/c                  2,00,000
    (Being assets & liabilities taken over at agreed values)

(3) Liquidator of A Ltd A/c          Dr  90,00,000
        To Equity Share Capital A/c            75,00,000
        To Securities Premium A/c              15,00,000
    (Being PC discharged by issue of shares at a premium)
```

**Answer:** Amalgamation results in a **Capital Reserve of ₹2,00,000** (no goodwill).

**> Full-marks tip:** In the purchase method the balancing figure is goodwill *or* capital reserve — here PC is *less than* net assets, so it is a **Capital Reserve**. Route the ₹15,00,000 excess over face value to **Securities Premium**, not to the asset side.

### Q12. Identifying Reportable Segments — AS 17 (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** Vividh Ltd has five business segments with the following data (₹ in crore). Identify the reportable segments as per AS 17.

| Segment | External revenue | Inter-segment revenue | Segment result (profit/(loss)) | Segment assets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 100 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
| B | 40 | 10 | (8) | 30 |
| C | 8 | 2 | 5 | 8 |
| D | 6 | 0 | 2 | 5 |
| E | 4 | 0 | 1 | 3 |

**Model Answer:**
**Governing principle (AS 17):** A segment is **reportable** if it satisfies **any one** of three 10% thresholds (para 27): (i) segment revenue (external + inter-segment) ≥ 10% of total revenue of all segments; (ii) absolute segment result ≥ 10% of the greater of total profit of profit-making segments and total loss of loss-making segments; (iii) segment assets ≥ 10% of total assets. Additionally, reportable segments must together contribute **at least 75% of total external revenue** (para 28).

**Working Notes:**

*WN1 — Revenue test:* Total revenue = 120 + 50 + 10 + 6 + 4 = **190**; 10% = 19. Qualify: **A (120), B (50)**.

*WN2 — Result test:* Total profits = 30 + 5 + 2 + 1 = 38; Total losses = 8. Greater absolute = **38**; 10% = 3.8. Qualify (|result| ≥ 3.8): **A (30), B (8), C (5)**.

*WN3 — Assets test:* Total assets = 40 + 30 + 8 + 5 + 3 = **86**; 10% = 8.6. Qualify: **A (40), B (30)**.

*WN4 — Reportable (any one test met):* **A, B and C.**

*WN5 — 75% external-revenue test:* External revenue of A+B+C = 100 + 40 + 8 = 148; total external revenue = 158; 148 ÷ 158 = **93.7% (≥ 75%)** → no additional segment need be designated.

**Answer:** **Reportable segments are A, B and C.** (D and E are aggregated into "all other segments".)

**> Full-marks tip:** For the result test, the denominator is the **greater of total profits (38) and total losses (8)** — a very common error is to net them. Show all three tests and then confirm the **75% coverage** check.

**> Full-marks tip:** Recognise at the **lower of FV and PV of MLP** (₹9,50,970, not ₹10,00,000), and depreciate over the **shorter of lease term and useful life** because ownership does not transfer. Show the finance-charge table separately.

### Q14. Revenue Recognition — Interest, Royalties, Dividends — AS 9 (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the bases prescribed by AS 9 for recognising revenue arising from the use by others of enterprise resources yielding interest, royalties and dividends, and the condition common to all revenue recognition.

**Model Answer:**
**Provision (AS 9, para 13):** Revenue arising from the use of enterprise resources by others should be recognised when **no significant uncertainty as to measurability or collectability exists**, on the following bases:

- **Interest** — recognised on a **time-proportion basis** taking into account the amount outstanding and the effective/applicable rate.
- **Royalties** — recognised on an **accrual basis** in accordance with the terms of the **relevant agreement**.
- **Dividends** — recognised when the **owner's right to receive payment is established** (i.e., when declared/approved).

**Common overriding condition (para 9):** Where the ability to assess the ultimate collection with reasonable certainty is lacking at the time of raising the claim, revenue recognition is **postponed** to the extent of the uncertainty; in such cases it is recognised only when it becomes **reasonably certain** that the ultimate collection will be made.

**Conclusion:** Interest → time basis; Royalty → accrual per agreement; Dividend → right to receive established — all subject to no significant uncertainty regarding measurement or collection.

**> Full-marks tip:** Give the *specific basis* for each of the three (time-proportion / accrual per agreement / right to receive) plus the common "no significant uncertainty" condition — one line each, with the para reference.

### Q15. Monetary vs Non-Monetary Items — AS 11 (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between monetary and non-monetary items under AS 11 and explain how each is measured at the reporting date. Where are exchange differences recognised?

**Model Answer:**
**Definition (AS 11, para 7):** **Monetary items** are money held and assets/liabilities to be received or paid in **fixed or determinable amounts of money** (e.g., cash, receivables, payables, loans). **Non-monetary items** are all other items — those *not* carrying a right to receive/obligation to pay a fixed sum of money (e.g., fixed assets, inventories, investments in equity shares).

**Measurement at each balance-sheet date (para 11):**
- **Monetary items** — reported using the **closing rate**.
- **Non-monetary items carried at historical cost** — reported using the **exchange rate at the date of the transaction** (i.e., not restated).
- **Non-monetary items carried at fair value** — reported using the **exchange rates that existed when the fair values were determined**.

**Recognition of exchange differences (para 13):** Exchange differences arising on the settlement or restatement of **monetary items** are recognised as **income or expense in the period** in which they arise (i.e., in the statement of profit and loss).

**Conclusion:** Only monetary items are restated at the closing rate; the resulting exchange differences flow through profit and loss (subject to the para 46A option, where availed).

**> Full-marks tip:** The examiner wants the crisp rule: **monetary → closing rate; non-monetary at cost → transaction-date rate (not restated)**; and that exchange differences on monetary items go to **P&L**.

### Q16. Government Grants — Accounting Approaches — AS 12 (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the two broad approaches for accounting of government grants under AS 12, and briefly state the prescribed treatment for (i) grants related to specific fixed assets, (ii) grants related to revenue, and (iii) grants in the nature of promoters' contribution.

**Model Answer:**
**Two broad approaches (AS 12, para 5):**
1. **Capital approach** — the grant is treated as part of shareholders' funds, on the reasoning that it is a form of finance and there is no repayment obligation.
2. **Income approach** — the grant is taken to income over one or more periods, on the reasoning that grants are receipts from a source other than shareholders and should be matched with the related costs they are intended to compensate.

**AS 12 adopts these selectively depending on the nature of the grant:**

- **(i) Grants related to specific fixed assets (para 8):** Either **deduct the grant from the gross value of the asset** (net cost is depreciated), or treat it as **deferred income** and recognise it in P&L over the useful life in proportion to depreciation. (Income approach.)
- **(ii) Grants related to revenue (para 9):** Recognised in the **P&L** — either as "other income" or as a **deduction** from the related expense, in the period(s) in which the related costs are incurred. (Income approach.)
- **(iii) Grants in the nature of promoters' contribution (para 10):** Given as total investment with no repayment obligation and not related to any specific asset → credited to **Capital Reserve** as part of shareholders' funds. (Capital approach.)

**Conclusion:** AS 12 applies the income approach to asset-related and revenue grants and the capital approach to promoters'-contribution grants and non-monetary grants at concessional rates.

**> Full-marks tip:** Name both approaches, then map each grant type to its treatment; the promoters'-contribution → **Capital Reserve** point is the one most often dropped.

### Q17. Related Party Disclosures — AS 18 (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain what constitutes a "related party" under AS 18. List the relationships covered and any two relationships specifically **not** deemed related parties. What disclosures are required if related-party transactions exist?

**Model Answer:**
**Definition (AS 18, para 10.1):** Parties are considered related if, at any time during the reporting period, **one party has the ability to control the other party** or **exercise significant influence** over the other in making financial and/or operating decisions.

**Relationships covered (para 3):**
1. Enterprises that directly/indirectly through intermediaries **control**, are controlled by, or are under common control with, the reporting enterprise (holding, subsidiaries, fellow subsidiaries).
2. **Associates and joint ventures**, and the investing party/venturer.
3. **Individuals** owning (directly/indirectly) an interest in voting power giving significant influence, and their **relatives**.
4. **Key Management Personnel (KMP)** and their relatives.
5. **Enterprises** over which any person in (3) or (4) is able to exercise significant influence.

**Relationships NOT deemed related (para 5) — any two:** (a) two companies merely having a **director in common** (unless the director can affect both companies' policies in their mutual dealings); (b) a single **customer, supplier, franchiser or financier** with whom there is significant volume of business, merely by economic dependence; (c) providers of finance, trade unions, public utilities, and government departments in the course of their normal dealings.

**Disclosures required (para 23-24):**
- The **name of the related party** and **nature of the relationship** where control exists (irrespective of whether transactions occurred).
- Where transactions have taken place: **nature of transactions, volume (amount or proportion), outstanding amounts** at the balance-sheet date, provisions for doubtful debts, and amounts written off/back.

**> Full-marks tip:** State the *control/significant influence* test up front, list the covered relationships, and give at least one "**not related**" example (director-in-common / economic dependence) — that contrast usually carries a full mark.

### Q18. Classification & Reclassification of Investments — AS 13 (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** How are investments classified under AS 13, and at what value are transfers (reclassifications) between the categories recorded?

**Model Answer:**
**Classification (AS 13, para 9):** Investments are classified into:
- **Current investments** — investments that are by their nature **readily realisable** and intended to be held for **not more than one year** from the date of acquisition.
- **Long-term investments** — investments **other than current investments** (intended to be held beyond one year).

**Carrying amounts (para 14, 17):** Current investments → **lower of cost and fair value**; Long-term investments → **cost**, less a provision for any **other-than-temporary** diminution.

**Reclassification / transfers (para 22-23):**
- **Long-term → Current:** transferred at the **lower of cost and carrying amount** at the date of transfer.
- **Current → Long-term:** transferred at the **lower of cost and fair value** at the date of transfer.

**Conclusion:** Reclassification is always recorded at a value that does **not** carry forward an unrecognised loss — the transfer value is struck at the lower of the two relevant amounts.

**> Full-marks tip:** Get the two transfer rules the right way round — **long-term→current = lower of cost & carrying amount**; **current→long-term = lower of cost & fair value**. Reversing them is the classic error.

### Q19. Segment Reporting — Primary/Secondary & Disclosures — AS 17 (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain how an enterprise determines its **primary** and **secondary** reporting formats under AS 17, and state the basis for identifying business and geographical segments.

**Model Answer:**
**Basis of identifying segments (AS 17, para 5, 20):**
- A **business segment** is a distinguishable component engaged in providing an **individual product or service or a group of related products/services** that is subject to risks and returns **different** from those of other segments (factors: nature of products, production processes, type of customers, distribution methods, regulatory environment).
- A **geographical segment** is a distinguishable component engaged in providing products/services within a **particular economic environment** subject to risks and returns different from other environments.

**Primary and secondary formats (para 26):** The dominant source and nature of an enterprise's **risks and returns** governs whether business segments or geographical segments are its **primary** reporting format:
- If risks/returns are affected **predominantly by differences in products/services**, the **primary format is business segments** and the secondary is geographical segments.
- If risks/returns are affected **predominantly by the geographical areas** of operation, the **primary format is geographical segments** and the secondary is business segments.
- Internal organisation and management structure (and the internal financial reporting system) are normally the basis for identifying the predominant source of risks and returns.

**Disclosures:** Extensive disclosures (segment revenue, result, assets, liabilities, etc.) are required for the **primary** format; a **reduced** set of disclosures is required for the **secondary** format.

**> Full-marks tip:** Anchor the primary/secondary choice to the **dominant source of risks and returns** (products vs geography) and mention that primary format gets **full** disclosures while secondary gets **limited** ones.

### Q20. Types of Amalgamation & Merger Conditions — AS 14 (marks: 6) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between the two types of amalgamation under AS 14. State the conditions that must be satisfied for an amalgamation to qualify as an "amalgamation in the nature of merger", and the accounting method applicable to each type.

**Model Answer:**
**Two types (AS 14, para 3):**
1. **Amalgamation in the nature of merger** — a genuine pooling of the assets, liabilities and shareholders' interests of the amalgamating companies.
2. **Amalgamation in the nature of purchase** — one where the conditions for a merger are **not all** satisfied.

**Five conditions for "amalgamation in the nature of merger" (para 3(e)) — ALL must be met:**
1. **All assets and liabilities** of the transferor company become, after amalgamation, the assets and liabilities of the transferee company.
2. **Shareholders holding not less than 90%** of the face value of the equity shares of the transferor company (other than shares already held by the transferee/its subsidiaries/nominees) become equity shareholders of the transferee.
3. The consideration to those equity shareholders is discharged **wholly by the issue of equity shares** of the transferee, except that **cash** may be paid for **fractional shares**.
4. The **business** of the transferor company is **intended to be carried on** by the transferee after the amalgamation.
5. **No adjustment** is intended to be made to the **book values** of the assets and liabilities of the transferor, except to ensure **uniformity of accounting policies**.

**Accounting methods (para 7):**
- **Merger → Pooling of Interests Method:** assets, liabilities and reserves recorded at **book values**; the difference between the consideration and the share capital of the transferor is adjusted in **reserves**. Statutory reserves and other reserves are **carried forward**.
- **Purchase → Purchase Method:** assets and liabilities recorded at **agreed/fair values**; excess of consideration over net assets = **goodwill** (amortised, ordinarily within 5 years), shortfall = **capital reserve**; the transferor's reserves (except statutory reserves) are **not** carried forward.

**> Full-marks tip:** List **all five** merger conditions — the "90% equity holders" and "consideration wholly in equity shares (except cash for fractions)" points are the discriminating ones; then correctly pair merger → pooling and purchase → purchase method.

### Q2. Construction contract — expected loss (marks: 4) [Practical]
**Question:** A contractor took a contract of ₹200 lakhs. Cost incurred to date is ₹90 lakhs and estimated further cost to complete is ₹150 lakhs. Progress billings of ₹80 lakhs have been raised. How much revenue, cost and profit/loss should be recognised as per AS 7?

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable standard:** AS 7 para 35 — when it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue, the **expected loss is recognised as an expense immediately, in full**, irrespective of the stage of completion.

**Working Note — Expected outcome**
| Particulars | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Total estimated cost (90 + 150) | 240 |
| Contract price | 200 |
| **Expected total loss** | **(40)** |

**Stage of completion** = 90 ÷ 240 = 37.5%

**Recognition in Statement of P&L**
| Particulars | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Contract revenue (200 × 37.5%) | 75 |
| Contract cost (balancing to give full loss) | 115 |
| **Loss recognised** | **(40)** |

The cost charged is *revenue recognised (75) + full expected loss (40) = 115*, so that the entire ₹40 lakh loss hits the P&L now.

**Final answer: Recognise revenue ₹75 lakhs and the full expected loss of ₹40 lakhs immediately.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Do not spread the loss over the stage of completion — AS 7 requires the *whole* foreseeable loss now. State para 35 explicitly.

### Q3. AS 9 — timing of revenue recognition (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** State, with reasons, when revenue should be recognised in each of the following independent situations as per AS 9:
(i) Goods sold to a customer on 28.03.2026 for ₹5,00,000 on "sale or return" basis; the approval period expires on 15.04.2026 and no confirmation has been received by year-end.
(ii) A company sold goods for ₹10,00,000 but installation (a significant part of the contract) is pending on 31.03.2026.
(iii) Interest of ₹40,000 accrued on a fixed deposit for the year ended 31.03.2026.
(iv) Dividend of ₹25,000 declared by an investee company on 10.04.2026 for FY 2025-26.
(v) An advertising commission where the advertisement appeared before year-end but the invoice is raised in April.

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable standard:** AS 9 *Revenue Recognition*. Revenue from sale of goods is recognised when the seller has transferred property in the goods / significant risks and rewards and no significant uncertainty exists as to collectability (para 6.1); revenue from services on completion / proportionate completion (para 7); interest on a time-proportion basis, dividends when the right to receive is established (para 13).

**Point-wise answer:**
- **(i) Sale on approval — NOT recognised.** Significant risks and rewards have not passed as the approval period had not expired and no acceptance was received. Recognise only after approval or on expiry of the period on 15.04.2026. **Revenue in FY 2025-26 = Nil.**
- **(ii) Installation pending — NOT recognised.** Where installation is a *significant* element of the contract, revenue is recognised only when the goods are installed and accepted by the buyer. **Revenue = Nil** for FY 2025-26.
- **(iii) Interest ₹40,000 — RECOGNISE.** Interest accrues on a time-proportion basis by reference to the amount outstanding and rate applicable. **Recognise ₹40,000.**
- **(iv) Dividend ₹25,000 — NOT recognised.** The right to receive arises only when the dividend is *declared* (10.04.2026), which is after the balance-sheet date. **Recognise in FY 2026-27, not 2025-26.**
- **(v) Advertising commission — RECOGNISE.** For advertising, revenue is recognised when the advertisement *appears* before the public. Since it appeared before year-end, recognise the commission in FY 2025-26 regardless of the invoice date.

**> Full-marks tip:** Anchor each answer to the trigger event (risk/reward transfer, service completion, time-proportion accrual, right-to-receive) — a bare "yes/no" without the AS 9 reason loses half the mark.

### Q4. AS 22 — deferred tax on timing differences (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** For the year ended 31.03.2026, X Ltd. reports accounting profit before tax of ₹20,00,000. Depreciation as per books is ₹5,00,000 while depreciation allowable under the Income-tax Act is ₹8,00,000. Donations disallowed under tax amount to ₹50,000. The enacted tax rate is 30%. Compute current tax, deferred tax and total tax expense, and pass the journal entry.

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable standard:** AS 22 *Accounting for Taxes on Income*. Tax expense = current tax + deferred tax. Deferred tax arises on **timing differences** (differences that originate in one period and reverse in one or more subsequent periods). Permanent differences (e.g. disallowed donations) do **not** create deferred tax.

**Working Note 1 — Current tax (taxable income)**
| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Accounting profit before tax | 20,00,000 |
| Add: Depreciation as per books | 5,00,000 |
| Add: Donation disallowed (permanent) | 50,000 |
| Less: Depreciation as per Income-tax | (8,00,000) |
| **Taxable income** | **17,50,000** |
| **Current tax @ 30%** | **5,25,000** |

**Working Note 2 — Deferred tax**
Timing difference = Tax depreciation − Book depreciation = 8,00,000 − 5,00,000 = **3,00,000** (tax depreciation higher → taxable income lower now, will reverse later → **Deferred Tax Liability**).
DTL = 3,00,000 × 30% = **₹90,000**.
(Donation is a *permanent* difference → no deferred tax.)

**Total tax expense = 5,25,000 + 90,000 = ₹6,15,000**

**Journal Entry:**
```
Profit & Loss A/c ............................ Dr   6,15,000
    To Current Tax (Provision for Tax) A/c            5,25,000
    To Deferred Tax Liability A/c                        90,000
(Being current and deferred tax for the year recognised)
```

**Final answer: Current tax ₹5,25,000, Deferred Tax Liability ₹90,000, total tax expense ₹6,15,000.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Exclude permanent differences from deferred tax and correctly label the balance as a *liability* (tax depreciation > book depreciation). Reversing the DTA/DTL direction is the classic mark-losing error.

### Q5. AS 22 — deferred tax asset on carry-forward loss (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Y Ltd. incurred a business loss of ₹12,00,000 (as per both books and tax) for the year ended 31.03.2026, which is available for carry-forward and set-off under the Income-tax Act. In addition, there is a timing difference: book depreciation ₹2,00,000 exceeds tax depreciation ₹1,40,000. The tax rate is 30%. The company has entered into a binding export contract that makes it virtually certain that sufficient future taxable income will be available. Show the deferred tax accounting.

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable standard:** AS 22. A deferred tax **asset** on unabsorbed depreciation and carry-forward losses is recognised **only to the extent that there is virtual certainty supported by convincing evidence** that sufficient future taxable income will be available (para 17). For ordinary deductible timing differences, "reasonable certainty" suffices (para 15–16).

**Working Note 1 — DTA on carry-forward business loss**
Virtual certainty exists (binding export contract) → recognise DTA.
DTA on loss = 12,00,000 × 30% = **₹3,60,000**.

**Working Note 2 — DTA on timing difference (depreciation)**
Book depreciation (2,00,000) > Tax depreciation (1,40,000) → excess ₹60,000 charged in books now but allowed in tax later → **deductible timing difference → DTA**.
DTA = 60,000 × 30% = **₹18,000**.

**Total Deferred Tax Asset = 3,60,000 + 18,000 = ₹3,78,000**

**Journal Entry:**
```
Deferred Tax Asset A/c ....................... Dr   3,78,000
    To Profit & Loss A/c                              3,78,000
(Being DTA recognised on carry-forward loss and timing difference,
 virtual certainty existing)
```

**Final answer: Recognise a Deferred Tax Asset of ₹3,78,000 (credited to P&L), reducing the reported loss.**

**> Full-marks tip:** For losses/unabsorbed depreciation you must cite the *virtual certainty + convincing evidence* test (para 17) — merely "reasonable certainty" for a carry-forward loss is wrong and loses marks.

### Q6. AS 26 — research vs development expenditure (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** During 2025-26 Z Ltd. incurred the following on an internal project to develop a new manufacturing process:
- Research phase costs (before technical feasibility): ₹8,00,000
- Development phase costs after all recognition criteria were met: ₹15,00,000
- Administrative overheads (not directly attributable): ₹1,20,000
- Cost of testing the process to prepare it for intended use: ₹2,00,000
- Selling costs of the new product produced during trial: ₹90,000

The process is expected to have a useful life of 5 years and was ready for use on 01.10.2025. State the amount to be capitalised as an intangible asset and the amortisation for the year.

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable standard:** AS 26 *Intangible Assets*.
- **Research phase** expenditure is **always charged to the P&L** as incurred (para 41); an internally generated intangible cannot arise from research.
- **Development phase** expenditure is **capitalised only** if all six criteria are met (technical feasibility, intention to complete, ability to use/sell, probable future economic benefits, availability of resources, ability to measure cost reliably) (para 44).
- Cost of an internally generated intangible includes directly attributable costs to create/produce/prepare it for use; it **excludes** selling, administrative and general overheads and clearly identified inefficiencies (para 53–55).

**Working Note — Cost eligible for capitalisation**
| Particulars | Treatment | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Research phase costs | Expense (P&L) | — |
| Development phase costs (criteria met) | Capitalise | 15,00,000 |
| Administrative overheads (not attributable) | Expense | — |
| Testing to prepare for intended use | Capitalise (directly attributable) | 2,00,000 |
| Selling costs during trial | Expense | — |
| **Capitalised cost of intangible asset** | | **17,00,000** |

**Amortisation (para 63, 72):** Straight-line over useful life; asset ready for use on 01.10.2025 → amortise for 6 months.
Annual amortisation = 17,00,000 ÷ 5 = 3,40,000 → for 6 months = **₹1,70,000**.

**Final answer: Capitalise ₹17,00,000; charge ₹8,00,000 research + ₹1,20,000 admin + ₹90,000 selling to P&L; amortisation for the year ₹1,70,000.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Research is *never* capitalised — expense it and only capitalise development after all six criteria are met. Exclude selling/admin overheads from cost, and pro-rate amortisation from the date the asset is ready for use.

### Q8. AS 29 — provision for warranty (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** W Ltd. sells goods with a warranty covering repairs of manufacturing defects for one year from sale.
```
Warranty Expense A/c ......................... Dr   2,25,000
    To Provision for Warranty A/c                      2,25,000
```

### Q9. Buyback — maximum number of shares (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** The Balance Sheet of P Ltd. as on 31.03.2026 shows:
- Equity share capital (1,20,000 shares of ₹10 each, fully paid): ₹12,00,000
- General reserve: ₹15,00,000
- Securities premium: ₹4,00,000
- Profit & Loss (credit): ₹1,00,000
- Secured loans: ₹10,00,000
- Unsecured loans: ₹3,00,000

The company proposes to buy back equity shares at ₹30 per share. Determine the maximum number of shares that can be bought back as per the Companies Act, 2013.

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable provision:** Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. Buyback must satisfy **three limits**; the maximum permissible is the **lowest** of the three.

**Test 1 — Shares Outstanding Test (≤ 25% of paid-up equity capital)**
25% × 1,20,000 shares = **30,000 shares**.

**Test 2 — Resources Test (buyback ≤ 25% of paid-up capital + free reserves)**
Free reserves incl. securities premium = 15,00,000 + 4,00,000 + 1,00,000 = 20,00,000.
Paid-up capital + free reserves = 12,00,000 + 20,00,000 = 32,00,000.
Maximum amount = 25% × 32,00,000 = **₹8,00,000**.
Maximum shares = 8,00,000 ÷ 30 = **26,666 shares** (round down).

**Test 3 — Debt-Equity Ratio Test (post-buyback debt ≤ 2 × equity)**
Total debt = 10,00,000 + 3,00,000 = 13,00,000.
Minimum equity (owners' funds) required after buyback = 13,00,000 ÷ 2 = 6,50,000.
Present owners' funds = 32,00,000. Maximum amount usable = 32,00,000 − 6,50,000 = 25,50,000.
Maximum shares = 25,50,000 ÷ 30 = **85,000 shares**.

**Conclusion — maximum permissible buyback**
| Test | Shares |
|---|---|
| Shares outstanding test | 30,000 |
| Resources test | 26,666 |
| Debt-equity ratio test | 85,000 |
| **Maximum (lowest)** | **26,666 shares** |

**Final answer: Maximum number of shares that can be bought back = 26,666 shares.**

**> Full-marks tip:** Always apply *all three* tests and pick the minimum; convert the resources/debt-equity limits from rupees into shares using the buyback price and round *down* to whole shares.

The balance is funded out of free reserves. The company has adequate general reserve and securities premium. Pass the necessary journal entries.

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable provision:** Sections 68 & 69, Companies Act 2013. Premium on buyback is met out of securities premium/free reserves; the **nominal value of shares bought back, to the extent not funded by a fresh issue, is transferred to the Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR)** (Section 69).


### Q12. AS 25 — measurement of costs in interim period (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** S Ltd. prepares quarterly interim financial reports. For the quarter ended 30.06.2025 the following items arise. Explain the treatment as per AS 25:
(i) The company expects an annual bonus of ₹24,00,000 to be paid; a present obligation exists and payment is probable.
(ii) A major planned advertising campaign costing ₹8,00,000 was incurred in the first quarter but benefits the whole year.
(iii) Annual repairs and maintenance of ₹12,00,000 are usually incurred in the fourth quarter.
(iv) Estimated annual effective income-tax rate is 30%; interim pre-tax profit is ₹40,00,000.

**Model Answer:**

**Applicable standard:** AS 25 *Interim Financial Reporting*. Interim measurement uses a **"discrete period with an integral view" for certain items** — the entity applies the **same recognition and measurement principles** as in the annual report; it does *not* anticipate or defer costs that would not qualify as assets/liabilities at year-end (para 28–29). An expense is recognised in an interim period only if a liability/asset criterion is met at the interim date.

**Point-wise answer:**
- **(i) Bonus ₹24,00,000 — provide proportionately.** A present obligation exists and payment is probable, so accrue on a systematic basis. Charge for the quarter = 24,00,000 × 3/12 = **₹6,00,000**.
- **(ii) Advertising ₹8,00,000 — expense in full in Q1.** A cost that benefits future interim periods is deferred *only* if it meets the definition of an asset. Advertising/promotion costs do not create an asset → charge the entire **₹8,00,000** in the first quarter.
- **(iii) Annual repairs ₹12,00,000 — do not accrue in advance.** No present obligation exists until the repairs are incurred; anticipating a future cost is not permitted. Recognise **nil** now; charge when incurred (Q4).
- **(iv) Income tax — apply estimated annual effective rate.** Interim tax = 40,00,000 × 30% = **₹12,00,000**. AS 25 requires the estimated *weighted-average annual* effective tax rate to be applied to interim pre-tax income.

**> Full-marks tip:** Accrue only items that meet asset/liability definitions at the interim date; do NOT pre-provide for costs like annual repairs, and apply the *estimated annual effective tax rate* — not the interim marginal rate — for tax.

### Q14. AS 9 — conditions for revenue recognition (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions to be satisfied for recognition of revenue from the sale of goods and from the rendering of services as per AS 9. Also state when revenue recognition is postponed.

**Model Answer:**

**Standard:** AS 9 *Revenue Recognition*.

**Revenue from sale of goods (para 6.1) — recognise when both:**
- The seller has **transferred to the buyer the property in the goods**, or **all significant risks and rewards of ownership** have passed and the seller retains no effective control; and
- **No significant uncertainty exists** regarding the amount of consideration and its **collectability**.

**Revenue from rendering of services (para 7) — recognised when:**
- Performance is achieved, measured either under the **completed service contract method** or the **proportionate completion method**; and
- No significant uncertainty exists as to the amount and collectability of consideration.

**Revenue from use of resources by others (para 13):**
- **Interest** — on a **time-proportion basis** taking into account the amount outstanding and rate.
- **Royalties** — on an **accrual basis** as per the terms of the relevant agreement.
- **Dividends** — when the **owner's right to receive payment is established** (i.e., declared).

**Postponement of recognition (para 9):**
- When at the time of raising a claim it is **unreasonable to expect ultimate collection**, revenue recognition is **postponed** to the extent of that uncertainty. Where collectability becomes uncertain *after* revenue is already recognised, a **separate provision** for the uncollectable amount is made rather than adjusting revenue.

**Conclusion:** Revenue is recognised when performance is complete/measurable and consideration is reasonably certain of collection; otherwise it is postponed.

**> Full-marks tip:** Distinguish *no significant uncertainty of collectability* (a condition of recognition) from a later doubt about collection (handled by a separate provision, not by reversing revenue) — examiners specifically test this nuance.

### Q15. AS 24 — discontinuing operations disclosures (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Define a "discontinuing operation" under AS 24 and state the initial disclosure event and the key disclosures required in the financial statements.

**Model Answer:**

**Standard:** AS 24 *Discontinuing Operations*.

**Definition (para 3):** A discontinuing operation is a **relatively large component** of an enterprise that:
- the enterprise, pursuant to a **single plan**, is disposing of substantially in its entirety, disposing of piecemeal, or terminating through abandonment; and
- represents a **separate major line of business or geographical area of operations**; and
- **can be distinguished operationally and for financial reporting** (its assets, liabilities, income and expenses can be directly attributed to it).

**Initial disclosure event (para 15):** Disclosure begins in the earlier of when the enterprise has:
- entered into a **binding sale agreement** for substantially all the assets; or
- the board/similar governing body has both **approved a detailed formal plan** and **made an announcement** of the plan.

**Key disclosures (para 20 and onwards):**
- A **description** of the discontinuing operation and the business/geographical segment it is reported in.
- The **date and nature** of the initial disclosure event and the expected timing/manner of completion.
- The **carrying amounts of total assets and liabilities** to be disposed of.
- The **revenue, expenses and pre-tax profit/loss** from ordinary activities attributable to it, and the related **income-tax expense**.
- The **net cash flows** attributable to operating, investing and financing activities.
- Amount of **pre-tax gain/loss on disposal** of assets/settlement of liabilities and related tax.

**Note:** AS 24 deals only with **disclosure and presentation**; it does **not** establish any new recognition or measurement principles (those follow other standards, e.g. AS 28, AS 29).

**> Full-marks tip:** State clearly that AS 24 is a *disclosure* standard (no new measurement rules) and give the "earlier of binding sale agreement / approved-and-announced plan" test for the initial disclosure event.

**Model Answer:**

**Standard:** AS 26 *Intangible Assets*.

**General recognition criteria (para 20–21):** An intangible asset is recognised only if:
- it is **probable** that the future economic benefits attributable to the asset will flow to the enterprise; and
- the **cost** of the asset can be **measured reliably**.
An intangible must also meet the definition — **identifiable** (separable or arising from contractual/legal rights), controlled by the enterprise, non-monetary, without physical substance.

**Development phase — capitalise only if ALL are demonstrated (para 44):**
- **Technical feasibility** of completing the asset so it will be available for use or sale;
- **Intention** to complete and use or sell it;
- **Ability** to use or sell it;
- how it will generate **probable future economic benefits** (existence of a market or usefulness);
- availability of adequate **technical, financial and other resources** to complete it; and
- ability to **measure reliably** the expenditure attributable to it during development.

**Research phase (para 41):** Expenditure is **always expensed** — no intangible from research is recognised.

**Items never recognised as internally generated intangibles (para 51, 56):**
- Internally generated **goodwill**;
- Internally generated **brands, mastheads, publishing titles, customer lists** and items similar in substance — because their cost cannot be distinguished from the cost of developing the business as a whole.

**Conclusion:** Only development expenditure meeting all six criteria is capitalised; research, internally generated goodwill and brands are expensed.

**> Full-marks tip:** Memorise the six development-capitalisation conditions and explicitly state that internally generated goodwill/brands are prohibited — listing all six earns the full mark; a partial list is scaled down.

### Q17. AS 28 — indicators of impairment (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the external and internal indications an enterprise should consider, at each balance-sheet date, in assessing whether an asset may be impaired under AS 28.

**Model Answer:**

**Standard:** AS 28 *Impairment of Assets*, para 8. At each balance-sheet date an enterprise assesses whether there is any indication of impairment; if any indication exists, the recoverable amount is estimated.

**External sources of information:**
- A **significant decline** in the asset's **market value**, more than expected from normal use/passage of time.
- **Significant adverse changes** in the technological, market, economic or legal environment in which the enterprise operates or in the market for the asset.
- **Increase in market interest rates / rates of return** that is likely to increase the discount rate used in computing value in use and materially decrease recoverable amount.
- The **carrying amount of net assets exceeds the enterprise's market capitalisation**.

**Internal sources of information:**
- Evidence of **obsolescence or physical damage** to the asset.
- **Significant adverse changes** in the extent/manner in which the asset is used or expected to be used — e.g., plans to **discontinue/restructure** the operation, dispose of the asset before the previously expected date, or the asset becoming idle.
- Evidence from **internal reporting** indicating that the **economic performance** of the asset is, or will be, **worse than expected**.

**Conclusion:** Even if no such indication exists, an intangible asset with an indefinite useful life or not yet available for use, and goodwill acquired in an amalgamation, may require testing; otherwise, presence of any indicator triggers estimation of recoverable amount.

**> Full-marks tip:** Split the answer cleanly into *external* and *internal* sources (that split itself carries marks) and give at least three under each — a jumbled list without the two-way classification is scored down.

### Q18. AS 29 — provision vs contingent liability (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between a "provision" and a "contingent liability" as per AS 29. Also state the treatment of contingent assets.

**Model Answer:**

**Standard:** AS 29 *Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets*.

**Provision (para 10, 14):** A provision is a **liability which can be measured only by using a substantial degree of estimation**. It is recognised when:
- there is a **present obligation** as a result of a past event;
- it is **probable** that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will be required to settle it; and
- a **reliable estimate** can be made of the amount.
A provision **is recognised** (charged to P&L and shown as a liability).

**Contingent liability (para 10, 27):** A contingent liability is:
- a **possible obligation** arising from past events whose existence will be confirmed only by future uncertain events **not wholly within the enterprise's control**; **or**
- a **present obligation** that is **not recognised** because either an outflow of resources is **not probable** or the amount **cannot be reliably measured**.
A contingent liability is **NOT recognised** in the accounts; it is **disclosed** by way of a note (unless the possibility of outflow is remote, in which case even disclosure is not required).

**Key distinction:**
| Basis | Provision | Contingent liability |
|---|---|---|
| Obligation | Present, probable outflow, reliably measurable | Possible, or present but not probable/not measurable |
| Accounting | Recognised as liability | Only disclosed (unless remote) |

**Contingent assets (para 30–34):** A contingent asset is a **possible asset** arising from past events whose existence is confirmed by uncertain future events. It is **NOT recognised** (recognising it may result in income never realised). It is **disclosed** in the **approving authority's report** (e.g. Board's report), **not in the financial statements**, when the inflow of economic benefit is **probable**. When realisation becomes **virtually certain**, the asset is no longer contingent and is recognised.

**> Full-marks tip:** The recognition line is the whole answer — *provision recognised, contingent liability only disclosed, contingent asset neither recognised nor disclosed in FS (disclosed in Board's report only when inflow is probable)*. Get those three treatments exactly right.

### Q19. AS 25 — components and minimum content of an interim report (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the minimum components of an interim financial report and the periods for which they are to be presented as per AS 25.

**Model Answer:**

**Standard:** AS 25 *Interim Financial Reporting*, para 9–10.

**Minimum components of an interim financial report (para 9):**
- A **condensed balance sheet**;
- A **condensed statement of profit and loss**;
- A **condensed cash flow statement**; and
- **Selected explanatory notes**.

**Form/content (para 10):** If an enterprise presents a *complete* set of financial statements in its interim report, the form and content should conform to AS-required full statements; if it presents a *condensed* set, it should include, at a minimum, each of the **headings and sub-headings** included in its most recent annual statements and the selected explanatory notes required by AS 25, together with basic and diluted **EPS** on the face of the statement of profit and loss.

- **Cash flow statement** — **cumulatively for the current financial year to date**, with a comparative for the comparable year-to-date period of the preceding year.

### Q20. Buyback — conditions and sources under the Companies Act (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions to be fulfilled and the sources from which a company may buy back its own shares under Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013.

**Model Answer:**

**Provision:** Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013.

**Sources of buyback (Sec. 68(1)):** A company may purchase its own shares/other specified securities out of —
- its **free reserves**;
- the **securities premium account**; or
- the **proceeds of the issue of any shares or other specified securities**.
(Buyback **cannot** be made out of the proceeds of an **earlier issue of the same kind** of shares/securities.)

**Conditions to be satisfied (Sec. 68(2)):**
- Buyback is **authorised by the Articles of Association**.
- A **special resolution** has been passed in general meeting authorising the buyback. *(A board resolution suffices where the buyback is 10% or less of the total paid-up equity capital and free reserves.)*
- The buyback is **≤ 25% of the aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves**; and for equity shares, **≤ 25% of the total paid-up equity capital** in that financial year.
- The **ratio of debt** (secured + unsecured) to **capital and free reserves after buyback is not more than 2:1**.
- All the shares/securities bought back are **fully paid-up**.
- The buyback of listed securities is in accordance with **SEBI regulations**; unlisted per prescribed rules.

**Further conditions:**
- Buyback must be **completed within 12 months** of the special/board resolution.
- After buyback out of free reserves, an amount equal to the **nominal value bought back is transferred to the Capital Redemption Reserve** (Sec. 69).
- No further buyback within a period of **one year** from the completion of the previous buyback.
- The company must **not issue the same kind of shares within 6 months** of buyback (except bonus, or discharge of subsisting obligations like conversion of warrants, ESOP, sweat equity, preference shares).

**> Full-marks tip:** Separate the *sources* (free reserves / securities premium / fresh issue proceeds) from the *conditions* (25% limit, 2:1 debt-equity, fully paid, 12-month completion, CRR), and mention the board-resolution route for buybacks ≤10% — the examiner allots marks head-wise.

### Q1. Amalgamation in the nature of merger — the five conditions and the two methods (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions to be satisfied under AS 14 for an amalgamation to be treated as an "amalgamation in the nature of merger". Name the method of accounting applicable to each type of amalgamation.

**Model Answer:**
**As per AS 14 "Accounting for Amalgamations" (para 3(e)), an amalgamation is in the nature of merger only when ALL the following five conditions are satisfied:**
1. **All assets and liabilities** of the transferor company become, after amalgamation, the assets and liabilities of the transferee company.
2. **Shareholders holding not less than 90%** of the face value of the equity shares of the transferor company (other than the equity shares already held by the transferee, or its subsidiaries/nominees) become **equity shareholders of the transferee** company.
3. The **consideration** payable to the above shareholders is discharged **wholly by the issue of equity shares** in the transferee company, except that cash may be paid for **fractional shares**.
4. The **business** of the transferor company is intended to be **carried on** by the transferee company after the amalgamation.
5. **No adjustment** is intended to be made to the **book values** of assets and liabilities of the transferor company, except to ensure **uniformity of accounting policies**.

**Methods of accounting (para 7):**
- **Amalgamation in the nature of merger → Pooling of Interests Method** (record at book values; no goodwill/capital reserve; difference between consideration and share capital adjusted in reserves).
- **Amalgamation in the nature of purchase** (any amalgamation not satisfying all five conditions above) **→ Purchase Method** (assets/liabilities at fair values or book values; difference between consideration and net assets recorded as **goodwill or capital reserve**).

**> Full-marks tip:** List **all five** conditions — the word "**all**" is examined; missing even one (esp. "90% of face value" and "wholly by equity shares") loses the mark. Pair each amalgamation type with the correct method name.

### Q2. Key definitions under AS 14 (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Define the following terms as per AS 14: (i) Transferor company, (ii) Transferee company, (iii) Consideration, (iv) Reserve.

**Model Answer:**
**Definitions as per AS 14 (para 3):**
- **(i) Transferor company** — the company which is amalgamated **into** another company (the vendor / selling company whose existence is extinguished).
- **(ii) Transferee company** — the company **into which** a transferor company is amalgamated (the purchasing company that survives).
- **(iii) Consideration** (para 3(g)) — the **aggregate of the shares and other securities issued and the payment made** in the form of cash or other assets by the transferee company **to the shareholders of the transferor company**. *It does NOT include amounts paid to debenture-holders or to settle other outside liabilities/creditors, nor amounts paid to discharge liabilities of the transferor company.*
- **(iv) Reserve** — the portion of earnings, receipts or other surplus of an enterprise (whether capital or revenue) **appropriated by the management** for a general or specific purpose, other than a **provision** for depreciation, diminution in value of assets or a known liability.

**> Full-marks tip:** In the definition of **consideration**, the marks are for the exclusion — state clearly it is paid **only to shareholders**, NOT to creditors/debenture-holders. This distinction is the most examined point.

### Q3. Purchase consideration — Net Assets vs Net Payments method (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** A Ltd agreed to take over the business of B Ltd. The agreed values are: Goodwill ₹60,000; Land & Building ₹4,00,000; Plant ₹1,80,000; Stock ₹1,40,000; Debtors ₹1,00,000 (a provision of ₹5,000 to be created); Cash ₹50,000. Liabilities taken over: Sundry Creditors ₹1,20,000. A Ltd discharged the consideration by issuing 40,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at ₹12 per share, paying cash ₹2,00,000 and issuing 12% Debentures of ₹1,25,000. Compute the purchase consideration under **(a) the Net Assets method** and **(b) the Net Payments method** and comment.

**Model Answer:**

**(a) Net Assets Method** — agreed value of assets taken over *less* liabilities taken over:

| Assets taken over (agreed values) | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Goodwill | 60,000 |
| Land & Building | 4,00,000 |
| Plant | 1,80,000 |
| Stock | 1,40,000 |
| Debtors (1,00,000 − 5,000 provision) | 95,000 |
| Cash | 50,000 |
| **Total assets (A)** | **9,25,000** |
| *Less:* Sundry Creditors taken over (B) | (1,20,000) |
| **Purchase Consideration (A − B)** | **8,05,000** |

**(b) Net Payments Method** — total of payments made to the shareholders of B Ltd:

| Mode of discharge | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| 40,000 equity shares of ₹10 issued at ₹12 (40,000 × ₹12) | 4,80,000 |
| Cash | 2,00,000 |
| 12% Debentures | 1,25,000 |
| **Purchase Consideration** | **8,05,000** |

**Comment:** Both methods yield **₹8,05,000**, confirming the working. Under AS 14 the consideration is the amount agreed to be paid to the **shareholders**; the Net Payments method reflects this directly. (Note: creditors of ₹1,20,000 are *not* part of consideration — they are settled by taking over the corresponding assets.)

**> Full-marks tip:** Under Net Payments, take shares at the **issue price ₹12** (not face ₹10). Under Net Assets, use **agreed values** and deduct the provision on debtors. State the final PC in bold.

### Q5. Amalgamation in the nature of merger — entries in the transferee's books (Pooling of Interests) (marks: 8) [Practical]
**Question:** Q Ltd takes over P Ltd in an amalgamation in the nature of **merger**. Balance Sheet of P Ltd: Equity Share Capital (40,000 shares of ₹10) ₹4,00,000; General Reserve ₹1,00,000; Profit & Loss A/c ₹50,000; Creditors ₹80,000. Assets: Fixed Assets ₹3,50,000; Stock ₹1,20,000; Debtors ₹90,000; Cash ₹70,000. Q Ltd issued 45,000 equity shares of ₹10 each (consideration ₹4,50,000) to the shareholders of P Ltd. Pass the journal entries in the books of Q Ltd.

**Model Answer:**

**Method: Pooling of Interests (AS 14, paras 33–35)** — assets, liabilities and reserves recorded at **book values**; the difference between consideration (₹4,50,000) and the recorded equity share capital of P Ltd (₹4,00,000) is **adjusted in reserves** (here reserves are reduced by ₹50,000).

*WN — Reserves to be recorded:* General Reserve ₹1,00,000 + P&L ₹50,000 = ₹1,50,000, *less* excess of consideration over capital ₹50,000 = **₹1,00,000** carried.

**Journal entries in the books of Q Ltd:**
```
(1) Business Purchase A/c            Dr.  4,50,000
        To Liquidator of P Ltd A/c            4,50,000
    (Consideration due on merger)

(2) Fixed Assets A/c                 Dr.  3,50,000
    Stock A/c                        Dr.  1,20,000
    Debtors A/c                      Dr.    90,000
    Cash A/c                         Dr.    70,000
        To Creditors A/c                        80,000
        To Reserves A/c (bal. fig.)           1,00,000
        To Business Purchase A/c             4,50,000
    (Incorporation of assets, liabilities & reserves at book value)

(3) Liquidator of P Ltd A/c          Dr.  4,50,000
        To Equity Share Capital A/c          4,50,000
    (Issue of 45,000 equity shares of ₹10 in discharge of PC)
```

### Q9. Inter-company owings and unrealised profit on stock (marks: 6) [Practical]
**Question:** H Ltd absorbs S Ltd. On the date of absorption: (i) H Ltd's Debtors include ₹20,000 due from S Ltd, and S Ltd's Creditors include the corresponding ₹20,000 due to H Ltd; (ii) The stock of S Ltd includes goods purchased from H Ltd for ₹30,000, on which H Ltd had charged a profit of 25% on cost. Explain and pass the adjustment/elimination entries in the books of H Ltd (transferee) after incorporating the assets and liabilities.

**Model Answer:**

**(i) Elimination of mutual owings:** After the assets and liabilities of S Ltd are incorporated in H Ltd's books, the ₹20,000 shown as **Debtors** and ₹20,000 shown as **Creditors** represent the *same* obligation, now within one entity, and must be cancelled:
```
Creditors A/c              Dr.  20,000
      To Debtors A/c                  20,000
(Cancellation of inter-company owing on absorption)
```

**(ii) Unrealised profit on stock:** S Ltd's stock includes goods bought from H Ltd at cost + 25%. Profit element loaded by H Ltd:
*WN:* Unrealised profit = ₹30,000 × (25 / 125) = **₹6,000.**
This profit is not realised outside the combined entity, so it must be eliminated by creating a **Stock Reserve** (charged against H Ltd's revenue reserves/P&L):
```
Profit & Loss A/c (or General Reserve) A/c   Dr.  6,000
      To Stock Reserve A/c                          6,000
(Elimination of unrealised profit on inter-company stock)
```
The stock reserve is shown as a **deduction from Stock** on the assets side (net stock reduced by ₹6,000).

**> Full-marks tip:** Profit "**on cost**" ⇒ use **25/125**, not 25/100. Cancel debtors *against* creditors (equal amounts). Show the stock reserve as a **deduction from stock**, not as a separate liability.

### Q10. Amalgamation with inter-company shareholding (marks: 7) [Practical]
**Question:** A Ltd (transferee) already holds **2,000 equity shares** in B Ltd (transferor), acquired at a cost of ₹30,000. B Ltd has 10,000 equity shares of ₹10 each. The net assets of B Ltd taken over are valued at ₹5,00,000. A Ltd agrees to pay the **outside shareholders** at the intrinsic value per share, discharged in equity shares. Compute (a) the purchase consideration payable to the outside shareholders, and (b) the goodwill/capital reserve arising in A Ltd's books.

**Model Answer:**

**WN 1 — Intrinsic value per share of B Ltd:** ₹5,00,000 ÷ 10,000 shares = **₹50 per share.**

**WN 2 — Shares held by outsiders:** 10,000 − 2,000 (held by A Ltd) = **8,000 shares.**

**(a) Purchase Consideration (to outside shareholders only):**
= 8,000 shares × ₹50 = **₹4,00,000** (discharged in equity shares of A Ltd).
*(A Ltd does not pay itself for the 2,000 shares it already holds — consideration under AS 14 is payable only to the transferor's shareholders, and A Ltd's own holding is cancelled.)*

**(b) Goodwill / Capital Reserve in A Ltd's books:**

| | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Net assets of B Ltd acquired | 5,00,000 |
| *Less:* Purchase Consideration to outsiders | (4,00,000) |
| *Less:* Cost of investment in B Ltd (cancelled) | (30,000) |
| **Capital Reserve** | **70,000** |

**Journal (incorporation, key balancing):** the ₹30,000 "Investment in B Ltd" is cancelled and the excess of net assets over (PC + investment cost) of ₹70,000 is credited to **Capital Reserve**.

**> Full-marks tip:** Pay consideration **only for the outsiders' shares**. In computing goodwill/capital reserve, deduct **both** the PC **and** the cost of the shares A Ltd already held — a very common slip is to forget to cancel the existing investment.

### Q11. Internal reconstruction vs external reconstruction (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between internal reconstruction and external reconstruction.

**Model Answer:**
**Distinction between Internal and External Reconstruction:**

| Basis | Internal Reconstruction | External Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaning** | Re-organisation of the capital structure of a company **without liquidating** it or forming a new company | The existing company is **liquidated** and a **new company** is formed to take over its business |
| **New company** | No new company is formed | A new company is formed |
| **Tribunal/legal** | Requires confirmation of the **Tribunal (NCLT)** under **Sec 66** of the Companies Act, 2013 (capital reduction) | Effected as an **amalgamation/absorption**; governed by AS 14 |
| **Accounting** | Losses/over-valued assets written off against **Capital Reduction A/c**; no goodwill arises | Purchase method applied; **goodwill or capital reserve** may arise |
| **Effect on shareholders** | Existing shareholders continue, usually with **reduced** claims | Shareholders of the old company become shareholders of the new company |

**> Full-marks tip:** The single most important line: internal reconstruction = **no new company / no liquidation**; external reconstruction = **old company liquidated, new company formed**. State Sec 66 for internal reconstruction.

### Q15. Meaning of purchase consideration — to whom is it payable? (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** "Purchase consideration under AS 14 is the amount payable to the shareholders of the transferor company." Explain, and state whether the following are part of purchase consideration: (i) payment to debenture-holders, (ii) liquidation expenses borne by the transferee, (iii) cash paid to equity shareholders, (iv) creditors taken over.

**Model Answer:**
**Meaning (AS 14, para 3(g)):** Purchase consideration is the **aggregate of shares and other securities issued and the payment made in cash or other assets by the transferee company to the shareholders of the transferor company**. The defining feature is that it is payable **only to the shareholders** — not to any other party.

**Application to the items:**
- **(i) Payment to debenture-holders — NOT part of PC.** Debenture-holders are creditors of the company, not shareholders; amounts to settle/take over debentures are excluded.
- **(ii) Liquidation expenses borne by the transferee — NOT part of PC.** Such expenses are treated as the transferee's own cost (debited to Goodwill/Capital Reserve or P&L as agreed), not consideration to shareholders.
- **(iii) Cash paid to equity shareholders — YES, part of PC.** It is a direct payment to shareholders.
- **(iv) Creditors taken over — NOT part of PC.** Taking over a liability is not a payment to shareholders.

**> Full-marks tip:** The whole answer turns on one test — "**is it received by the shareholders?**" Anything paid to creditors/debenture-holders or spent on liquidation is **excluded**.

### Q16. Reduction of share capital under Sec 66, Companies Act 2013 (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the legal provisions and modes of reduction of share capital under Section 66 of the Companies Act, 2013.

**Model Answer:**
**Legal provisions (Sec 66, Companies Act 2013):**
1. A company limited by shares may, if authorised by its **Articles**, reduce its share capital by passing a **special resolution**, subject to **confirmation by the Tribunal (NCLT)**.
2. The reduction cannot be made if the company is **in arrears in repayment of any deposits** accepted (or interest thereon).
3. The Tribunal gives notice to the **Central Government, ROC, SEBI** (for listed companies) and the **creditors**, and considers representations before confirming.

**Modes of reduction (Sec 66(1)):**
- **(a) Extinguish or reduce the liability on any of its shares** in respect of share capital **not paid up** (e.g., ₹10 share, ₹6 paid → reduce to a ₹6 fully-paid share).
- **(b) Cancel any paid-up share capital which is lost or is unrepresented by available assets** (write off accumulated losses/over-valued assets — the core of internal reconstruction).
- **(c) Pay off any paid-up share capital which is in excess** of the wants of the company (return surplus capital to shareholders).

The order confirming the reduction must be **registered with the ROC**, and the Tribunal may require the words "**and reduced**" to be added to the name (rarely, nowadays).

**> Full-marks tip:** Marks are for the **three modes (a/b/c)** and the twin requirements — **special resolution + Tribunal (NCLT) confirmation** + authorisation in Articles.

### Q20. Amalgamation vs Absorption vs External Reconstruction (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between amalgamation, absorption and external reconstruction.

**Model Answer:**
**Distinction (all three are accounted for under AS 14 using the same principles, but differ in form):**

| Basis | Amalgamation | Absorption | External Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meaning** | Two or more existing companies are wound up and a **new company** is formed to take over all of them | One existing company **takes over** another existing company; **no new company** is formed | An existing (usually loss-making) company is liquidated and a **new company** is formed to take over its business |
| **Number of companies** | Two or more transferor companies + one **new** transferee | Both transferor and transferee are **existing** companies | One transferor + one **newly formed** transferee (of essentially the same business) |
| **New company formed?** | Yes | No | Yes |
| **Objective** | Combination / synergy of two or more businesses | One company acquires another | Re-organisation of a **single** company's finances via a new shell |
| **Companies liquidated** | All the combining (transferor) companies | The absorbed (transferor) company | The single old company |

**> Full-marks tip:** Key discriminators — **is a new company formed?** (Yes for amalgamation & external reconstruction; No for absorption) and **how many businesses combine?** (two+ for amalgamation; one re-organised for external reconstruction). All three follow AS 14.

### Q10. Consolidation — Unrealised Profit on Inter-company Stock: Subsidiary is Seller (marks: 8) [Practical]
**Question:** H Ltd holds 80% of the equity of S Ltd. During the year S Ltd sold goods to H Ltd for ₹50,000, invoiced at cost plus 25%. Half of these goods remain unsold in H Ltd's closing stock. Show how the unrealised profit is eliminated in the consolidated financial statements and state the amount charged to consolidated Profit & Loss and to Minority Interest.

**Model Answer:**
**Step 1 — Unrealised profit in closing stock.** Goods still in stock = ½ × 50,000 = ₹25,000 (at invoice). Loading = cost + 25% ⇒ profit element = 25/125 = 20% of invoice. **Unrealised profit = 25,000 × 25/125 = ₹5,000.**

**Step 2 — Elimination.** The entire ₹5,000 is removed from **consolidated inventory** (a Stock Reserve of ₹5,000 is deducted from stock in the CBS).

**Step 3 — Apportionment of the charge.** Because the **subsidiary (S Ltd) is the seller**, the unrealised profit belongs to S Ltd and is therefore shared between the parent and the minority in the profit-sharing ratio:
- Holding company's share (charged to Consolidated P&L): 80% × 5,000 = **₹4,000**
- Minority's share (reduced from Minority Interest): 20% × 5,000 = **₹1,000**

**Final answer:** Consolidated inventory reduced by **₹5,000**; ₹4,000 debited to Consolidated P&L and ₹1,000 reduced from Minority Interest.

**> Full-marks tip:** The *whole* unrealised profit is knocked off stock, but the charge is split only when the **subsidiary is the seller**. If the *holding company* were the seller, the full ₹5,000 would hit the consolidated P&L with **no** minority adjustment.

### Q14. Foreign Operations — Integral vs Non-Integral (AS 11) (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between an "integral foreign operation" and a "non-integral foreign operation" under AS 11 and explain the translation treatment and the accounting for the resulting exchange difference for each.

**Model Answer:**
**Definition (AS 11, paras 7, 20–24):**
- An **integral foreign operation (IFO)** carries on business as though it were an extension of the reporting enterprise's operations (e.g. it only sells goods imported from the head office and remits proceeds back). Its cash flows directly affect the parent's cash flows.
- A **non-integral foreign operation (NFO)** accumulates cash and other items, generates income and incurs expenses largely in the local currency, and operates with a significant degree of autonomy; its cash flows are insulated from the parent.

**Translation of an Integral Foreign Operation** (same as the reporting enterprise's own foreign-currency items):
1. **Monetary items** (cash, receivables, payables) — **closing rate**.
2. **Non-monetary items** carried at historical cost (fixed assets, inventory at cost) — **rate at the date of transaction** (historical rate).
3. **Income and expenses** — rate at the date of transaction (average rate as an approximation).
4. Resulting **exchange difference is recognised in the Profit & Loss Account.**

**Translation of a Non-Integral Foreign Operation:**
1. **All assets and liabilities (monetary and non-monetary)** — **closing rate**.
2. **Income and expense items** — rate at the dates of transactions (average rate approximation).
3. Resulting **exchange difference is accumulated in a Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR)** under shareholders' funds until the operation is disposed of; on disposal it is recognised in the Profit & Loss Account.

**Conclusion:** The key difference is that an IFO's exchange difference hits the **P&L immediately**, whereas an NFO's exchange difference is **parked in FCTR** (equity) and does not affect current profit.

**> Full-marks tip:** State that **all** NFO items go at closing rate but **only monetary** IFO items do, and clearly name the FCTR (for NFO) versus P&L (for IFO) — the routing of the exchange difference is the marks-differentiator.

### Q15. Consolidated Financial Statements — Meaning of Control & Exclusion of a Subsidiary (AS 21) (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the meaning of "control" under AS 21 and state the circumstances in which a subsidiary is *excluded* from consolidation.

**Model Answer:**
**Meaning of Control (AS 21, para 4):** Control means either —
1. The **ownership, directly or indirectly through subsidiary(ies), of more than one-half of the voting power** of an enterprise; **OR**
2. **Control of the composition of the Board of Directors** (in the case of a company) or of the corresponding governing body, so as to obtain economic benefits from its activities.

A **subsidiary** is an enterprise that is controlled by another enterprise (the parent). When such control exists, consolidated financial statements must be prepared by combining the financials of the parent and its subsidiaries line by line.

**Exclusion from consolidation (AS 21, para 11):** A subsidiary is excluded from consolidation when —
1. **Control is intended to be temporary** because the subsidiary is acquired and held **exclusively with a view to its subsequent disposal in the near future**; or
2. The subsidiary **operates under severe long-term restrictions** which significantly impair its ability to transfer funds to the parent.

In these cases the investment is accounted for in accordance with **AS 13 (Accounting for Investments)** instead of being consolidated. **The reasons for not consolidating must be disclosed.**

**Conclusion:** Control (via voting power *or* board composition) is the trigger for consolidation; only the two para-11 situations justify exclusion, and even then disclosure is mandatory.

**> Full-marks tip:** Give **both** limbs of the control definition (voting power AND board composition) and both exclusion grounds. Add that excluded subsidiaries are accounted for under **AS 13** — a commonly missed mark.

### Q16. Investments in Associates — Equity Method (AS 23) (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** What is an "associate" under AS 23, and how is an investment in an associate accounted for in consolidated financial statements? Explain the equity method.

**Model Answer:**
**Definition (AS 23):**
- An **associate** is an enterprise in which the investor has **significant influence** and which is **neither a subsidiary nor a joint venture** of the investor.
- **Significant influence** is the power to participate in the financial and/or operating policy decisions of the investee **but not control** over those policies. It is presumed to exist where the investor holds, directly or indirectly, **20% or more of the voting power** (and presumed absent below 20%), unless it can be clearly demonstrated otherwise.

**Accounting — Equity Method (in Consolidated Financial Statements):**
1. The investment is **initially recorded at cost**, identifying any **goodwill or capital reserve** (difference between cost and the investor's share of the associate's net assets at acquisition) — disclosed separately.
2. The carrying amount is **increased/decreased** to recognise the **investor's share of the post-acquisition profits or losses** of the associate, taken through the consolidated Profit & Loss Account.
3. The carrying amount is **reduced by distributions received** (dividends) from the associate.
4. Adjustments are also made for the investor's share of changes in the associate's reserves that have not passed through its P&L (e.g. revaluation reserve), taken directly to consolidated reserves.

**Note:** In the investor's **separate (standalone)** financial statements, the investment in the associate is carried under **AS 13** (at cost less permanent diminution). The equity method applies **only in the consolidated financial statements**.

**> Full-marks tip:** Emphasise "significant influence, not control" and the **20% presumption**, and that the equity method is a **CFS-only** treatment — the standalone books still follow AS 13.

### Q17. Interests in Joint Ventures — Proportionate Consolidation (AS 27) (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Define a joint venture under AS 27, state its forms, and explain how a "jointly controlled entity" is reported in the venturer's consolidated financial statements.

**Model Answer:**
**Definition (AS 27):** A **joint venture** is a contractual arrangement whereby two or more parties undertake an economic activity that is subject to **joint control** — i.e. the contractually agreed sharing of control such that no single venturer can control the activity unilaterally. The existence of a **contractual arrangement** distinguishes a joint venture from an associate.

**Forms of joint ventures (AS 27):**
1. **Jointly controlled operations** — each venturer uses its own assets and incurs its own expenses/liabilities; each recognises the assets it controls, the liabilities and expenses it incurs, and its share of income in its own financial statements.
2. **Jointly controlled assets** — the venturers jointly own assets; each venturer recognises its **share of the jointly controlled assets, liabilities, income and expenses** in its own books.
3. **Jointly controlled entities** — a joint venture that involves the establishment of a **separate entity** (company, partnership, etc.) in which each venturer has an interest.

**Reporting a jointly controlled entity in CFS — Proportionate Consolidation:** In the consolidated financial statements, the venturer reports its interest in a jointly controlled entity using the **proportionate consolidation method** — the venturer's **share of each of the assets, liabilities, income and expenses** of the jointly controlled entity is combined **line by line** with similar items in its own financial statements (or reported as separate line items). Any **goodwill or capital reserve** arising is identified and disclosed.

**Conclusion:** Joint control via a contractual arrangement is the hallmark; a jointly controlled *entity* is brought into the CFS by **proportionate consolidation**, unlike the equity method used for associates.

**> Full-marks tip:** List **all three forms** and name **proportionate (line-by-line share) consolidation** for jointly controlled entities — do not confuse it with the equity method (that is AS 23 for associates).

### Q18. Internal Reconstruction — Concept and Distinction from External Reconstruction (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** What is meant by internal reconstruction? How does it differ from external reconstruction? Briefly state the forms it may take.

**Model Answer:**
**Meaning of Internal Reconstruction:** Internal reconstruction is a scheme by which the **assets and liabilities and the capital structure of a company are re-arranged, revalued or reduced without winding up the existing company and without forming a new company.** It is generally undertaken by a company that has accumulated losses and over-valued assets, to write them off and present a healthier balance sheet. A **reduction of share capital requires confirmation of the Tribunal (NCLT) under Section 66 of the Companies Act, 2013.**

**Distinction from External Reconstruction:**

| Basis | Internal Reconstruction | External Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| Existence of company | The **same company continues**; no liquidation | The **existing company is wound up** and a **new company** is formed to take over its business |
| Formation of new company | No new company is formed | A new company is formed |
| Approval | Requires NCLT confirmation (Sec. 66) | Effected through amalgamation, accounted under **AS 14** |
| Capital reduction | Involves reduction/alteration of capital | Purchase consideration is discharged to the old company |

**Forms of Internal Reconstruction:**
1. **Alteration of share capital** (Sec. 61) — consolidation, sub-division, conversion of shares.
2. **Reduction of share capital** (Sec. 66) — reducing the paid-up/uncalled capital.
3. **Variation of shareholders' rights** (Sec. 48).
4. **Compromise / arrangement** with creditors and members (Secs. 230–232) — including sacrifices by debentureholders and creditors.

**Conclusion:** Internal reconstruction rehabilitates a company **in situ**, whereas external reconstruction replaces it with a new company and is accounted for as an amalgamation.

**> Full-marks tip:** Cite **Section 66** for capital reduction and note that external reconstruction is now dealt with as an **amalgamation under AS 14** — these statutory references separate a top answer from an average one.

### Q19. Consolidation — Goodwill/Capital Reserve and Components of Minority Interest (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain how goodwill or capital reserve arises on consolidation and state the components that make up the minority interest under AS 21.

**Model Answer:**
**Goodwill / Capital Reserve on consolidation (AS 21, paras 13–14):** On the date of acquisition, the parent compares the **cost of its investment** in the subsidiary with the **parent's share of the equity (net assets) of the subsidiary as on that date** (i.e. its share of share capital plus pre-acquisition reserves/profits, after any revaluation of assets and liabilities):
- If **cost of investment > parent's share of net assets** ⇒ the excess is **Goodwill**, shown as an asset in the CBS.
- If **cost of investment < parent's share of net assets** ⇒ the deficit is a **Capital Reserve**, shown under reserves in the CBS.
- Only **pre-acquisition** profits/reserves enter this computation; **post-acquisition** profits of the parent's share are added to consolidated reserves/P&L.

**Components of Minority Interest (AS 21, para 21):** Minority interest is the portion of the net results of operations and of the net assets of a subsidiary attributable to interests **not owned, directly or indirectly through subsidiaries, by the parent.** It comprises:
1. The minority's proportion of the **share capital** (equity and, where applicable, preference) of the subsidiary; **plus**
2. The minority's proportion of the subsidiary's **reserves and surplus / accumulated profits** (both pre- and post-acquisition), including its share of any **revaluation**; **less**
3. The minority's share of any **accumulated losses / debit balance of P&L**.

Minority interest is presented **separately** from the parent's shareholders' funds and from liabilities in the Consolidated Balance Sheet.

**Conclusion:** Goodwill/capital reserve is fixed at the acquisition date on *pre-acquisition* equity, while minority interest is measured on the subsidiary's *total* (whole) equity at the reporting date.

**> Full-marks tip:** State clearly that goodwill uses **pre-acquisition** figures for the *parent's share* only, whereas minority interest is on the **entire** (pre + post) equity — mixing these up is the classic error.

### Q20. Branch Accounting — Types of Branches; Dependent vs Independent (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Classify the different types of branches for accounting purposes and distinguish between a dependent branch and an independent branch.

**Model Answer:**
**Classification of branches (for accounting purposes):**
1. **Inland (domestic) branches** —
   - **(a) Dependent branches** — branches that do **not maintain their own complete set of books**; all records are kept by the Head Office. Accounted for by one of: (i) **Debtors System**, (ii) **Stock & Debtors System**, (iii) **Final Accounts System**, or (iv) **Wholesale Branch System**.
   - **(b) Independent branches** — branches that **maintain a complete set of double-entry books**, prepare their own trial balance and final accounts, and are later incorporated with the Head Office.
2. **Foreign branches** — branches situated outside the country whose trial balance is in a foreign currency and must be **translated as per AS 11** (as an integral or non-integral operation) before incorporation.

**Dependent vs Independent Branch:**

| Basis | Dependent Branch | Independent Branch |
|---|---|---|
| Books of account | Kept by **Head Office**; branch keeps only memorandum records | Branch keeps a **complete double-entry set** |
| Autonomy | Little autonomy; buys only from HO | Operates autonomously; buys/sells independently |
| Goods | Received from HO, at cost or invoice price | May purchase from HO **and** from outside |
| Reciprocal account | Not maintained by branch | Maintains a **Head Office Account**; HO maintains a **Branch Account** (reciprocal) |
| Profit ascertainment | Computed by HO (Branch A/c / Adjustment A/c) | Branch prepares its **own** Trading and P&L A/c |
| Adjustments on combining | Not applicable | Goods-in-transit, cash-in-transit and unrealised profit on stock must be reconciled/eliminated |

**Conclusion:** The distinction turns on whether the branch keeps a **complete set of books** (independent) or is accounted for entirely by the Head Office (dependent).

**> Full-marks tip:** Name all four accounting systems for **dependent** branches and mention **AS 11** for **foreign** branches; the reciprocal Branch/HO account and in-transit adjustments are the hallmarks of an **independent** branch.
