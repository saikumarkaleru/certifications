# Upside/Downside Tasuki Gaps & Side-by-Side Lines

Gaps are among the most information-rich events on any chart. When price jumps a clean space between one candle's close and the next candle's open, it means orders piled up on one side so heavily that no trading occurred across a whole band of prices — a raw display of imbalance. Japanese candlestick tradition developed a small family of patterns specifically to interpret *what happens after a gap*: does the trend that produced the gap continue, or does the gap get filled and fail? The **Tasuki Gap** patterns and the **Side-by-Side White/Black Lines** are the tools for reading exactly this. They are subtle, often-overlooked continuation signals, and in the gap-happy world of Indian equities — where overnight news, ADR cues, and SGX/GIFT Nifty moves produce frequent opening gaps — they are unusually practical.

## What it is & why it works

A **Tasuki Gap** ("tasuki" refers to a sash worn crossed over the shoulder, evoking the crossing shape of the candles) is a three-candle continuation pattern built around a gap.

The **Upside Tasuki Gap** (bullish, appears in an uptrend):
1. A green candle in an ongoing uptrend.
2. A second green candle that **gaps up** above the first, leaving an open window.
3. A third candle that is **red**, opening *within* the body of the second green candle and closing *lower* — but crucially, it **does not fully close the gap**. The gap window between candle 1 and candle 2 remains at least partly open.

The signal: the red candle is a normal profit-booking reaction to the gap, but sellers are too weak to fill the window. Because the gap survives, the bullish trend is deemed intact, and the pattern predicts continuation higher.

The **Downside Tasuki Gap** (bearish, in a downtrend) is the mirror: a red candle, a second red candle gapping *down*, then a green candle that opens within the second candle's body and rises but fails to close the down-gap. The surviving gap signals the downtrend will continue.

**Why it works** comes down to the psychology of the unfilled gap. A gap represents a zone where the losing side has no willing traders. When a counter-move (the third candle) attempts to fill that zone and *fails*, it proves the counter-trend crowd lacks the firepower to reverse the imbalance. An old trader's maxim — "gaps that don't fill quickly tend to run" — is the essence of the Tasuki. The pattern is a formalised test: the market *tries* to fill the gap, *can't*, and therefore the original impulse is validated.

The **Side-by-Side White Lines** (also called Side-by-Side Lines) are a related but distinct gap-continuation pattern:
- **Bullish version (in an uptrend):** a green candle, then a gap up to a second green candle, then a *third* green candle that **opens at roughly the same level as the second candle's open and has a similar body** — two green candles "side by side" of similar size sitting above the gap. The gap holds, and the parallel green candles show buyers absorbing supply at the elevated level without any give-back. Continuation up.
- **Bearish version (in a downtrend):** here is the important quirk — a bearish Side-by-Side *White* Lines pattern also uses two green candles, but they appear *after a gap down* in a downtrend. Two green candles side by side after a down-gap look bullish, yet in classic interpretation they represent a failed bounce that cannot fill the gap, so the downtrend resumes. This counterintuitive reading is why the pattern is frequently misapplied — more on that under Pitfalls. Side-by-side *black* (red) lines after a gap are far rarer and generally weak signals.

## Mechanics, formation rules & settings

**Upside Tasuki Gap — precise conditions:**
1. Prevailing uptrend.
2. Candle 1: green.
3. Candle 2: green, opens **above** candle 1's high (a true gap / window).
4. Candle 3: red, **opens inside candle 2's real body**, closes **below candle 2's open** but **above the top of the gap** (i.e., above candle 1's close). The window is not filled.
5. Ideally candles 2 and 3 have similar-sized bodies.

**Downside Tasuki Gap** — invert: downtrend, red, gap-down red, then green that opens inside the second body, closes above its open but below the bottom of the gap.

**Side-by-Side White Lines (bullish continuation):** uptrend → green → gap-up green → third green with an open near the second's open and a comparable body, gap intact.

**Key mechanical point across all of them:** the **survival of the gap is the entire signal**. The moment a candle closes the window, the pattern is invalidated and its predictive value collapses. So your first job on spotting one of these is to mark the gap edges with horizontal rays and watch whether they hold.

**Settings for Indian charts.** These patterns are most useful on the **daily** timeframe, because that is where India's structural gaps live — the overnight gap between yesterday's close and today's open, driven by US markets, crude, results and global cues. Nifty and Bank Nifty gap on the open several times a week; individual stocks gap on results and block deals. On **intraday** charts (15-min, 5-min) true gaps only appear at the 9:15 open (since NSE trades continuously through the session), so the daily chart is the natural home. A caution specific to India: because so much gapping is *news-driven overnight*, the "psychology" interpretation must be tempered — a gap from a genuine fundamental surprise behaves differently from a liquidity gap, and Tasuki logic is cleaner on the latter.

## Worked India example (levels & ₹)

Take a realistic reconstruction of an **Upside Tasuki Gap on Infosys** (daily; illustrative, verify on your chart).

Infosys is trending up into a results season, moving from ₹1,850 to ₹1,920 over a fortnight. On results day the stock closes strong at ₹1,930 — a green **candle 1**. Overnight, positive guidance and a healthy deal pipeline lift the ADR; the next morning Infosys **gaps up** and opens at ₹1,955, runs to close ₹1,970. That is **candle 2**, a green candle that has opened well above candle 1's high of ₹1,935, leaving an open window between roughly ₹1,935 and ₹1,955.

The third day sees natural profit-booking. Infosys opens at ₹1,965 (inside candle 2's body), sells off through the morning as short-term traders book the gap-up gains, and closes red at ₹1,948 — **candle 3**. But note where it stopped: ₹1,948 is *above* the top of the gap zone (₹1,955? — here the close at 1,948 is inside the window). Let me be precise: for a textbook Tasuki, the red candle should close above ₹1,955 (the gap top). Suppose instead it closes at ₹1,958 — above the ₹1,955 gap top, so the window from ₹1,935–₹1,955 remains fully open. The Upside Tasuki Gap is complete: the market tried to sell the gap-up and *failed to fill the window*.

The trade. Enter on a break above candle 3's high, or above candle 2's close of ₹1,970, using a buy-stop around ₹1,972. The stop goes **below the bottom of the gap** — the window's lower edge is ₹1,935 (candle 1's high), so a stop just under it at ₹1,930 is the structural line: if the gap fills completely, the pattern is void. Entry ₹1,972, stop ₹1,930 → risk ₹42. Target the measured continuation: the prior leg was roughly ₹80 (₹1,850→₹1,930); projecting a comparable leg from the breakout points toward ₹2,050, with an interim target at the round ₹2,000 and prior resistance. Risk ₹42 for a reach to ₹2,050 (₹78) is ~1.85:1, respectable for a continuation setup, with ₹2,000 as the partial-booking level.

A subtle refinement Indian traders should note: many Tasuki traders use a **tighter stop just below the gap top rather than the gap bottom**, reasoning that any re-entry into the window shows weakness. That tighter stop improves reward-to-risk but increases the chance of being shaken out by an intraday wick — a classic trade-off between stop-tightness and whipsaw risk.

## How to trade it — entry, stop, target

**Entry.**
- *Standard:* buy-stop above the high of the third (reaction) candle, so you only enter if the trend actually resumes.
- *Aggressive:* buy on the close of the third candle once you can confirm the gap survived.
- For **Side-by-Side White Lines**, enter on a break above the second/third candle highs, since the two parallel green candles already show the gap holding.

**Stop-loss.** The logical stop for an Upside Tasuki or bullish Side-by-Side is **just below the lower edge of the gap** (the level that, if breached, means the window filled). For a Downside Tasuki, place it just above the upper edge of the down-gap. Choose the conservative (gap-bottom) or aggressive (gap-top) variant based on the instrument's volatility — for choppy names, give it gap-bottom room.

**Targets.** Measured move from the impulse leg, next resistance/support, then trail. Because gaps often mark the *middle* of a move (the "runaway" or "measuring" gap), a useful target technique is to **double the distance from the trend's origin to the gap**, projecting the gap as the midpoint of the whole move. Book partials at the first resistance and let a runner ride the trend with a moving-average trail.

## Confluence — including OI

- **Gap type matters.** Confluence is strongest when the gap is a *continuation/runaway gap* in the middle of a trend, not an *exhaustion gap* after an extended run. A Tasuki after a parabolic move that has already run 30% is more likely an exhaustion trap. Position within the trend is the first filter.
- **Volume.** A high-volume gap candle (candle 2) followed by a *lower-volume* reaction candle (candle 3) is ideal — it says the pushing side is committed and the fading side is thin.
- **Support/resistance and moving averages.** A gap that leaps a prior resistance level (turning it to support) and holds is far more trustworthy. Alignment with a rising 20-EMA reinforces the continuation bias.
- **Broader market.** For stocks, confirm Nifty/sector direction agrees.

**Option-chain / OI confluence.** Gaps in Nifty and Bank Nifty are the perfect place to consult the option chain, because gaps often *redistribute* the entire OI map overnight. For an **Upside Tasuki Gap in Nifty**: after the gap up, watch whether **Call writers at the strikes just overhead capitulate** (Call OI falling as price holds above the gap) — that unwinding removes resistance and fuels continuation. Simultaneously, if **Put writers aggressively add OI at the gap-bottom strike**, they are effectively defending the unfilled window with real capital, which is powerful agreement with the candlestick thesis. Conversely, if after the gap up you see *fresh Call writing* stacking at the strike right above spot, option-sellers are betting the gap fails — a reason to demand the break-of-high entry and keep targets modest. In short: unfilled gap + Put writing at the gap floor + Call unwinding overhead = high-conviction Upside Tasuki; unfilled gap but heavy Call writing overhead = fragile, treat with caution.

## Pitfalls

1. **The bearish Side-by-Side White Lines trap.** Two green candles after a *down-gap* in a downtrend look bullish to the untrained eye, but classically signal *continuation of the downtrend* (a failed bounce that can't fill the gap). Many traders buy this and get run over. Always anchor the interpretation to the *prevailing trend and the gap direction*, not the candle colours alone.
2. **Trading a filled gap.** The instant the window closes, the pattern is void. If you entered and the gap subsequently fills, the thesis is broken — exit. Do not rationalise "it'll bounce."
3. **Exhaustion gaps masquerading as continuation.** A gap at the *end* of a long, stretched trend is often exhaustion, and a Tasuki there fails frequently. Judge the trend's maturity: early/mid-trend gaps continue, late-trend gaps trap.
4. **News gaps in India.** Overnight results, global crashes, and policy shocks create gaps whose follow-through is driven by fundamentals, not the neat candlestick psychology. A results-day mega-gap may reverse on "sell the news" regardless of a pretty Tasuki. Weight the *cause* of the gap.
5. **Loose gap definition on liquid names.** In very liquid stocks and indices, "gaps" can be small; make sure it is a genuine window (candle 2's low above candle 1's high), not just a strong open. Tiny pseudo-gaps produce unreliable signals.
6. **Rarity and low frequency.** These are uncommon patterns; forcing them onto every chart leads to seeing them where they aren't. Better to trade a handful of clean ones than many marginal ones.
7. **Ignoring the whole-market gap on index-open.** When *the entire market* gaps (a global-cue morning), a single stock's Tasuki is really the market's Tasuki — analyse the index first, then the stock.

## Interview-ready summary

The Tasuki Gap and Side-by-Side White Lines are **three-candle, gap-based continuation** patterns whose central idea is simple: *a gap that the market tries and fails to fill validates the trend that produced it.* In an **Upside Tasuki Gap** (uptrend), a gap-up green candle is followed by a red reaction candle that opens inside it but cannot close the window; continuation up is expected. The **Downside Tasuki Gap** mirrors this in a downtrend. **Side-by-Side White Lines** show two parallel same-size green candles holding above (or, in the counterintuitive bearish case, appearing after a down-gap and still signalling continuation *down*). Trade them with a break-of-high (or -low) entry, a stop just beyond the gap edge — the unfilled gap is the entire thesis, so a filled gap voids the trade — and measured-move targets, often treating the gap as the midpoint of the move. Confluence comes from the gap being a mid-trend runaway rather than an exhaustion gap, a high-volume gap with a low-volume reaction, and, for index/F&O names, option-chain agreement: Put writing defending the gap floor and Call unwinding overhead. The chief errors are misreading the bearish Side-by-Side pattern as bullish, holding after the gap fills, and trusting the neat psychology on a fundamentally driven Indian news-gap where follow-through is dictated by the news, not the candles.
