# Setups: Gap & Opening-Range (5 setups)

The first thirty minutes of an Indian trading session concentrate more information, emotion, and opportunity than any other part of the day. Overnight, the world moves — the US closes, Asia opens, crude and the dollar shift, a company reports earnings, the government announces policy — and all of it is repriced into a single violent moment at 9:15 a.m. The gap between yesterday's close and today's open is the market's judgement on everything that happened while India slept, and the way price behaves in the opening range that follows tells you whether that judgement will hold or crumble. Gap-and-opening-range trading is the discipline of reading and trading these opening dynamics.

The reason this niche has genuine, durable edge is structural. The open is where the largest imbalance between overnight order flow and available liquidity occurs — pending orders, stop-losses from yesterday, fresh institutional programs, and retail reacting to news all hit at once. This creates predictable behavioural patterns: gaps that are "too emotional" get filled as rationality returns; gaps that represent genuine repricing hold and extend; and the initial range price carves out in the first 15-30 minutes acts as a battle line that, once broken, unleashes trapped traders' stops. Because so much of retail India trades the open on impulse, the disciplined structural trader is repeatedly on the other side of predictable mistakes.

Two definitions to anchor everything. A **gap** is a difference between today's open and yesterday's close; it is "up" or "down," and it is measured against yesterday's high/low (a "gap outside the prior range" is far more significant than a gap that opens still inside yesterday's range). The **opening range (OR)** is the high and low established in the first fixed window of the session — commonly the first 5, 15, or 30 minutes. The OR high and low become the day's first meaningful support and resistance, and most opening setups revolve around whether price breaks out of, or reverts within, that range.

A caution before the setups: the open is fast, thin at the very first prints, and prone to violent whipsaws. Slippage is real, spreads on the first ticks are wide, and a single news headline can reverse everything. These setups demand pre-defined rules, hard stops, and — critically — the patience to let the range *form* before trading it. The trader who fires blind at 9:15:01 is gambling; the trader who waits for the 9:30 range to complete and then trades its break or its failure is operating a business. Also mind the calendar: expiry days (weekly on Nifty/Bank Nifty), RBI and Fed days, and Budget day produce distorted, gamma-driven opens where these patterns behave abnormally — reduce size or stand aside.

## Setup 1 — Gap-Fill Fade (Exhaustion Gap)

Not all gaps deserve to survive. An **exhaustion gap** is an emotional, news-driven, or over-extended opening jump that overshoots fair value — a stock gaps up 4% on decent-but-priced-in results, or the index gaps down hard on a global scare that India shrugs off. Because the gap represents emotion rather than sustained buying/selling, price tends to drift back and *fill* the gap, closing the space back toward the prior day's close. The gap-fill fade sells the exhaustion gap-up (or buys the exhaustion gap-down) and targets the fill.

The edge lies in identifying gaps that are *likely* to fail: gaps against the prevailing trend, gaps on no fresh material news, gaps that open into a strong prior resistance/support, and gaps that show immediate rejection (the open is the high of the gap-up, and price starts sliding from the first candle). A gap that opens and *stalls* — no follow-through, sellers immediately in control — is a fade candidate. A gap that opens and *drives* further in the gap direction is not.

**Worked India example.** Suppose a stock closed at ₹1,200 and gaps up to ₹1,248 (a 4% gap) on results that merely met expectations. The first 15-minute candle makes a high of ₹1,250 and closes weak at ₹1,238 — immediate rejection, no follow-through buying. You short near ₹1,236 with a stop above ₹1,252 (above the opening high). Target is the gap fill toward ₹1,205-1,200. Risk ~₹16, reward ~₹35.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Extended/emotional gap that shows immediate rejection (no follow-through), especially against the trend |
| Entry | On the rejection of the opening high/low (short ₹1,236 gap-up / long the gap-down bounce failure) |
| Stop | Beyond the opening extreme (above ₹1,252) |
| Target | The gap fill — prior day's close (₹1,205 → ₹1,200); scale in |
| Timeframe | Intraday; decision made on the first 5-15 min candle |
| Regime | Emotional/over-extended gaps, counter-trend gaps; NOT genuine breakaway gaps on hard news |

**Confluence.** A gap that opens directly into a well-established resistance zone or a heavily Call-written option strike is a prime fade — the ceiling and the exhaustion align. Weak volume on the gap (no real institutional commitment) and a failure to hold above the prior day's high strengthen the case. Watch India VIX: a gap-down on a VIX spike that immediately starts reversing lower signals fear-exhaustion and favours the long gap-fill. On the option chain, a gap-up that stalls while Call OI builds at the opening-high strike confirms writers fading the pop.

**Pitfalls.** The fatal error is fading a **breakaway gap** — a gap on genuine, material, unpriced news (a big earnings beat, a large order win, an M&A announcement) that keeps running and *never* fills that day, or fills only days later. Do not fade gaps with real fresh catalysts and strong follow-through. The discipline: demand *immediate rejection* before shorting a gap-up; if the gap holds and extends in the first 15-30 minutes, it is a continuation gap, not an exhaustion gap. Stand aside. Second pitfall: fading too early at 9:15 before the rejection is confirmed — let the first candle close.

## Setup 2 — Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

The opening range breakout is the most popular intraday setup in the world, and for good reason: it is simple, mechanical, and captures the day's directional expansion. Define the opening range as the high and low of the first N minutes (15 minutes is the classic for Indian indices; some use 5, some 30). A close above the OR high is a long trigger; a close below the OR low is a short trigger. The logic: the opening range is the initial battle between buyers and sellers, and a decisive break signals which side won and that the day is likely to trend in that direction, with the OR boundary now acting as support/resistance.

The ORB works best on days with a *directional* character — a trend day where price breaks the range early and extends. It works worst on rotational, choppy days where price oscillates across the range and every "breakout" is a false one. Because you cannot know the day's character in advance, the ORB is traded with a filter: require the breakout on *expanding volume*, prefer breaks in the direction of the prior trend or the gap, and use the OR boundary as a hard stop so failed breakouts cost little.

**Worked India example.** Nifty's first 15 minutes (9:15-9:30) carve a range: high 24,720, low 24,660. At 9:40 a strong candle closes at 24,735, breaking the OR high on rising volume. You go long near 24,738 with a stop below the OR high at 24,700 (or below the OR low 24,660 for a wider stop). Target measured moves: T1 = OR width (60 pts) added to breakout = 24,780; T2 = prior day's high or the next round number.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Price closes beyond the opening-range high/low (first 15 min) on expanding volume |
| Entry | On the breakout close (long 24,738 above OR high / short below OR low) |
| Stop | Back inside the OR — below OR high 24,700 (tight) or below OR low 24,660 (wide) |
| Target | T1 = OR-width projection (24,780); T2 = PDH/PDL, round number, or trail |
| Timeframe | Intraday; 15-min OR is standard for indices |
| Regime | Trend/directional days; volume expansion filter essential |

**Confluence.** ORB odds jump when the breakout aligns with the gap direction (gap-up + OR-high break = strong) and with the prior day's structure — a break above OR high that also clears yesterday's high is powerful. Volume expansion on the breakout candle is the key filter separating real breaks from fakes. VWAP support (price holding above VWAP after the break) confirms the directional day. On index options, an OR-high break accompanied by Put writing (option sellers betting on higher prices) and rising futures OI with rising price (long build-up) is high-conviction. India VIX ticking up modestly can accompany a genuine trend-day expansion.

**Pitfalls.** The ORB's weakness is the false breakout on rotational days — price nudges above the OR high, triggers longs, then reverses back inside and hits the OR low. On choppy, low-volume, event-uncertainty days the ORB whipsaws mercilessly. Defences: require a *close* beyond the range, not a wick; demand volume expansion; consider the "second break" (a first false break followed by a genuine second break often has better odds); and keep the stop tight so false breaks are cheap. Avoid ORB entirely on known-chop days (pre-event, thin holidays-adjacent sessions).

## Setup 3 — Opening Range Fade (OR Reversion)

The mirror image of the ORB, for rotational days. On a balanced, non-trending session, price does *not* break out and run; instead it repeatedly rejects the opening-range boundaries and reverts to the middle or the opposite side. The OR fade sells the OR high and buys the OR low, targeting the mean or the opposite boundary. This is essentially range-fading applied to the day's first structure, and it is the correct tool when the ORB is failing — i.e., when the day is rotational, not directional.

The whole game is regime classification: is today a trend day (trade the ORB) or a rotational day (trade the OR fade)? Early tells of a rotational day: the gap is small or the market opens inside yesterday's range; the first breakout attempt fails and reverts; price crosses VWAP repeatedly; India VIX is low and stable; there is no fresh catalyst. On such days the boundaries hold and fading them pays.

**Worked India example.** Bank Nifty opens nearly flat, inside yesterday's range. First 15 minutes: OR high 51,850, OR low 51,750. Price pushes to 51,845, fails to break out, and rejects. You short near 51,838 with a stop above 51,875 (beyond the OR high). Target the OR mid 51,800 for a partial and the OR low 51,760 for the rest. When it later drops to test 51,760 and holds, you can flip long and fade back up.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Failed breakout of the OR boundary on a rotational day; rejection candle at OR high/low |
| Entry | On the rejection at the boundary (short OR high 51,838 / long OR low ~51,760) |
| Stop | Just beyond the OR boundary (above 51,875) — tight |
| Target | T1 = OR midpoint (51,800); T2 = opposite OR boundary (51,760) |
| Timeframe | Intraday; requires the OR to be established and a rotational read |
| Regime | Rotational/balanced days ONLY; small gap, price inside prior range, repeated VWAP crosses |

**Confluence.** The OR fade is strongest when the OR boundary coincides with VWAP, the prior day's close, or a high-OI option strike. A failed ORB (a breakout attempt that reverts back inside) is itself the best signal that the day is rotational and the fade is live. Low, stable India VIX and a quiet global tape favour rotation. On the option chain, if the day's expected range (from ATM straddle pricing) is narrow and Max Pain sits near the current price, rotation and OR-fading are favoured.

**Pitfalls.** Fading the OR on a trend day is the classic disaster — you short the OR high, it breaks out, and runs 300 points against you. The regime filter is everything: if the OR breaks *decisively on volume and holds*, abandon the fade immediately (that is the ORB's day, not yours). Never fade a strong gap-and-go open. Keep the stop tight beyond the boundary and accept being wrong quickly. The OR fade and the ORB are two sides of one coin — the skill is diagnosing which day you are in, ideally by 9:45-10:00.

## Setup 4 — Gap-and-Go Continuation (Breakaway Gap)

Some gaps are the *start* of a move, not the end. A **breakaway gap** occurs on genuine, material news — a strong earnings beat, a major contract, a sector-wide catalyst, a decisive global move — and represents real institutional repricing, not emotion. These gaps do not fill; they *extend.* The gap-and-go setup buys the strength of a powerful gap-up (or shorts a powerful gap-down) that shows immediate follow-through, riding the continuation rather than fading it. It is the deliberate opposite of Setup 1, and knowing which regime you are in is the entire skill.

The tells of a gap-and-go: the gap is on hard, fresh, unpriced news; the gap opens *outside* the prior day's range (a true gap, not an overlap); the first candle is strong and closes near its high (gap-up) with no rejection; volume is heavy from the open; and price holds above the opening 5-15 minute range and the VWAP. When these align, you do not fade — you join.

**Worked India example.** A stock closes at ₹880 and gaps up to ₹930 on a blockbuster order win — genuine, material, unpriced news. The first 15-minute candle is strong, closing at ₹938 near its high on huge volume, holding well above the prior range and VWAP. You buy the OR-high break near ₹940 with a stop below the opening-range low ₹924 (or below VWAP). Target is an extension — measured moves, prior swing highs, or trail with VWAP/a short EMA. The stock runs to ₹975+ intraday.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Strong gap on hard fresh news, opening outside prior range, with immediate follow-through and heavy volume |
| Entry | On the OR-high break / first-pullback-hold above VWAP (long ₹940) |
| Stop | Below the opening-range low / below VWAP (below ₹924) |
| Target | Extension — measured moves, prior highs; trail with VWAP or a fast EMA |
| Timeframe | Intraday; can extend into a multi-day continuation on daily charts |
| Regime | Breakaway/news gaps with strength; strong-trend days; NEVER fade these |

**Confluence.** The best gap-and-go trades combine: heavy volume from the open (institutional commitment), price holding above VWAP all session (trend-day signature), the gap clearing a significant prior resistance (breakout + gap = fuel), and — on F&O names — aggressive Put writing and long build-up (rising OI with rising price) confirming smart money is positioned for continuation. A "first pullback that holds VWAP" is often a lower-risk entry than the initial break, offering a tight stop against VWAP.

**Pitfalls.** The danger is chasing an already-extended gap that is actually exhausting — buying at ₹975 after a ₹95 run when the move is done. Enter on the OR break or the first VWAP-holding pullback, not five candles into a vertical spike. Second pitfall: mistaking an emotional gap for a breakaway gap — the filter is the *quality of the news* and *whether price holds and extends versus rejects.* If a "gap-and-go" loses VWAP and closes the opening range back below, it is failing — exit; it may be turning into a gap-fill fade. Third: gap-and-go is a trend-day tool; on rotational days it produces false continuations.

## Setup 5 — Previous-Day-Levels Break (PDH/PDL Opening Play)

Yesterday's high and low (PDH/PDL) are among the most-watched, most-defended levels in intraday trading. Countless traders place orders and stops around them, so they act as magnets and battle lines at the open. The PDH/PDL opening play trades the interaction of the open with these levels: a gap that opens beyond PDH/PDL and holds signals a directional day (continuation); a push to PDH/PDL that rejects signals a reversion back into yesterday's range; and a break of PDH/PDL after an inside open often triggers a run as stops fire.

This setup is prized because the levels are objective (no drawing subjectivity — they are simply yesterday's high and low) and because the crowd's stops cluster there, providing predictable fuel. It combines naturally with the gap and OR concepts: where does the gap sit relative to PDH/PDL? Is price attacking or defending these levels?

**Worked India example.** Nifty's PDH is 24,800 and PDL is 24,600. Today it gaps up modestly to 24,760, opening *inside* yesterday's range, then in the first hour drives up and breaks PDH at 24,800 on a strong candle. The break triggers stops from yesterday's shorts. You go long on the PDH break near 24,808 with a stop below 24,780 (back inside the range). Target: measured extension above PDH, next round number 24,900, and trail. Conversely, had price pushed to 24,800 and *rejected* sharply, you would fade short back toward the range middle.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Break of (or rejection at) PDH/PDL — break = continuation long/short; rejection = reversion fade |
| Entry | Break play: on the close beyond PDH/PDL (long 24,808). Fade play: on rejection candle at PDH/PDL |
| Stop | Break: back inside the level (below 24,780). Fade: beyond the level |
| Target | Break: next level/round number (24,900), trail. Fade: prior day's mid / VWAP |
| Timeframe | Intraday; levels defined by the completed prior session |
| Regime | Break play on directional days; fade play on rotational days |

**Confluence.** PDH/PDL plays are strongest when the level coincides with other structure — a round number, VWAP, an option-chain high-OI strike, or a prior swing. A PDH break that also breaks the opening range and holds above VWAP is a high-conviction continuation. On the option chain, a PDH break accompanied by Put writing at that strike (writers confident price stays above) confirms the level flip from resistance to support. India VIX ticking up on the break can accompany a genuine expansion day. The rejection/fade version is confirmed by a clear rejection candle and price failing to close beyond the level.

**Pitfalls.** The main trap is the *false break* of PDH/PDL — price pokes just beyond, triggers stops, then reverts (a level "sweep"). Ironically, this sweep-and-reverse is itself a tradable reversal (a failed breakdown/upthrust), so the loss on a false break can be flipped. Defence: require a *closing* break beyond the level, and keep the stop just back inside so false breaks are cheap. Second pitfall: trading these levels blindly without the day's regime read — a PDH break on a rotational, low-volume day is more likely to fail than one on a directional, high-volume day. Always ask: is today a trend day or a rotation day?

## Interview-ready summary

Gap-and-opening-range trading exploits the structural information and liquidity imbalance concentrated in the first thirty minutes of the Indian session, where overnight news is violently repriced and predictable behavioural patterns emerge. The five setups cover both directions of the central question — *will the opening move hold or reverse?* The **Gap-Fill Fade** sells emotional, exhausted, counter-trend gaps that overshoot and revert to the prior close. The **Opening Range Breakout** rides directional days by trading the decisive break of the first 15-minute range on expanding volume. The **Opening Range Fade** does the opposite on rotational days, selling the OR high and buying the OR low back to the mean. The **Gap-and-Go Continuation** joins — never fades — powerful breakaway gaps on hard fresh news that hold VWAP and extend. And the **Previous-Day-Levels Break** trades the crowd's stops clustered at PDH/PDL, going with the break on directional days and fading the rejection on rotational ones. The through-line across all five is **regime diagnosis by roughly 9:45-10:00 a.m.**: is today a trend day (favour ORB, gap-and-go, PDH breaks) or a rotational day (favour gap-fill and OR fades)? The tells are the gap's size and location relative to the prior range, immediate follow-through versus rejection, volume expansion, VWAP behaviour, and India VIX. Non-negotiable disciplines: let the opening range *form* before trading it rather than firing at 9:15; demand *closing* breaks and volume confirmation to filter false moves; keep stops tight against the OR boundary or the level so failed breaks cost little; and reduce size or stand aside on expiry, RBI/Fed, and Budget days when gamma and event-risk distort the open. Layer option-chain confluence — high-OI strikes, Put/Call writing, straddle-implied ranges, Max Pain — onto these price structures, and the chaotic opening becomes the most reliably tradable window of the Indian trading day.
