# Separating Lines & Thrusting Patterns

Not every two-candle combination is a reversal. A large part of skilled tape-reading is learning to distinguish signals that *warn* of a turn from signals that merely *pause* and then let the trend continue. Two closely related two-candle patterns sit right at this fault line: the **Separating Lines**, a genuine continuation signal, and the **Thrusting Line**, an ambiguous "weak counter-attack" that usually resolves as continuation but occasionally warns of a turn. They belong to the same conceptual neighbourhood as the In-Neck, On-Neck, and Piercing patterns — a spectrum of "how far did the counter-candle push back?" — and understanding that spectrum is what makes these patterns useful rather than confusing. For an Indian trader watching a trending Nifty or a momentum stock, these patterns answer a precise, valuable question: *when a candle opens against the trend, is the trend broken or is this just noise to be bought/sold?*

## What it is & why it works

**Separating Lines** are a two-candle continuation pattern defined by a shared *open*. In a **bullish Separating Line** (within an uptrend):
1. Candle 1 is a **red** candle (a counter-trend down-day inside the uptrend).
2. Candle 2 **opens at the same price as candle 1's open** and then rallies to close green.

The name comes from the two candles "separating" from a common opening level in opposite directions. The signal is bullish because, despite the previous red candle, buyers dragged price all the way back to the prior open at the next session's start and then pushed higher — the counter-trend dip was fully rejected and the uptrend resumes. (Contrast this with the *Meeting Lines* pattern, where the two candles share the same *close* rather than the same open.)

The **bearish Separating Line** (within a downtrend) mirrors it: candle 1 is a green counter-trend up-day, candle 2 opens at the *same open* and sells off to close red, rejecting the bounce and resuming the downtrend.

**Why it works:** the shared open is a psychological reset. After a counter-trend candle, the market gapping or opening right back at the same starting price and then moving *with* the primary trend shows that the counter-move attracted no lasting commitment. The trend followers reasserted themselves immediately at the open. It is a compact statement that "the pullback is over, we're going again."

**Thrusting Line** is a *different* animal — it belongs to the "counter-attack that falls short" family. In a downtrend:
1. Candle 1 is a **long red** candle.
2. Candle 2 **gaps down and opens below candle 1's low, then rallies to close back up into candle 1's body — but the close finishes below the *midpoint* of candle 1's body.**

The green candle "thrusts" up into the prior red body but *fails to reach the halfway point*. Because it falls short of the midpoint, it is a **weak** signal — generally read as **bearish continuation** (the bounce was feeble, sellers still dominate). This is the key contrast with the **Piercing Line**, where the green candle closes *above* the midpoint and is therefore a *bullish reversal*. The midpoint is the dividing line between a failed bounce (Thrusting, continuation) and a genuine reversal attempt (Piercing).

The family, ranked by how far the counter-candle penetrates the prior body, is worth memorising:
- **On-Neck line:** close reaches only about the prior candle's *low/close* — weakest bounce, strong continuation.
- **In-Neck line:** close just barely into the prior body — very weak, continuation.
- **Thrusting line:** close *into* the body but *below the midpoint* — weak, usually continuation.
- **Piercing line:** close *above the midpoint* — strong, reversal.

A bullish Thrusting Line exists too (uptrend, gap-up red candle that pushes down into the prior green body but fails to reach its midpoint), read as bullish continuation. But the classic, most-discussed version is the bearish one in a downtrend.

## Mechanics, formation rules & settings

**Bullish Separating Line — conditions:**
1. Established uptrend.
2. Candle 1: red (counter-trend).
3. Candle 2: opens **at (or extremely close to) candle 1's open**, closes green, ideally a strong body pushing to new local highs.
4. Marubozu-like candle 2 (little lower wick) strengthens the signal — it means buyers controlled from the open.

**Bearish Separating Line** — invert: downtrend, green candle 1, candle 2 opens at the same open and closes red.

**Thrusting Line (bearish) — conditions:**
1. Established downtrend.
2. Candle 1: long red.
3. Candle 2: opens **below candle 1's low** (a gap down), rallies, closes **inside candle 1's body but below its 50% midpoint.**

The two make-or-break measurements are (a) for Separating Lines, the *equality of the opens* — a genuine shared open, not a rough approximation; and (b) for Thrusting, the *midpoint* of candle 1 — the close must land below it. Always mark the 50% level of the prior candle to classify Thrusting vs Piercing correctly.

**Settings for Indian charts.** Separating Lines require the two candles to share an open, which on Indian equities most often happens as an *opening print at the prior open* — realistic on the **daily** chart given overnight gaps that can reset the open to a prior level. On intraday charts a near-identical open between consecutive 15-minute candles is common, so Separating Lines can be used intraday on index futures. Thrusting Lines depend on a **gap-down open**, which in NSE cash means the 9:15 open on the daily chart, again making the daily the natural timeframe. Because both patterns hinge on precise open levels and (for Thrusting) a gap, they are cleaner on daily charts than on continuously-traded intraday bars where "gaps" are rarer. Neither pattern scans well on Chartink; they are read by eye, ideally with the prior candle's open and midpoint drawn as rays.

## Worked India example (levels & ₹)

**Example 1 — Bullish Separating Line on Tata Motors (daily, illustrative).**

Tata Motors is trending up from ₹680 to ₹740 over three weeks. A broad-market down-day drags it lower: candle 1 opens at ₹738, sells off, and closes red at ₹726. The next morning, despite the weak close, positive auto-sales data lifts sentiment and the stock **opens right back at ₹738** — the same open as the prior day — then rallies strongly all session to close green at ₹749, near the day's high (a near-marubozu). Candle 1 and candle 2 *separated* from the common ₹738 open in opposite directions. This is a bullish Separating Line, and it says the one-day dip was fully rejected.

The trade: enter on the close of candle 2 at ₹749, or on a next-day break above ₹750. The stop goes **below the low of candle 2** (the rejection candle) — say the low was ₹737, so a stop at ₹733 (also below the shared open) is logical: if price falls back below the open that both candles respected, the "reset higher" thesis fails. Entry ₹749, stop ₹733 → risk ₹16. Target the trend continuation toward the prior swing high projection near ₹770 (₹21) and then ₹785, giving better than 1:1 to the first target and ~2.2:1 to the second. Because the stop is tight, position size can be generous for a fixed rupee risk.

**Example 2 — Bearish Thrusting Line on Bank Nifty (daily, illustrative).**

Bank Nifty is in a corrective downtrend, sliding from 52,000 to 50,500. One session prints a **long red candle**: opens 50,900, closes 50,300 (a 600-point red body; midpoint = 50,600). The next day opens with a gap down at 50,200 (below the prior low), and a relief bounce lifts it through the morning. It closes at **50,500** — up on the day, back inside the prior red body, but **below the 50,600 midpoint.** That is a bearish Thrusting Line: the bounce thrust into the prior body but *failed to reclear the halfway mark*, signalling the bounce is weak and the downtrend likely resumes.

The trade: sell/short on a break below the Thrusting candle's low, or below the gap-down open of 50,200, with a stop **above the midpoint / prior candle's high** — a stop at 50,720 (just above the 50,600 midpoint, allowing a little room) invalidates the pattern, because a close back above the midpoint would convert the read from "failed bounce" to "piercing-style reversal." Entry ~50,190 (on the break of the low), stop 50,720 → risk ~530 points; target the continuation toward the round 49,500 and then 49,000, roughly 690–1,190 points, a healthy reward-to-risk on the lower target. In practice, index traders would size this via Bank Nifty futures or a bear put spread, and the wide stop argues for the defined-risk option structure.

## How to trade it — entry, stop, target

**Separating Lines.**
- *Entry:* on the close of candle 2, or a break of its extreme in the trend direction.
- *Stop:* just beyond the shared open on the far side (below the open for bullish; above for bearish) — that shared open is the pattern's pivot, and a violation kills the thesis.
- *Target:* trend-continuation measured move, next swing high/low, then trail with a 20-EMA.

**Thrusting Lines.**
- *Entry:* wait for confirmation — a break of the counter-candle's low (bearish) or high (bullish) in the trend direction. Because Thrusting is inherently a *weak* signal, the confirmation break is important; do not pre-empt it.
- *Stop:* just beyond the prior candle's **midpoint** on the far side. The midpoint is the classification boundary: cross it and the pattern flips character toward a Piercing-style reversal.
- *Target:* trend continuation; keep targets realistic because the pattern only tells you the *bounce failed*, not the size of the next leg.

Across both, honour the trend context: these are *continuation* patterns and belong inside an existing trend. Neither is a reversal signal, and treating them as such is the fastest way to lose money on them.

## Confluence — including OI

- **Location within the trend.** Both patterns are strongest when they form after a shallow, early/mid-trend pullback, near a rising (bullish) or falling (bearish) moving average that has already been respected.
- **The counter-candle failing at a known level.** A bearish Thrusting Line whose bounce dies exactly at a prior support-turned-resistance or a 20/50-EMA from below is far more reliable — two independent reasons the bounce failed.
- **Volume.** For Separating Lines, a strong-volume candle 2 confirms real commitment. For Thrusting, *low volume on the bounce candle* confirms the counter-move is thin and likely to fail.
- **Trend/market alignment.** For a stock, verify Nifty and the sector agree.

**Option-chain / OI confluence.** In index and F&O names these two-candle signals gain conviction from positioning:
- For a **bearish Thrusting Line in Bank Nifty**, the failed bounce is corroborated if **Call writers add OI at the strike where the bounce stalled** (they are capping the very level the Thrusting candle failed at) while **Put writers unwind** below (removing the floor) — both point to continuation down. If, on the other hand, the bounce coincides with heavy Put writing building just below, option-sellers are defending a floor and the Thrusting continuation may fizzle; demand the break-of-low confirmation before committing.
- For a **bullish Separating Line in Nifty**, continuation up is reinforced when **Put writers add OI at the shared-open strike** (defending the reset level with capital) and **Call writers unwind overhead** as price pushes through. The candlestick's "dip rejected, trend resumes" message and the option-sellers' positioning then agree.

The general principle: these small two-candle patterns are *low-information on their own* and become tradeable when a second, independent source — a key level, a moving average, volume, or the option chain — points the same way.

## Pitfalls

1. **Confusing Thrusting with Piercing.** This is the cardinal error. The *only* difference is whether the counter-candle closes below (Thrusting → continuation) or above (Piercing → reversal) the prior body's midpoint. Always draw the 50% line before deciding. Misclassify it and you will trade continuation when it's a reversal, or vice versa.
2. **Sloppy "shared open" for Separating Lines.** The pattern requires the *same open*, not merely a similar open. A rough approximation dilutes the signal to meaninglessness. Be strict.
3. **Treating them as reversals.** Both are *continuation* patterns. Their entire logic assumes an existing trend to continue. In a rangebound market they are noise.
4. **Over-trusting a weak signal.** The Thrusting Line is explicitly a *weak* member of its family — it merely says a bounce failed. It gives you direction, not magnitude. Keep targets modest and always require confirmation.
5. **Ignoring the counter-candle's message.** In a bearish Thrusting Line, the counter-candle *did* manage to bounce into the prior body; a *series* of increasingly deep thrusts (On-Neck → In-Neck → Thrusting → Piercing) can be a slow-motion reversal building. Watch the sequence, not just one candle.
6. **News-driven gaps in India.** A Thrusting Line's gap-down open may be a one-off news shock; the subsequent bounce may be a genuine value-buy rather than a failed bounce. Weigh the cause of the gap.
7. **Forcing them on the wrong timeframe.** They need clean opens and (for Thrusting) real gaps; the daily chart delivers these best. On sub-15-minute charts they degrade into randomness.

## Interview-ready summary

**Separating Lines** and **Thrusting Lines** are two-candle *continuation* patterns that answer the question "is this counter-trend candle a warning or just noise?" A **bullish Separating Line** (uptrend) is a red candle followed by a candle that opens at the *same price* and closes green — the dip is rejected from a shared open and the uptrend resumes; the bearish version mirrors it. A **Thrusting Line** (classically bearish, in a downtrend) is a long red candle followed by a gap-down candle that bounces *into* the prior body but closes *below its midpoint* — a failed, weak bounce that signals continuation down. The midpoint is the crucial boundary that separates a Thrusting Line (continuation) from a Piercing Line (reversal), and it sits on a spectrum with the On-Neck and In-Neck lines by how far the counter-candle penetrates. Trade both only within an established trend: enter on the trend-direction break, place the stop beyond the shared open (Separating) or the prior candle's midpoint (Thrusting) — the levels that define the pattern — and target a measured continuation with a trailing exit. Because each is individually low-information (especially the deliberately weak Thrusting Line), require confluence: a moving average or support/resistance where the counter-move failed, a volume signature (strong resumption candle, thin bounce), and — for index/F&O names — supportive option-chain positioning such as Call writing capping a failed bounce or Put writing defending a bullish reset. The classic mistakes are confusing Thrusting with Piercing by not measuring the midpoint, accepting a merely-similar rather than identical open for a Separating Line, and mistaking these continuation signals for reversals.
