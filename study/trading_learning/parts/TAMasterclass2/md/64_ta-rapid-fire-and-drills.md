# TA Rapid-Fire Questions & Chart-Reading Drills

The previous banks built depth through long-form reasoning. This chapter builds *speed and recall* — the ability to answer instantly under pressure, whether in a viva, an interview lightning round, or the real thing: a chart moving in front of you where hesitation costs money. It is split into two halves. First, **rapid-fire Q&A** — one- or two-line answers you should be able to fire back without thinking, organised by theme. Second, **chart-reading drills** — described chart snapshots with a guided "how to read it" so you train the pattern-recognition muscle. Use the rapid-fire section as flashcards (cover the answer, test yourself) and the drills as a daily warm-up. All levels are illustrative India-market reconstructions; verify on live charts. As always: probabilities, not certainties.

---

## Part A — Rapid-Fire Q&A

### Trend & structure

**Q. Define an uptrend in one line.** A series of higher highs and higher lows.

**Q. Define a downtrend.** A series of lower highs and lower lows.

**Q. What is a range/sideways market?** Price oscillating between roughly horizontal support and resistance with no higher-high/lower-low structure.

**Q. First sign a trend may be changing?** The trend fails to make a new high/low, then breaks the most recent swing point.

**Q. What is a pullback vs. a reversal?** A pullback is a counter-move *within* an intact trend; a reversal breaks the trend's structure.

**Q. Higher timeframe or lower timeframe wins on conflict?** Higher — trade with the dominant timeframe's bias.

**Q. What is a swing high?** A candle with lower highs on both sides — a local peak.

**Q. The trend is your…?** Friend — trade with it until structure breaks.

### Support / resistance

**Q. Support and resistance are lines or zones?** Zones.

**Q. What happens to broken support?** It flips to resistance (and vice versa) — polarity/role reversal.

**Q. Round numbers matter — example?** Nifty 22,000 / 23,000; Bank Nifty 48,000 — psychological levels attract orders.

**Q. More touches make a level stronger or weaker?** Stronger as *validation*, but weaker as *demand is consumed* — heavily-tested levels eventually break.

**Q. What is a retest?** Price returning to a broken level to confirm it before continuing.

### Candlesticks

**Q. What does a doji signal?** Indecision — open ≈ close; meaningful at extremes.

**Q. Hammer — what and where?** Small body, long lower wick, at the *bottom* of a downtrend — bullish reversal.

**Q. Shooting star — what and where?** Small body, long upper wick, at the *top* of an uptrend — bearish reversal.

**Q. Bullish engulfing?** A large up-candle fully engulfing the prior down-candle's body — bullish reversal.

**Q. What makes a pin bar valid?** A long rejection wick at a key level, ideally against the immediate move.

**Q. A candle's most important feature at a level?** The wick — it shows where price was rejected.

**Q. Marubozu meaning?** A full-body candle with little/no wick — strong one-sided conviction.

### Patterns

**Q. Head and shoulders signals what?** Trend reversal (bearish at a top); target = head-to-neckline height projected from the break.

**Q. Double top / double bottom?** Twin failure at a level — reversal pattern (M for top, W for bottom).

**Q. Bull flag?** A sharp move (pole) then a shallow, low-volume pullback — continuation in trend direction.

**Q. Ascending triangle bias?** Bullish — flat resistance, rising support, usually breaks up.

**Q. Descending triangle bias?** Bearish — flat support, falling resistance, usually breaks down.

**Q. Symmetrical triangle bias?** Neutral — trade the breakout direction.

**Q. Cup and handle?** Bullish continuation — rounded base then a small pullback (handle) before breakout.

**Q. Measured move for a rectangle/range breakout?** Add the range height to the breakout point.

### Indicators — instant recall

**Q. RSI default period?** 14.

**Q. RSI overbought/oversold levels?** 70 / 30 — but only reliable in *ranges*.

**Q. RSI trend-shifted ranges?** ~40-80 in uptrends, ~20-60 in downtrends.

**Q. MACD components?** MACD line (12-26 EMA), signal (9-EMA of MACD), histogram.

**Q. What does ADX measure?** Trend *strength*, not direction.

**Q. ADX below 20 means?** Weak trend / range — avoid trend systems.

**Q. +DI above −DI means?** Bullish directional bias.

**Q. What does ATR measure?** Volatility — used for stops and position sizing, not direction.

**Q. Bollinger Band settings?** 20-SMA ± 2 standard deviations.

**Q. Bollinger squeeze signals?** Volatility contraction → likely big move (direction unknown).

**Q. VWAP resets when?** Every session (intraday).

**Q. Above VWAP = ?** Intraday bullish control.

**Q. What is the Point of Control (POC)?** The price with the most traded volume in a Volume Profile — a magnet/S-R.

**Q. Ichimoku — most useful piece for beginners?** The cloud (Kumo): above = bullish, below = bearish.

**Q. Stochastic vs RSI — which for ranges?** Stochastic (faster, range-oriented).

**Q. Fibonacci golden ratio level?** 61.8%.

**Q. Shallow (38.2%) vs deep (61.8-78.6%) retracement tells?** Shallow = strong trend; deep = weakening trend.

**Q. 50-DMA and 200-DMA cross names?** Golden cross (50 above 200, bullish); death cross (50 below 200, bearish).

**Q. Best RSI/MACD signal — level or divergence?** Divergence.

### Volume & OI

**Q. Volume confirms what?** Conviction behind a move — breakouts need above-average volume.

**Q. Volume spike after a long trend?** Possible climax/exhaustion — a turning signal.

**Q. Price up + OI up means?** Fresh longs — strong bullish.

**Q. Price up + OI down means?** Short covering — bullish but weaker.

**Q. Price down + OI up means?** Fresh shorts — strong bearish.

**Q. Price down + OI down means?** Long unwinding — bearish but weaker.

**Q. Heavy Call OI at a strike =?** Likely resistance (call writers defending).

**Q. Heavy Put OI at a strike =?** Likely support (put writers defending).

**Q. PCR above 1 leans?** Bullish (more puts written) — with context.

**Q. Max Pain is?** The strike where option buyers lose the most / writers profit — a gravitational reference near expiry.

**Q. Indices have volume?** No — use futures volume and options OI as proxies.

### Risk & psychology

**Q. First thing to define on any trade?** The stop — where the thesis is wrong.

**Q. Minimum acceptable risk-reward?** Typically at least 1:1.5 to 1:2.

**Q. Risk per trade rule of thumb?** ~1-2% of capital.

**Q. What is position sizing based on?** Stop distance and rupee risk, not gut feel.

**Q. Averaging down on a loser is?** Usually a discipline failure — adding to a wrong thesis.

**Q. Best trade to take on an ambiguous setup?** No trade — standing aside is a position.

**Q. What is a fake-out / whipsaw?** A false break that reverses, trapping breakout traders.

**Q. Regime first — why?** It decides which toolkit (trend vs range) to trust.

**Q. Can TA predict the future?** No — it improves odds and timing with defined risk.

## Part B — Chart-Reading Drills

Each drill describes a chart. Read the description, form your own read *before* reading the answer, then compare. Train to see context → setup → plan → invalidation in seconds.

### Drill 1 — The clean pullback

*Chart:* Nifty daily. Price has risen from 21,000 to 23,000 in higher highs/higher lows. It now pulls back three sessions to 22,600, landing on the rising 20-EMA and the 38.2% Fibonacci retracement of the last leg, and prints a bullish hammer on average volume.

*How to read it:* Regime = clear uptrend (respect it). The pullback is *shallow* (38.2%) — a sign of a *strong* trend. Confluence stacks: 20-EMA + 38.2% Fib + hammer at the same zone. This is a **buy-the-dip continuation**. Plan: long at the hammer close (~22,650), stop below the hammer low and the EMA (~22,480), target a new high / measured move toward 23,300+. Invalidation: a close below 22,480 breaks the structure and the EMA. Odds favour continuation; the tight stop makes the risk-reward attractive.

### Drill 2 — The failed breakout

*Chart:* A midcap stock, daily. It has ranged 480-500 for a month. It spikes to 512 intraday on a news pop, then closes back at 495 — a long upper wick, back *inside* the range, on high volume.

*How to read it:* This is a **failed breakout / bull trap**. Price cleared resistance (500) but could not *close* above it and fell back inside, trapping breakout buyers at 505-512. High volume on the failure = real supply. The message: the breakout lacked follow-through; the range is still in control and the failure often leads to a move to the *opposite* end. Plan: fade — short toward 480 (range low), stop above the wick (~515). Invalidation: a *close* above 512 revives the breakout. Lesson: a breakout is only valid on a *close* beyond the level with follow-through; an intraday poke that closes back inside is a trap.

### Drill 3 — The trend exhaustion

*Chart:* Bank Nifty daily. A strong 3-week rally from 46,000 to 49,500. The last two candles: a huge up-day to a new high on the highest volume in months, followed by a bearish engulfing candle closing at 48,900. RSI printed a *lower* high versus the prior swing (divergence).

*How to read it:* Multiple exhaustion signals converge: extended run + climactic volume + bearish engulfing at the high + RSI divergence. This is a **potential top / distribution**. But — one cluster of candles against a strong trend is a *warning*, not a confirmed reversal. Action: defense first — tighten stops on longs, book partial profit; the risk-reward of holding a fully-extended long has collapsed. For a *short*, require confirmation: a follow-through close below the engulfing low (~48,600) or a break of the rising trendline. Then short toward the last consolidation (47,500), stop above the high (49,600). Lesson: exhaustion evidence changes risk management immediately, direction only on confirmation.

### Drill 4 — The VWAP intraday map

*Chart:* Reliance, 5-minute, intraday. Opens at 2,950, drops to 2,935, reclaims and holds above a flattening-then-rising VWAP. Pulls back to VWAP twice at 2,948 and 2,952 — both hold with small bullish wicks. Now approaching the day's high at 2,965.

*How to read it:* Above a rising VWAP with defended pullbacks = **intraday bullish control**. The two VWAP holds confirm buyers are active at fair value. Plan: on a third VWAP pullback that holds, go long, stop below VWAP (~2,944), target a break of the day-high toward 2,975+. Or trade the day-high *breakout* on volume. Caveat: repeated VWAP taps weaken it — respect the stop. If price *loses* VWAP with momentum, the intraday bias flips and you stand aside or reverse. Lesson: VWAP is the intraday battle-line; which side of it price defends tells you who is in control.

### Drill 5 — The bear trap / spring

*Chart:* An IT stock, daily. Support has held at 1,600 three times. On the fourth test it breaks to 1,575 on a spike of volume, then *the next day* closes back at 1,608 — above the broken support.

*How to read it:* Classic **failed breakdown / Wyckoff spring**. The break below 1,600 triggered stops and fresh shorts; the quick reclaim traps them, forcing short-covering that fuels a bounce. Failed breaks are strong signals the *other* way. Plan: long on the reclaim close (~1,610), stop below the trap low (~1,570), target the top of the prior range (say 1,660) or higher as shorts cover. Invalidation: a close back below 1,570. Lesson: shakeouts below well-tested support that immediately reverse are among the higher-probability long setups — the trapped sellers become your fuel.

### Drill 6 — The breadth warning

*Chart:* Nifty daily closes green (+0.4%) at 23,100. But the advance-decline line is sharply negative, the Nifty Midcap index is down 1.2%, and only three heavyweight stocks account for the index's gain.

*How to read it:* A **narrow rally / breadth divergence**. The index is masking broad weakness — the *average* stock is falling. Green index on red breadth is a rally to *distrust*. This is a *context* signal: don't blindly buy the index up-move; it is fragile and depends on a few leaders. Action: favour the actual leaders, keep sizing modest, and watch whether breadth *broadens* (bullish resolution) or the leaders *roll over* (index follows the broad market down). Lesson: healthy trends have broad participation; when the generals advance without the troops, be cautious.

### Drill 7 — The squeeze before the move

*Chart:* USDINR daily. Three weeks of tight 83.10-83.35 range. Bollinger Bands at their narrowest in six months; ADX below 15.

*How to read it:* **Volatility squeeze in a range** — energy coiling, a big move likely *coming* but direction *unknown*; ADX < 15 confirms no trend yet. Do not pre-pick a direction. Plan: bracket the range — long on a decisive *close* above 83.35, short on a close below 83.10, each targeting a measured move (~range height). Consider waiting for a retest of the broken edge, since the first move out of a squeeze can fake. Watch catalysts (Fed/RBI/oil/DXY). Note the USDINR quirk: RBI intervention can cap or delay the expansion. Lesson: a squeeze tells you *when* (soon), not *which way* — prepare both sides.

### Drill 8 — The OI-aware index level

*Chart:* Nifty at 22,950, drifting up. Option chain: heavy Call OI building at 23,000; heavy Put OI at 22,800; PCR 0.8.

*How to read it:* Price is approaching a **call-writer wall** at 23,000 (resistance); Put support sits at 22,800; PCR 0.8 leans slightly cautious. Base case: 23,000 caps price unless writers get squeezed. Plan A (rejection): fade near 23,000 toward 22,850, stop above 23,050 — valid while Call OI at 23,000 keeps *building*. Plan B (squeeze): if price *closes above 23,000* and that Call OI starts *unwinding* (falling), flip long for a short-covering pop. The tell is watching OI behaviour at the strike as price arrives. Lesson: OI turns "near resistance" into a positioning-aware plan — know where the writers are and let them show their hand.

### Drill 9 — Relative strength selection

*Chart:* Two PSU bank stocks. Stock A is printing fresh 52-week highs; Stock B is 18% below its highs, basing sideways. The PSU Bank index breaks out of a multi-month base on volume.

*How to read it:* Sector tailwind is on. **Buy the leader (Stock A)** — new highs mean accumulation and *no overhead supply* (no trapped buyers to sell into strength), so it moves freely. Stock B faces layers of resistance up to its old highs and lagged for a reason. Plan: long Stock A on a pullback or continuation breakout, stop below the breakout level. Consider Stock B only later, *if* money visibly rotates into it (it starts outperforming). Lesson: strength begets strength — in an early/mid sector move, own the leader, not the hopeful laggard.

### Drill 10 — Putting it together (the A+ setup)

*Chart:* A large-cap, daily. Uptrend intact (higher highs/lows). Pulls back to a zone where *four* things align: a prior breakout level now acting as support (₹1,780), the rising 50-EMA, the 61.8% Fib of the last leg, and a bullish engulfing candle — all on a pickup in volume. RSI turns up from 45. In the derivatives data, Put writing is building at the 1,780 strike.

*How to read it:* This is a **high-confluence continuation long** — the kind you wait for. Regime (uptrend) ✔, structure (support zone) ✔, dynamic support (50-EMA) ✔, Fib ✔, candle trigger ✔, momentum turning up ✔, volume ✔, and OI (put writers defending 1,780) ✔. When this many *independent* signals agree, odds improve materially. Plan: long at the engulfing close (~1,795), stop below the zone and candle low (~1,760), target a new high / measured move (₹1,880+) for a risk-reward near 1:2.5. Invalidation: a close below 1,760 breaks the confluence. Lesson: you don't need to trade often — you need to wait for the setup where everything lines up, then act decisively with a defined stop. That patience *is* the edge.

### How to drill effectively

- **Daily warm-up:** before the market opens, mark one index and two stocks — regime, key levels, and one "if X then Y" plan each. Compare to what actually happens.
- **Flashcard the rapid-fire:** cover answers, aim for instant recall; you should not have to *think* about "price up + OI up."
- **Screenshot journal:** save real setups matching these drills; over months you build a personal pattern library that beats any book.
- **Always name the invalidation.** A read without a stop is a wish, not a plan.
- **Stay honest:** even the A+ setup in Drill 10 loses sometimes. The edge is in *repeating* good setups with controlled risk, not in being right every time.
