# Swing, Weekly & Positional Playbook

If intraday trading is a sprint fought in the noise, swing and positional trading is a patient hunt in the signal. Here you hold for days, weeks, or months, and the daily and weekly charts — not the 1-minute — are your world. This is, for most people with jobs, families, and finite screen-time, the *right* timeframe: it demands an hour of analysis in the evening rather than six hours of white-knuckle screen-staring, it pays you the fat part of trends that intraday traders keep giving back to slippage and theta, and it aligns you with the genuine forces that move Indian markets — earnings cycles, sector rotation, and FII/DII flows. This playbook covers the character of the swing-to-positional horizon, the setups that pay across it, position sizing and pyramiding, and the specific rhythms of the Indian calendar.

## The character of the swing-to-positional horizon

The three horizons blur into one another but have distinct personalities:

- **Swing (2-15 trading days):** riding one leg of a move — a breakout to its first target, a pullback bounce, an oversold reversal. Daily chart primary, weekly for context. This is the sweet spot for the working trader.
- **Weekly / short positional (2-8 weeks):** holding a full swing or an earnings run, riding a stock through a sector theme. Weekly chart primary.
- **Positional / investing-adjacent (2-12 months):** capturing a whole trend leg — a stock breaking out of a multi-year base into a bull phase, a sector at the start of a rotation cycle. Weekly and monthly charts primary.

The defining advantage of this horizon is **signal dominance**: on a daily and weekly chart, the noise averages out and what remains is real supply/demand. A breakout on the weekly is a genuine shift in the ownership of the stock, not an algo pinging the spread. This is why patterns "work better" on higher timeframes — you are reading intention, not jitter.

The defining *cost* is **overnight and gap risk**. Unlike the intraday trader who is flat by 3:30, the swing trader carries positions through the night, the weekend, the budget, the RBI policy, the Fed, and — most brutally — earnings. A gap through your stop is not a stop; it's a hole. Position sizing and event-awareness, not tight stops, are how you manage this. Your stop is *where you're wrong*, but your *size* is what determines whether being wrong is survivable when the gap ignores your stop.

A third feature: **this is where fundamentals and technicals marry.** Intraday, fundamentals are irrelevant noise. Positionally, the technical breakout you're buying is usually *powered* by a fundamental engine — an earnings beat, a margin-expansion story, a sector tailwind, an order-book surge. The best positional trades are technically clean *and* fundamentally propelled. The chart tells you *when*; the fundamentals tell you *why it will keep going*.

## Instruments & what suits the horizon

- **Cash equities (delivery):** the natural home of positional trading. No expiry, no theta, no rollover — you own the stock and can hold as long as the thesis holds. Ideal for the multi-week and multi-month horizon.
- **Stock futures:** leverage for swing trades in liquid F&O names, but carry monthly rollover cost and expiry. Good for 2-4 week swings; roll before expiry if holding longer.
- **Index positions (Nifty/Bank Nifty futures or spreads):** for trading the market's swing itself. Options spreads (bull call, bear put, calendars) let you take a positional directional view with defined risk and reduced theta bleed versus naked buying.
- **Positional options (spreads, not naked buys):** naked long options are a terrible positional instrument — theta bleeds you for weeks even if you're right on direction. Use *spreads* to define risk and finance theta, or use futures/cash for directional holds.

The rule: **the longer the hold, the more you should prefer cash or defined-risk spreads over naked long options.** Time is the enemy of the option buyer, and positional holds give time every chance to win.

## Six swing/positional setups with exact rules

### Setup 1 — Breakout from a base (the core positional setup)

A stock consolidates in a well-defined base (rectangle, cup-and-handle, ascending triangle) for weeks or months, then breaks out on volume. The longer and tighter the base, the more explosive the move.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Daily/weekly close above base resistance on volume ≥1.5× the 50-day average |
| Stop | Below the base / below the breakout candle's low (or 50-DMA for positional) |
| Target | Measured move (base height projected); trail with 20/50-DMA |
| Timeframe | Daily entry, weekly context |
| Regime | Trending / early-bull market; best when the sector is also breaking out |

*Worked example (approximate reconstruction — verify on chart):* A capital-goods name bases between ₹1,180-1,260 for four months on declining volume (accumulation). A daily candle closes at ₹1,285 on 2× average volume with the weekly RSI turning up through 60. Buy ₹1,290, stop ₹1,225 (below the base, ₹65 risk). Measured move: base height ₹80 → target ₹1,340 (T1), then trail the 50-DMA as the stock runs toward ₹1,450 over eight weeks. A ₹65 risk that captures a ₹160 move is ~2.5R with the 50-DMA trail giving room for more.

### Setup 2 — Pullback to moving average in an uptrend

The highest-probability continuation setup. A stock in a confirmed uptrend (above a rising 50-DMA, making higher highs) pulls back to a key moving average and resumes.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Pullback to the rising 20/50-DMA, then a bullish reversal candle (hammer, engulfing) on the daily |
| Stop | Below the pullback swing low / below the 50-DMA by a buffer |
| Target | Prior high, then trail; add on the break to new highs |
| Timeframe | Daily |
| Regime | Established uptrend only |

The discipline: only buy pullbacks in a *confirmed* uptrend, and only when the MA is rising. Buying a "pullback" in a downtrend is just catching a falling knife with better marketing.

### Setup 3 — Sector rotation / relative-strength leadership

Money rotates through Indian sectors — IT, banks, auto, pharma, metals, PSU, defence, realty — in identifiable cycles. Buy the sector *and stock* showing the strongest relative strength as the rotation begins.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Sector index breaks out / makes a new relative-strength high vs Nifty; buy the leading stock within it on its own setup |
| Stop | Below the stock's breakout/pullback level |
| Target | Ride the rotation; exit when relative strength rolls over or the sector index breaks its trend |
| Timeframe | Weekly primary, daily for entry |
| Regime | Any — rotation happens in bulls and bears (defensives lead in weakness) |

*Worked example:* PSU banks and defence lead a rotation as the sector index outperforms Nifty for three straight weeks. You buy the strongest constituent breaking out of its own base rather than a laggard "value" name. Relative strength — the stock/sector outperforming the index — is the single most reliable positional filter: **buy strength, not the cheapest thing.**

### Setup 4 — Weekly-chart reversal from major support

A quality stock sells off to a major weekly support (prior base, long-term trendline, 200-DMA) and prints a weekly reversal, offering a low-risk positional entry near a structural floor.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Weekly bullish reversal candle (hammer/engulfing) at major support, ideally with bullish divergence on weekly RSI/MACD |
| Stop | Below the weekly support / reversal low |
| Target | Prior swing highs; a big move if it's a genuine bottom |
| Timeframe | Weekly primary |
| Regime | Late-correction / bottoming; requires a fundamentally sound stock (don't bottom-fish falling businesses) |

The R:R is superb — stop just below a major floor, target a full recovery — but only on stocks whose *business* isn't the reason they fell. A broken chart on a broken company keeps breaking.

### Setup 5 — Earnings-anticipation swing (pre-results run-up)

Stocks with strong momentum often run up into results as positioning builds. A technically-driven pre-earnings swing captures the run-up while *exiting before* the binary event.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Strong stock breaking out or trending 1-2 weeks before results |
| Stop | Below the breakout/trend support |
| Target | Book *before* the result date — do not hold naked through earnings |
| Timeframe | Daily, 1-3 week hold |
| Regime | Bull/momentum phase, results season |

The hard rule: **holding a leveraged position through an earnings gap is gambling, not trading.** The pre-run is a technical trade; the result itself is a coin flip you exit before. If you want to hold through results, do it in cash with size you can survive a 15% gap on, or use a defined-risk spread.

### Setup 6 — Nifty/Bank Nifty positional trend (index swing)

Trading the market itself over weeks using the index's own trend structure, sized to survive gaps.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Index breaks a multi-week range / bounces off a rising 20-week EMA with breadth confirming |
| Stop | Below the swing structure (index points → hedge with a spread if using options) |
| Target | Next major level / measured move; trail the 20-week EMA |
| Timeframe | Weekly primary, daily for timing |
| Regime | Trending market; confirm with market breadth (advance-decline, % above 200-DMA) |

Use a **bull call spread or bear put spread** rather than naked index options for positional index views — it defines risk, survives the weekend, and dramatically reduces theta bleed over a multi-week hold.

## Position sizing, pyramiding & portfolio heat

This horizon is won on portfolio construction, not single-trade cleverness.

- **Risk per trade:** fixed fraction of capital, typically 1-2% per position. Your stop distance sets your quantity: quantity = (capital × risk%) ÷ (entry − stop). Never let the position size drive the stop.
- **Portfolio heat (the master control):** the sum of open risk across all positions. Cap total heat at, say, 6-8% of capital. Ten positions each risking 1% is 10% heat — one bad market day and you're down a tenth. Fewer, well-chosen positions beat a scattered book.
- **Correlation awareness:** five bank stocks are one bet, not five. Correlated positions compound risk on a sector down-day. Diversify across *uncorrelated* themes, not just across tickers.
- **Pyramiding (adding to winners):** as a trade moves in your favour and you trail the stop to breakeven+, add a second (smaller) tranche on the next breakout, then a third. Each add uses fresh risk while locking the prior tranche's gains. This is how you turn a good trade into a great one — but only *add to winners*, never average down losers. Pyramiding scales into strength; averaging down scales into being wrong.
- **The trailing stop:** the engine of positional profit. Trail under the 20-DMA (aggressive), the 50-DMA (standard), or successive higher swing lows. The trail lets the fat tail of a trend run while capping the give-back. "Cut losses, let winners run" is executed *through the trailing stop* — it is not a slogan, it is a mechanic.

## The Indian calendar — timing the horizon

Positional trading must respect the rhythm of the Indian market year:
- **Earnings season (quarterly — Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct):** the biggest recurring driver. Individual stocks gap violently on results. Plan entries and exits around results dates; never carry leveraged size blindly through them.
- **Union Budget (Feb 1):** a market-wide event with sector winners and losers (infra, defence, railways, taxation-sensitive names). Volatility spikes around it; some traders flatten, some position for themes.
- **RBI monetary policy (bi-monthly):** rate decisions move banks, autos, realty, NBFCs. A hawkish surprise hits rate-sensitives; a cut fuels them.
- **F&O expiry (last Thursday, monthly; weeklies too):** the monthly expiry drives rollovers and volatility; positional futures holders must roll before expiry to avoid settlement.
- **Global cues:** the Fed, US CPI, DXY, crude, and global risk sentiment gap Indian markets at the open. A positional trader is always exposed to overnight global risk — which is precisely why *size*, not stop-tightness, is the primary defence.
- **Seasonality:** budget-season volatility, muted Diwali-to-year-end holiday liquidity, results-season stock-specific moves. Awareness of the calendar prevents "why did my stock gap 8%?" surprises.

## Pitfalls

- **Under-sizing the gap risk / over-tightening stops.** A stop is where you're wrong, but a gap ignores it. The defence is *size*, not a stop so tight normal wiggle takes you out before the thesis plays.
- **Holding leveraged positions through earnings.** The single most common positional disaster. Exit the leverage before the binary event or hold survivable cash size.
- **Averaging down losers.** Adding to a position moving against you because it's "cheaper" turns a small loss into a portfolio wound. Add only to winners.
- **Portfolio-heat blindness.** Each trade looks fine at 1% risk, but ten correlated positions is a hidden 10%+ bet. Track total heat and correlation.
- **Buying weakness instead of strength.** The lowest-relative-strength "value" name feels safe and keeps falling. Positional edge is in *leaders* — buy relative strength.
- **Impatience / cutting winners early.** The whole point of the horizon is to capture the fat part of the trend. Booking a 1R gain because it's "nervous-making" while the trend has weeks to run is how positional traders underperform their own setups. Trail, don't snatch.
- **Ignoring the trend of the market.** Even great stock setups fail in a falling market. Positional longs work best when Nifty's own trend and breadth are supportive.

## Interview-ready summary

Swing and positional trading works on the **daily and weekly charts** over days to months, where **signal dominates noise** — a weekly breakout reflects a real ownership shift, not intraday jitter, which is why patterns are more reliable on higher timeframes. The cost is **overnight and gap risk**, managed not by tight stops (a gap ignores them) but by **position size and event-awareness**. This is where **technicals marry fundamentals**: the chart says *when*, the earnings/sector engine says *why it keeps going*. The six workhorse setups are the **base breakout** (the core positional trade), the **uptrend pullback to a rising MA**, **sector-rotation / relative-strength leadership** (buy strength, not cheapness), the **weekly reversal off major support** (superb R:R on sound businesses only), the **pre-earnings run-up** (exit before the binary result), and the **index positional trend** (traded via defined-risk spreads, not naked options). The horizon is won on **portfolio construction**: fixed 1-2% risk per trade, a capped 6-8% total **portfolio heat**, correlation awareness, **pyramiding into winners** (never averaging down), and a **trailing stop** (under the 20/50-DMA) as the engine that lets winners run. All of it is timed to the **Indian calendar** — quarterly earnings, the February Budget, bi-monthly RBI policy, monthly F&O expiry, and ever-present overnight global cues. Prefer cash or spreads over naked long options the longer you hold, because time is the option buyer's enemy and positional trades give time every chance to win.
