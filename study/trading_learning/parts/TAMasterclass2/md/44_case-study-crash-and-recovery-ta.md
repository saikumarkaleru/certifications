# Case Studies: Crash & Recovery (TA View)

Crashes are where technical analysis earns its reputation — and where it most often gets misused. In a crash, correlations go to one, liquidity evaporates, gaps swallow stops, and the orderly patterns of a trending market dissolve into pure fear. Yet crashes are also the most *analysable* events in markets, because panic is mechanical: forced selling, margin calls, volatility spikes, and capitulation all leave unmistakable technical footprints. And every crash in market history has been followed by a recovery — the question is never *whether* but *at what shape and speed.* This chapter reconstructs three crash-and-recovery episodes through Indian market examples: the COVID crash and V-recovery of 2020, a sharp sectoral drawdown-and-heal, and the anatomy of a slower grinding correction. Levels and dates are **approximate teaching reconstructions** — verify exact figures on your charts.

The through-line: a crash is not a single event but a *sequence of technical phases* — distribution, breakdown, panic/capitulation, basing, and recovery — each with its own signature and its own appropriate action. Confusing the phases is how traders die. Buying during breakdown (falling knife) or shorting during capitulation (into the low) are the two classic ways to be right about direction and still lose everything.

## The anatomy of a crash: five phases

Before the case studies, the map. Every serious decline moves through recognisable technical phases:

1. **Distribution / topping.** The uptrend stalls. Momentum diverges (price makes higher highs, RSI/breadth make lower highs). High-volume down days appear amid the highs. The 50-DMA flattens. This can last weeks. *Action: reduce longs, tighten stops, no new aggressive longs.*

2. **Breakdown.** A key support — the base low, the 200-DMA, a trendline — breaks on volume. The character flips from "buy the dip" to "sell the rally." Lower highs and lower lows establish. *Action: exit longs, do not bottom-fish; the trend is now down.*

3. **Panic / capitulation.** The waterfall. Gaps down, huge volume, VIX spikes, everything sells regardless of fundamentals. This phase is *fast and violent* — often the sharpest part of the whole move. It ends in a *climax*: a massive-volume, wide-range day (often with a long lower wick / hammer) as forced sellers are exhausted. *Action: do NOT short into the climax; watch for the reversal signature.*

4. **Basing / repair.** After the climax low, a period of high volatility chop — sharp rallies and retests. The market tests whether the low holds. A successful *higher low* above the capitulation low is the first structural repair. Volatility (India VIX) starts falling from its spike. *Action: begin building longs on confirmed higher lows with defined risk.*

5. **Recovery / markup.** Price reclaims key MAs, the 50-DMA turns up, breadth thrusts appear (a burst of new highs, advance-decline surges). The new uptrend is confirmed. *Action: hold and add; the crash is over.*

The two hardest disciplines are **not buying in Phase 2** (breakdown feels like a bargain, it isn't) and **not shorting in Phase 3** (capitulation feels like the world is ending exactly when the low is being made). Master those two "don'ts" and you survive every crash.

## Case Study 1 — The COVID crash and V-recovery (Nifty, 2020)

**Context.** By January 2020 the Nifty 50 was near all-time highs around 12,400, in a mature uptrend. Valuations were full; breadth had been quietly narrowing (a handful of heavyweights carrying the index — a Phase 1 distribution tell). Then a genuine exogenous shock — a global pandemic — arrived. This is the archetypal modern crash: a fast, deep, exogenous shock followed by an equally fast, policy-driven recovery. The V.

**Phase 1-2 (distribution → breakdown).** In February 2020 the Nifty rolled over from ~12,250. The breakdown was swift: within days it sliced through the 50-DMA (~12,000) and then the 200-DMA (~11,300) on rising volume. The break of the 200-DMA in late February was the unambiguous "get out" signal for trend followers — the character had flipped. Anyone who respected the 200-DMA break exited near 11,300-11,500 and avoided the ensuing carnage.

**Phase 3 (panic / capitulation).** March 2020 was one of the most violent panics in Indian market history. The Nifty fell from ~11,300 to a low near **7,511** on 24 March — a ~40% drawdown from the January peak in roughly six weeks. India VIX exploded from the low-to-mid teens to an intraday spike above **80** — a level never seen before or since. Multiple *lower-circuit* days halted trading. This is textbook capitulation: near-vertical decline, record volume, record volatility, indiscriminate selling. The falling-knife trap claimed every "value" buyer at 10,000, at 9,000, at 8,000.

**The climax and reversal signature.** The 7,511 low on 24 March printed on colossal volume, and the following sessions produced enormous wide-range *up* days — the character of exhaustion reversal. India VIX peaking above 80 *as* price bottomed is the classic inverse relationship: **volatility peaks at price lows.** A trader watching VIX for a *peak-and-roll-over* (rather than trying to pick the price low directly) had a superior signal — when VIX crested 80 and then started falling while price stopped making new lows, the capitulation was ending.

**Phase 4-5 (basing → recovery).** What made 2020 a "V" rather than a "U" or "L" was the speed of the policy response — coordinated global monetary and fiscal stimulus. Technically, the recovery was breathtaking: the Nifty reclaimed the 7,511 low decisively, built a *higher low* near 8,000 in early April, then thrust upward. It reclaimed the 50-DMA within weeks and, remarkably, was back above 11,000 by August and printing *new all-time highs* by November-December 2020. A breadth thrust — a sudden surge in the advance-decline line and percentage of stocks above their 50-DMA — confirmed the new bull leg in April-May.

| Phase | Nifty level (approx) | Signature | Right action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distribution | ~12,250 top | Narrowing breadth, momentum divergence | Reduce longs |
| Breakdown | ~11,300 (200-DMA break) | Support break on volume | Exit longs |
| Capitulation | ~7,511 low, VIX >80 | Lower circuits, record volume/vol | Do NOT short into it; watch VIX peak |
| Basing | ~8,000 higher low | VIX rolls over, higher low holds | Begin defined-risk longs |
| Recovery | reclaim 11,000+ by Aug | 50-DMA turns up, breadth thrust | Hold and add |

**The trade.** The disciplined re-entry was *not* at 7,511 (unknowable in real time) but on the *confirmed higher low* around 8,000-8,300 with a stop below the 7,511 capitulation low, and adds as price reclaimed the 50-DMA and as VIX fell back under ~40. Even entering "late" at 8,500 with a stop at 7,400 (~13% risk) against a move back to 11,000+ delivered a reward:risk north of 2.5:1 on the first leg alone and multiples more for those who held into the new-high breakout.

**The lesson.** Three lessons dominate. First, **respect the 200-DMA breakdown** — it got you out at 11,300 and preserved the capital that let you re-enter at 8,000. Second, **use VIX as a capitulation gauge**: extreme spikes (India VIX above ~35-40, and certainly above 80) mark *proximity to lows,* not opportunities to short. Third, **re-enter on the higher low, not the absolute low** — you cannot catch 7,511, but you can catch the confirmed repair at 8,000-8,300, and that is enough. The V-recovery also teaches humility about *shape*: had you assumed a slow U-shaped grind and waited for a "proper base," you'd have missed the entire move. Policy-driven crashes can recover as fast as they fell.

## Case Study 2 — A sharp sectoral drawdown and heal (Bank Nifty style)

**Context.** Crashes aren't only index-wide. Sectoral crashes — a sudden shock to one industry — are more frequent and offer cleaner risk-reward because the rest of the market provides a reference. Consider a sharp Bank Nifty-style drawdown driven by a credit-quality scare or a large NBFC/bank-specific shock (the IL&FS-triggered NBFC squeeze of 2018, or a sudden spike in bond yields hitting financials). The pattern: financials, which had led the market, suddenly become the epicentre of selling.

**Phase 1-2.** Bank Nifty had been the market leader, say trading near 28,000 (using illustrative 2018-era levels). Distribution appeared as the sector stopped making new highs while the broader Nifty pushed on — *relative-strength divergence,* a powerful sector-specific warning. Then a credit event broke a key support (the multi-month base low / 200-DMA near 25,500) on heavy volume. The relative-strength line of Bank Nifty versus Nifty broke down *before* the absolute price did — the sharpest early warning available to a sector trader.

**Phase 3.** The panic in financials was concentrated and vicious — NBFC names fell 40-60% in weeks as a liquidity freeze fed on itself; the index fell toward ~23,000-24,000. Because the pain was sector-specific, the capitulation showed up as *individual stock waterfalls* (specific NBFCs limit-down) more than a single clean index climax. The tell that the worst was passing: the *weakest* names stopped making new lows even as headlines worsened — selling exhaustion in the epicentre.

**Phase 4-5.** The heal was slower than 2020's V because the driver (a credit/liquidity concern) took time to resolve via RBI liquidity measures. Bank Nifty built a *higher low,* chopped sideways to repair for several weeks, then recovered as liquidity fears eased and relative strength versus Nifty turned back up. The confirmation was two-fold: (a) price reclaiming the 50-DMA, and (b) the RS line turning up — the sector regaining leadership.

**The trade.** The cleanest sector re-entry used *relative strength as the primary signal.* Rather than guess the price low, wait for Bank Nifty's RS-vs-Nifty line to stop falling and turn up while price holds a higher low. Enter on that confluence with a stop below the higher low. Because sector rotations are powerful, catching the moment leadership returns often gives the sharpest early outperformance.

| Signal | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| RS line breaks down before price | Sector losing leadership | Exit sector longs early |
| Epicentre stocks limit-down, then stop making lows | Capitulation in the weakest names | Watch for basing |
| Higher low + 50-DMA reclaim | Structural repair | Begin longs |
| RS line turns back up | Leadership returning | Add; overweight the sector |

**The lesson.** For *sector* crashes, **relative strength is the master signal** — it warns you out before absolute price breaks, and it confirms the recovery before the index does. The second lesson: sector heals driven by *policy/liquidity plumbing* (RBI measures) are typically **slower U-shapes**, not V's — don't expect the COVID snap-back rhythm when the cause is structural credit stress. Patience through the multi-week basing pays; front-running the base gets you chopped in the epicentre volatility.

## Case Study 3 — The slow grinding correction (a bear that doesn't announce itself)

**Context.** Not every decline is a dramatic crash. The most *psychologically punishing* declines are the slow grinds — a market that falls 20-25% over many months in a staircase of lower highs and lower lows, with frequent hope-restoring bounces that each fail. Think of a broad-market correction where the headline index (Nifty) falls modestly but the *broader market* (mid- and small-caps) grinds down 25-35% over six to nine months. This is the "death by a thousand cuts" decline that wears traders out.

**The signature.** Unlike a crash, there is no single capitulation climax to mark the bottom — that is precisely what makes it hard. Instead you get a *series* of scary down-legs and relief rallies. The technical map:

- The Nifty holds up relatively (heavyweights masking the damage) while **breadth deteriorates badly** — the percentage of stocks above their 200-DMA falls to 20-30%, the advance-decline line makes persistent new lows. *The index lies; breadth tells the truth.*
- Each relief rally fails at a *lower high,* typically at or below the falling 50-DMA. The 50-DMA below a falling 200-DMA (a "death cross" configuration that then persists) confirms the grind.
- Sentiment slowly bleeds from complacency to disgust. The bottom of a grind is marked not by a VIX spike but by *capitulation of participation* — volumes dry up, retail gives up, "nobody cares anymore."

**The trap and the tell.** The grind's trap is *the failing bounce.* Every 8-12% rally screams "the correction is over," and every one fails at a lower high under the falling 50-DMA. The rule mirrors the falling-knife rule from stock trends: **in a confirmed grind, rallies are for selling/reducing, not chasing, until the structure of lower highs is broken.** The bottom tell is subtle: breadth stops deteriorating even as price makes a marginal new low (a *breadth divergence* — fewer stocks making new lows than at the prior low), and then a *breadth thrust* fires — a sudden surge in stocks reclaiming their 50-DMA, an advance-decline explosion. The grind's recovery, when it comes, is often confirmed by breadth *before* the headline index makes it obvious.

| Feature | Crash (Case 1) | Grind (Case 3) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Fast, weeks | Slow, many months |
| Bottom signature | VIX spike + volume climax | Breadth divergence + participation exhaustion |
| Bounces | Few, then V-recovery | Many failing lower-high rallies |
| Index vs breadth | Both fall together | Index masks; breadth reveals |
| Recovery confirm | 50-DMA reclaim, VIX rolls over | Breadth thrust, structure of lower highs breaks |

**The trade.** In a grind you *do not* try to be a hero. You reduce exposure when breadth deteriorates and lower highs establish, you sit in cash through the failing bounces, and you re-engage only on the *breadth thrust and the break of the lower-high structure.* Re-entry is confirmed, not anticipatory. Missing the exact low is irrelevant; what matters is not bleeding capital through six months of failing rallies. The trader who preserved capital in cash re-enters with full firepower when the breadth thrust confirms the new leg.

**The lesson.** The grind teaches that **breadth is the truth-teller when the index lies.** A cap-weighted index masks broad-market destruction; only breadth (% above 200-DMA, advance-decline, new-highs-new-lows) reveals it — and only breadth reliably signals the turn. The second lesson: **grinds have no VIX climax**, so tools that work for crashes (buy the VIX spike) fail here; the grind bottoms on *exhaustion of participation and a breadth thrust,* not a volatility spike. The third and most important: **in a grind, doing nothing is a position.** Sitting in cash through the failing rallies is the winning trade; the itch to "catch the bounce" is what the grind punishes.

## Cross-cutting principles for crashes and recoveries

**1. Know which phase you are in.** Distribution, breakdown, capitulation, basing, recovery — each demands opposite actions. The fatal errors are buying in breakdown (falling knife) and shorting in capitulation (into the low). Phase identification is the whole discipline.

**2. Volatility and breadth are your crash instruments, not price alone.** VIX spikes mark *crash* lows (buy the peak-and-roll, don't short into it). Breadth divergence and thrusts mark *grind* lows. Match the tool to the decline's personality.

**3. Never catch the falling knife; re-enter on the confirmed higher low / breadth thrust.** You cannot buy 7,511 or the exact grind low. You *can* buy the confirmed repair — the higher low above the capitulation low, the reclaim of the 50-DMA, the breadth thrust. Missing the low by 5-15% and buying confirmation beats catching a "low" that was a ledge.

**4. Recovery *shape* depends on the crash's *cause.*** Exogenous shock + policy response = V (2020). Structural credit/liquidity stress = slower U (Case 2). Valuation/breadth unwind = long grind (Case 3). Diagnose the cause to set expectations for the recovery's speed and shape.

**5. Respect the 200-DMA as the regime line.** In every case, the 200-DMA break defined "trend is now down," and its reclaim defined "trend is repairing." It is the single most important line on the crash chart.

## Interview-ready summary

- A crash is a **five-phase sequence**: distribution → breakdown → capitulation → basing → recovery. The two fatal errors are **buying in the breakdown (falling knife)** and **shorting into the capitulation climax**.
- The **2020 COVID crash** is the archetypal **V**: Nifty ~12,250 → ~7,511 (−40% in ~6 weeks) with **India VIX spiking above 80** at the low, then a policy-driven snap-back to new highs by year-end. Lesson: **respect the 200-DMA break to exit, use the VIX spike as a capitulation gauge, re-enter on the ~8,000 higher low — not the unknowable exact low.**
- For **sector crashes** (Bank Nifty style), **relative strength is the master signal** — it warns before absolute price breaks and confirms the recovery before the index; policy/liquidity-driven heals are **slower U-shapes**, not V's.
- The **slow grind** has **no VIX climax**; it bottoms on **breadth divergence, participation exhaustion, and a breadth thrust**. **Breadth is the truth-teller when the cap-weighted index lies**, and **sitting in cash through failing lower-high rallies is the winning trade**.
- **Recovery shape follows the cause**: exogenous shock + policy = V; structural credit stress = U; valuation/breadth unwind = long grind. Diagnose the cause to set recovery expectations.
- Across all crashes, **you re-enter on confirmation** — a higher low, a 50/200-DMA reclaim, a breadth thrust — not on cheapness. **Capital preservation in the decline is what funds the recovery entry.**
