# Tri-Star & Unique Three River Bottom

Some candlestick patterns are workhorses — the hammer, the engulfing, the doji — that you will spot several times a week on liquid Indian names. Others are collector's items: rare, fussy about their conditions, but carrying an outsized psychological message when they do appear. The **Tri-Star** and the **Unique Three River Bottom** belong to this second category. You may go months without a textbook Tri-Star on the Nifty daily chart, and the Unique Three River is rarer still. But learning them sharpens your reading of exhaustion and capitulation, and both patterns teach a deeper lesson: reversals are not single events, they are *processes* that leave a signature across two or three sessions.

This chapter treats them together because they are cousins. Both are three-candle reversal formations, both depend heavily on where the *body* of each candle sits, and both are frequently misread by beginners who count only the shape and ignore the context. We will define each precisely, explain the crowd psychology, walk through Indian examples with rupee levels, give exact trading rules, layer in option-chain confluence, and — importantly — be honest about how often they fail.

## What they are and why they work

### The Tri-Star

A Tri-Star is a three-doji reversal pattern. Recall that a doji is a candle whose open and close are almost identical, producing a tiny or non-existent body — a session in which buyers and sellers fought to a standstill. One doji is already a warning of indecision after a strong trend. Three dojis in a row, arranged with a gap, form the Tri-Star.

The **bullish Tri-Star** appears at the bottom of a downtrend:

- Candle 1: a doji, consistent with the prevailing selling.
- Candle 2: a doji that **gaps down** below the first — the star, sitting at the low of the formation.
- Candle 3: a doji that **gaps up** away from the second.

The **bearish Tri-Star** is the mirror image at the top of an uptrend: doji, gap-up doji (the star), then gap-down doji.

Why does it matter? A single doji after a long trend says "the dominant side hesitated." Three consecutive dojis say the trend has run entirely out of conviction — neither side can push price meaningfully in either direction for three whole sessions, and the middle candle's gap shows one last emotional thrust (a final gap-down in a downtrend, a final gap-up in an uptrend) that immediately failed to follow through. The third candle gapping back the other way is the tell: momentum has flipped. It is exhaustion made visible.

The honest caveat for Indian markets: **true gaps are the problem.** The classical Tri-Star, drawn from Japanese rice-market and later Western daily charts, assumes clean gaps between the dojis. Nifty and Bank Nifty gap regularly at the open because of overnight SGX Nifty / GIFT Nifty moves and US closes, but *individual liquid stocks* intraday rarely leave three clean gapped dojis. So a picture-perfect Tri-Star is genuinely uncommon. Most practitioners relax the gap rule to "near-doji bodies clustered with the middle one extended," which is more of a **doji cluster** than a strict Tri-Star. Know the pure definition, but trade the spirit.

### The Unique Three River Bottom

The Unique Three River Bottom is a bullish reversal, and its name is a translation quirk — think of it as "three rivers" flowing to a common bottom. It is a three-candle bottoming pattern with a very specific anatomy:

- Candle 1: a **long bearish (black/red) candle**, in keeping with the downtrend. This sets the tone.
- Candle 2: another **bearish candle** that opens within candle 1's body, trades to a **new low** (making the low of the whole pattern), but then closes well off that low — often forming a hammer-like or long-lower-shadow body that closes *above* candle 1's close. The new low is the "river bottom"; the recovery into the close is the first sign of buyers.
- Candle 3: a **small-bodied candle** (often a small bullish or a spinning top) that stays entirely within candle 2's range and does **not** make a new low. It confirms that the selling has dried up.

The message: the downtrend makes one final flush to a new low on candle 2, buyers absorb it and drag price back up by the close, and then candle 3 shows sellers can no longer force a lower low. It is a capitulation-and-stabilisation sequence. Unlike the Tri-Star, it does not need gaps, which makes it slightly more findable on Indian charts — but it demands that new low on candle 2 followed by a genuine recovery, and that is what separates it from a plain three-candle drift.

Both patterns are, at heart, **stalling-of-momentum stories**. The Tri-Star is stalling by total indecision (three dojis); the Unique Three River is stalling by failed downside continuation (a new low that could not hold). Neither is a green light on its own — they are *setups for a trigger*, and the disciplined trader waits for that trigger.

## Mechanics and settings

There is no formula or indicator setting here — these are pure price-action patterns — but there are measurable conditions worth codifying so your eye (or a Chartink screener) is consistent.

**Tri-Star conditions (practical Indian version):**

| Element | Strict rule | Practical relaxed rule |
|---|---|---|
| Prior trend | Clear down (bullish) or up (bearish) over 8-15 sessions | Same |
| Body size, all 3 | |close − open| ≤ ~5% of the day's range (true doji) | ≤ ~10-15% of range (near-doji) |
| Middle candle | Gaps in trend direction; is the extreme | Extends beyond the two outer candles; gap optional |
| Third candle | Gaps back against the trend | Closes back inside/against candle 2 |
| Volume | Ideally rising into candle 2, easing after | Confirm on candle 3 / trigger day |

**Unique Three River Bottom conditions:**

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | Established downtrend |
| Candle 1 | Long bearish body |
| Candle 2 | Bearish; opens inside C1 body, prints the pattern's lowest low, closes above C1's close (long lower shadow / hammer-ish) |
| Candle 3 | Small body, entirely within C2's range, no new low, ideally mildly bullish |
| Confirmation | Next candle closes above C2's high (or above C3's high) |

For **Chartink-style screening**, the Tri-Star is hard to encode cleanly (the gap and triple-doji logic is fiddly), so most people screen for "three consecutive small-body candles after a down-move" and eyeball the rest. The Unique Three River is easier: screen for a new N-day low today with a close in the upper third of the range, preceded by a long red candle — then read the following day by hand.

## Worked India example — bullish Tri-Star (reconstruction)

*Levels below are an approximate reconstruction to illustrate the reading; verify exact prices on your own charts before acting.*

Imagine **Nifty 50** in a grinding decline from roughly 24,600 to 23,300 over three weeks. Sentiment is sour — FIIs are net sellers, global cues weak. Then:

- **Day 1** — Nifty opens near 23,350, sells to 23,290, rallies to 23,410, and closes at 23,345. Open 23,350, close 23,345: a doji. Range ~120 points, body ~5 points. Textbook indecision after a long fall.
- **Day 2** — Global markets wobble overnight; Nifty gaps down to open at 23,280, prints a low of 23,230 (the pattern's low), pushes up to 23,320, and closes at 23,275. Another doji, gapped below Day 1. This is the "star" — the point of maximum despair, yet the day couldn't close near its low.
- **Day 3** — GIFT Nifty is firm pre-open; Nifty gaps up to open at 23,360, ranges 23,320-23,430, and closes at 23,370. A third doji, gapped back *up* above Day 2. The momentum has visibly flipped: the downside gap of Day 2 has been erased.

The three small bodies clustered with a gapped-down middle and a gapped-up third form a bullish Tri-Star at ~23,230-23,430. The message: after a 1,300-point slide, the market spent three sessions unable to extend lower, made its low on the middle candle, and reversed the emotional gap on the third.

**The trade.** You do not buy the third doji blindly. You wait for a **trigger candle**: Day 4 must confirm by closing above the pattern high (~23,430) or, more aggressively, by breaking Day 3's high intraday. Suppose Day 4 opens 23,380 and pushes through 23,430.

- **Entry:** buy on the break/close above 23,430.
- **Stop:** below the pattern low 23,230 (the Day 2 river-bottom equivalent) — call it 23,200, giving ~230 points of risk. On Nifty that is wide; size accordingly, or use a Bank Nifty / index option structure to cap absolute rupee risk.
- **Target 1:** the prior consolidation / first resistance, ~23,750 (about 320 points, ~1.4R).
- **Target 2:** the swing origin, ~24,100-24,200 (~3.5R) if the reversal has legs.

## Worked India example — Unique Three River Bottom (reconstruction)

Take a real-feeling single stock, say **Tata Steel**, in a downtrend from ~₹150 to ~₹128 over a month on weak global steel prices. *(Approximate reconstruction — confirm on charts.)*

- **Candle 1:** a long red day, open ₹132, close ₹128.5 — a decisive down session, extending the trend.
- **Candle 2:** opens ₹129 (inside C1's body), sellers hammer it to ₹125.2 (a fresh multi-week low — the river bottom), but buyers absorb the flush and it closes ₹128.8, above C1's close, leaving a long lower shadow. Volume spikes — classic capitulation.
- **Candle 3:** a quiet, small-bodied day: open ₹128.9, high ₹130.1, low ₹128.2 (no new low), close ₹129.6. It sits inside C2's range and tilts mildly bullish. Sellers could not force a lower low.

That is a Unique Three River Bottom around ₹125-130. The narrative: one last washout to ₹125.2, absorbed; then stabilisation.

**The trade.** Confirmation is a close above C2's high (₹129) — arguably already met by C3's close, so a purist waits for a candle closing above C3's high (₹130.1).

- **Entry:** buy on close above ₹130.1, say ₹130.5.
- **Stop:** below the river-bottom low ₹125.2 — place at ₹124.5. Risk ~₹6.
- **Target 1:** ₹136 (prior support-turned-resistance), ~₹5.5, ~0.9R — thin; better to look further.
- **Target 2:** ₹142-144 (the ₹132-150 range midpoint / gap fill), ~₹12-13, ~2R.

Manage by trailing under each higher swing low once ₹136 is reclaimed.

## How to trade them — a common framework

Because both are exhaustion patterns, the same execution discipline applies:

1. **Demand a prior trend.** These are *reversal* patterns. A Tri-Star in the middle of a sideways range is just three boring dojis — noise, not signal. No established trend, no trade.
2. **Never enter on the third candle alone.** Both patterns describe *potential* bottoms/tops. The edge lives in the confirmation candle. Waiting one more day costs you a little entry price but filters out a large share of failures.
3. **Anchor the stop to the pattern extreme.** For the bullish versions, the stop sits just below the lowest low (the middle doji for Tri-Star, candle 2 for Unique Three River). If price returns there, the reversal thesis is dead — no averaging down.
4. **Define targets by structure, not hope.** Use the prior swing highs, supply zones, and gap fills. Reversal patterns often only carry price to the first meaningful resistance; treat the second target as a runner, not the base case.
5. **Size for the wide stop.** These patterns can have large distances between entry and the pattern low. On indices that means using options or smaller quantity so a single failure is 0.5-1R of your risk budget, not a portfolio dent.

## Confluence — including option-chain reads

A rare candlestick pattern is far more trustworthy when other, independent evidence agrees. Layer these:

**Support / resistance & moving averages.** A bullish Tri-Star or Unique Three River that forms *at* a well-tested support — a prior demand zone, the 200-DMA, a round number like Nifty 23,000 or Bank Nifty 48,000 — is worth far more than one hanging in mid-air. In the Tata Steel example, if ₹125 coincides with the rising 200-DMA, conviction jumps.

**Momentum divergence.** The best exhaustion reversals show **bullish RSI divergence**: price makes the new low on the middle candle while RSI makes a *higher* low. That divergence and the candlestick pattern are telling the same story from different angles.

**Volume.** For the Unique Three River, a **volume spike on candle 2** (the capitulation flush) followed by lighter volume on candle 3 is ideal — heavy selling absorbed, then sellers gone. For the Tri-Star, look for volume to *contract* across the three dojis and *expand* on the confirmation candle.

**Option chain (indices and F&O stocks).** Around a suspected index bottom:

- **Put writing returning at the pattern low.** If on the middle/second candle the highest open-interest Put strike (say Nifty 23,200 PE) sees OI *building* — writers selling puts — while price holds, that is smart money marking a floor. A Tri-Star low that coincides with a fat Put wall is a strong confluence.
- **PCR turning up from an extreme.** A Put-Call Ratio that has fallen to a fearful extreme (e.g., ~0.7) and starts ticking up as the pattern completes signals sentiment stabilising.
- **Falling India VIX on the confirmation day.** Panic bottoms often coincide with a VIX spike on the flush candle and a sharp VIX drop as the reversal confirms — fear draining out.
- **IV crush.** After the capitulation candle, a collapse in put implied volatility tells you the demand for downside protection is fading, consistent with a bottom.

For a bearish Tri-Star at a top, invert these: **Call writing building** at the highest-OI Call strike as the star forms, PCR rolling over from a greedy extreme, VIX ticking up off complacent lows.

## Pitfalls

**1. Forcing the pattern.** By far the biggest error. Beginners see any three small candles and shout "Tri-Star." Without a genuine prior trend, without the middle extreme, without the momentum flip, it is not the pattern — it is a range. Rarity is the point; if you're spotting one a day, you've loosened your rules too far.

**2. Ignoring the gap reality.** On Indian *single stocks* intraday, clean gaps are scarce, so a true Tri-Star almost always shows up on *daily* charts of gap-prone instruments (indices, stocks reacting to news/results). Don't expect three gapped dojis on a 15-minute Reliance chart.

**3. Trading without confirmation.** The dojis/candles describe indecision — by definition the outcome is *not yet decided*. Jumping in on candle three is trading a coin-flip. The confirmation candle is the whole edge.

**4. Mis-locating the stop.** Putting the stop just under the confirmation candle instead of under the pattern low gets you shaken out on a normal retest. The structural stop is the pattern extreme.

**5. Over-trusting rare patterns.** Precisely because they are uncommon, there is little large-sample statistical comfort. Treat them as *context enhancers* — one input alongside support, divergence, volume and OI — not as standalone signals. A Unique Three River at random support with nothing else agreeing is a low-conviction trade.

**6. Wrong regime.** In a violent, news-driven trend (a war scare, a rate shock), exhaustion patterns get run over repeatedly. These formations work best in ordinary conditions where crowds mean-revert, not in one-way liquidation.

## Interview-ready summary

- **Tri-Star** = three consecutive **dojis**; the middle one gaps in the trend's direction (the "star"), the third gaps back against it. Bullish version at a downtrend bottom, bearish at an uptrend top. It signals total momentum exhaustion — three sessions of pure indecision after a run. **Rare**, and pure gapped versions are uncommon on Indian single stocks; usually a relaxed "doji cluster."
- **Unique Three River Bottom** = bullish three-candle bottom: long red candle, then a second red candle that makes a **new low but closes higher** (the river bottom / capitulation), then a **small candle with no new low** (stabilisation). Confirm on a close above candle 2/3's high.
- Both are **exhaustion/reversal** patterns needing a prior trend; both demand a **confirmation candle** before entry; both anchor the **stop below the pattern low**.
- **Confluence multiplies reliability:** support/200-DMA, RSI divergence, capitulation volume, and — for F&O names — Put-writing/PCR/VIX turning at the low.
- Honest stance: **low frequency, no large-sample edge in isolation.** Use them to *strengthen* a location-based thesis, size for the wide stop, and never trade them naked without confirmation and confluence.
