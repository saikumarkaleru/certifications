# Pharma, FMCG, Metals & Energy Sector TA

Beyond the twin engines of banking and IT sit four sectors that give the Indian market its rotation, its defensiveness, and its cyclical juice: **Pharma, FMCG, Metals, and Energy.** Each has a distinct technical personality driven by a distinct fundamental engine — regulatory news for pharma, monsoon and consumption for FMCG, the global commodity cycle and the dollar for metals, and crude/policy for energy. A trader who reads all four can always find a sector in trend when the index is stuck, and can position the rotation *between* defensive (pharma, FMCG) and cyclical (metals, energy) baskets. This chapter is the technical field guide to all four.

## The defensive-cyclical spectrum

Place these four on a spectrum. **FMCG and Pharma are defensives** — low beta, steady earnings, they hold up when the market falls and lag when it rips. **Metals and Energy are deep cyclicals** — high beta to the global economy, feast-or-famine earnings, they lead in reflationary risk-on phases and collapse in slowdowns. The single most useful macro-technical read is *which end of this spectrum money is rotating toward*. When defensives outperform, the market is fearful; when metals and energy lead, the market believes in growth. Build a simple ratio — (Nifty FMCG + Nifty Pharma) / (Nifty Metal + Nifty Energy) — and its trend tells you the market's risk mood better than any oscillator.

## Pharma: news-driven, event-heavy, bifurcated

Nifty Pharma is led by Sun Pharma, Cipla, Dr Reddy's, Divi's Labs, and Aurobindo, with a large mid-cap tail (Lupin, Zydus, Torrent, Alkem, Mankind). Its technical character:

- **USFDA is the wildcard.** Indian pharma exports heavily to the US. A single USFDA plant inspection outcome — a clean EIR, an OAI classification, a warning letter, or an import alert — can gap a stock 8-15% overnight with no technical warning. This makes pharma the most *headline-fragile* sector. TA sets levels; FDA decides whether those levels survive.
- **It splits into two very different books.** *US-generics* names (Sun, Dr Reddy's, Aurobindo, Cipla's US book) trade on FDA news and US pricing pressure. *Domestic/branded* names (Mankind, Torrent, Abbott-style franchises) and *CDMO/API* names (Divi's, Syngene) trade on steadier, more chart-respecting fundamentals. Do not lump them together.
- **Defensive rotation magnet.** When the broad market corrects on domestic or global risk-off, pharma (with FMCG) is where money hides. Pharma indices often *rise* during Nifty corrections — a classic relative-strength tell.

### Worked pharma example (approximate reconstruction — verify on your charts)

Sun Pharma is trending up, price ~1,650, above a rising 50-DEMA at ~1,590. It consolidates in a 1,620-1,700 range for a month. The broad Nifty then begins a 4-5% correction on FII selling. Sun Pharma *refuses to fall* — it holds 1,620 while the index drops, and its relative-strength line (Sun / Nifty) turns sharply up. This is the defensive-rotation tell.

The trade: buy the hold of **1,620** support as the RS line confirms outperformance, stop below the 50-DEMA at **1,585**, target the range-top breakout at **1,700** and then a measured move to **1,760**. Confluence: the Nifty Pharma index itself outperforming, no pending USFDA decision on Sun's key plants (check the calendar — this is the pharma-specific homework), and RSI holding above 50 while the market's RSI breaks down. The edge here is *relative* strength during weakness, which is pharma's signature.

The critical caveat: before any multi-day pharma hold, you *must* check for scheduled results and any known FDA inspection timelines. A perfect chart means nothing if an import alert lands on the plant that makes 30% of the company's US revenue.

## FMCG: the slowest, steadiest, most mean-reverting

Nifty FMCG is led by ITC, Hindustan Unilever (HUL), Nestlé India, Britannia, Varun Beverages, and Tata Consumer. Technical character:

- **Lowest beta in the market.** These are ballast stocks — pension-and-SIP favourites that move in slow, orderly trends. Daily ranges are narrow; a 2% FMCG day is a big day. This makes FMCG poor for intraday momentum but excellent for *positional trend-following* and *mean-reversion to moving averages*.
- **Rural and monsoon-driven.** Volume growth depends on rural demand, which depends on the monsoon, MSPs, and rural wage trends. A good-monsoon narrative lifts the whole basket; input-cost inflation (palm oil, wheat, milk, crude-linked packaging) squeezes margins and caps the charts.
- **Bond-proxy behaviour.** Because FMCG earnings are stable, these stocks trade partly like bonds — falling interest-rate expectations lift their valuations, rising rates compress them. In a rate-cut cycle, FMCG re-rates.
- **ITC is its own story.** As a cigarettes-plus-FMCG-plus-hotels-plus-paper conglomerate with a huge weight, ITC often diverges from the pure-play FMCG names and reacts sharply to Budget-day taxation news on cigarettes.

### Worked FMCG example (approximate reconstruction — verify on your charts)

HUL has pulled back from ~2,700 to ~2,450 over two months on margin worries, and is now sitting exactly on its rising 200-DEMA at ~2,440, with weekly RSI oversold near 38 — an unusual reading for a defensive. Historically HUL reverts from its 200-DEMA in bull markets. The setup is a **200-DEMA reversion buy**: enter near **2,450** with a stop below **2,390** (a decisive 200-DEMA loss on volume), targeting reversion to the 50-DEMA near **2,600** and then the prior high near **2,700**. Confluence: a benign input-cost backdrop (palm oil, crude cooling), a normal-monsoon narrative, and the broader FMCG index also basing. This is a low-volatility, high-patience trade — precisely FMCG's character. You are not chasing momentum; you are buying a quality defensive at a statistically stretched discount to its trend.

## Metals: the global cyclical, dollar-inverse

Nifty Metal is led by Tata Steel, JSW Steel, Hindalco, Vedanta, Coal India, Jindal Steel, and NMDC. This is the most *externally* driven sector on the Indian market.

- **China is the swing factor.** China consumes roughly half the world's steel and base metals. Chinese stimulus announcements, property-sector news, and PMI prints move Tata Steel and Hindalco more than anything domestic. A China-reflation narrative is rocket fuel for Indian metals; a China-slowdown narrative caps them.
- **Dollar-inverse.** Commodities are priced in USD. A weakening dollar (DXY falling) is broadly bullish for metals; a strengthening dollar is a headwind. Overlay DXY inversely on your metals charts.
- **LME and global peers lead.** London Metal Exchange prices for aluminium, copper, zinc, and steel futures set the tone. Hindalco tracks LME aluminium and copper; Tata Steel tracks global steel spreads. The overnight LME move often pre-figures the Indian open.
- **High beta, sharp turns.** Metals make explosive multi-month runs and equally violent drops. They are the purest *momentum-and-cycle* trade — you ride the trend and respect that turns are sudden.

### Worked metals example (approximate reconstruction — verify on your charts)

China announces a large property-and-infrastructure stimulus. LME copper and aluminium gap up overnight; DXY softens. Hindalco, which had been basing at ~620 below a falling 50-DEMA, gaps to 655 and closes strong on huge volume — a base-breakout on a fundamental catalyst. The setup: **cyclical breakout on catalyst.** Enter on the breakout above the multi-week base high at **~650**, or on a controlled retest of it, stop below **615** (back inside the base), first target the prior swing high near **720**, then trail with the 20-DEMA as the trend runs. Confluence: LME copper/aluminium also breaking out, DXY rolling over, the whole Nifty Metal index gapping, and Tata Steel/JSW confirming. Because metals trend hard once a cycle turns, the right management is to hold with a trailing stop rather than take quick profits — the fat tail is the whole point of trading a cyclical. The mirror risk: if the China narrative reverses, exit fast; metals give back cyclical gains brutally.

## Energy: crude, policy, and the OMC/upstream/downstream split

Nifty Energy blends Reliance (dominant weight), ONGC, NTPC, Power Grid, Coal India, BPCL, IOC, and increasingly renewables/utilities. This is really several sub-sectors, and the split matters enormously.

- **Reliance is a market unto itself.** With its retail, Jio telecom, and O2C (oil-to-chemicals) segments, Reliance trades on its own narrative (retail IPO chatter, Jio tariff hikes, new-energy capex) as much as on crude. Given its Nifty heavyweight status, a Reliance breakout can single-handedly move the index — treat its chart as a top-line signal in itself.
- **Crude direction splits the sector.** *Upstream* (ONGC, Oil India) benefits from *rising* crude — higher realisations. *Downstream OMCs* (BPCL, IOC, HPCL) benefit from *falling/stable* crude and healthy marketing margins, and suffer when crude spikes (margin squeeze, subsidy risk). So the same crude move is bullish for one half of the energy basket and bearish for the other. Never trade "energy" as one thing.
- **Power/utilities (NTPC, Power Grid, Tata Power)** trade on capacity addition, PLFs, renewable transition, and act semi-defensively — steadier charts, dividend-driven, re-rating on the power-demand and green-energy theme.
- **Policy overhang.** Fuel-price deregulation, windfall taxes on upstream/refining, and subsidy decisions are recurring government levers that gap these stocks.

### Worked energy example (approximate reconstruction — verify on your charts)

Brent crude falls from ~$90 to ~$75 over a month. BPCL, an OMC, has been suppressed near ~580 on high-crude margin worries. As crude cools, marketing margins expand; BPCL breaks its downtrend line and reclaims the 50-DEMA at ~600 on rising volume. The setup: **downstream-OMC long on falling crude.** Enter on the 50-DEMA reclaim and downtrend-line break at **~605**, stop below **575** (below the reclaim and the recent swing low), target the prior consolidation high at **660** and then **700**. Confluence: Brent in a confirmed downtrend, HPCL and IOC showing the same reclaim (sector agreement), no pending windfall-tax headline. Simultaneously you would expect ONGC (upstream) to be *weak* on the same falling-crude backdrop — the intra-sector divergence is itself the confirmation that the crude read is correct and driving the sector as expected.

## The four setups you will reuse across these sectors

| Setup | Sector fit | Trigger | Stop | Target | Regime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Defensive RS hold | Pharma, FMCG | Stock holds support / rising while Nifty corrects; RS line turns up | Below key MA / support | Range-top breakout + measured move | Market risk-off |
| 200-DEMA reversion buy | FMCG (quality large-cap) | Tag of rising 200-DEMA with oversold weekly RSI | Decisive 200-DEMA loss on volume | Reversion to 50-DEMA, then prior high | Bull-market dip |
| Cyclical catalyst breakout | Metals, Energy (Reliance) | Base breakout on China/crude/DXY catalyst with volume | Back inside the base | Prior swing high, then trail 20-DEMA | Reflation / risk-on |
| Intra-sector divergence read | Energy (up vs down stream), Pharma (US vs domestic) | One sub-group confirms the macro driver, the other diverges | Per-trade level | Follow the confirming sub-group | Any |

## Confluence and cross-checks

- **Pharma:** always check the *USFDA/results calendar* before a hold; watch the Nifty Pharma RS line versus Nifty for the defensive-rotation tell; separate US-generics from domestic/CDMO.
- **FMCG:** watch input costs (palm oil, wheat, milk, crude-linked packaging), the monsoon narrative, and the interest-rate cycle (bond-proxy re-rating); respect that ranges are narrow — size positionally, not for intraday scalps.
- **Metals:** overlay LME base metals, DXY (inverse), and China macro/stimulus news; demand sector agreement across Tata Steel, JSW, Hindalco before trusting a move; hold winners with trailing stops because the cyclical tail is fat.
- **Energy:** decide *which* crude regime you are in and trade the correct sub-sector; treat Reliance's own chart as an independent index signal; watch for windfall-tax/subsidy policy headlines.

## Pitfalls

- *Trading a defensive like a momentum stock.* Chasing FMCG breakouts intraday is a slow bleed; these are positional, mean-reverting instruments.
- *Ignoring the event calendar in pharma.* A flawless setup is worthless the morning an import alert hits.
- *Treating metals as domestic.* If you are not watching China, LME, and the dollar, you are trading metals blind.
- *Lumping energy into one basket.* Going long "energy" on falling crude means you are simultaneously right on OMCs and wrong on upstream — pick the correct sub-sector.
- *Forgetting rotation.* These four sectors are how the market expresses fear (into pharma/FMCG) and greed (into metals/energy). Fighting the rotation — buying cyclicals in a risk-off tape or defensives in a raging reflation — is fighting the flows.

## Interview-ready summary

These four sectors span the defensive-cyclical spectrum and give the market its rotation. **Pharma** is news-fragile (USFDA can gap a stock overnight) and bifurcated into US-generics versus domestic/CDMO, but it shines as a defensive relative-strength play when the Nifty corrects — trade the RS hold, always checking the FDA/results calendar. **FMCG** is the lowest-beta, most mean-reverting basket — a bond-proxy sensitive to the monsoon, input costs, and the rate cycle — best traded positionally with 200-DEMA reversion buys, not intraday momentum. **Metals** are the purest global cyclical: China-, LME-, and dollar-driven, high-beta, trending hard once a cycle turns — trade catalyst-driven base breakouts and hold with trailing stops. **Energy** must be split by driver — rising crude helps upstream (ONGC) and hurts downstream OMCs (BPCL/IOC), falling crude does the reverse, utilities trade semi-defensively, and Reliance is a heavyweight narrative unto itself. Above all, read the defensive-versus-cyclical rotation ratio: it tells you the market's mood and which of these four playbooks the tape is currently rewarding.
