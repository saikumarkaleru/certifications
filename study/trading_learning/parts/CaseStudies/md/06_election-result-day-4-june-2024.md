# Case Study: Election Result Day — 4 June 2024

*Price levels here are approximate reconstructions of a real event; pull up the actual Nifty and Bank Nifty charts for 3–4 June 2024 to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

The 2024 Lok Sabha counting day is the cleanest textbook example of **event risk** the Indian market has handed retail traders in a decade. Two consecutive sessions delivered a euphoric gap-up and then the single largest one-day crash in Nifty's history to that point — followed, cruelly, by a full recovery to new highs within days. If you want to understand why professionals fear leverage around binary events, live inside these two candles.

## Case A — The exit-poll trap (Monday, 3 June 2024)

### The setup
Exit polls released after market close on Saturday 1 June projected a landslide NDA sweep (many houses called 350–400 seats). Markets had been grinding higher into the event on the "policy continuity" narrative. On Monday 3 June the market opened to price in a foregone conclusion.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Time | Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| Fri close (31 May) | ~22,530 | Nervous drift into results |
| Mon open (3 Jun) | ~23,180 | Gap-up on exit-poll euphoria |
| Mon 11:00 | ~23,320 | Fresh record high, PSU/infra stocks limit-up |
| Mon close | ~23,264 | +~3.3% day; Bank Nifty +~4% |

PSU stocks, defence, railways and capital-goods names went vertical — some hit upper circuits. Retail chatter was uniformly one-directional: "buy anything, 400 seats is coming."

### The trade
The typical retail action: chase the gap. A trader buys Nifty via ATM call options or takes leveraged long futures Monday afternoon at ~23,250, reasoning "the trend is up and results confirm it tomorrow." No hedge, full margin deployed, because "it's a sure thing." Position: say 5 lots Nifty futures (lot size 25 in 2024 for that period, ~₹29 lakh notional) on ₹5–6 lakh margin.

### What happened
Nothing bad — yet. Monday closed near the highs. The account showed a fat mark-to-market gain overnight. The trader felt validated and, dangerously, many *added* on Tuesday morning's open. The trap wasn't the loss; it was the confidence the green candle manufactured before the real event printed.

### The lesson
Exit polls are not results. The market on 3 June priced in the *most optimistic* interpretation of a *poll*, not a *count*. When price has already fully discounted the bull case before the actual catalyst, you are not buying opportunity — you are buying the top of a rumour with zero margin of safety. A professional treats an un-hedged, full-margin long into an unconfirmed binary as the worst risk-reward on the board.

## Case B — Counting day (Tuesday, 4 June 2024)

### The setup
Actual counting began 8:00 AM. Within the first hour, EC trends showed the NDA well short of exit-poll numbers and the BJP short of a single-party majority. The "landslide" priced in on Monday evaporated in real time.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Time | Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| Open (4 Jun) | ~23,180 | Flat-to-up, early trends still hazy |
| 09:45 | ~22,700 | Trends turn; selling begins |
| 10:45 | ~22,000 | Panic accelerates, PSU names lock lower |
| 12:30 | ~21,750 | Bank Nifty down ~7–8% |
| Intraday low | ~21,280 | ~ -8.5% from prior close; ~ -2,000 points |
| Close | ~21,884 | Closed off the lows, still ~ -5.9% |

Bank Nifty was worse, down roughly 8% intraday. PSU stocks that were upper-circuit on Monday were lower-circuit on Tuesday — a 15–20% swing in 24 hours. India VIX had already been elevated; realised volatility blew past what most option models priced.

### The trade — two traders, same day

**The leveraged retail long (Case A trader):** Entered Monday at ~23,250, 5 lots. Tuesday's low at ~21,280 is ~1,970 points against him = **₹1,970 × 25 × 5 ≈ ₹2.46 lakh** loss at the bottom, roughly **40–50% of margin gone**, with many receiving margin calls forcing liquidation *at the lows*. If he held short calls or naked options, the vol expansion compounded the damage.

**The disciplined trader:** Went into the event either flat or with a defined-risk hedge (long put / put spread bought when it was cheap on Monday's euphoria). On Tuesday he did nothing until the panic showed exhaustion. Around midday, with Nifty ~21,300–21,500 and the tape stabilising, he bought a **small** long position — say 2 lots — with a hard stop below 21,200 (risk ~₹5,000/point buffer) and no expectation of catching the exact low.

### What happened
Nifty closed 4 June at ~21,884, already ~600 points off the low. Over the next three sessions it recovered the entire crash and, within a week, printed **new all-time highs above 23,300**. The disciplined buyer's midday 2-lot long, held with a trailing stop, rode ~1,500–2,000 points of the snap-back over the following days — a clean multi-R winner *because the risk was defined and small*. The leveraged Monday long, if he survived the margin call, spent the recovery just getting back to breakeven; if he was force-liquidated at 21,300, he ate the full loss and missed the rebound entirely.

### The lesson
The move that destroyed accounts and the move that made them was the *same 2,000-point range* — the difference was entirely **position sizing, leverage, and when you acted**. Retail lost money not because they were wrong about India's long-term direction (the market hit new highs within days) but because leverage converted a temporary drawdown into a permanent, forced loss. You can be directionally right and still be wiped out if size and timing are wrong.

The professional's edge on 4 June wasn't prediction — nobody knew the seat count. It was **survival architecture**: no leverage into the binary, a cheap hedge bought during euphoria, and dry powder to buy the panic *after* it showed signs of exhaustion rather than trying to catch the falling knife at 22,000.

## Transferable rules
- **Never carry un-hedged, full-margin leverage into a binary event.** The gap can exceed your stop; margin calls liquidate you at the worst price.
- **A rumour rally (exit polls) is not the catalyst (results).** When price has already discounted the best case, the risk-reward of chasing is terrible.
- **Buy panic only after exhaustion, small and with a hard stop** — never average into a crash with size on the way down.
- **Position sizing, not prediction, is the survival variable.** The same range wiped out the leveraged and enriched the disciplined.
- **Keep dry powder for the event, not just a view.** Cash is a position — it's what lets you act when the panic finally hands you a price.
