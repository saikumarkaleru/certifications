# Case Study: Riding a Real Multi-Month Trend

*Price levels below are approximate reconstructions of a real event; pull up the actual chart to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

Everyone can find a chart that went up 300%. Almost nobody actually held it. This chapter is about the boring, hard skill that separates people who *watch* trends from people who *earn* from them: buying a real base breakout, adding on pullbacks, and — the part that hurts — sitting still for months while a trailing rule, not your emotions, decides when you leave.

We'll walk two real Indian trends from the 2023–24 capex/PSU/defence bull run.

---

## Case 1 — Bharat Electronics (BEL): the defence-capex trend of 2023–24

### The setup

**Instrument:** Bharat Electronics Ltd (BEL), NSE cash / futures.
**Period:** roughly March 2023 through mid-2024.
**Context:** After the Union Budgets of 2023 and 2024 kept pushing defence indigenisation ("Atmanirbhar Bharat"), defence PSUs went from ignored value traps to the market's favourite theme. BEL had a fat order book, was actually converting it to profit, and — crucially for a trend trader — was *already* moving up before the crowd noticed. (Note: BEL did a 2:1 bonus in 2024, so old charts show pre-bonus prices roughly double; I use post-adjustment levels below for consistency.)

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level (adj.) | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Mar–Apr 2023 | ₹95–100 | Sideways 8–10 weeks, tightening range, volume drying up |
| Breakout | May 2023 | ₹105 | Closes above range on a big volume day |
| Pullback 1 | Jun 2023 | ₹110 → ₹102 | Retests the breakout zone, holds the 50-DMA |
| Trend leg | Jul–Dec 2023 | ₹110 → ₹150 | Series of higher highs / higher lows, never closes below 50-DMA |
| Pullback 2 | Jan 2024 | ₹150 → ₹138 | Sharp 3-day dip, bounces off rising 50-DMA |
| Blow-off | Feb–Jul 2024 | ₹150 → ₹330 | Parabolic, angle steepens, gap-ups |
| Trend break | Aug 2024 | ₹330 → closes below 50-DMA | First decisive weekly close under the trailing line |

### The trade

- **Entry (initial):** ₹106 on the May 2023 breakout retest, 1 lot of futures — assume lot size ~475 shares (illustrative), so ~₹50,000 notional at that price, but margin ~₹15,000. To keep it clean, treat this as a **cash position of 500 shares at ₹106 = ₹53,000**.
- **Add 1:** 300 shares at ₹104 on the June pullback that held the 50-DMA.
- **Add 2:** 200 shares at ₹140 after the January 2024 higher-low held.
- **Total position:** 1,000 shares, average cost ≈ ₹115.
- **Initial stop:** ₹96 (below the base). Risk on first tranche ≈ (106 − 96) × 500 = **₹5,000**, i.e. 1R.
- **Trailing rule (decided in advance):** exit everything on the **first weekly close below the 50-day moving average.** No target — trends don't have targets, they have endings.

### What happened

The 50-DMA stop got trailed up week after week. It sat around ₹128 in early 2024, ₹210 by spring, ₹300 by summer. The August 2024 weekly close broke below it near **₹315**.

- Exit: 1,000 shares at ~₹312.
- Gross P&L: (312 − 115) × 1,000 = **₹1,97,000**.
- Costs (2026, delivery): STT 0.1% each side, brokerage (flat/discount ~₹20 or 0.03%), exchange + GST + stamp — round the round-trip drag to roughly ₹700–900 on this turnover. Net ≈ **₹1,96,000** on capital deployed of about ₹1.15 lakh.
- **R-multiple: ~39R** on the initial risk. Fifteen months, one instrument, four decisions.

### The lesson

The professional's edge here was *doing almost nothing.* The entire return came from four actions across fifteen months and then the discipline to hold through two scary pullbacks — each of which felt, in the moment, like "the top."

The typical retail trader took the same breakout at ₹105… and sold at ₹118 for a "nice 12%." Then watched ₹150, ₹220, ₹330 go by, either sitting out or repeatedly buying and getting shaken out on the pullbacks they'd have held if they had a *rule* instead of a *feeling.* Booking a small win early feels responsible; in a real trend it is the single most expensive habit you have.

## Case 2 — REC Ltd: the PSU-financier leg, and knowing when it ended

### The setup

**Instrument:** REC Ltd (power-sector financier PSU).
**Period:** mid-2023 to mid-2024, then the reversal.
**Context:** The same PSU re-rating wave lifted power financiers REC and PFC. Cheap valuations, rising dividends, government capex tailwind. REC ran from the ₹100s to well over ₹500 (split/adjusted levels vary — verify on your chart). This case matters because it also shows the *end*: PSUs peaked around the mid-2024 election result week and many gave back 30–40%.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breakout | Jun 2023 | ₹135 | Clears a multi-year resistance shelf |
| Trend | Jul 2023–Apr 2024 | ₹135 → ₹450 | Clean 50-DMA trend, textbook higher-lows |
| Climax | May 2024 | ₹450 → ₹620 | Vertical into election optimism |
| Reversal | 4 Jun 2024 | ₹620 → ₹500 intraday | Election-result day: huge red candle, closes below 20-DMA |
| Confirmation | Jun 2024 | breaks 50-DMA | Trailing stop triggers |

### The trade

- **Entry:** ₹140, 700 shares = ₹98,000, on the June 2023 breakout.
- **Stop:** ₹122 (below breakout). Risk = (140 − 122) × 700 = **₹12,600 = 1R.**
- **Trailing rule:** exit on first close below the 50-DMA (same rule, different stock).

### What happened

The 4 June 2024 counting-day crash was a genuine one-day −19% event on many PSUs. REC's huge red candle didn't *quite* close below the 50-DMA that day, but the follow-through days did, near **₹500**.

- Exit: 700 shares at ~₹498.
- Gross P&L: (498 − 140) × 700 = **₹2,50,600.**
- Net after 2026 costs ≈ **₹2,49,700.**
- **R-multiple: ~19.8R.**

Notice: the trailing rule gave back roughly ₹120/share from the ₹620 peak. That "lost" ₹84,000 is the *price of the rule* — and it's the correct price. Nobody sells the exact top; you sell the *confirmation of the end.*

### The lesson

The professional accepts that a mechanical trailing exit will always leave money on the table at the top and will occasionally exit you on a false break. That is the fee you pay to *never* ride a winner all the way back to breakeven. The retail trader who had no exit rule watched REC round-trip from ₹620 back toward ₹350–400 in the correction, converting a 4-bagger into "why didn't I sell."

---

## Transferable rules

- **The money is in the sitting, not the picking.** A great entry with a weak-hearted exit underperforms an average entry held to the trailing signal.
- **Decide the exit before you enter, and make it mechanical** (e.g. weekly close below the 50-DMA). A rule survives greed and fear; an opinion does not.
- **Add only on pullbacks that confirm the trend** (higher low holding a rising MA), never on breakouts into thin air — and never average a position that's *falling.*
- **A trailing stop is designed to give back the top.** Judge it by the whole trend captured, not by the ticks surrendered at the end.
- **One clean trend, held properly, can out-earn a hundred scalps** — and costs you far less in brokerage, taxes, and stress.
