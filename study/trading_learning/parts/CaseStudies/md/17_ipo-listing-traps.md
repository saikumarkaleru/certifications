# Case Study: IPO & Listing Traps (Paytm, LIC)

*Price levels below are approximate reconstructions of a real event; pull up the actual chart to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

The IPO is the one trade where the entire retail crowd, the financial media, and the company's own marketing budget are all pushing you the *same* direction on the *same* day. That should make you suspicious, not excited. This chapter dissects two of the most instructive listings in recent Indian history — Paytm and LIC — where "India's biggest IPO" hype collided with the arithmetic of valuation and the mechanics of who's actually selling to you on listing day.

---

## Case 1 — Paytm (One97 Communications): Nov 2021

### The setup

**Instrument:** One97 Communications (Paytm), NSE/BSE.
**Date:** IPO priced at **₹2,150** (upper band); listed **18 November 2021.**
**Context:** ₹18,300 crore issue — the largest Indian IPO at the time — into a euphoric, liquidity-drunk market at the tail of the 2021 tech-and-startup mania. The catch: Paytm was deeply loss-making, the issue was priced at a valuation that implied years of flawless execution, and much of the offer was an *offer-for-sale* (early investors cashing out) rather than fresh growth capital. The "grey market premium" (GMP) chatter beforehand suggested a strong listing. It was wrong.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date/time | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issue price | 8–10 Nov 2021 | ₹2,150 | Upper band, "apply for listing gains" |
| Listing | 18 Nov, open | ₹1,950 | Opens ~9% *below* issue — instant red |
| Day 1 close | 18 Nov | ₹1,560 | Closes ~**−27%** from issue |
| Day 2 | 19 Nov | ₹1,360 | Another leg down, panic |
| Grind | Nov 2021 → 2022 | ₹1,360 → ₹500s | Steady bleed through the year |
| Trough | late 2022 | ~₹440–500 | Roughly −75%+ from issue |

### The "trade" (the retail trap)

The hype-buyer's playbook and its cost:

- **Applied for listing gains** on GMP rumours: got allotment, "sure-shot profit."
- **Held through day 1** expecting the dip to recover: −27% by close.
- **"Averaged" on day 2** at ₹1,360 to lower cost: added to a name in free-fall.

Suppose 6 shares allotted @ ₹2,150 (~₹12,900), then buy 10 more @ ₹1,360 (₹13,600). Total 16 shares, cost ₹26,500, avg ≈ ₹1,656. Mark at ₹500:

- Value: 16 × ₹500 = **₹8,000.**
- Loss: **−₹18,500 (−70%).**

### The lesson

**GMP is a rumour, not a valuation.** The grey market is a thin, unregulated sentiment gauge that evaporates the instant real supply hits on listing day. On day one, the sellers are institutions and anchor investors who bought lower and pre-IPO holders taking profit — the informed money is *distributing to* the excited retail buyer. A loss-making company priced for perfection has no valuation floor to catch a falling price. The professional's stance on a richly-valued, OFS-heavy IPO is simple: *there's no rush.* Let it list, let it find a real base, let the numbers prove out over two or three quarters. You can always buy a good business later; you can't un-buy a bad entry.

## Case 2 — LIC: May 2022

### The setup

**Instrument:** Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC).
**Date:** IPO priced ~**₹949** (upper band; retail/policyholder discounts applied); listed **17 May 2022.**
**Context:** The government's mega-divestment — pitched to millions of first-time and policyholder investors as a patriotic, "can't-lose" blue-chip. But it listed into a *falling* market (2022's global rate-hike, risk-off tape), embedded-value debates dogged the pricing, and the float dynamics plus a general de-rating of the stock worked against it. A "safe" name is not a safe *entry* if the price is wrong for the tape.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issue price | May 2022 | ₹949 | "India's most trusted brand" |
| Listing | 17 May, open | ₹867 | Lists ~**8–9% below** issue |
| Day 1 close | 17 May | ₹875 | Red from day one |
| Drift | mid–late 2022 | ₹875 → ₹650 | Slow grind lower |
| Trough | early 2023 | ~₹530–600 | ~−40% from issue |

### The "trade"

- Retail applicant gets allotment @ ~₹904 (after ₹45 retail discount), 16 shares ≈ ₹14,464.
- "It's LIC, it'll recover" — holds, even adds 16 @ ₹700 (₹11,200). Total 32 shares, ₹25,664, avg ≈ ₹802.
- Mark at ₹580: value 32 × ₹580 = **₹18,560** → loss **−₹7,100 (−28%).**

Less catastrophic than Paytm — but the *lesson is identical*: a brand-name, "safe," heavily-marketed listing still handed a patient-seeming investor a sustained loss because the **entry price and the market context were wrong.**

### The lesson

Two anchor-unwind mechanics matter after any big IPO. First, **the anchor/lock-in unwind:** anchor investors who bought pre-listing have a 30-day (and staggered 90-day) lock-in; when it lifts, fresh supply can press the price — a known post-listing air-pocket. Second, **the government/promoter overhang:** a large state seller or future divestment tranches cap upside. "Trusted brand" and "good long-term company" are statements about the *business*; your P&L is a statement about the *price you paid and when.* The two are not the same, and IPO marketing is engineered to make you forget that.

## Listing-day euphoria vs post-listing reality

The structural asymmetry every IPO trader must internalise:

- **The seller is informed, the buyer is excited.** Anchors, pre-IPO VCs/PE, and promoters know their cost and the fair value far better than a listing-day retail buyer chasing GMP.
- **Marketing budget ≠ margin of safety.** The louder the "biggest IPO ever" drumbeat, the more the price already reflects the hype.
- **Listing-day price is the most emotional price the stock will ever print.** Real bases form weeks or months later, once anchor lock-ins expire and a couple of results are on the tape.

---

## Transferable rules

- **GMP is sentiment, not analysis.** Never size a position on grey-market premium; it vanishes the moment real supply lists.
- **A great company can be a terrible listing-day trade** — the variable that determines your P&L is *entry price*, not brand quality.
- **Never average down a broken listing in the first sessions.** Day-1/day-2 weakness on a richly-priced OFS issue tends to continue, not reverse.
- **Wait for the anchor lock-in to unwind and for a real base to form.** There is no prize for buying on day one; the good businesses are still buyable months later at saner prices.
- **When the crowd, the media, and the issuer all point the same way on the same day, assume you're the one being sold to** — and demand a valuation floor before you commit capital.
