# Case Study: Budget & Policy Days (RBI/Fed)

*Price levels here are approximate reconstructions of real events; pull up the actual Nifty and Bank Nifty charts for the dates below to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

Scheduled event days — the Union Budget, RBI monetary policy, and US Fed decisions — are the most reliable **option-buyer traps** on the calendar. Everyone knows the date in advance, so implied volatility gets bid up into the event; then the event passes and volatility collapses, often gutting option buyers *even when they get the direction right*. This chapter walks three dated examples of how event days reward patience and punish the crowd that buys premium hoping for a lottery ticket.

## Case A — The pro-growth Budget rip (1 February 2021)

### The setup
The Union Budget 2021 landed with the economy clawing out of COVID. The market had been nervous; option markets priced a big move. The Finance Minister delivered a capex-heavy, no-new-tax, pro-growth budget — the market's dream scenario — and the tape exploded.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Time (1 Feb 2021) | Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|-------------------|----------------|----------------|
| Prev close (29 Jan) | ~13,635 | Weak, jittery into the event |
| Open | ~13,760 | Mildly positive |
| 11:00–13:00 | ~14,150 | Speech turns bullish; banks/infra rip |
| Close | ~14,281 | **+~4.7% day**; Bank Nifty +~8% |

This was a rare day where the directional bet paid *and* the move was big enough to beat the vol crush. But notice: the biggest, safest gains were in **buying the reaction** — cash equities and futures that simply rode the confirmed trend — not in pre-positioning a guess.

### The trade — buyer vs reactor
**The pre-event option buyer** bought a straddle (ATM call + put) on 29 Jan for, say, ~₹250 combined premium, betting on "a big move either way." IV was already elevated. Even on this +4.7% day, the call side gained but the put side went to near-zero, and the *net* straddle profit was modest because he paid up for both legs into inflated IV.

**The reactor** waited. He did nothing during the speech. Once the budget's pro-growth thrust was clear and Nifty broke and *held* above ~13,950 with volume, he went long futures at ~14,000 with a stop at ~13,850 (risk ~150 points). He rode to the close near 14,280 — a clean ~2R with the direction *confirmed*, not guessed, and with IV crush working in the underlying's favour rather than against his premium.

### The lesson
Even on the best possible budget outcome, buying pre-event options is a poor bet because you overpay for inflated IV. The reactor who waits for the market to *reveal* direction and then rides confirmed momentum gets a better risk-reward with none of the vol-crush drag.

## Case B — The "sell the news" neutral budget (1 February 2020)

### The setup
Budget 2020, delivered just before COVID, was long, technical, and offered no big-bang stimulus. The market had rallied *into* it on hope. Reality disappointed the bulls.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Time (1 Feb 2020) | Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|-------------------|----------------|----------------|
| Prev close | ~11,962 | Priced in optimism |
| During speech | ~11,850 → 11,650 | Hope fades as speech drags |
| Intraday low | ~11,600 | Steady bleed |
| Close | ~11,662 | **~ -2.5% day** |

Here the crowd was positioned long on "budget hope." The event delivered nothing, and the market **sold the news** — the classic pattern where a rally into a known event unwinds once the event removes the uncertainty.

### The trade
The option *seller* who understood IV crush sold expensive premium before the budget and profited from both the vol collapse and the modest move. The retail *call buyer* who bet on "budget rally" lost twice: wrong direction *and* vol crush. The lesson repeats — being positioned into the event on the crowded side is where money is lost.

## Case C — The RBI / Fed whipsaw

### The setup — an RBI policy surprise
On several RBI decision days (e.g., surprise pause or a hawkish-cut cocktail), Bank Nifty is the amplifier. A representative pattern: RBI holds rates but the *commentary* is more hawkish than expected. Bank Nifty spikes up on the "no hike" headline, then reverses hard on the tone during the Governor's statement.

### Price-action walkthrough (representative RBI day)

| Time | Bank Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|------|---------------------|----------------|
| Pre-decision | ~44,000 | Coiled, low volume |
| 10:00 headline | ~44,350 | Knee-jerk pop on "rates unchanged" |
| 10:20 commentary | ~43,700 | Hawkish tone; the pop is sold |
| Close | ~43,850 | Round-trip whipsaw |

### The trade — the whipsaw trap
The retail trader buys a Bank Nifty ATM call at the 10:00 headline pop (~44,350), paying peak IV. Twenty minutes later price is back at 43,700 *and* IV has collapsed post-decision — the call loses on delta and vega simultaneously. This is the Fed-day pattern too: the first move after a policy statement is frequently faked out by the press conference or the fine print, and option premium bleeds the entire time.

The professional's play: **do not trade the headline spike.** Let the knee-jerk and the reversal both print. Once the day settles into a direction — say Bank Nifty breaks back below 43,700 and holds after the commentary — take a *directional* position in the future or a *spread* (not a naked long option) so IV crush doesn't work against you.

### The lesson
Scheduled events compress a week of information into minutes, and the first reaction is routinely wrong. Naked option buyers get hit by the double whammy of a whipsawing underlying and collapsing IV. Waiting 20–30 minutes for the *reaction to the reaction* costs you the first move but saves you from the trap that catches the crowd.

## Transferable rules
- **Implied volatility is elevated into every scheduled event and collapses right after.** Buying naked options pre-event means overpaying — you can be right on direction and still lose.
- **Trade the reaction, not the prediction.** Let the market reveal direction, then ride confirmed momentum with a defined stop.
- **A rally into a known event often "sells the news."** Beware being positioned on the crowded, hopeful side going in.
- **On policy days, the first move is frequently faked.** The press conference / fine print reverses the headline — wait for the second move.
- **If you must use options around events, use spreads, not naked longs**, so vol crush works with you (or is neutralised) instead of against you.
