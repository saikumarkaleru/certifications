# Gap Days: Gap-Fill vs Gap-and-Go

*Price levels here are approximate reconstructions of real events; pull up the actual chart to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

A gap is the market shouting at you before the first candle even forms. Overnight — a big US move, a domestic policy surprise, a heavyweight's results after hours — new information arrives while the exchange is shut, and the open simply *jumps* to a new level. The question every gap poses is binary: does the gap **fill** (price reverses back toward yesterday's close, rewarding the fade) or **go** (price continues in the gap's direction, rewarding the continuation)? Getting this call right is worth a fortune; getting it reflexively wrong — "gaps always fill," a dangerous half-truth — is how traders fight one-way trends all morning. The decision rule is not the size of the gap but the **cause behind it and the behaviour of the first 30 minutes.** Two dated cases, one of each outcome.

## The decision framework

Before the cases, the read that separates a fade from a continuation:

- **What caused the gap?** A gap driven by a *genuine change in fundamentals* (strong earnings, a policy shift, a decisive global risk move) tends to **go** — the information is real and demands repricing. A gap driven by *sentiment or an overnight overreaction* with no domestic follow-through tends to **fill.**
- **Where did it gap relative to structure?** A gap that clears a prior resistance/support level and *holds above/below it* is a continuation tell. A gap into the middle of a prior range, with nothing behind it, invites a fade.
- **The first 30 minutes — the decider.** If the market *extends* the gap and the first pullback holds, it is a gap-and-go: trade continuation. If the market immediately starts *bleeding back toward the prior close* in the first candles, the gap is filling: trade the fade.

You do not guess at 09:15. You let the opening range and the first pullback tell you, then act.

## Case 1 — Nifty gap-and-go on a decisive US move (approx. 12 Nov 2024)

**The setup.** A powerful overnight US session (a sharp risk-on rally, GIFT Nifty strongly higher) sets up a large gap-up. Crucially the cause was a *genuine global risk move*, not a domestic sentiment blip — the kind of gap that tends to go. Nifty opened well above the prior close and above a prior resistance shelf.

**Price-action walkthrough.** (Approximate 5-minute levels; prior close ≈ 23,880.)

| Time | Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| 09:15 | 24,060 | Gaps up ~180 pts, above prior resistance ~24,000 |
| 09:15–09:30 | 24,030–24,110 | Opening range holds *above* the prior close and resistance |
| 09:45 | 24,050 | First pullback holds well above the gap origin — the go tell |
| 10:00–13:00 | 24,110→24,280 | Continuation, orderly higher |
| 15:15 | ~24,310 | Closes near the high; gap never filled |

The decisive evidence at 09:45: the pullback held far above yesterday's close. A gap that is going to fill sags back toward the prior close early; this one refused.

**The trade — continuation.** Long on the 5-min close above the opening-range high 24,110 at ~**24,120**, stop below the pullback low and prior resistance at **24,000** (120-pt risk; Nifty lot = 25 → ₹3,000 = 1R). After the pullback held, stop raised to breakeven. Trailed and exited into the close near **24,290**: +170 pts × 25 = ₹4,250; after costs (≈ ₹200) ≈ **+₹4,050, about +1.35R.**

**The lesson.** The professional identified a *fundamental* cause and a gap that held above structure, so played continuation and refused the reflexive fade. The retail trader shorted the open "because gaps fill," fought a one-way tape, added to the loser, and either stopped out red or held a painful short all day. The rule: **a gap on real information that holds above prior resistance is a go, not a fade** — the "gaps fill" adage applies to sentiment gaps, not information gaps.

## Case 2 — Reliance results-gap that filled (approx. a quarterly results Friday)

**The setup.** A heavyweight — Reliance Industries — reports quarterly results after hours; the stock gaps up at the open on a headline that looks good. But the move is a *sentiment reaction to a headline*, and as the market digests the actual numbers (margins, debt, guidance) the enthusiasm fades. A gap driven by a first-glance headline, not a decisive fundamental beat, is prone to filling. (Levels reconstructed; the *behaviour* — pop then bleed back to prior close — is the classic results-gap fade.)

**Price-action walkthrough.** (Approximate levels; prior close ≈ ₹2,940.)

| Time | Reliance (approx) | What happened |
|------|------------------|---------------|
| 09:15 | 2,995 | Gaps up ~₹55 on the results headline |
| 09:15–09:25 | 2,985–3,005 | Spikes to 3,005, then stalls — no follow-through buyers |
| 09:35 | 2,975 | Starts bleeding back toward prior close — the fill tell |
| 10:00 | 2,960 | Breaks the opening-range low, sellers in control |
| 12:00–14:00 | 2,948→2,935 | Grinds down, fills the gap to prior close ~2,940 |
| 15:15 | ~2,932 | Closes at/below prior close — gap fully filled |

The tell at 09:35: instead of extending, the stock sagged back toward 2,940 — buyers who chased the pop were already underwater.

**The trade — the fade.** Short on the break of the opening-range low at **2,975**, stop above the opening spike high at **3,010** (₹35 risk). Reliance F&O lot ≈ 250, so risk = 35 × 250 = **₹8,750 = 1R** (size accordingly to your capital — this is illustrative). Target the prior close / gap-fill at ~2,940. Covered near **2,942**: +₹33 × 250 = ₹8,250; after costs (≈ ₹300) ≈ **+₹7,950, about +0.9R.** A patient runner to 2,932 would have added a little more.

**The lesson.** The professional read the *cause* (a headline pop, not a decisive fundamental beat) and the *behaviour* (spike, stall, bleed) and faded toward the obvious magnet — the prior close. The retail trader did the opposite of Case 1's retail error: here they *chased the gap-up*, bought the 3,005 spike expecting a "results rally," and watched it fill all the way down. The symmetry is the whole point: in Case 1 the fade was wrong; here the chase was wrong. **Neither the fade nor the go is a rule — the cause and the first-30-minute behaviour decide which game you are in.**

## The core lesson

Two gaps, opposite plays, one framework. The Nifty gap ran because it was information-driven and held above structure; the Reliance gap filled because it was a headline reaction that stalled and bled. The trader who hardwires "gaps fill" gets destroyed by Case 1; the trader who hardwires "gaps go" gets destroyed by Case 2. The edge is the *diagnosis* — cause plus first-half-hour behaviour — not a fixed bias.

## Transferable rules

- Never pre-decide fade or go by gap size — judge the *cause* (information vs sentiment) and the first 30 minutes of behaviour.
- Information-driven gaps that hold above/below prior structure tend to go: trade continuation on the opening-range break and trail.
- Headline/sentiment gaps that spike, stall, and bleed back toward the prior close tend to fill: fade toward the prior close as your magnet.
- Let the first pullback (or first sag) confirm the call before committing — the open itself is a question, not an answer.
- "Gaps fill" is a half-truth that applies to sentiment gaps only; applying it to a real-information gap means fighting a one-way trend all day.
