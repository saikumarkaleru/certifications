# Case Study: Falling-Knife Disasters (YES Bank, DHFL, PC Jeweller)

*Price levels below are approximate reconstructions of a real event; pull up the actual chart to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

Some of the largest, most permanent losses in Indian retail portfolios came not from a bad trade but from a bad *instinct*: "it's fallen so much, it's cheap now." This chapter is the graveyard tour — three real stocks that went from market darlings to near-zero, and the exact psychological trap ("averaging down") that turned a −40% into a −95%. None of these were obscure penny stocks at the top; they were widely held, index-adjacent names that "everyone owned." That is precisely why they were so dangerous.

---

## Case 1 — YES Bank: from ₹400 to single digits (2018–2020)

### The setup

**Instrument:** YES Bank, NSE cash — one of India's most widely held retail stocks.
**Period:** peak ~August 2018 (~₹400, split-adjusted terms vary) to the March 2020 moratorium (₹5–16).
**Context:** YES Bank was the growth-bank story of the 2010s. Then the RBI declined to extend promoter Rana Kapoor's term (Sept 2018), asset-quality skeletons (hidden bad loans / divergences) tumbled out, and confidence in the loan book collapsed. This was not a dip — it was a solvency question.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak | Aug 2018 | ₹400 | All-time high, "buy the leader" |
| First shock | Sep 2018 | ₹400 → ₹180 | RBI cuts CEO tenure; "cheap now!" begins |
| Grind down | 2019 | ₹180 → ₹40 | NPA divergences, capital-raise fears, each bounce sold |
| Capitulation | early 2020 | ₹40 → ₹20 | Survival in doubt |
| Moratorium | 6 Mar 2020 | ₹16 → ₹5 | RBI moratorium, reconstruction, equity near-wiped |

### The "trade" (the trap)

The falling-knife buyer didn't take *one* trade — they took *many*, averaging down:

- Buy 500 @ ₹300 (Sept 2018) = ₹1,50,000 — "50% off the top, bargain."
- Add 500 @ ₹180 = ₹90,000 — "even cheaper."
- Add 1,000 @ ₹90 = ₹90,000 — "it can't go lower."
- Add 2,000 @ ₹40 = ₹80,000 — "averaging my cost down."
- **Total: 4,000 shares, ₹4,10,000 invested, avg ≈ ₹102.**

### What happened

Post-moratorium the stock sat near ₹5–15. Mark the position at ₹12:

- Value: 4,000 × ₹12 = **₹48,000.**
- Loss: **−₹3,62,000 (about −88%).**

There was never a stop. Each average-down *felt* like lowering risk; it was *adding* risk to a thesis that was actively disintegrating.

### The lesson

Averaging down works for a diversified value investor buying a *solvent* business at a lower price. It is lethal on a stock where the core question is *"does the equity survive?"* When the news is structural (fraud, hidden NPAs, solvency), a lower price is not a discount — it is the market pricing in a higher probability of zero.

## Case 2 — DHFL: from ~₹600 to near-zero (2018–2019)

### The setup

**Instrument:** Dewan Housing Finance (DHFL), NSE cash.
**Period:** ~₹600+ in 2018 to near-zero / delisting-territory in 2019–21.
**Context:** DHFL got hit by the post-IL&FS NBFC liquidity crisis (Sept 2018), then by January 2019 media allegations of fund diversion. On **1 Feb 2019** the stock fell ~**60% intraday** on those allegations. This one had *both* signals flashing red at once: price *and* news.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-crisis | Sep 2018 | ₹610 | NBFC darling |
| IL&FS contagion | Sep 2018 | ₹610 → ₹350 | One-day ~−40% liquidity scare |
| Dead-cat bounce | late 2018 | ₹350 → ₹250 | "Recovery" that wasn't |
| Fraud allegation | 1 Feb 2019 | ₹250 → ₹100 | ~−60% intraday |
| Terminal | 2019–20 | ₹100 → ₹10 → NCLT | Default, insolvency, equity wiped |

### The "trade"

- Buy 1,000 @ ₹350 after the "40% off" IL&FS crash = ₹3,50,000.
- Add 1,000 @ ₹200 = ₹2,00,000.
- Add 2,000 @ ₹100 (post-allegation "surely oversold") = ₹2,00,000.
- **4,000 shares, ₹7,50,000, avg ₹187.**

### What happened

DHFL went into insolvency (NCLT); equity holders were effectively wiped out. Mark at ₹15:

- Value: 4,000 × ₹15 = **₹60,000.**
- Loss: **−₹6,90,000 (about −92%).**

### The lesson

The **1 Feb 2019 −60% day was the exit, not the entry.** A single-session collapse on a *specific credibility/fraud allegation* is the market telling you the risk is now binary. The professional's rule: when price breaks *and* the news is about solvency or fraud, you don't buy the dip — you make sure you're out. "It's already down 60%, how much lower can it go?" The answer, mathematically, is always: another 100%.

## Case 3 — PC Jeweller: the −90%+ slow bleed

### The setup

**Instrument:** PC Jeweller, NSE cash.
**Period:** peak ~₹600 (early 2018) to ₹10–20 within a couple of years — a >90% destruction.
**Context:** PC Jeweller cracked in 2018 on a cancelled buyback, promoter-pledge and related-party concerns, and collapsing confidence. Unlike a one-day event, this was a *staircase down* — each step luring in "value" buyers.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak | early 2018 | ₹600 | High-flyer |
| First crack | Feb–May 2018 | ₹600 → ₹150 | Buyback cancelled, pledge worries |
| Lower highs | 2018–19 | ₹150 → ₹60 | Every bounce a lower high |
| Terminal drift | 2019–20 | ₹60 → ₹15 | Confidence gone |

### The "trade"

- Buy 2,000 @ ₹150, add 3,000 @ ₹60, add 5,000 @ ₹25 — ₹5,25,000 in, 10,000 shares, avg ₹52.50.
- Mark at ₹18: value ₹1,80,000 → loss **−₹3,45,000 (−66% and still falling).**

### The lesson

The staircase is more dangerous than the cliff because it *repeatedly rewards* the averaging-down instinct with small bounces before the next leg down. Lower highs and lower lows are a completed sentence: the trend is down until *price itself* proves otherwise (a real base, a higher high on volume) — not until it "feels cheap."

---

## Why both signals matter: price AND news

In all three, the two independent alarm systems agreed:

- **Price structure:** lower highs, lower lows, closes below every support, no base.
- **News/fundamentals:** solvency, fraud, promoter-pledge, hidden NPAs — *structural*, not cyclical.

When *both* break, a lower price is not value; it's the market discounting a rising probability of permanent capital loss. The retail error is to treat one as offsetting the other ("bad news, but look how cheap!"). They compound, they don't cancel.

---

## Transferable rules

- **Never average down a falling knife.** Adding to a loser that's making lower lows is adding risk while telling yourself you're reducing it.
- **A single-session −30% to −60% on stock-specific fraud/solvency news is an exit signal, not a buy signal.** "How much lower can it go" always has the same answer: −100%.
- **Demand confirmation from price *and* news.** A cheap valuation on a broken chart with structural bad news is a value trap, not a value buy.
- **A downtrend is intact until price proves otherwise** — a real base and a higher high on volume — not until it *feels* oversold.
- **Position for zero.** If a name *can* go to zero (leverage, fraud risk, solvency doubt), size so that its going to zero doesn't end your account — and use a stop, because there is no floor under a knife.
