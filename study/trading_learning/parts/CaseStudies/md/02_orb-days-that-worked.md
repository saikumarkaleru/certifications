# Opening Range Breakout Days That Worked

*Price levels here are approximate reconstructions of real events; pull up the actual chart to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

The Opening Range Breakout (ORB) is the simplest trend-following idea on an intraday chart: let the market print its first 15 to 30 minutes, mark the high and low of that range, and trade the direction it breaks with conviction. It works because the opening range is where overnight information gets priced in and where the day's buyers and sellers first test each other. When one side wins decisively, the losers are trapped and must cover — and that fuel drives a trend day. The two cases below are days when the break was real and ran. The next chapter shows you the days it lied. Study both, because the setup looks identical at the moment of entry — the difference is context.

## Case 1 — Bank Nifty, budget-day trend run (approx. 1 Feb 2025)

**The setup.** Union Budget day. Bank Nifty is the most budget-sensitive index on the exchange — banking, tax, and capex announcements hit it directly — so volatility is guaranteed; the only question is direction. Overnight cues were constructive and GIFT Nifty pointed to a firm open. The market gapped up modestly and, crucially, the Budget speech that morning carried no nasty surprise for banks. When a feared event passes cleanly, trapped bears cover — a classic trend-day ignition.

**Price-action walkthrough.** (Approximate 5-minute levels.)

| Time | Bank Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|------|--------------------|---------------|
| 09:15 | 48,650 | Opens up, first candle strong green |
| 09:15–09:45 | 48,600–48,900 | Opening 30-min range builds; high 48,900, low 48,600 |
| 10:05 | 48,920 | 5-min candle closes above range high on rising volume |
| 10:05–12:30 | 48,900→49,450 | Steady stair-step higher, shallow pullbacks hold above prior highs |
| 12:30–14:30 | 49,450→49,780 | Trend continues, no lower-low all session |
| 15:15 | 49,820 | Closes near the high of the day |

The tell of a trend day: every pullback was shallow and bought, and the index never printed a lower low after the breakout. Price stayed pinned to the upper half of its range all day.

**The trade.** Entry on the 5-minute close above 48,900, filled around **48,920**. Stop below the range mid and the breakout candle low, at **48,720** — a 200-point risk. Target: trail, don't fix, on a trend day. Position size one lot (Bank Nifty lot = 15). Risk in ₹ = 200 × 15 = **₹3,000 = 1R.**

**What happened.** Rather than a fixed target, the stop was trailed under each new 15-minute swing low. It was never seriously threatened. The position was closed into the strong close near **49,780**, an 860-point gain. Gross = 860 × 15 = ₹12,900. After costs on a futures round-trip (brokerage, exchange, GST, STT on the sell side, stamp — call it roughly ₹250–300), net ≈ **₹12,600, about +4.2R.**

**The lesson.** The professional read the *context* first — a binary event resolving without a shock — and only then trusted the breakout, letting a trailing stop harvest the whole trend. The typical retail trader did one of two things: booked a quick 100 points at 49,000 "to be safe," missing 700 more; or waited for a pullback that never came, then chased an entry at 49,400 with a wide stop and got shaken out on the one shallow dip. On trend days, the money is in *holding*, and holding requires having decided in advance to trail rather than to grab.

## Case 2 — Nifty 50, gap-and-go on strong global cues (approx. 3 Dec 2024)

**The setup.** A strong overnight US session (S&P and Nasdaq up sharply) and a firm GIFT Nifty pointed to a gap-up open with follow-through. Gap-and-go days — where the market opens with a gap and simply keeps going in the gap's direction — are among the highest-probability trend setups, *provided the first pullback holds.* The context here was clean: no domestic event risk, broad global risk-on.

**Price-action walkthrough.** (Approximate 5-minute levels.)

| Time | Nifty (approx) | What happened |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| 09:15 | 24,180 | Gaps up ~90 pts from prior close 24,090 |
| 09:15–09:30 | 24,150–24,240 | First 15-min range: high 24,240, low 24,150 |
| 09:40 | 24,255 | 5-min close above range high, volume expanding |
| 09:55 | 24,225 | Shallow pullback holds *above* the breakout level — the key tell |
| 10:00–13:00 | 24,255→24,420 | Trends up, orderly |
| 15:15 | 24,450 | Closes near day's high |

The decisive moment was 09:55: the first pullback held above the broken range high. A gap that is going to fail usually gives back the gap in the first hour; this one refused to.

**The trade.** Entry on the 5-minute close above 24,240, filled ~**24,255**. Stop below the pullback low and the range, at **24,145** — 110 points of risk. Nifty lot = 25. Risk = 110 × 25 = **₹2,750 = 1R.** After the pullback held, the stop was raised to breakeven, making the rest of the trade effectively free.

**What happened.** The index trended into the close. Exit was scaled: half at 24,380 (partial into strength) and half trailed out near **24,440** at the close. Blended exit ≈ 24,410, a ~155-point gain. Gross = 155 × 25 = ₹3,875. After costs (≈ ₹200), net ≈ **₹3,675, about +1.3R** — modest in R because the risk was tight and the day, while trending, was less explosive than Bank Nifty's budget run.

**The lesson.** The professional used the **first-pullback-holds** filter to distinguish a real gap-and-go from a gap-and-fade, then moved the stop to breakeven so the trade could not turn into a loss. The retail trader typically "faded the gap" reflexively — shorting the open because "gaps fill" — and spent the whole session fighting a one-way trend, adding to a losing short. Fading is a range-day tactic; on a trend day it is financial suicide. The gap tells you nothing on its own; the behaviour of the *first pullback* tells you everything.

## Transferable rules

- Trade the ORB only after you have read the day's context — a binary event resolving cleanly, or strong one-way global cues, is what turns a break into a trend.
- Require a *close* (not a wick) beyond the opening range high/low before entering; the trigger is the discipline.
- On a suspected trend day, trail the stop instead of taking a fixed target — the money is in holding, and you must decide to hold *before* the move.
- Use the first pullback as your lie-detector: if it holds above the breakout level, trust the trend and move your stop to breakeven.
- A shallow, well-bought pullback and no lower-lows are the signature of a real trend day — pin your patience to that, not to a profit number.
