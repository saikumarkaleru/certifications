# Case Study: Real Breakout Swing Trades (Win and Loss)

*Price levels below are approximate reconstructions of a real event; pull up the actual chart to verify — the transferable value is the mechanics and the lesson, not the exact ticks.*

A breakout is only half a trade. The other half is what the price does *right after* it breaks out — the retest, the volume, and whether structure holds. This chapter puts a winning breakout and a losing breakout side by side, on purpose, because the *only* honest way to teach breakouts is to show one that failed and cost real money. Same setup logic, same discipline — different outcome. The edge isn't being right; it's being small when you're wrong.

---

## Case 1 — The winner: Tata Motors base-breakout-retest, late 2023

### The setup

**Instrument:** Tata Motors, NSE cash (delivery swing).
**Date:** roughly October–December 2023.
**Context:** Tata Motors had spent months basing after a strong run, with JLR turning profitable, domestic PV market share holding, and debt reduction as the story. The stock built a tight multi-week base under a clear resistance shelf around **₹650**. Tight base + improving fundamentals + broad market strength = the environment a breakout swing *wants.*

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistance shelf | Sep–Oct 2023 | ₹640–650 | Rejected 3 times, forming a flat ceiling |
| Base tightening | Oct 2023 | ₹610–650 | Range narrows, volume falls (coiling) |
| Breakout day | Late Oct 2023 | close ₹665 | Closes above ₹650 on ~2x average volume |
| Retest | Early Nov 2023 | ₹665 → ₹648 → holds | Pulls back to the breakout level, *holds* on light volume |
| Measured move | Nov–Dec 2023 | ₹665 → ₹720+ | Runs to and past the target |

### The trade (rule: enter on *confirmation of the retest*, not the breakout candle)

- **Entry:** ₹655 on the retest that held the ₹650 shelf (not on the breakout candle — waiting for the retest cut the risk).
- **Stop:** ₹628, below the retest low and back inside the base. A close back inside the base = the breakout failed.
- **Risk/share:** ₹655 − ₹628 = **₹27.**
- **Position size:** risking ₹10,000 → ₹10,000 / 27 ≈ **370 shares** (round to 350), capital ≈ ₹2.29 lakh.
- **Target (measured move):** base height ≈ ₹650 − ₹590 = ₹60, projected from ₹650 → **₹710.** Reward/risk on ₹655 entry ≈ (710 − 655) / 27 ≈ **2R.**

### What happened

The retest held, volume expanded on the next up-leg, and the measured-move target at ₹710 was hit within about four weeks.

- Exit: 350 shares at ₹710.
- Gross P&L: (710 − 655) × 350 = **₹19,250.**
- Costs (2026 delivery): STT 0.1% both sides ≈ ₹490 total on this turnover, plus brokerage/exchange/GST/stamp ≈ ₹150 → drag ≈ **₹640.**
- Net ≈ **₹18,600 ≈ 1.86R.**

### The lesson

The winning detail was **waiting for the retest.** Buying the breakout candle at ₹665 would have meant a wider stop and a worse entry; the retest to ₹648 gave a tighter stop (₹628) *and* proof that old resistance had become support. Volume confirmed both times — expansion on breakout, contraction on the pullback. The retail trader who chased the ₹665 breakout with a mental stop "somewhere below" got shaken out on the ₹648 dip — the exact spot the disciplined trader was *buying.*

## Case 2 — The loss: a failed breakout that stopped out

### The setup

**Instrument:** A mid-cap that had run hard into a resistance level (treat as a generic real 2024 mid-cap; verify any specific name on your chart).
**Date:** early 2024, during frothy mid-cap conditions.
**Context:** This is the trap version of Case 1. Same-looking base, same-looking breakout — but into an *extended, overbought* broader mid-cap tape, with the breakout volume looking suspiciously like a one-day spike rather than sustained demand. The setup passed the pattern checklist but the *context* was worse.

### Price-action walkthrough

| Phase | Approx date | Approx level | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistance | Jan 2024 | ₹500 | Multi-week ceiling |
| Breakout day | Feb 2024 | close ₹512 | Closes above on a volume spike |
| Failed retest | Feb 2024 | ₹512 → ₹495 | Pulls back but *slices through* the breakout level |
| Breakdown | Feb 2024 | ₹495 → ₹470 | Closes back inside the base — breakout dead |

### The trade

- **Entry:** ₹510 (this time taken on the breakout close — a slightly more aggressive entry).
- **Stop:** ₹492, below the breakout level.
- **Risk/share:** ₹18.
- **Position size:** risking ₹10,000 → ~550 shares, capital ≈ ₹2.8 lakh.
- **Target:** measured move to ₹560 (~2.7R).

### What happened

The retest didn't hold. Price cut *through* ₹500 the next session and closed back inside the base — the textbook definition of a failed breakout.

- Exit: stop hit at ₹492 (assume ~₹491 fill with slippage) on 550 shares.
- Gross P&L: (491 − 510) × 550 = **−₹10,450.**
- Costs add ≈ ₹560 → net ≈ **−₹11,000 ≈ −1.1R.**

The stop got hit within two days. It *stung* — but it was small, defined, and honoured.

### The lesson

**Honouring the stop is the entire trade.** The retail failure mode isn't taking the loss — it's *refusing* it: "let me give it room," "it'll come back above ₹500," then averaging down at ₹470 and ₹450 and turning a clean −1R into a −4R disaster (that's the next chapter). The professional's post-mortem is unemotional: the pattern was fine, the *context* (overbought tape, spike-not-trend volume) was the tell, and the −1.1R is simply the cost of doing business. Note the arithmetic: **the ₹18,600 winner more than paid for the ₹11,000 loser.** You do not need to be right often; you need your winners bigger than your losers and your losers *closed.*

## Transferable rules

- **Breakout + retest + volume.** Best entries come *after* old resistance proves itself as support on a low-volume pullback — not on the breakout candle itself.
- **Volume must confirm:** expansion on the break, contraction on the retest. A one-day volume *spike* with no follow-through is a warning, not a green light.
- **Context beats pattern.** The same base breaks better from a healthy tape than from an overbought, extended one. Check the broader index before you take the stock.
- **Size from the stop.** Fix your ₹ risk first, put the stop below structure, and let that math decide quantity — never the other way round.
- **The stop is sacred.** A −1R that you close is a rounding error; a −1R you "give room to" is how accounts die. Winners fund losers only if the losers stay small.
