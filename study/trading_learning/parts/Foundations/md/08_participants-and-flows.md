# Participants & Market Flows

## Why this matters

Retail traders stare at price and imagine a faceless "market." Pros see *who* is on the other side. The Indian market is a contest between distinct participant classes — retail, HNIs, DIIs, FIIs/FPIs, prop desks, and market makers — each with different capital, horizon, and information. The daily FII/DII flow figures, delivery percentages, and bulk/block deal disclosures are published *for free*, and they tell you which pool of capital is buying or selling and with what conviction. Reading flow won't give you an entry signal, but it gives you **bias and context**: fighting a persistent FII sell wave in index futures, or ignoring that promoters/DIIs are absorbing a stock, is how retail ends up on the wrong side of the big money. This chapter teaches you to read the players.

## The essentials

**The participant classes:**

| Participant | Who | Horizon / behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Retail** | Individuals | Small size, sentiment-driven, dominant in **options buying** and small-caps |
| **HNI / NRI** | Wealthy individuals | Larger tickets, often via PMS; move mid/small-caps |
| **DII** | Domestic Institutions — mutual funds, insurers (LIC), pension, banks | Steady, valuation-driven, **SIP-fuelled buying**; often absorb FII selling |
| **FII / FPI** | Foreign Portfolio Investors | Largest swing factor in large-caps & index; driven by global risk, USDINR, rates |
| **Prop desks** | Broker proprietary books | Short-horizon, arbitrage, high-frequency, liquidity provision |
| **Market makers** | Registered quote providers | Provide two-sided quotes in options/ETFs; earn the spread |

**FII/DII daily flow data.** NSE, BSE, and every finance site publish **daily FII and DII net buy/sell** in the cash (equity) segment, plus **FII derivatives** data (index futures, index options, stock futures long/short). This is the single most-watched flow print in India. A day of "FII sold ₹4,200 cr, DII bought ₹3,800 cr" tells you foreign money is exiting large-caps while domestic SIP money absorbs it — a tug-of-war that often produces range-bound indices.

**FII derivatives positioning.** Beyond cash, watch **FII Long/Short ratio in index futures** and their **net index options** stance. A falling FII long-short ratio in index futures signals bearish positioning; a very low ratio (heavily short) can precede short-covering bounces. *(Read via NSE's participant-wise OI / the "FII Derivative Statistics" report — verify current format.)*

**Delivery % vs intraday.** For cash stocks, exchanges publish **deliverable quantity as a % of traded quantity**. High delivery % = buyers taking actual possession (investment/accumulation), stronger hands. Low delivery % with high volume = churn/intraday speculation, weaker signal. A breakout on **rising delivery %** is more trustworthy than one on pure intraday froth.

**Bulk & block deals.** SEBI mandates disclosure:
- **Bulk deal** — a single client trades **>0.5% of a company's listed shares** in a day (across trades); disclosed post-market.
- **Block deal** — large negotiated trades (minimum ₹10 crore) in a **separate block window** at the day's open. 
These reveal *named* big buyers/sellers — a marquee fund taking a stake, or a promoter/PE exiting. Recurring bulk buys by quality names = accumulation signal; large block exits = supply overhang.

**Who moves what:** FIIs dominate **Nifty large-caps, index futures, USDINR**; DIIs provide the **structural bid** via SIP inflows; retail dominates **weekly option buying and small-caps**; prop/market makers dominate **intraday liquidity and options spreads**. Knowing the dominant player in *your* instrument tells you whose behaviour to track.

## Worked example — reading a day's flow

Suppose on a Tuesday:
- **FII cash: −₹5,100 cr** (net sell). **DII cash: +₹4,600 cr** (net buy).
- **FII index futures:** long-short ratio drops from 1.8 to 1.1 (adding shorts).
- **Nifty:** flat, −0.2%, in a tight range.
- **USDINR:** up (rupee weakening) — consistent with FII outflow.

**Reading:** Foreign money is de-risking large-caps and pressuring the rupee; domestic SIP money is absorbing the supply, capping the downside — hence the flat, coiled index. Bias: **range-bound with downside risk if DII buying tires**; a break *below* the range likely needs FII selling to continue, a break *above* likely needs FIIs to cover shorts (that low long-short ratio is fuel for a squeeze).

Now a **single stock**: XYZ Ltd rises 6% on **3× average volume** with **delivery % jumping from 35% to 62%**, and a **bulk-deal disclosure** shows a well-known mutual fund bought 0.8% of the company. **Reading:** this isn't intraday froth — real money took delivery, a named institution accumulated. Bias: constructive; pullbacks into the breakout zone are likely bought. Contrast: a 6% pop on **low delivery % and no institutional footprint** is more likely an operator-driven intraday move to fade.

## How pros do it / common mistakes

**Pros:**
- Treat FII/DII prints as **context, not a trigger** — bias for the day/week, sized with a real setup.
- Watch **FII derivatives positioning** for squeeze/flush risk, not just cash flows.
- Confirm breakouts with **rising delivery %** and, where possible, **bulk/block** footprints.
- Know the **dominant participant** in their instrument and don't fight structural flows blindly.
- Cross-check with **USDINR and global cues** — FII behaviour is often a currency/risk story.

**Retail mistakes:**
- Using a **single day's** FII/DII figure as a buy/sell signal (it's noisy; look at the trend over days/weeks).
- Ignoring **delivery %** and trusting volume alone.
- Chasing a stock that popped on **low delivery / operator churn** with no institutional footprint.
- Being **short into an FII short-covering squeeze** because they only watched cash, not derivatives positioning.
- Forgetting that **retail is the crowd** — in weekly options, retail is usually the net *buyer* funding institutional/prop sellers, which is a core reason most option buyers lose.

## Checklist / drill

- [ ] Today's **FII & DII cash** net, and the **multi-day trend** (not one print).
- [ ] **FII index-futures long-short** direction (squeeze/flush risk).
- [ ] For a stock: **delivery %** vs its average, and any **bulk/block** disclosure.
- [ ] The **dominant participant** in the instrument I'm trading.
- [ ] **USDINR / global risk** consistent with the flow story.

**Drill:** For two weeks, log daily FII cash, DII cash, and FII futures long-short ratio, alongside Nifty's close. At the end, mark days where flow and price agreed vs diverged. You'll build an intuition for when domestic buying is absorbing foreign selling (range) versus when both align (trend) — the difference between a market that grinds and one that runs.
