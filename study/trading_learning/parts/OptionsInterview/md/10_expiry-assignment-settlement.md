# Expiry, Assignment, Settlement & the STT Trap

Expiry mechanics are where careless traders lose money that has nothing to do with their market view — pin risk, delivery obligations, and the infamous STT-on-exercise trap. An experienced NSE F&O trader treats expiry as its own risk regime. Rules and figures are date-stamped as of July 2026; the STT hike and SEBI expiry-day/lot-size changes are live-moving items — verify current NSE/SEBI circulars before quoting.

**Q1. What actually happens to OTM, ATM and ITM options at expiry — index vs stock?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* clean settlement mechanics.
- *Deep answer:* At expiry, OTM options expire worthless — you keep the premium if short, lose it if long. ITM options settle at intrinsic value. The critical split is index vs stock. Index options (Nifty, Bank Nifty, FinNifty, Sensex) are European and cash-settled: ITM value is credited/debited in cash against the final settlement price (weighted average of the last half-hour of the underlying on expiry day), no delivery. Single-stock options are European but physically settled (since Oct 2019, still current 2026): an ITM stock option that isn't squared off results in a delivery obligation — the call buyer takes delivery of shares, the put buyer delivers shares, at the strike, requiring full contract value. ATM at expiry is the dangerous middle — tiny moves flip it ITM/OTM (pin risk). So the same "ITM" outcome is a harmless cash credit on index but a full-value stock delivery on single names.
- *Say it crisply:* OTM expires worthless, ITM settles at intrinsic — but index options settle in cash while stock options physically deliver shares, so an ITM stock option left open becomes a full-value delivery obligation.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "What's the index settlement price?" The weighted average price of the underlying index over the last 30 minutes of expiry day.

**Q2. Explain expiry-day gamma and pin risk. How do you handle it?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* the practical danger of short options at expiry.
- *Deep answer:* On expiry day, ATM options have enormous gamma — time to expiry is near zero, so delta flips between ~0 and ~1 on tiny spot moves. If you're short an ATM strike, your delta is wildly unstable: the market oscillating a few points around the strike can whip your P&L and make hedging a losing chase. Pin risk is the tendency of spot to gravitate near a heavily-traded strike (often near max pain) into the close, leaving you uncertain whether your strike finishes ITM or OTM — a genuine problem for short stock options because it decides whether you get assigned/delivered. My handling: I don't hold ATM short gamma into the last hours — I close or cap. On stock options I square off anything near-the-money well before close to avoid the delivery lottery. On index I'm more relaxed (cash-settled, no delivery) but still respect that expiry-day gamma can turn a calm book violent, so I keep expiry-day short-gamma small.
- *Say it crisply:* Expiry-day ATM gamma makes delta flip on tiny moves and pin risk leaves you unsure if you finish ITM — so I close or cap ATM shorts before the close, especially on physically-settled stocks.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Why not just delta-hedge it?" Because expiry-day gamma makes the hedge whip against you — you chase your own delta and bleed; closing is cleaner.

**Q3. What is max pain, and do you actually trade off it?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* whether you treat max pain as signal or folklore.
- *Deep answer:* Max pain is the strike at which the total value of outstanding options (the aggregate payout to option buyers) is minimised — i.e. where the largest rupee amount of options expires worthless, theoretically the "most painful" price for the aggregate of option holders. The idea is that spot tends to gravitate there by expiry because option writers (who are net long the market's short gamma) hedge in ways that pull price toward it. In practice I treat it as a weak, contextual reference, not a signal to trade off blindly — it's descriptive of where open interest sits, and it shifts as OI changes intraday. It can help me understand where pin risk clusters and which strikes are "heavy," but positioning around max pain alone is folklore-grade edge. I use it to inform strike selection and expiry-day pin awareness, never as a standalone thesis.
- *Say it crisply:* Max pain is the strike where the most option value expires worthless and price often drifts toward it, but I use it only as a pin-risk and OI reference — never as a standalone trade signal.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Does price always pin to max pain?" No — it's a tendency, not a law; big directional flows override it easily.

**Q4. Walk me through the STT-on-exercise trap on ITM options. This is important.**
- *Interviewer is testing:* the single most famous India expiry mistake.
- *Deep answer:* STT on options has two very different rates. On a normal sale (squaring off) of options, STT is charged on the premium at the sell-side rate. But if you let an option be exercised (i.e. hold a long ITM option to expiry and it's exercised/settled), STT is levied at a much higher rate on the FULL intrinsic settlement value — historically 0.125% of the intrinsic value on exercise, versus a tiny premium-based STT if you square off. Because settlement value (strike-level notional intrinsic) is huge relative to the premium, the exercise STT can be many multiples of the option's remaining value — it can wipe out or exceed your entire profit. This is the "STT trap." The fix every experienced trader knows: square off deep-ITM long options in the market before expiry rather than letting them go to exercise, so STT is charged on premium (small) not on intrinsic (large). Note: from April 2026 the STT on options SALE rose to ~0.1% of premium (verify current NSE/CBDT circular) — that raises round-trip costs generally, but the exercise-vs-square-off gap remains the trap to avoid.
- *Say it crisply:* Letting a long ITM option exercise charges STT on the full intrinsic value (~0.125%), which can dwarf the premium — so you square off ITM options in the market, where STT is only on the small premium.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Does it hit the writer too?" The punitive exercise STT is on the exercising long side; but the whole ecosystem squares off ITM to avoid it — so as a writer you're usually assigned less than notional would suggest.

**Q5. Give me a worked STT-trap example with numbers.**
- *Interviewer is testing:* can you quantify the trap.
- *Deep answer:* Suppose I'm long a Nifty 24,000 call, Nifty settles at 24,300, so intrinsic = 300 points. Lot size 75 (verify current NSE lot), so intrinsic settlement value = 300 × 75 = ₹22,500 per lot; notional strike value at exercise is based on settlement price 24,300 × 75 = ₹18,22,500. If I let it exercise, exercise STT ≈ 0.125% of the intrinsic/settlement notional. Using the intrinsic-value basis of ₹22,500 that's modest, but historically the punitive STT applied on the far larger settlement notional — on ₹18.2 lakh that's ~₹2,278 per lot, versus squaring off where STT is ~0.1% (from Apr 2026) of the premium, say premium ₹305 × 75 = ₹22,875 → STT ≈ ₹23. So exercise could cost ~₹2,278 vs ~₹23 by squaring off — roughly a 100× difference, potentially eating most of a thin ITM profit. Exact base and rate have changed over time and with the Apr-2026 revision — verify the current CBDT/NSE numbers — but the lesson is invariant: square off ITM rather than exercise.
- *Say it crisply:* On an ITM Nifty call, letting it exercise can cost thousands of rupees in STT on the settlement notional versus ~₹20 if I square off on premium — so I always sell ITM options in the market before expiry.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Which base — intrinsic or full notional?" Historically the punitive rate hit the settlement/notional value, which is why it's so large — verify the exact current base, but the trap is real either way.

**Q6. Explain physical settlement of stock options — the delivery obligation and do-not-exercise.**
- *Interviewer is testing:* India stock-option expiry mechanics.
- *Deep answer:* All single-stock F&O in India is physically settled. Any stock option ITM at expiry that isn't squared off results in a delivery obligation at the strike: a long ITM call takes delivery (pays full contract value for the shares), a long ITM put delivers shares, a short ITM call delivers shares, a short ITM put takes delivery and pays. This needs full contract value, not option margin — a step-change in capital. To manage the case where you're long a slightly-ITM option you don't want to exercise (because delivery + STT isn't worth it), exchanges provide a "Do Not Exercise" (DNE) facility for close-to-the-money long options — though brokers' DNE handling has narrowed over time, so you can't fully rely on it and the safe practice is to square off. Brokers also aggressively margin stock F&O in expiry week (physical delivery margins ramp up over the last days) and may auto-square-off positions you can't fund. Bottom line: never drift into stock-option expiry ITM unless you intend and can fund delivery.
- *Say it crisply:* Stock options physically settle, so an ITM option left open becomes a full-value delivery obligation needing full contract value — I square off before expiry and never rely solely on Do-Not-Exercise.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "When does the delivery margin ramp?" Over the last few days before expiry brokers progressively block full delivery-value margin on ITM/near-ITM stock positions.

**Q7. Cash vs physical settlement — why index one way and stocks the other, and how it changes your trading?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* connecting settlement design to strategy.
- *Deep answer:* Index options are cash-settled because you can't deliver an index — settlement is a cash difference against the final index price. Single stocks are physically settled by SEBI design (from 2019) to curb pure speculative expiry manipulation and align derivatives with deliverable shares. The practical consequence for me: I run naked/undefined short-vol structures far more freely on index (Nifty/Bank Nifty) because the worst expiry case is a cash debit, no delivery surprise. On single stocks I heavily prefer defined-risk spreads, I close before expiry, and I treat expiry week as a distinct regime because an ITM drift means real shares and real full-value capital. It also affects arbitrage and cash-futures basis behaviour on stocks near expiry. So settlement design directly dictates where I'm willing to carry tail risk into expiry — index yes (within gamma limits), single stocks no.
- *Say it crisply:* Index is cash-settled (no delivery), stocks physically settle real shares — so I carry short-vol into index expiry within gamma limits, but on stocks I use defined risk and close before expiry to avoid delivery.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Why did SEBI mandate physical for stocks?" To reduce speculative expiry manipulation and tie single-stock derivatives to actual deliverable shares.

**Q8. When does assignment happen and can you be assigned early?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* European vs American mechanics in India.
- *Deep answer:* All exchange-traded options in India — index and stock — are European style, so there is no early exercise and no early assignment; assignment/settlement only happens at expiry. This is different from US equity options (American style), where a short option can be assigned any day, especially around dividends. In India, as a writer I only face settlement at expiry: index shorts settle in cash against the final settlement price; stock shorts, if ITM, get assigned into a delivery obligation. So my "assignment risk" is entirely an expiry-day event, which is why expiry-day management — squaring off ITM stock shorts, watching pin risk — is where all the action is. There's no mid-cycle assignment surprise to defend against, which simplifies management versus American markets but concentrates all the risk into expiry day.
- *Say it crisply:* Indian options are European, so no early assignment — settlement only at expiry, which concentrates all assignment/delivery risk into expiry day, especially for physically-settled stock shorts.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "So dividend-driven early exercise?" Not in India — it's a US American-option issue; here it's irrelevant because everything is European.

**Q9. Given the current expiry-day and lot-size changes, how has your expiry approach adapted in 2026?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* awareness of recent SEBI/NSE structural changes.
- *Deep answer:* SEBI/NSE have been actively reshaping the F&O expiry landscape — rationalising the number of weekly expiries (moving toward one benchmark weekly expiry per exchange), standardising expiry days (Nifty/Sensex weekly expiries shifted around Tuesday/Thursday depending on exchange and circular), raising contract/notional sizes and revising lot sizes upward, and hiking margins and STT (options sale STT up from Apr 2026). Exact current expiry days, lot sizes and which weeklies survive are live-moving — verify the latest NSE/BSE/SEBI circular before trading. My adaptation: fewer weekly expiries means each expiry carries more concentrated OI and gamma, so pin risk and expiry-day moves are chunkier — I size expiry-day gamma down accordingly. Larger lot/notional sizes mean each lot is a bigger rupee risk, so my position sizing in lots dropped. Higher STT means I square off even more disciplined (the exercise trap plus higher round-trip cost punishes sloppy expiry handling). Net: I trade expiry more conservatively and verify the specs each cycle rather than assuming last year's rules.
- *Say it crisply:* With fewer weeklies, bigger lots and higher STT in 2026, expiries carry more concentrated gamma per lot — so I size expiry-day risk down, square off ITM even more strictly, and re-verify the specs every cycle.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Which exact expiry days now?" They've been revised more than once — I verify the current NSE/BSE circular each cycle rather than quote a stale day.

**Q10. Summarise the expiry-day checklist you actually run.**
- *Interviewer is testing:* an operational, no-surprises routine.
- *Deep answer:* My expiry-day routine: (1) Identify every position that could finish ITM and flag physically-settled stock options separately — those get squared off well before close, no exceptions, to avoid delivery and the exercise STT trap. (2) Reduce ATM short gamma early — I don't want unstable delta into the last hour. (3) Watch pin risk / heavy strikes (max pain and large OI) to anticipate where spot may gravitate and which of my strikes sit on the knife-edge. (4) On index, decide by mid-afternoon whether to let clearly-OTM shorts expire worthless (fine) or close near-ATM ones (safer). (5) Never let a long ITM option go to exercise — square it off so STT is on premium not intrinsic. (6) Keep margin buffer — expiry days can spike vol and margin. (7) Re-verify current settlement price mechanics, STT rate and lot sizes each cycle since these are moving in 2026. The whole point is that on expiry day I take no delivery or STT surprises — everything ITM is a deliberate, funded decision, not an accident.
- *Say it crisply:* On expiry day I square off all ITM stock options and long ITM index options before close, cut ATM short gamma early, watch pin risk, and keep a margin buffer — so nothing settles by accident.
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "What's the one non-negotiable?" Never let an ITM option drift into settlement unfunded — square off ITM to dodge both delivery and the STT trap.
