# India F&O Specifics (Bank Nifty, Weeklies, Rules 2026)

The micro-structure questions. A desk head wants to know you *live* on the NSE tape — that you know why Bank Nifty behaves differently from Nifty, how weeklies decay, what SPAN does to your margin, and that you're current on the 2026 SEBI/STT regime. Numbers here are as of July 2026 — **verify current** before quoting in an interview, these change.

**Q1. Contrast the trading character of Nifty, Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty. How does that change what you trade in each?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* whether you understand index composition drives volatility, and you adapt strategy to the instrument.
- *Deep answer:* Nifty 50 is broad, ~50 stocks across sectors, so it's the least jumpy of the three — realized vol typically runs a few points under Bank Nifty. Bank Nifty is ~12 heavily-weighted financials (HDFC Bank, ICICI, SBI, Kotak, Axis dominate), so it's concentrated, rate- and credit-sensitive, and moves 1.5–2x the range of Nifty on a given day. Fin Nifty overlaps heavily with Bank Nifty (banks + NBFCs + insurers) so its beta sits between the two but its options book is thinner. Post the 2024–25 SEBI rationalisation, each exchange runs a single weekly expiry: on NSE the surviving weekly is Nifty (Tuesday as of 2026 — verify, it has moved between Thursday/Wednesday/Tuesday); Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty lost their separate weeklies and trade **monthly** now. That's the single biggest structural fact for 2026: the weekly-expiry gamma game is now overwhelmingly a Nifty story. Practically: I scalp gamma and run expiry-day structures on Nifty because that's where the weekly decay and liquidity are; I express directional financials/rate views on Bank Nifty monthlies where I accept wider spreads and hold longer. I keep Fin Nifty for correlation/relative-value only, sizing down for the thinner book.
- *Say it crisply:* "Nifty is broad and calm, Bank Nifty is concentrated financials and moves ~2x, Fin Nifty sits between but is thinner — and post-2024 only one weekly per exchange survives, so weekly gamma trading is now a Nifty game while Bank/Fin Nifty are monthly."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Which is more sensitive to RBI policy?" → Bank Nifty, by a wide margin — it's ~90% rate-sensitive financials, so I widen my event vol assumptions there.

**Q2. Weekly vs monthly options — how does the decay and gamma profile differ, and how does it shape when you're a buyer vs a seller?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* intuition for theta/gamma acceleration into expiry.
- *Deep answer:* Theta isn't linear — it accelerates as the square root of time collapses, so a weekly's last two sessions bleed extrinsic value far faster than a monthly's. On a Nifty weekly ATM, an option can lose 40–60% of its remaining premium in the final day. The flip side is gamma: near-expiry ATMs have huge gamma, so a small spot move flips delta violently. That's the seller's dilemma — you collect fat theta but you're short enormous gamma, so one gap eats a week of premium. My rule of thumb: I *sell* weeklies when IV is rich relative to what I expect realized to be and there's no event, and I keep the short-gamma book small and delta-hedged into the last day. I *buy* options (or buy the monthly) when I want a directional or long-vol view without the weekly's brutal theta — monthlies give me time for the thesis to play out and much gentler daily bleed. A common junior mistake is buying weekly ATMs for a "3-day view" and getting carried out by theta even when direction is right.
- *Say it crisply:* "Weeklies decay far faster and carry violent gamma near expiry, so I sell them only when vol's rich and no event's due and stay hedged, and I buy monthlies when I actually want time for a directional or vol thesis."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "When is weekly theta highest?" → The final session, especially the last few hours as gamma and theta both spike on the ATM.

**Q3. Walk me through SPAN + exposure margin. How is margin actually computed and why does it matter to how you size?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* that you understand upfront margining, not just "some blocked money."
- *Deep answer:* NSE uses SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk). SPAN scans your *whole portfolio* through a grid of scenarios — spot moved up/down by the scan range (a multiple of daily vol) crossed with vol up/down — and charges you the worst-case single-day loss. On top sits the **exposure margin** (an additional buffer, roughly 3% of notional on index positions, more on stocks) and, for short options, a **short-option minimum**. Because SPAN is portfolio-based, a hedged spread margins far lower than the two legs separately — a Nifty bull call spread might need a fraction of a naked short put's margin, because SPAN sees the offsetting scenario losses. Since 2021 margins are collected **upfront** and peak-margin penalties apply intraday, so I can't leg into a short-vol position and post margin later — the broker blocks it at order time. This matters for sizing two ways: first, my capital efficiency comes from being hedged (defined-risk structures free up margin to redeploy); second, on a big vol spike SPAN parameters get *hiked* mid-session, so margin can balloon on an existing short book and force liquidation exactly when it's worst. I keep a margin cushion (I run at ~60–70% utilisation, never 100) precisely for that scenario.
- *Say it crisply:* "SPAN charges the worst-case one-day portfolio loss across a spot-and-vol scenario grid, exposure margin adds a buffer, it's collected upfront and it's portfolio-netted — so hedged structures are far cheaper, and I keep a cushion because SPAN gets hiked in a vol spike."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Why does a naked short option need so much margin?" → No offsetting leg, so SPAN's worst-case scenario is unmitigated plus the short-option minimum kicks in.

**Q4. Explain the 2026 STT change on options and the classic ITM-exercise STT trap.**
- *Interviewer is testing:* current tax awareness and a subtle P&L leak that catches amateurs.
- *Deep answer:* STT on options *sales* was hiked to roughly **0.1% of premium** in Oct 2024, and further increases toward the ~0.15%-of-premium level have been signalled/implemented into 2026 — **date-stamp and verify current**, because the exact rate and effective date matter. The bigger trap is exercise: STT on an option that expires **in-the-money and is exercised** is charged on the **intrinsic value of the settlement (notional), not the tiny premium** — historically ~0.125%. So an option you bought for ₹5 that ends ₹200 ITM gets STT on the *full settlement value*, which can be a large multiple of the premium and can even exceed a thin profit. The seasoned move: **square off ITM long options before expiry rather than letting them auto-exercise**, unless you specifically want physical delivery (relevant for stock options). On index options it's cash-settled but the STT-on-settlement logic still bites, so I close ITM longs in the last hour. Junior traders "hold to expiry to save one leg's brokerage" and get hit with an STT bill many times larger.
- *Say it crisply:* "Sell-side STT is being hiked toward ~0.15% of premium in 2026 — verify the exact rate — and the classic trap is that STT on an exercised ITM option is charged on intrinsic/settlement value, not premium, so I square off ITM longs before expiry instead of letting them exercise."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Does this apply to shorts too?" → The exercise STT is on the long/exercising side; but always model it, because it can turn a winning long into a loss.

**Q5. What changed with the 2024–25 SEBI expiry-day and contract-size rules, and how did it change your trading?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* that you're current, not quoting a 2022 playbook.
- *Deep answer:* Key changes to know (as of 2026, **verify current**): (1) **One weekly expiry per exchange** — SEBI cut the every-day-is-an-expiry circus, so NSE keeps a single weekly benchmark (Nifty) and BSE keeps Sensex; Bank Nifty/Fin Nifty weeklies were discontinued. (2) **Larger minimum contract value** — the notional per lot was raised (target band roughly ₹15–20 lakh, up from ~₹5–10 lakh), which raised **lot sizes** and per-lot margin, pushing out very small retail. (3) **Extra ELM/margin on expiry day** for short options to curb tail-day blowups, and removal of calendar-spread margin benefit on the expiry-day leg. (4) **Upfront premium collection** from option buyers. Net effect on me: fewer expiry days means gamma events are concentrated (I plan around one big Nifty expiry a week instead of five), position sizing is chunkier because a single lot is now bigger notional, and expiry-day short-vol carries more margin so the risk/reward of naked expiry selling worsened — I lean more on defined-risk spreads.
- *Say it crisply:* "SEBI moved to one weekly expiry per exchange, raised contract notional and lot sizes, added expiry-day margin on shorts and upfront premium collection — so I trade fewer, chunkier, more-hedged expiry structures instead of daily naked selling."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "Why did SEBI raise contract size?" → To reduce speculative retail participation after data showed the vast majority of F&O retail traders lose money.

**Q6. How do you think about liquidity and slippage in far-OTM and deep-ITM strikes?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* execution realism, not just theory.
- *Deep answer:* Liquidity in NSE index options is a smile of its own — concentrated in the ATM and near-OTM strikes of the front weekly/monthly, and it thins fast as you go far OTM or deep ITM, and further out in expiry. On Nifty near-ATM weekly you get penny-wide spreads and huge depth; go 8–10% OTM and the spread widens to ₹1–3 on a ₹5 option, i.e. 20–40% of premium, and market depth is a few lots. Deep-ITM options are worse — wide spreads, and they trade near intrinsic so market-makers won't tighten. Practical consequences: (1) I *never* market-order a far strike; I work limits at mid or use spreads so the illiquid leg is offset. (2) For tail hedges I accept I'm paying up and size the cost in. (3) Deep-ITM I avoid buying outright — I'd rather replicate the exposure with a future plus a cheap OTM option (put-call parity) to dodge the spread. (4) On exit, illiquid longs into expiry can be un-closable at a fair price, which is another reason to manage ITM positions early. Slippage is a real, recurring cost line in my P&L, not a rounding error — on far strikes it can dwarf brokerage and STT.
- *Say it crisply:* "Liquidity lives in the front-expiry near-ATM strikes; far-OTM and deep-ITM have wide spreads and thin depth, so I work limits, use spreads to offset the illiquid leg, replicate deep-ITM via future-plus-option, and budget slippage as a real cost."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "How wide before you walk away?" → If the spread is more than ~10–15% of the option's price and I can't better it with a limit, the edge is usually gone.

**Q7. A lot size changed on Bank Nifty — walk me through the notional and margin impact of trading one lot.**
- *Interviewer is testing:* comfort with the actual numbers a trader lives with.
- *Deep answer:* Take Bank Nifty at ~52,000 with a lot size of, say, 30 (**verify — lot sizes are revised periodically by NSE**). One lot's notional = 52,000 × 30 = ₹15.6 lakh. That's why a naked short straddle sold at, say, ₹1,200 total premium (₹36,000 per lot collected) can still require ₹1.3–1.6 lakh of SPAN+exposure margin per lot — the margin tracks the *notional risk*, not the premium. So the return-on-margin on that straddle is roughly ₹36,000 max collected against ~₹1.5 lakh blocked, before you've hedged. When SEBI raised minimum contract value, lot sizes rose so a single lot is now a chunkier bet — you can't finely size a small account, and one adverse Bank Nifty gap of 800 points is 800 × 30 = ₹24,000 move per lot on a delta-one leg. I always translate any position back to "rupees per point per lot" and "worst-case one-day move in rupees" before I put it on — that's how you avoid the trap of thinking in premium and forgetting the notional behind it.
- *Say it crisply:* "One Bank Nifty lot is ~₹15–16 lakh notional, so margin runs to lakhs even when premium collected is tens of thousands — I always convert to rupees-per-point-per-lot and worst-case daily move before sizing."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "How do you size across a portfolio then?" → I budget total portfolio SPAN and worst-case scenario loss to a fixed % of capital, and back out lots from that, not the other way round.

**Q8. India VIX — what does it tell you and how do you use it in options decisions?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* whether you use the vol index as a live tool, not a headline number.
- *Deep answer:* India VIX is NSE's 30-day forward implied vol of Nifty, annualised, computed from the near/next-month option chain (VIX-methodology). Two uses. First, **regime**: sub-11 is complacent/cheap-vol (I favour being long optionality / long gamma, or at least not selling cheap), mid-teens is normal, 20+ is stressed (rich premium — I lean to being a net seller of vol, carefully). Second, **expected daily move**: VIX/√252 ≈ the one-standard-deviation *daily* move in %. At VIX 14, that's 14/15.87 ≈ 0.88%/day, so on Nifty ~24,000 that's a ~±210-point 1-sigma day — I sanity-check my strike selection and straddle pricing against that. I also watch VIX *term structure and spikes*: VIX jumps into events (budget, elections, Fed) and crushes after — that pre-event run-up/post-event collapse is itself the trade. And I never sell a straddle "because premium looks big" without checking whether VIX is high *because* an event is priced; the premium is big for a reason.
- *Say it crisply:* "India VIX is Nifty's 30-day implied vol; I read the level for regime (cheap under ~11, rich over ~20) and use VIX/√252 as the expected 1-sigma daily move to sanity-check strikes and premium."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "What's a 1-sigma day at VIX 20?" → About 1.26%/day, so ~300 points on a 24,000 Nifty.

**Q9. How does physical settlement of stock options change how you manage a position into expiry?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* awareness that stock F&O ≠ index F&O at expiry.
- *Deep answer:* Since 2019 all NSE **stock** options and futures are **physically settled** — if your stock option is ITM at expiry and you don't square off, you take/give **delivery of the underlying shares**, needing the full contract value in cash (for a bought call / assigned put) or the shares in demat. That's a huge cash/margin event versus the small option premium. It also means margins on ITM/near-ITM stock options ramp up in expiry week (physical-delivery margin), and there's **pin risk** — an option sitting right at the strike near the close can flip ITM/OTM, leaving you unsure whether you'll be assigned. My management: for stock options I *square off ITM positions before expiry* unless I genuinely want the delivery; I watch near-the-money shorts for assignment risk in the last two days; and I factor the delivery-margin ramp into whether the position is even worth holding into expiry week. Index options (Nifty/Bank Nifty) are **cash-settled**, so this doesn't apply there — but the STT-on-exercise point still does. Confusing the two is a classic rookie error.
- *Say it crisply:* "Stock options are physically settled, so an ITM position at expiry means taking or giving actual share delivery plus a big margin ramp and pin risk — I square off ITM stock options before expiry unless I want the stock; index options are cash-settled so it doesn't apply."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "What's pin risk?" → When spot sits right at a short strike near expiry and you can't tell if you'll be assigned — the delta whipsaws and hedging is treacherous.

**Q10. Given all these 2026 India specifics, how do they concretely shape *what and when* you trade?**
- *Interviewer is testing:* synthesis — can you turn micro-structure into a coherent playbook.
- *Deep answer:* It shapes a few decisions. **Instrument**: weekly gamma/expiry structures → Nifty (the only surviving liquid weekly); directional financials/rate views → Bank Nifty monthly, sized for wider spreads; Fin Nifty only for relative-value, sized down. **Timing within the week**: theta acceleration means I put on short-vol later (mid-week) and manage or close it by the final session rather than carrying naked gamma into the close; long-vol/directional I do on monthlies for time. **Structure choice**: raised expiry-day margins and STT hikes push me toward *defined-risk spreads* over naked shorts — the margin efficiency and capped tail justify giving up some premium. **Tax/exercise discipline**: I square off ITM longs before expiry to dodge settlement-STT (index) or physical delivery (stocks). **Around events**: I respect that VIX-rich premium is rich for a reason and prefer calendars/spreads to naked short straddles into budget/RBI/Fed. **Sizing**: chunkier lots and upfront margining mean I run a margin cushion and think in worst-case rupees-per-lot, not premium. The through-line: liquidity, decay, margin and tax all reward being hedged, current-expiry, and disciplined about ITM management.
- *Say it crisply:* "Nifty for weekly gamma, Bank Nifty monthlies for directional financials, defined-risk over naked shorts given margin and STT, square off ITM before expiry, respect event-rich VIX, and size in worst-case rupees-per-lot with a margin cushion."
- *Follow-up they may ask:* "One thing you changed most in 2026?" → I stopped running daily naked expiry straddles across Bank Nifty — with weeklies gone there and expiry margins up, I moved to Nifty spreads.
