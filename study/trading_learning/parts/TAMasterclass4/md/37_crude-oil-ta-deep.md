# Crude Oil TA (Deep)

Crude oil is the most macro-sensitive, headline-driven, and unforgiving instrument the average Indian retail trader will ever touch on the MCX. It is where fortunes are made in an afternoon and margin calls arrive by SMS the same evening. Oil does not respect chart patterns the way a well-behaved stock does; a single OPEC headline, a US inventory number, or a Middle East flashpoint can vaporise a technical setup in one candle. And yet oil *is* deeply technical — precisely because so many participants trade it on levels, it honours support, resistance, and trend with brutal consistency between the shocks. This chapter is the working playbook: oil's character and drivers, the levels and behaviour that repeat, the setups that survive the volatility, a full worked trade, and the risk discipline oil absolutely demands.

## The instrument's character and its real drivers

MCX Crude Oil is quoted in rupees per barrel, tracking the international **WTI (West Texas Intermediate)** benchmark priced in US dollars per barrel. The main contract is **CRUDEOIL (100 barrels)** — one rupee of price move is 100 rupees P&L. Near 2026 prices around ₹6,000-7,500 per barrel, that is 6-7.5 lakh of notional per lot with far more daily movement than metals. The essential smaller contract is **CRUDEOILM (CrudeOil Mini, 10 barrels)** where one rupee = 10 rupees — the correct size for almost every retail trader. There is effectively 10x more volatility-per-rupee here than in gold; treat the full CRUDEOIL contract with the respect you would give a loaded, cocked instrument.

MCX crude is a derived price:

```
MCX Crude (per barrel)  ≈  WTI ($/bbl) × USDINR
```

No import duty complication like metals — it is close to a clean WTI × USDINR translation, which means USDINR still matters but WTI dominates. The forces that move WTI:

1. **Supply — OPEC+ policy above all.** Production cuts or increases from OPEC and its allies (notably Saudi Arabia and Russia) are the dominant scheduled driver. OPEC+ meeting outcomes reprice oil by dollars in seconds. US shale production and the US rig count are the counterweight.
2. **Demand — the global growth cycle.** China's demand, global PMIs, recession fear, and seasonal driving/heating demand. Oil is the ultimate growth-sensitive commodity — it falls hard on recession fear.
3. **Inventories — the weekly data pulse.** The **EIA Crude Oil Inventory** report (Wednesday, typically 8:00 PM IST) and the prior evening's **API** private estimate are the biggest scheduled intraday volatility events. A larger-than-expected inventory *draw* (demand strong / supply tight) is bullish; a *build* is bearish.
4. **Geopolitics — the shock overlay.** Middle East conflict, tanker/Strait-of-Hormuz threats, sanctions, hurricanes in the US Gulf. These are the unscheduled gaps that stop-hunt everyone.

Two more structural realities: oil markets carry a **contango/backwardation** term structure (front-month vs later months) that reflects storage costs and supply tightness, and MCX crude has the same **overnight gap risk** as metals — MCX is closed while WTI trades on NYMEX/Globex nearly around the clock, so the 9:00 AM MCX open is a catch-up to the overnight international move.

## Key levels and behaviour you must respect

- **Oil is a superb trending instrument between shocks.** It builds clean channels and honours the 20/50 EMA on the daily and 4-hour for weeks — until a headline breaks the regime. So trade trend-following *within* the calm and go flat or tiny into scheduled shocks.
- **Round numbers rule.** WTI's $60, $70, $80, $90, $100 marks and MCX's ₹6,000, ₹6,500, ₹7,000, ₹7,500 per barrel are decisive psychological levels. $80 and $100 WTI in particular are macro battle lines.
- **The 8:00 PM Wednesday EIA is the week's heartbeat.** Oil coils into it and explodes out of it. The single most important recurring event on the crude calendar. The Tuesday-evening API often front-runs the reaction.
- **Whipsaw around news is extreme.** Oil's reaction candles to EIA and OPEC headlines routinely run 100-200+ MCX points in minutes, then reverse. The spike is a trap for market orders.
- **Overnight gaps are frequent and large.** WTI moves on US-session inventory, Asian-session Chinese data, and 24-hour geopolitics. The MCX 9:00 AM gap can be huge.
- **ATR expands violently around OPEC weeks and conflict.** Position size must contract as ATR expands. A fixed rupee-stop is meaningless without reference to current ATR.
- **Liquidity is strong in Indian evening hours** (overlapping the US session and the EIA) and thinner late at night — but crude is generally the most liquid MCX energy contract.

## The best setups on crude

**Setup 1 — EIA reaction breakout-and-retest (Wednesday evening).** The professional's staple. Do not guess the inventory number. Let the 8:00 PM candle print its violent move, mark the level it breaks, wait for the retest, and enter on confirmation with a stop beyond the spike. If the EIA number and price reaction *disagree* with each other (bullish draw but price falls), respect the price — the tape is telling you positioning or a bigger macro force dominates.

**Setup 2 — Trend pullback to the 20 EMA (intraday/swing).** In a clean crude trend on the 4-hour or daily (price above rising 20/50 EMA, orderly higher highs/lows), buy pullbacks to the 20 EMA or the prior breakout shelf, stop below the swing low, target the prior high and trail. This is the highest-quality *between-shocks* trade and it exploits oil's genuine trending nature.

**Setup 3 — Range fade with a flip rule (balanced weeks).** When supply/demand is balanced, crude rotates in a rupee range. Fade the edges with tight stops beyond the boundary, and flip to breakout the moment a candle *closes* outside on volume. Crude's ranges are cleaner than silver's but still stop-hunt the edges — wait for the poke-and-reclaim.

**Setup 4 — OPEC/geopolitics gap play (event/next-morning).** After an OPEC surprise or a weekend geopolitical escalation, MCX gaps at the 9:00 AM open. Trade the *catch-up direction* only after the first 15-minute candle confirms, never in the thin first minutes. If the gap over-extends beyond fair WTI × USDINR value, a fade back toward fair value is the play; if it merely aligns MCX to the overnight WTI move, follow the trend.

## A worked India example

It is a Wednesday in 2026. WTI has been in a mild uptrend, MCX CRUDEOILM (10 bbl) grinding higher, price above the rising daily 20/50 EMA. Crude has consolidated 6,820-6,980 per barrel for five sessions ahead of the 8:00 PM EIA report. Bias from the daily trend: *lean long, but let the number decide.*

- **The read.** WTI ~$78.6, USDINR 87.1 → MCX fair ~6,845; MCX trading 6,910, tracking WTI cleanly, no dislocation. The Tuesday API had hinted at a crude *draw*, so the market leans bullish into the print, but you hold no position into the number.
- **The event.** 8:00 PM: EIA confirms a large crude *draw* (bullish — inventories fell more than expected). Crude spikes. CRUDEOILM rips from 6,910 through the 6,980 range top to 7,090 on a huge 5-minute candle.
- **The professional entry.** You do not chase the spike. You mark 6,980 (old range top, now support). Price pulls back over 15 minutes to 6,995 and holds above the broken range. You go long CRUDEOILM at 7,010, stop at 6,940 (back inside the range — a re-entry invalidates the breakout). Risk = 70 points × 10 rupees = 700 rupees per CRUDEOILM lot.
- **Management.** Measured move: range height ~160 points (6,820-6,980), projected from breakout ≈ 7,140. Price grinds to 7,150 into the US session. Book half at 7,140 (~1.9R), trail the rest under the rising 15-minute swing lows. It closes the MCX session near 7,180. The second half trails out next morning at 7,160 on the first 5-minute lower low after a quiet overnight.
- **Result.** Roughly +130 and +150 points on the two halves — about 2,800 rupees on 700 risk, ~4R. Clean because the edge was the *reaction and retest of a bullish inventory shock in an existing uptrend*, not a pre-EIA gamble.

The counter-lesson: had a naked long been held *into* 8:00 PM and the EIA printed a surprise *build*, crude would have gapped down through 6,820, and the "safe" stop would have been leapfrogged in the spike. The plan is always trade-the-reaction, never trade-the-guess — and in crude that rule is not optional, it is survival.

## How to use crude for bias and timing across your book

Crude oil is a **growth-and-inflation signal** with direct consequences for the Indian market, because India imports over 80% of its oil. Rising crude is a headwind for the rupee (wider trade deficit → USDINR up), for inflation, and specifically for oil-marketing companies (BPCL, HPCL, IOC — higher input cost squeezes margins) and paint/aviation/logistics stocks (crude derivatives and fuel costs). Falling crude is a tailwind for the rupee, for inflation, and for those same import-sensitive sectors. So crude's chart feeds directly into your USDINR view and your sector rotation on the NSE. A crude spike on geopolitics is also a risk-off warning for broad Nifty longs. Watching crude, USDINR, and Nifty together is a genuine intermarket edge no single chart gives you.

For timing crude itself, use the daily 20/50 EMA relationship as a regime filter (trend-follow when aligned, range-tactics when whipsawing), and *always* structure the week around the Wednesday EIA and known OPEC meeting dates.

## Pitfalls and risk notes specific to crude

- **Volatility and leverage are the whole risk.** Crude's per-rupee volatility dwarfs metals. The full CRUDEOIL (100 bbl) contract can swing tens of thousands of rupees in an hour. Use CRUDEOILM until your edge and capital are proven, and size off ATR every single time.
- **Never hold naked into the EIA or OPEC.** The signature crude account-killer is carrying a large tightly-stopped position into 8:00 PM Wednesday or an OPEC decision and getting gapped through the stop. Either be flat, or accept a wide gap-sized stop and tiny size.
- **Overnight gap risk is severe and frequent.** MCX is closed while WTI trades 24 hours on geopolitics and Asian data. Size for the gap, not the intraday range. Weekends carry Middle East headline risk.
- **The spike is a trap.** Reaction candles reverse. Market orders into the EIA/OPEC spike get filled at the worst tick. Wait for the retest; use limits.
- **Price over narrative.** When the EIA number is bullish but price falls (or vice versa), the tape wins — positioning or a larger macro force is dominant. Trade what price does, not what the number "should" mean.
- **Contango and rollover.** MCX crude expires roughly mid-month (around the 19th-20th cycle). Roll to the liquid front month; do not get caught in the thin, expiry-week gyrations or delivery mechanics.
- **USDINR overlay.** A correct WTI view can still lose on MCX if the rupee moves against you, though WTI usually dominates. Be aware you are implicitly trading USDINR too.
- **Don't average down.** Crude's speed makes averaging into a loser a route to ruin. One entry, one stop, defined risk, and respect it without exception.

## Interview-ready summary

MCX Crude Oil is a rupee-denominated derivative of dollar-priced WTI — approximately WTI × USDINR, with no import-duty layer, so WTI dominates and USDINR is the secondary overlay. WTI is driven by supply (OPEC+ policy above all, US shale as counterweight), demand (the global growth cycle, China, PMIs, recession fear), weekly inventories (the Wednesday 8:00 PM IST EIA report, front-run by Tuesday's API), and geopolitical shocks (Middle East, Hormuz, sanctions, Gulf hurricanes). On the chart, crude is an excellent trending instrument that honours round numbers and the daily 20/50 EMA between shocks, but exhibits extreme whipsaw around news and large, frequent overnight gaps because MCX is closed while WTI trades 24 hours. The best setups are the EIA reaction breakout-and-retest, the trend-pullback-to-20-EMA between shocks, the range fade with a hard flip-to-breakout rule, and the OPEC/geopolitics morning-gap catch-up. The defining risks are crude's leverage and volatility (use CRUDEOILM, size off ATR), never holding naked into the EIA or OPEC, severe overnight and weekend gap risk, and the trap of chasing the reaction spike. Because India imports over 80% of its oil, crude also drives USDINR, inflation, and NSE sector rotation (OMCs, paints, aviation, logistics) — making its chart a genuine intermarket edge. Trade the reaction and the retest, never the guess before the number; in crude that discipline is survival, not preference.
