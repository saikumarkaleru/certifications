# Multi-Timeframe Drills

Multi-timeframe analysis (MTFA) is where good technicians separate from screen-watchers. Any single chart lies to you — it shows one slice of the market's fractal structure and hides the context that decides whether the pattern in front of you is a gift or a trap. The 15-minute breakout that looks perfect is a lower-high inside a daily downtrend. The daily doji that scares you out is a routine pullback inside a weekly uptrend still riding its 20-week average. The cure is a disciplined habit of reading three timeframes together and forcing them to agree before you risk a rupee. This chapter is a drill book: a stack of realistic India-first scenarios where you are handed the higher, trading and lower timeframe picture and must produce a decision — direction, entry, stop, target, or "no trade." Each drill has a full answer key with the reasoning, because the reasoning is the skill; the answer is just the by-product.

## The three-timeframe framework you'll drill against

Before the drills, lock the framework so your answers are consistent. Use a roughly 1:4 to 1:6 ratio between adjacent timeframes.

| Trader type | Higher TF (context/bias) | Trading TF (setup) | Lower TF (trigger/entry) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positional (swing) | Weekly | Daily | 1-hour |
| Intraday swing | Daily | 1-hour | 15-min |
| Scalper / BTST | 1-hour | 15-min | 3-5 min |

The three timeframes have three different jobs and you must never let them trade each other's role:

- **Higher timeframe = bias and permission.** It answers one question: which direction am I allowed to trade? Trend structure (higher highs/higher lows), the 20/50 EMA slope, and location relative to major support/resistance. If the weekly says up, you take longs on the daily and treat shorts as counter-trend scalps only.
- **Trading timeframe = the setup.** This is where the actual pattern forms — the flag, the pullback to a moving average, the range breakout, the double bottom. You qualify the setup here.
- **Lower timeframe = the trigger and the risk.** It gives you a precise entry and a tight stop so your risk-reward is not bloated. A daily setup entered on the daily close risks 3%; the same setup triggered on a 15-min structure break risks 1%.

The golden rule: **trade in the direction of the higher timeframe, at the location the trading timeframe gives you, with the trigger and stop the lower timeframe defines.** When all three align, you have an A+ trade. When the higher and trading timeframes conflict, you either stand aside or take a reduced-size, tight-stop counter-trend scalp — never a full-size positional bet.

A second concept you'll use throughout: **timeframe continuity.** The strongest trends show the same story on all three charts — weekly up, daily up, hourly up. The best entries happen when the lowest timeframe is temporarily *against* the trend (a pullback) while the higher two remain aligned. You are buying the lower-timeframe dip inside the higher-timeframe uptrend. That is the entire game.

## How to attempt these drills

For each drill: read the higher-TF context first and write your bias in one word (LONG / SHORT / NEUTRAL). Only then read the trading and lower TF. Decide: trade or no-trade; if trade, give entry zone, stop, first target and the invalidation. Then check the answer. Do not read the answer first — the value is in being wrong and understanding why.

## Drill 1 — Nifty, positional

**Weekly:** Nifty is at 24,850, making higher highs and higher lows for eight months, trading above a rising 20-week EMA (23,900). Last week was a small red candle after a strong run.
**Daily:** Price pulled back four sessions from 25,100 to 24,780, tagging the rising 20-day EMA (24,760) with a bullish hammer today on above-average volume.
**1-hour:** After the hammer, the hourly made a higher low and just broke a small descending trendline connecting the pullback highs.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** LONG — A+ setup. Weekly bias is clearly up (HH/HL, above rising 20W EMA). Daily gives the location: a controlled pullback into the 20-day EMA, a classic buy-the-dip zone, confirmed by a hammer on rising volume. The hourly gives the trigger: higher low plus trendline break. Entry on the hourly break, around 24,830. Stop below the daily hammer low and the 20-day EMA, say 24,720 (risk ~110 points). First target the prior swing high 25,100, then measured continuation toward 25,400+. Risk-reward roughly 1:2.5 to first target. All three timeframes align with the lowest one turning back up into the higher-TF trend — textbook continuity entry.

## Drill 2 — Bank Nifty, the trap

**Weekly:** Bank Nifty rolled over from 54,000 two months ago, now at 49,500, below a flattening-to-falling 20-week EMA (50,800). Lower highs on the weekly.
**Daily:** A sharp three-day bounce from 48,200 to 49,500 with strong green candles; RSI recovered from 32 to 55.
**15-min:** A clean bullish flag broke to the upside 20 minutes ago; momentum looks great.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** NO TRADE for a positional long; at most a small counter-trend intraday scalp. The 15-min flag and the daily bounce are seductive, but the weekly bias is DOWN. You are being invited to buy a lower-timeframe rally straight into overhead weekly resistance (falling 20W EMA at 50,800 and the lower-high structure). Bounces in downtrends are the sharpest and most convincing rallies — that is precisely what suckers longs in. If you must trade the flag, size it at a fraction, target the 50,800 resistance only, and use a tight 15-min stop, accepting that you're fighting the higher timeframe. The professional play is to *wait* for price to reach 50,500-50,800 and look for a daily bearish reversal to short *with* the weekly. Never let a beautiful lower-timeframe pattern override a hostile higher timeframe.

## Drill 3 — Reliance, the alignment check

**Weekly:** Reliance is consolidating in a broad 1,380-1,480 range for five months; 20W EMA is flat at 1,420. No trend.
**Daily:** Price is at 1,455, near the top of the range, with a rising 20-day EMA and three weeks of higher lows.
**1-hour:** Overbought — hourly RSI at 78, price extended well above the hourly VWAP and 20-EMA.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** NEUTRAL bias from the weekly (range, no trend), so this is a range trade, not a trend trade. Within a range you buy support and sell resistance — you do NOT buy near the top of the range. The daily strength has carried price to 1,455, just under weekly resistance at 1,480, and the hourly is overbought and extended. This is a place to take *profits* on existing longs or to watch for a short scalp back toward the range middle (1,430) — not to initiate a fresh long. New longs belong near 1,390-1,400 (range support) with the daily turning up. Lesson: when the higher timeframe is a range, location within the range is everything, and an extended lower timeframe near range resistance is a warning, not a green light.

## Drill 4 — Nifty intraday, the gap

**Daily (higher):** Uptrend intact, price above rising 20-day EMA at 24,600, yesterday closed strong at 24,900.
**1-hour (trading):** Today Nifty gapped up to open at 25,050, then spent the first hour building a tight range 25,020-25,080.
**15-min (lower):** The 15-min just broke above 25,080 on a strong candle with volume expanding.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** LONG with discipline — but manage the gap risk. Daily bias up, so intraday longs are permitted. The gap-up followed by a tight first-hour range (rather than an immediate fade) shows the gap is being accepted, not rejected — bullish. The 15-min break above the range high (25,080) is the trigger. Entry 25,085, stop below the range low 25,010 (risk ~75 points), target measured from the range height projected up, roughly 25,150 then 25,220. Key nuance: if instead the first hour had faded and filled the gap back to 24,900, the whole thesis flips to a possible short. Gap-up + acceptance + range-break-up = continuation long; gap-up + rejection + gap-fill = failure. The lower timeframe tells you which one you're in.

## Drill 5 — Tata Motors, conflicting momentum

**Weekly:** Strong uptrend, price 980, well above rising 20W EMA at 880.
**Daily:** Price has been sideways-to-down for two weeks, 950-1,010, and today closed below the 20-day EMA at 965 for the first time in months, on a red candle.
**1-hour:** Made a lower high and lower low; hourly clearly in a short-term downtrend.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** NO fresh long yet; NO short either — WAIT. The weekly is strongly up, so shorting is fighting the dominant trend. But the daily and hourly have both turned down short-term — the first close below the daily 20-EMA in months means the easy long is over for now. This is a "trend intact, momentum paused" state. The correct action is patience: let the daily pullback mature toward deeper support (the 50-day EMA or the prior breakout zone around 920-930) and wait for the hourly to make a *higher* low again — that higher low inside the weekly uptrend is your re-entry trigger. Buying today catches a falling knife; shorting fights the weekly. Standing aside until the lower timeframe turns back up is the highest-expectancy choice. Not every day has a trade.

## Drill 6 — Fin Nifty, the false breakout

**Daily:** Range-bound 23,400-23,900 for three weeks.
**1-hour:** Price spiked above 23,900 to 23,960, then within two hourly candles fell back below 23,900 and closed at 23,850.
**15-min:** Sharp rejection candle at 23,960, now printing lower highs.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** SHORT the failed breakout (bull trap). The daily range's upper boundary is 23,900. A break above that reversed immediately — price poked to 23,960 and got slammed back inside the range on the hourly. A breakout that fails to hold is one of the most reliable reversal signals: trapped breakout buyers become forced sellers. Entry on the 15-min lower-high confirmation around 23,840, stop above the false-breakout high 23,975 (risk ~135), target the opposite end of the range 23,450. Risk-reward ~1:2.9. This is a range-context trade — the daily gave you the range, the hourly gave you the failure, the 15-min gave you the trigger. Rule: failed breakouts often move as far in the opposite direction as the range is tall.

## Drill 7 — HDFC Bank, the moving-average sandwich

**Weekly:** Just turned up — price crossed back above a flattening 20W EMA (1,660) after a long base; first higher high forming.
**Daily:** Price at 1,690, above both 20-day (1,672) and 50-day (1,655) EMAs, which are curling up.
**1-hour:** Pulling back to the hourly 50-EMA at 1,684, holding, with a small bullish engulfing.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** LONG — early-trend continuation. This is a high-quality setup because the weekly has *just* turned, meaning the trend is young with room to run rather than extended. Daily is above stacked, upturning EMAs (bullish structure). The hourly pullback to its 50-EMA with a bullish engulfing is a low-risk entry inside the new uptrend. Entry 1,692, stop below the hourly structure and the daily 20-EMA, say 1,668 (risk ~24 points on this scale). Targets: prior resistance 1,720, then 1,750. The best positional longs come right after a weekly trend change confirms, when most participants are still doubting the move. Early trend + tight lower-TF entry = excellent expectancy.

## Drill 8 — Nifty, the divergence warning

**Weekly:** Uptrend, price at a new all-time high 25,300.
**Daily:** New price high 25,300 but daily RSI made a lower high (68 vs 74 three weeks ago) — bearish divergence. Volume on the new high is lighter than the prior high.
**1-hour:** Still making higher highs, no reversal yet.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** HOLD existing longs but do NOT add; tighten stops. This is not a short signal — the hourly is still up and the weekly trend is intact. Bearish divergence on the daily is a *warning*, not a trigger; trends can run for weeks in divergence. The correct multi-timeframe read: the higher timeframes still say up, but the internal momentum is weakening, so you reduce risk rather than reverse. Trail your stop up under the most recent daily higher low, book partial profits, and stop adding new size. Your short trigger only arrives if the hourly breaks structure (a lower low) AND the daily closes below its 20-EMA. Acting on divergence alone is a classic way to short a trend that keeps going. Divergence lowers your conviction; it does not flip your direction.

## Drill 9 — Adani Ports, news gap-down

**Weekly:** Uptrend, price was 1,420 above rising 20W EMA at 1,300.
**Daily:** Gapped down 6% today on a news headline to open at 1,335, closing at 1,340 — landing exactly on the rising 20W EMA and the prior daily breakout level.
**1-hour:** After the gap, formed a base and printed a higher low in the last two hours.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** CAUTIOUS LONG, small size, defined risk. The gap-down was violent but it stopped precisely at confluence support: the rising 20-week EMA plus the prior breakout zone (old resistance turned support) at ~1,335. The weekly trend is still up above that EMA. The hourly higher low after the gap shows sellers are exhausting near support. This is a mean-reversion-into-support long with the higher-TF trend. Entry ~1,345 on the hourly higher-high confirmation, stop below the gap low and the weekly EMA at 1,320 (a close below there breaks the weekly uptrend — thesis invalid), target the gap-fill toward 1,400. Size small because news gaps are unpredictable and can extend. The key skill: recognizing that a scary gap that halts at major multi-timeframe confluence is an opportunity, not just a threat — but only with a hard invalidation.

## Drill 10 — Bank Nifty scalp, all three down

**1-hour:** Downtrend, lower highs, below falling hourly 20-EMA at 49,200.
**15-min:** Bounced to the 15-min 20-EMA at 49,180 and stalled with a shooting star.
**5-min:** Just broke below the last 5-min swing low.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** SHORT — clean intraday continuity. All three timeframes point down. The hourly gives the bias (down). The 15-min gives the setup: a pullback to the 20-EMA rejected by a shooting star at resistance — a lower-high forming inside the downtrend. The 5-min gives the trigger: break of the swing low. Entry ~49,140 on the 5-min break, stop above the shooting-star high and the 15-min EMA at 49,240 (risk ~100 points on Bank Nifty), target the prior swing low and beyond, 48,900 then 48,750. This is the mirror image of Drill 1: pullback-into-resistance short inside an aligned downtrend, triggered on the lowest timeframe. Continuity in the short direction is just as tradable as in the long direction.

## Drill 11 — The stacked conflict

**Weekly:** Up (price above rising 20W EMA).
**Daily:** Down (below falling 20-day EMA, lower highs for two weeks).
**1-hour:** Up (just made a higher high off support).

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** This is the classic three-way disagreement, and the answer is REDUCED-SIZE, QUICK long scalp at best — or no trade. When the middle timeframe (daily) opposes the higher timeframe (weekly), the trend is in a pullback and directionless in the near term. The hourly turning up could be the start of the daily resuming the weekly uptrend — OR just a bounce inside the daily pullback. You cannot know yet. The disciplined read: the weekly permits longs, but the daily pullback is not confirmed as over. Take a small hourly long with a tight stop, targeting the daily 20-EMA overhead, and only scale up if the daily *closes back above* its 20-EMA (which resolves the conflict in the bulls' favor). When timeframes stack in disagreement, position size is your primary risk tool — you trade small until they realign.

## Drill 12 — Nifty expiry-day whipsaw

**Daily:** Neutral, inside a 100-point range near 24,700 for four sessions ahead of monthly expiry.
**1-hour:** Choppy, overlapping candles, no clean structure.
**15-min:** Two false breaks in both directions already today.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** NO TRADE — or trade only defined range extremes with tiny size. Everything screams low-quality environment: a neutral daily, a choppy hourly with no structure, and the 15-min already whipsawing both ways. Expiry-day pinning near a round strike (24,700) makes trend-following a losing game — you get chopped to death. The multi-timeframe framework's most valuable output is sometimes "the timeframes have nothing coherent to say, so I don't trade." Preserve capital. If you must engage, fade the exact edges of the established range with hard stops and take quick profits, expecting mean-reversion, not trend. Recognizing a non-tradable regime is itself a professional skill; forcing trades in chop is how good weeks turn into bad ones.

## Drill 13 — Sector-index and stock alignment

**Nifty IT (weekly):** Fresh breakout above a year-long base, strong.
**Infosys (daily):** In a tight bullish flag just under 1,600 resistance.
**Infosys (1-hour):** Coiling, volume drying up, right at 1,595.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** LONG on the breakout trigger, high conviction — this adds a fourth dimension: the sector. The parent sector index (Nifty IT) breaking out of a long base is a powerful tailwind; leading stocks in a breaking-out sector are prime longs. Infosys's daily flag under 1,600 is a continuation pattern, and the hourly volume dry-up inside the coil is the classic pre-breakout signature. Entry on the hourly break above 1,600 with volume expansion, stop below the flag low ~1,575, target measured-move from the flag pole plus prior structure toward 1,660. The lesson: when the sector index and the stock line up across timeframes, your edge multiplies — you're trading a strong stock in a strong sector in an aligned structure. Always check the sector index above the stock; it is a de-facto higher timeframe of breadth.

## Drill 14 — The exhaustion gap

**Weekly:** Extended uptrend, price 30% above the 20W EMA — very stretched.
**Daily:** Gapped up 4% today to a new high on a euphoric news headline, then closed near the low of the day forming a bearish gap-up-and-reverse (a shooting star / island risk).
**1-hour:** Made a lower low into the close, breaking the morning's structure.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** EXIT longs; potential SHORT for the nimble. A gap up to a new high in an already over-extended weekly trend, that reverses to close near the day's low, is a textbook exhaustion signal — the last buyers have been used up. The daily bearish reversal plus the hourly lower low into the close is your confirmation. First priority: protect gains, exit or tighten stops hard on existing longs. Aggressive traders can short on a break of today's low, stop above today's high, targeting the gap-fill and the daily 20-EMA. But respect that shorting a strong trend is lower-probability; size accordingly. The multi-timeframe insight: an over-extended higher timeframe raises the odds that a lower-timeframe reversal is *the* top rather than a routine dip. Stretch + exhaustion candle + lower-TF break = the highest-quality counter-trend signal there is.

## Drill 15 — Building the habit

**Scenario:** You have a great daily setup on SBI — a clean cup-and-handle just under 640. You are excited. You have NOT looked at the weekly or the hourly.

**Your decision?**

**Answer:** STOP — do not trade until you've completed the timeframe stack. This drill has no chart data on purpose. The answer is procedural: never act on a single-timeframe setup, no matter how clean, until you've checked the timeframe above (for permission) and below (for the trigger). Pull the weekly: is SBI in an uptrend, or is this cup forming under falling weekly resistance? Pull the hourly: is there a low-risk trigger, or must you buy the daily close and bloat your stop? The excitement you feel about a single beautiful chart is exactly the emotion that leads to context-blind entries. Your routine must be mechanical: higher TF first, always, before the setup can even qualify. Discipline is checking all three even when the one in front of you looks perfect — *especially* then.

## Interview-ready summary

- **The three timeframes have three jobs:** higher = bias/permission, trading = setup, lower = trigger/stop. Never let one do another's job.
- **A+ trades occur at timeframe alignment (continuity):** higher and trading TF agree on direction, and the lower TF pulls back and then turns back into the trend — you buy the dip inside the uptrend (or sell the bounce inside the downtrend).
- **Conflict = smaller size or no trade.** When the middle timeframe opposes the higher one, the trend is pulling back; trade small until they realign, using position size as your risk lever.
- **The higher timeframe wins ties.** A beautiful lower-timeframe pattern against a hostile higher timeframe is usually a trap (Drills 2, 3, 14).
- **Failed breakouts, exhaustion gaps at stretch, and confluence-support halts** are among the highest-quality multi-timeframe signals because they combine location (higher TF) with a precise lower-TF trigger.
- **"No trade" is a valid, frequent answer** (Drills 5, 12) — recognizing incoherent or chop regimes preserves the capital that funds the A+ setups.
- **Always check the sector index** above an individual stock; it functions as an extra breadth-based higher timeframe (Drill 13).
- **The routine is mechanical:** higher TF first for permission, then qualify the setup, then trigger and stop on the lower TF — every time, especially when the single chart in front of you looks irresistible.
