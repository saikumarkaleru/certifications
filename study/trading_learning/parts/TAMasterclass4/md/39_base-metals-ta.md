# Base Metals TA (Copper, Zinc, Aluminium)

Base metals are the macro-economist's commodities. Where gold trades on fear and natural gas trades on weather, copper, zinc and aluminium trade on the pulse of global industrial demand — construction, autos, power grids, and above all China. For the MCX technician, the base-metals complex offers something the precious metals and energy contracts often lack: cleaner trends, better respect for classical technical levels, and a strong intermarket logic you can lean on. This chapter is a playbook for the three most-traded MCX base metals: what moves them, how their levels behave, the setups that work, a worked copper trade, and the risk discipline the complex demands.

## The instruments' character & drivers

The MCX base-metals contracts are rupee mirrors of the London Metal Exchange (LME) benchmarks, converted through USDINR. As with all MCX commodities, this means you are trading the LME price and the rupee simultaneously — and the LME is the leading chart you must watch on TradingView (or via LME/CME references). When the LME 3-month copper price moves in London afternoon trade, MCX copper follows.

The three contracts, with their MCX specifications and personalities:

| Metal | MCX lot size | Tick value (approx) | Personality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | 2,500 kg | Re 0.05/kg = Rs 125/tick | "Dr. Copper" — the macro bellwether, cleanest trends |
| Zinc | 5,000 kg | Rs 0.05/kg = Rs 250/tick | Supply-story metal, sharp squeezes |
| Aluminium | 5,000 kg | Rs 0.05/kg = Rs 250/tick | Energy-cost driven, slower, range-prone |

**Copper — the bellwether.** Copper earned its "Dr. Copper" nickname because its price is a real-time barometer of global economic health. It is in everything electrified: wiring, motors, transformers, EVs, and now the entire renewable-energy and grid build-out. The structural bull case for copper this decade is electrification and the supply deficit from underinvestment in new mines. On MCX, copper has traded roughly Rs 650 to Rs 950 per kg in recent years, tracking LME's roughly $7,500 to $11,000 per tonne range. Copper's drivers: Chinese PMI and property data, US and EU manufacturing indices, the US dollar (inverse), LME warehouse inventories, and mine-supply news from Chile, Peru and the DRC (strikes, grade declines, permitting).

**Zinc — the supply-squeeze metal.** Zinc is used overwhelmingly for galvanizing steel (rust protection), so its demand tracks construction and infrastructure. But zinc's price action is dominated by supply: mine closures, smelter treatment-charge collapses, and LME inventory drawdowns produce sharp, sometimes vicious backwardation-driven squeezes. Zinc can sit dead for months then rip 15-20% on a smelter shutdown. On MCX it has ranged roughly Rs 210 to Rs 320 per kg.

**Aluminium — the electricity metal.** Aluminium is often called "congealed electricity" because smelting is extraordinarily power-intensive — energy is 30-40% of the cost of production. Its price therefore tracks energy costs (especially European and Chinese power prices) as much as demand. Aluminium is the slowest, most range-bound of the three and the most prone to long consolidations. Drivers: Chinese production caps and the 45-million-tonne capacity ceiling, European smelter curtailments during energy crises, and demand from packaging, autos and construction. MCX aluminium has ranged roughly Rs 200 to Rs 280 per kg.

The unifying driver across all three is **China**, which consumes roughly half the world's base metals. Chinese stimulus announcements, property-sector news, and monthly PMI prints move the entire complex together. The second unifying driver is the **US dollar** — base metals are dollar-priced globally, so a strong DXY is a headwind and a weak DXY a tailwind. As a base-metals technician you must read these two macro inputs the way an equity trader reads the index trend.

## Key levels & behaviour

Base metals reward classical technical analysis better than the more news-whipped energy contracts. Here is how their levels behave:

**Trends are cleaner and longer.** Because base-metals moves are driven by slow-changing macro cycles (economic expansion/contraction, multi-year supply deficits), trends in copper especially can run for months with orderly pullbacks. Moving averages — the 50-day and 200-day in particular — are genuinely respected. A copper trend that holds its rising 50-DMA is a trend you can position with.

**LME inventories are a hidden level.** Watch LME warehouse stock levels. Falling inventories toward multi-year lows signal tightness and support prices; a low-stock backwardation can precede a squeeze in zinc especially. This is a fundamental "level" that overlays the chart.

**Round numbers matter, in both currencies.** On LME, copper respects the $500 increments ($8,000, $8,500, $9,000, $10,000) and $10,000 is a decade-defining psychological ceiling/floor. On MCX, the Rs 25 shelves (Rs 750, Rs 775, Rs 800) act as pause points.

**China-session vs London-session behaviour.** The MCX base-metals day (9:00 AM to 11:30 PM) spans both the Asian session (China/SHFE price discovery in the morning) and the London/LME session (afternoon-evening). Morning moves often reflect Chinese data and SHFE positioning; the biggest directional moves frequently come in the 1:00-7:00 PM IST window as London and then the US come online. Mark the previous LME close and the SHFE overnight move before the MCX open.

**Zinc's squeeze signature.** Watch for zinc going into backwardation with falling LME stocks — this is the setup for a supply squeeze that ignores normal resistance and gaps through levels. Aluminium, by contrast, respects range boundaries for long stretches until an energy-cost shock breaks the range.

## Best setups

**1. Copper trend-following on the 50-DMA pullback.** This is the flagship base-metals setup. In a macro copper uptrend (rising 50 and 200-DMA, weak dollar, Chinese stimulus in play), buy pullbacks to the rising 50-DMA or the anchored VWAP from the trend's origin. Copper's clean trends make this genuinely repeatable. Confirm with the LME chart and the DXY (you want the dollar rolling over or flat, not surging). Stop below the 50-DMA and the pullback swing low.

**2. Base-metals breakout from a multi-week base.** All three metals build long consolidations. A breakout above a well-defined multi-week range on a strong close, with LME confirming and ideally LME inventories falling, is a high-quality continuation setup. Aluminium and zinc especially spend long periods coiling and then trend once the range breaks. Volume expansion on the break is the confirmation.

**3. The zinc/aluminium supply-squeeze momentum trade.** When a genuine supply story hits (smelter curtailment, mine closure, energy shock), the metal can trend hard for days or weeks. The technical job is to join the trend on the first pullback after the initial gap, not to fade the news. These moves are momentum-driven and mean-reversion tools will get you run over.

**4. The relative-strength pairs read.** Because the complex moves together on China/dollar, the strongest technical trades come when one metal is leading. If copper is breaking out on its own supply story while aluminium lags, copper is the cleaner long. Reading intra-complex relative strength (which metal is making new highs, which is lagging) sharpens instrument selection.

**5. Dollar-inverse timing overlay.** Overlay the DXY inverted against copper. When the dollar is topping and rolling over, base metals get a tailwind; time your long entries to coincide with dollar weakness for the strongest setups. This intermarket confirmation is more reliable in base metals than in most other commodities.

## A worked example

A realistic 2025 copper trade. Through the first quarter, the structural electrification narrative and a series of mine-supply disruptions in Chile had kept copper firm. LME copper based above $9,000 and MCX copper held its rising 50-DMA around Rs 830. The US dollar index, meanwhile, had topped near 108 and begun rolling over as US rate-cut expectations built — a clean tailwind.

In April, a warmer round of Chinese stimulus (property-support measures plus an infrastructure package) hit the wires. LME copper broke $9,500 resistance and pushed toward $9,800. MCX copper rose from Rs 838 to Rs 872 over five sessions. A disciplined trader doesn't chase the vertical break; they wait for the pullback.

On the sixth session, a mildly disappointing Chinese industrial-production print triggered a two-day pullback. MCX copper fell from Rs 872 toward Rs 852, tagging the rising anchored VWAP and sitting just above the Rs 850 round-number shelf. On the daily chart, the pullback held well above the 50-DMA at Rs 838 — the trend structure was intact. On the 1-hour chart, a bullish hammer formed at Rs 852 during the London session with the LME chart printing the same reversal, and the DXY continued to weaken. Confluence: trend intact, VWAP support, round number, dollar tailwind, LME confirmation.

The trade:
- **Entry:** Rs 854 on the hammer close, 1 lot (2,500 kg).
- **Stop:** Rs 841, below the pullback low and above the 50-DMA. Risk = Rs 13/kg = Rs 32,500 per lot.
- **Target 1:** Rs 872 (prior high), book half.
- **Target 2:** Rs 895, trail under the rising VWAP.

Over the following two weeks the electrification bid and continued dollar weakness carried LME copper to $10,100 and MCX to Rs 898 before a dollar-bounce-driven pullback took the trailing stop out at Rs 886. Result: +Rs 18/kg on the first half (Rs 45,000) and +Rs 32/kg on the second half (Rs 80,000) against Rs 32,500 risk — a blended reward near 3.8R.

The honest counter-scenario: had China instead announced a disappointing stimulus dud and the dollar bounced hard, LME copper would have failed at $9,500 and rolled over. MCX copper would break Rs 850, then Rs 838 (the 50-DMA), stopping the trade at Rs 841 for a clean Rs 13/kg (Rs 32,500) loss. Because copper trends are cleaner than energy, the stop would likely fill near the level rather than gapping violently through it — one of the reasons base metals are a more forgiving complex than natural gas.

## Risk notes

**Mind the lot value.** Copper's 2,500 kg lot and zinc/aluminium's 5,000 kg lots mean rupee moves add up fast. A Rs 20/kg move in copper is Rs 50,000 per lot; in zinc it is Rs 1,00,000 per lot. Size positions off the rupee risk of the stop, not off "number of lots feels affordable."

**The complex is correlated — don't fake diversification.** Copper, zinc and aluminium move together on China and the dollar. Holding one lot each of all three is not three diversified trades; it is roughly one big base-metals bet. Aggregate your exposure and cap total complex risk at 2-3% of capital.

**Watch the dollar and China calendar.** The two scheduled risks are the FOMC/dollar events and the monthly Chinese data dump (PMI, industrial production, property sales, typically released around the 15th). These move the whole complex. Trim or hedge size into these prints.

**Zinc squeezes cut both ways.** Zinc's supply squeezes are violent up and violent down. When a squeeze exhausts (inventories rebuild, smelters restart), the reversal is fast. Do not marry a zinc position after a parabolic run — take momentum profits and stand aside.

**Use LME as truth.** As with all MCX metals, if MCX and LME diverge, LME leads and a stale USDINR print is distorting the mirror. Keep the LME chart open.

**Volatility-scale stops.** Base metals are less explosive than natural gas but far from calm; use ATR-based stops (1.5-2x the 14-period ATR). Copper's cleaner trends allow slightly tighter stops than energy, but never tuck a stop just under an obvious round number where everyone else's stop sits.

The honest summary: base metals are the most "technical-friendly" of the MCX commodities because macro cycles produce genuine trends and the levels are respected — but that friendliness is a trap for the over-leveraged. The large lot values mean a clean-looking chart can still hand you a Rs 50,000 loss on a single stop-out. Trade the trend, confirm with LME and the dollar, size for the rupee risk, and let copper's cleaner structure work for you rather than betting the account on a single squeeze.
