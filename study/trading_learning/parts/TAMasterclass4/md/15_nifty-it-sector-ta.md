# IT Sector TA

If Bank Nifty is the market's amplifier, Nifty IT is its shock absorber and its currency hedge rolled into one. The Indian IT index behaves unlike any domestic-facing sector: it dances to the tune of the US economy, the rupee, and global technology spending rather than to the RBI or the monsoon. That foreign DNA makes it a superb diversifier and a distinctive trading vehicle — it leads when the rupee weakens and global risk appetite for tech is high, and it becomes a defensive port when domestic cyclicals wobble. This chapter is a playbook for reading and trading Nifty IT: its character and drivers, the levels and behaviour that matter, the best setups, a worked example, and the risk notes.

## The instrument's character & drivers

**Nifty IT** is a top-heavy index dominated by a few global-scale services giants, with a mid-cap tail of newer names. Like Bank Nifty, it is a *concentrated* index — the two or three largest constituents drive most of the move, and their quarterly results and guidance can gap the whole index. But its drivers are almost entirely *external*, which is what gives it a unique chart personality.

**The three master drivers:**

1. **USDINR (the rupee).** Indian IT earns in dollars and reports in rupees. A weakening rupee (USDINR rising) directly inflates IT revenue and margins — it is a structural tailwind. A sharp rupee *strengthening* is a headwind. This is why Nifty IT and USDINR are positively correlated over the medium term, and why IT often rallies precisely when the broader market is nervous about a falling rupee. **IT is the market's natural rupee-depreciation hedge.**

2. **US economy & tech spending.** The bulk of Indian IT revenue comes from North America (BFSI, retail, tech clients). US recession fears, banking-sector stress among clients, or a pullback in enterprise/discretionary tech budgets hit order books directly. Nifty IT frequently tracks the *Nasdaq* more than it tracks Nifty — a heavy Nasdaq sell-off overnight is a bigger tell for the IT open than anything domestic.

3. **The AI / demand-narrative cycle.** From 2023 onward, the market's read on whether AI is a *tailwind* (new deal pipelines, productivity) or a *threat* (deflation of the traditional services model, client insourcing) has driven multi-quarter re-ratings and de-ratings. Guidance commentary on AI deal wins moves the index on results day.

**Character traits:**

- **Defensive, lower domestic beta.** IT is less sensitive to RBI policy, budgets and local politics than Banks or Realty. In a domestic-risk-off phase, money often rotates *into* IT and FMCG. It cushions portfolios.
- **Event-cluster behaviour.** IT trades in long calm ranges punctuated by violent quarterly-results gaps. The heavyweights all report within a tight window at the start of each earnings season, so the whole sector re-rates in a two-week burst four times a year.
- **Global correlation.** More than any domestic sector, IT gaps on *overnight US cues*. Your pre-market read must include Nasdaq futures and USDINR, not just SGX/GIFT Nifty.
- **Rotation partner.** IT is frequently the mirror image of PSU Banks/Capital Goods in the rotation cycle — when domestic cyclicals lead, IT lags; when the rupee weakens and cyclicals tire, IT takes leadership. It is a classic *late-cycle / defensive* rotation into which capital flees.

|---|---|---|
| USDINR | Weak rupee → margin/revenue tailwind | USDINR spot & trend |
| Nasdaq / US tech | Client-spend proxy; drives the gap | Nasdaq close & futures |

## Key levels & behaviour

**The USDINR overlay.** The single most useful non-price tool for IT is a USDINR chart alongside the Nifty IT chart. When both are rising, the IT uptrend has a structural tailwind and pullbacks are buyable. When Nifty IT rises but USDINR is *falling* (rupee strengthening), the rally lacks its natural fuel and is more likely a technical bounce than a durable trend. Divergence between the two is a caution flag.

**Overnight gap primacy.** IT is a *gap* index. Because the heavyweights carry large ADR/US listings and the clientele is American, the index frequently opens away from the prior close on US cues. Prior-day close, prior-day high/low, and the results-day gap levels become the key reference points. Intraday, many IT trades are simply gap-fill or gap-and-go plays off the open.

**Results-season regime shift.** Outside earnings season, IT often drifts in tight, low-volatility ranges — frustrating for momentum traders. The *character changes completely* during results: the first heavyweight to report sets the tone, and the index can trend hard for two weeks as each name reports. Structure your IT trading calendar around this: expect range outside season, expect trend and gaps within it.

**Weekly / daily structure.** The 20- and 50-EMA define the medium trend; the 200-DMA is the regime line. IT tends to build long bases (multi-month accumulation ranges) and then break out on a demand-narrative shift or a decisive rupee move — those base breakouts, confirmed by volume and a rising USDINR, are among the cleaner positional setups in the market.

**Relative strength read.** Track the Nifty IT / Nifty ratio. A rising RS line means IT is leading — this often coincides with rupee weakness or domestic risk-off. A rising RS *in a falling market* is the defensive-rotation signal: capital is hiding in IT. Falling RS in a roaring domestic bull means IT is the funding source (being sold to buy cyclicals).

## Best setups

### Setup 1 — Base breakout with USDINR confirmation (positional, highest quality)

On the daily/weekly chart, identify a multi-week consolidation base under a clear resistance. Enter on a volume-backed breakout **only when USDINR is also trending up or stable** and the IT RS line is rising. Stop below the base low. Target a measured move (base height projected from the breakout). The USDINR filter is the edge — an IT breakout against a strengthening rupee is far more likely to fail. This is the setup with the best positional expectancy in IT.

### Setup 2 — Results gap-and-go (event-driven)

When a heavyweight reports strong numbers and *raises or reassures on guidance*, the stock and the index gap up and often *continue* trending for days as analysts upgrade and the sector re-rates. Enter on strength after the gap holds the first 30–60 minutes above the opening range; stop below the gap-day low. Conversely, a guidance *cut* produces a gap-down-and-go short. The key discipline: let the gap *hold* before entering — fading the initial results spike blind is how traders get run over.

### Setup 3 — Defensive rotation long (macro-timed swing)

When the domestic market turns risk-off (Nifty cracking, cyclicals selling) *and* the rupee is weakening, IT frequently absorbs the rotating capital. Go long IT (or the strongest IT constituent) as its RS line turns up while the broad market falls — you are buying the sector money is fleeing *into*. Stop under the recent swing low. This is a counter-market trade justified entirely by RS and the USDINR tailwind, not by hoping.

### Setup 4 — Range fade outside earnings season (intraday/short swing)

Outside results season IT oscillates in defined ranges. Buy well-defined support and sell well-defined resistance with reversal candles and VWAP confirmation intraday, keeping targets modest and stops tight. This is a low-volatility, small-target grind — appropriate *only* in the calm between earnings, and to be abandoned the moment results season injects trend and gaps.

### Setup 5 — Rupee-driven pairs (market-neutral, advanced)

Because IT (export) and domestic cyclicals (import/rate-sensitive) respond oppositely to the rupee, a **long Nifty IT / short a domestic-cyclical basket** pair expresses a rupee-weakness view while hedging out broad-market direction. It profits from the *rotation* itself. Requires managing two legs and correlation, but it is a clean way to trade the IT theme without taking naked directional index risk.

## A worked example

**Setup: a rupee-depreciation, defensive-rotation window.** The rupee has been weakening steadily (USDINR making higher highs) on a strong-dollar global backdrop. Domestic cyclicals — Banks, Realty — are correcting after a long run, and Nifty is choppy-to-weak. On the daily chart, Nifty IT has spent several weeks building a tight base under a clear resistance zone, and its RS line versus Nifty has quietly turned up — capital is rotating in defensively.

**The signal.** A large heavyweight reports in-line results but strikes a *confident tone on AI deal wins and raises the revenue guidance band*. The stock gaps up on heavy volume, and Nifty IT gaps above its multi-week base resistance. USDINR is still trending up (tailwind intact). The gap holds through the first hour above the opening range. RS is rising. Every filter aligns: rupee tailwind, defensive-rotation flow, base breakout, results catalyst, heavyweight participation.

**The trade.**
- **Entry:** after the results-day gap holds the opening range, on the confirmed breakout above the base.
- **Stop:** below the gap-day low / base resistance-turned-support — a structural stop, not arbitrary.
- **Size:** 1% capital risk off the real stop distance. Because IT gaps hard on subsequent heavyweight results still to come that season, prefer a defined-risk structure over a naked position into the remaining earnings dates.
- **Target:** measured move = base height projected up from the breakout; book partial there, trail the rest under higher lows.

**Management.** Over the next two weeks the remaining heavyweights report and each in-line-to-good print extends the sector trend — exactly the "results-season regime shift." You book the first partial at the measured-move target and trail the balance. When USDINR eventually stalls and rolls over (rupee strengthening) *and* IT's RS flattens, the two-pillar case (rupee + rotation) weakens; you tighten stops and exit into strength. The exit is signalled by the *driver* (rupee turn) confirming the *chart* (RS flattening), not by price alone.

**Why it worked:** the trade stacked a macro tailwind (weak rupee), a flow tailwind (defensive rotation, rising RS), a chart trigger (base breakout), and a fundamental catalyst (guidance raise) with heavyweight confirmation — then managed risk with a structural stop and a driver-based exit.

## Risk notes

**Overnight gap risk is structural, not occasional.** IT gaps on US cues you cannot see coming while the Indian market is closed. A naked overnight IT position is exposed to Nasdaq moves and results-day guidance shocks. Size for gaps, use defined-risk option structures across earnings dates, and never assume a stop-loss will fill at your price on a results gap — it won't.

**Earnings-season concentration.** All the heavyweights report in a tight window, so IT-specific gap risk clusters four times a year. Know the exact results calendar. Being caught leveraged *through* a heavyweight's guidance miss is the classic IT account-killer. Trade the move *after* the print, not blindly *through* it.

**The rupee can betray the chart.** A technically clean IT breakout can fail if the rupee reverses and strengthens sharply (e.g., a dovish Fed weakening the dollar). Always cross-check USDINR before trusting an IT long. IT trades that ignore the rupee are trading half the picture.

**Narrative-driven volatility (AI).** The AI-tailwind-vs-AI-threat debate can swing IT sentiment violently on commentary and headlines, not just numbers. This makes IT prone to sharp sentiment gaps disconnected from near-term fundamentals. Respect that the index can re-rate on *tone* — position size accordingly and don't marry a thesis.

**Range-bound frustration outside season.** Between earnings, IT can grind sideways for weeks with low volatility. Forcing momentum trades in that calm bleeds capital via whipsaws and costs. Match the tactic to the regime: range-fade small in the calm, trend-trade in the season — do not run trend systems in the drift.

**Correlation and diversification illusion.** IT feels like a "hedge," but in a broad global risk-off (Nasdaq crashing, everything sold), IT falls *with* the market despite its defensive reputation — its US linkage cuts both ways. Do not overstate IT's diversification benefit during global-liquidity shocks.

**The honest probability.** As with every index, most retail option buyers in IT lose to theta and gap slippage. The genuine edge in IT is specificity: trade base breakouts confirmed by a rising rupee, trade the results move after the gap holds, use IT as a defensive-rotation vehicle when RS turns up in a weak market, and always — *always* — check USDINR and the earnings calendar before committing. IT rewards the trader who reads the rupee and the results tape; it punishes the one who treats it like just another domestic index.
