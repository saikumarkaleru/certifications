# Rapid-Fire Question Bank I

The mock tests train deep, multi-step reasoning under a moving tape. This chapter trains the opposite muscle: **instant recall** — the crisp, one-breath answers that a trading-desk interview, a viva, or your own pre-market checklist demands. There are **80 rapid-fire questions** across breadth, sentiment, intermarket, sector/stock TA (India), commodities/currency, risk, and psychology. Each answer is deliberately short — one to four sentences — because the whole point is to be able to say it *fast and correctly*.

**How to use it.** Cover the answers. Read a question, say your answer aloud in under ten seconds, then check. Track a running score. **Target: 65/80.** Below 55, the concept is not yet automatic — and a concept you can't recall in ten seconds is a concept you won't use in a live trade. Cycle the bank three times over a week; by the third pass the weak spots are obvious. Everything is India-first: NSE indices, real F&O names, MCX, USDINR, rupees, 2026-realistic levels.

## Set 1 — Breadth (Q1–Q12)

**Q1.** What does market breadth measure?
**A.** How *many* stocks are participating in a move, versus how much the cap-weighted index price moved — the health *underneath* the print.

**Q2.** Index at a new high, advance-decline line falling. Read?
**A.** Bearish breadth divergence — the rally is narrowing to a few heavyweights; average stock has already topped. Caution on longs.

**Q3.** What does "% of Nifty 500 above 50-DMA" tell you?
**A.** A breadth-participation gauge: falling % while the index rises = deteriorating internals; a rising % confirms a broad move.

**Q4.** What is a breadth thrust?
**A.** A sudden surge in the proportion of advancing stocks (e.g. from washed-out to overwhelmingly positive) — often marks the *start* of a durable rally off a low.

**Q5.** New highs shrinking while new lows expand under a flat index — what does it warn?
**A.** Internal deterioration; the index level is hollow/unreliable and a down-leg is likely.

**Q6.** What is the McClellan-style advance-decline oscillator used for?
**A.** To smooth net advances-minus-declines and flag overbought/oversold breadth extremes and divergences.

**Q7.** Midcap/large-cap ratio line falling — regime?
**A.** Risk-off rotation; money fleeing high-beta midcaps for large-cap safety. Favor large caps.

**Q8.** Why can an index rise on poor breadth for a while?
**A.** Because it is cap-weighted — two or three mega-caps (e.g. Reliance, HDFC Bank) can carry it even as most stocks fall.

**Q9.** What breadth condition supports a *high-conviction* index long?
**A.** New high confirmed by rising A-D line, expanding new highs, and a majority of stocks above their 50-DMA — broad participation.

**Q10.** What is the "generals vs soldiers" idea?
**A.** When only the large-cap "generals" advance while the broad "soldiers" retreat, leadership is narrow and the advance is fragile.

**Q11.** Nifty falls but advance-decline improves (fewer declines each day). Read?
**A.** Positive breadth divergence — selling is drying up internally; a bottom may be forming.

**Q12.** Why check breadth *before* trusting a breakout?
**A.** A breakout on narrow participation is more likely to fail; broad participation confirms real demand.

## Set 2 — Sentiment & Options Positioning (Q13–Q26)

**Q13.** What does India VIX measure?
**A.** The market's expected 30-day Nifty volatility implied by option prices — the "fear gauge."

**Q14.** VIX at a 12-month low — what does it signal?
**A.** Complacency, not safety; cheapest premium and highest risk of a volatility spike. Worst time for naked short vol.

**Q15.** VIX spikes into an RBI/Budget event — sell it?
**A.** No — that's correct pricing of a known binary; short vol into a scheduled catalyst is asymmetric risk. Sell the *post-event* crush instead.

**Q16.** What is PCR (put-call ratio)?
**A.** Put OI (or volume) divided by call OI — a positioning/sentiment gauge; high = many puts, low = many calls.

**Q17.** Is a high PCR always bullish-contrarian?
**A.** No. Mid-decline it reflects genuine hedging/momentum. It's only contrarian at an extreme *coinciding* with a price reversal.

**Q18.** What is max pain?
**A.** The strike at which the greatest rupee value of options expires worthless — price tends to gravitate there near expiry as writers defend.

**Q19.** Heavy call OI at 26,000, heavy put OI at 25,800 — what are these?
**A.** OI walls — resistance (call wall) and support (put wall); price tends to pin between them into expiry.

**Q20.** What breaks OI walls?
**A.** A large exogenous shock or aggressive directional futures flow that forces writers to unwind/hedge — turning a pin into a squeeze.

**Q21.** Unusually high Nifty futures premium + high rollover — read?
**A.** Crowded, leveraged longs; complacent optimism and elevated unwind risk.

**Q22.** Futures in discount to spot — what can it imply?
**A.** Bearish positioning / short build-up, or dividend/cost-of-carry effects; often risk-off sentiment.

**Q23.** Call writers add at 57,000, put writers at 56,000 on Bank Nifty — what's implied?
**A.** A range-bound expectation of ~56,000–57,000; writers boxing the move.

**Q24.** Best structure to trade an expected range?
**A.** A short iron condor — defined-risk, profits from time decay if price stays boxed.

**Q25.** What does an F&O ban on a stock signal?
**A.** Extreme crowding — market-wide OI hit 95% of the limit; consensus-maxed and prone to violent unwind. Only position reduction allowed.

**Q26.** Long build-up vs short covering — how to tell from OI + price?
**A.** Price up + OI up = long build-up (fresh bulls); price up + OI down = short covering (bears exiting).

## Set 3 — Intermarket & Currency (Q27–Q40)

**Q27.** Rupee strengthens (USDINR falls) — who is hurt?
**A.** Exporters — Nifty IT (TCS, Infosys) and pharma — lose on rupee translation of dollar revenue.

**Q28.** Rupee weakens (USDINR rises) — who benefits?
**A.** Exporters (IT, pharma); hurts importers and oil-heavy sectors and widens the current-account deficit.

**Q29.** Brent crude spikes — net Nifty bias?
**A.** Negative — India imports ~85% of oil; hurts OMCs, paints, tyres, aviation, weakens rupee, raises inflation.

**Q30.** Who *benefits* from a crude spike in India?
**A.** Upstream producers — ONGC, Oil India — on higher realizations.

**Q31.** US 10-year yield spikes + strong dollar — effect on FII flows?
**A.** Outflows from Indian equities as US assets get relatively more attractive; risk-off for Nifty.

**Q32.** Falling DXY + strong rupee + soft crude + easing yields — regime?
**A.** Risk-on for emerging markets; capital flows into India, led by financials.

**Q33.** Which sector leads a risk-on FII-inflow regime in India?
**A.** Banks/Financials — the FII favorites and largest index weight.

**Q34.** Gold and Nifty rising together — two causes?
**A.** Falling real rates / abundant liquidity lifting all assets, and/or a weakening rupee lifting MCX (rupee-priced) gold.

**Q35.** Why watch USDINR when trading Nifty IT?
**A.** IT earnings are dollar-linked; the rupee is often a bigger short-term driver than the company's operational results.

**Q36.** Rising USDINR — IT vs FMCG bias?
**A.** Bullish IT (exporters gain), headwind for import-cost-heavy FMCG.

**Q37.** Metals stocks rip but LME metal price is flat — trade or fade?
**A.** Fade/avoid — without underlying-metal confirmation it's a headline pop, not a cyclical trend.

**Q38.** Defensive sectors (FMCG) leading a falling Nifty — what regime?
**A.** Risk-off / late-cycle flight to safety; reduce beta, raise cash.

**Q39.** How does higher crude feed into rate-sensitive sectors?
**A.** Crude → inflation up → rate-cut hopes fade → Realty, Auto, Banks pressured by higher-for-longer rates.

**Q40.** What is intermarket analysis in one line?
**A.** Reading how bonds, currencies, commodities and equities move *together* to infer the driver and bias behind a market.

## Set 4 — Sector & Stock TA, India (Q41–Q54)

**Q41.** Bank Nifty near record but two constituents below 200-DMA — healthy or hollow?
**A.** Hollow — leaning on one or two leaders; fragile because financials are highly correlated.

**Q42.** What confirms a *healthy* Bank Nifty advance?
**A.** Broad participation — laggards reclaiming their 200-DMAs and a majority of constituents above their 50-DMA.

**Q43.** Best way to pick one long from a strong sector?
**A.** Relative strength *plus* the cleanest base structure and nearest logical stop — not the most extended chart.

**Q44.** Why is "buy the strongest chart" a trap?
**A.** The most-extended name is often the most overbought — worst entry, widest stop, least room.

**Q45.** Long upper wick, close in lower third, 3× volume at resistance — read?
**A.** Rejection/distribution — supply overwhelmed demand; bearish reversal warning.

**Q46.** Gap up on 4× volume clearing a 6-month base, then holds above the level — pattern?
**A.** Confirmed breakaway-gap breakout with successful retest; invalidation is a decisive close back below the breakout pivot.

**Q47.** Seven-week tight range, declining volume, Bollinger width at 12-month low — character?
**A.** Volatility contraction preceding expansion — a coiled spring; trade the break or buy volatility, don't pre-guess direction.

**Q48.** Execution risk specific to Adani/PSU F&O names?
**A.** High volatility, wide spreads and gap risk — stops slip badly; size smaller and use ATR-based stops.

**Q49.** Nifty Pharma at new high while Nifty stalls — what does it tell you?
**A.** Sector relative strength; concentrate longs in the leading sector, but still pick the best *structure* within it.

**Q50.** Why trade in the direction of the sector, not against it?
**A.** A stock inherits its sector's flow; a long in a weak sector fights the tide and fails more often.

**Q51.** HDFC Bank basing above 200-DMA on rising volume — read?
**A.** Constructive accumulation; leader building a base to support the index.

**Q52.** What is a relative-strength (RS) line and its use?
**A.** Stock price divided by the index — rising RS = outperformance; pick longs with rising RS, shorts with falling RS.

**Q53.** Three hammers at the same support, each failed — what does it foreshadow?
**A.** A breakdown — repeated failed defenses consume buyers; over-tested support tends to break.

**Q54.** Golden cross with price already 15% above 200-DMA, RSI 78 — act?
**A.** No — it's a lagging signal here; wait for a pullback to the rising averages. Crosses confirm trend, they don't time entries.

## Set 5 — Risk & Position Sizing (Q55–Q68)

**Q55.** What is the 1% rule?
**A.** Risk no more than 1% of account equity on any single trade — the cornerstone of survival.

**Q56.** Position size is a function of what?
**A.** Risk budget ÷ (stop distance × point/lot value) — size is an *output*, never an emotional input.

**Q57.** Account ₹10,00,000, 1% risk, stop loses ₹40 premium, lot 75 — how many lots?
**A.** ₹10,000 ÷ (₹40 × 75 = ₹3,000) = 3.33 → **3 lots** (always round down).

**Q58.** Margin vs risk — the beginner's confusion?
**A.** Margin is what you *can* buy; risk (stop × value) is what you *should* buy. Sizing off margin blows accounts.

**Q59.** What is R?
**A.** One unit of initial risk — the rupee amount from entry to stop. Everything is measured in multiples of R.

**Q60.** Expectancy formula?
**A.** (Win% × avg win in R) − (Loss% × avg loss in R). Positive = tradable edge.

**Q61.** 40% wins, +2.5R avg win, −1R avg loss — expectancy?
**A.** (0.4×2.5) − (0.6×1) = **+0.4R per trade** — a strong edge despite losing more often than winning.

**Q62.** Why does a 40% win-rate system feel awful?
**A.** You lose more often than you win and hit frequent 4–6 loss streaks; the discomfort makes traders quit a profitable system.

**Q63.** Why is a 40% drawdown far worse than it sounds?
**A.** It needs a ~67% gain just to break even — drawdowns are mathematically asymmetric.

**Q64.** MAR-style ratio for two systems: 30%/40% DD vs 18%/12% DD — which wins?
**A.** 18/12 = 1.5 beats 30/40 = 0.75 — the shallower-drawdown system is more efficient and survivable.

**Q65.** Why use ATR-based stops instead of a fixed rupee stop?
**A.** Stops must sit beyond an instrument's normal noise; ATR adapts to volatility, then you size so the ATR stop equals your risk budget.

**Q66.** Five positions: Nifty, Bank Nifty, ICICI, SBI, Axis — real risk?
**A.** One correlated bet on financials — beta-weighted exposure is several times what it looks like.

**Q67.** What metric catches hidden correlation risk?
**A.** Beta-weighted / correlation-adjusted net exposure (positions summed in index-delta terms).

**Q68.** Concrete trailing-stop method for a trending future?
**A.** Trail beneath each successively higher swing low (or a 2–3×ATR chandelier) — banks structure without choking the trend.

## Set 6 — Psychology & Trade Management (Q69–Q80)

**Q69.** Six valid setups stopped out in a row — what does it prove?
**A.** Nothing — it's normal variance in a positive-expectancy system; the next trade is not "due" to win.

**Q70.** What is revenge trading?
**A.** Increasing size to "make back" losses — maximizing risk exactly when judgment is worst; the classic account-killer.

**Q71.** The one rule that stops revenge trading?
**A.** A hard daily loss limit (e.g. stop at −3R/−3%), size-locked, no exceptions — removes the decision from a tilted brain.

**Q72.** What is the gambler's fallacy in trading?
**A.** Believing a win is "due" after a losing streak; independent trades have no memory.

**Q73.** House-money effect?
**A.** Treating profits as "the market's money" and taking sloppier risk after a good run — protect gains, don't gamble them.

**Q74.** Up 8% with three days left — should risk-per-trade change?
**A.** No — profits don't buy extra risk. Many pros *reduce* size after a strong run to protect the result.

**Q75.** Why is the best system the one you can execute, not the highest-CAGR one?
**A.** Because humans abandon systems in deep drawdowns; a survivable, executable edge compounds, a gut-wrenching one gets quit.

**Q76.** What is confirmation bias on the chart?
**A.** Seeing only signals that support your existing position and ignoring contradicting ones — cures with a pre-defined invalidation level.

**Q77.** Why write the reason before the trade?
**A.** It forces genuine reasoning over post-hoc justification and gives an objective exit if the reason breaks.

**Q78.** Credit spread at 42% of max profit but short strike now ATM near expiry — decision?
**A.** Take the partial profit / small loss and close — the remaining premium doesn't justify sitting through breach risk toward max loss.

**Q79.** A failed breakdown that reclaims support — what does it create?
**A.** A bear trap; trapped shorts must cover, fueling a high-conviction long on the reclaim with stop below the failure low.

**Q80.** The single habit that separates survivors from statistics?
**A.** For every signal, asking "what is the volume, breadth, flow, intermarket driver and crowding *underneath* this price?" — context over pattern.

## Scoring & How to Drill This

Tally your 80. **70–80:** recall is automatic — this material will actually surface in a live trade or interview. **60–69:** strong, with a few soft sets; note which *set* (breadth, sentiment, intermarket, sector, risk, psychology) lagged and re-run it. **Below 60:** the ideas aren't yet reflexive — cycle the full bank daily for a week; speed comes from repetition, not from re-reading the long chapters.

**Interview-ready summary.** A rapid-fire bank exists because trading rewards *automaticity*. In the moment, you don't have time to derive that a low VIX is complacency-not-safety, that position size is an output not an input, that a golden cross at RSI 78 is a lagging trap, or that six losses in a row is variance not failure — you either *know* it instantly or you make the emotional mistake. Recite these until the answers are one-breath reflexes. The traders who survive Indian markets are not the ones with the most exotic indicators; they are the ones for whom the fundamentals — breadth over price, risk before reward, context over pattern, process over outcome — have become so automatic that they hold even when the P&L is bleeding and the tape is screaming.
