# Pattern-Recognition Quiz

This is a *drills* chapter. There is no new theory here — Volumes I–III already taught you candlesticks, chart patterns, Elliott, Wyckoff and the rest. What this chapter does is force you to **read** rather than **recall**. The difference matters enormously. A trader who can recite the definition of a head-and-shoulders and a trader who can *spot one forming, mid-formation, on a live Nifty chart at 10:15 a.m. with money on the line* are two completely different animals. Recognition under pressure is a motor skill, and motor skills are built only by repetition.

Below are 22 scenarios. Each describes a real-feeling India-specific chart situation — Nifty, Bank Nifty, MCX crude, USDINR, real F&O stocks — in words and simple price tables. Your job: name the pattern (or say "no clean pattern"), state the bias, and give the trade logic *before* reading the answer. Cover the answer with your hand. The answer key that follows each block is deliberately long — the reasoning is the lesson, not the label.

## How to use this quiz

1. Read the scenario. Sketch the price path on paper if it helps — literally draw the candles.
2. Write down three things: **(a) pattern name, (b) directional bias, (c) the trigger and invalidation.**
3. Only then read the answer. Score yourself: 1 point for the right pattern, 1 for the right bias, 1 for a sensible trigger/stop. 66/66 is a perfect score.
4. Anything below 45/66 means go back to Volumes I–II before touching real capital. Recognition is not yet automatic for you.

Honesty note before you start: patterns give **edges, not certainties.** A textbook double-bottom on Nifty resolves upward maybe 60–65% of the time in a favourable market, less in a choppy one. The quiz teaches you to see the structure; risk management (next chapters) is what keeps you solvent when the 35% case hits.

## Section A — Candlestick reversals & continuations (Q1–Q7)

**Q1.** RELIANCE, daily. After a five-week slide from ₹1,480 to ₹1,210, a candle prints: open ₹1,214, low ₹1,188, close ₹1,242, high ₹1,246. The prior candle was a long red bearish body. Volume is 1.8× the 20-day average. Name it, bias, trigger.

**Q2.** Bank Nifty, 15-min, mid-session. Price has ranged 47,800–47,950 for two hours. A candle opens 47,905, spikes to 47,990, but closes 47,815 — a long upper wick, tiny body near the low. It sits right at the top of the range on the third touch. Read it.

**Q3.** Nifty daily. Uptrend intact. Three candles: a tall green body, then a small-bodied candle that gaps up and trades entirely above the first body's range, then a tall red candle that closes deep into the first green body. Name the three-candle pattern and bias.

**Q4.** TATASTEEL, daily, in a downtrend near ₹128. A candle with almost no body (open ≈ close at ₹129) and long wicks both sides prints on the third consecutive down-day. Next day opens ₹129, closes ₹136. What was the first candle warning, and what confirms?

**Q5.** MCX Crude Oil (₹/barrel), hourly. Strong up-move. Two candles: a large green body, then a small red body completely contained *inside* the prior green body's real body. Volume drops on the second. Name it, and say what it is NOT.

**Q6.** HDFCBANK daily at an all-time-high breakout. A single candle: opens at the day's low ₹1,720, closes at the day's high ₹1,768, essentially no wicks, huge range, volume 2.2×. What is this called and what does it signal at a breakout?

**Q7.** INFY, daily, uptrend. A candle gaps up hard, makes a small green body far above the prior close, with a long upper wick and negligible lower wick — a "shooting-star-like" candle but *green*. Does colour matter? Bias?

### Answer key A

**A1 — Bullish Engulfing (high-conviction).** The green body opens below the prior red close and closes above the prior red open, "engulfing" it, after an extended downtrend and *with* volume confirmation. Bias: bullish reversal. Trigger: buy above the engulfing candle's high (₹1,246); stop below its low (₹1,188). The 1.8× volume is what upgrades this from "maybe" to "actionable" — engulfings without volume are much weaker. Note the location: five weeks down means sellers may be exhausted, exactly where reversals are meaningful.

**A2 — Bearish Shooting Star / rejection at range resistance.** Long upper wick, close near low, at the *top of a range on the third touch* = supply defending the level. Bias: short-term bearish, fade the top. Trigger: short below the candle's low (47,815) targeting the range mid/low (47,800); stop above the wick high (47,990). This is a range-fade, not a trend trade — respect that the edge dies if price closes back above 47,950.

**A3 — Bearish Evening Star (via a gap-up star).** Tall green → gap-up small body (the "star," indecision at the top) → tall red closing into the green body. Classic three-candle top. Bias: bearish reversal of the uptrend. In India, index gaps are common at the open, so on Nifty this often forms across the overnight gap. Trigger: short below the third candle's low; invalidation above the star's high.

**A4 — Long-legged Doji, confirmed by a bullish day.** The doji (open ≈ close, long both wicks) after three down-days signals *balance returning* — sellers no longer dominate. It is only a warning, not a signal. The confirmation is the next day's strong green close (₹129→₹136), which turns "indecision" into "demand." Trigger: the confirmation candle itself; stop below the doji's low. Teaching point: dojis require confirmation — never trade the doji alone.

**A5 — Bullish Harami (inside candle).** Small red body inside the prior large green body, with falling volume = the up-move pausing, not reversing hard. It is NOT an engulfing (that would be the small candle engulfing the large — impossible here) and NOT necessarily a top. Bias: neutral-to-mild-pause; often a continuation after a breather in a strong trend. Wait for a break of the large candle's high (continuation) or low (reversal) to act.

**A6 — Bullish Marubozu at breakout.** No wicks, open = low, close = high, full-range green on 2.2× volume, right at an all-time-high breakout = maximal buyer control with no intraday selling. This is a *powerful* continuation signal. Trigger: the breakout is already valid; conservative entry is a small pullback into the breakout level (₹1,720 zone) with stop below it. Marubozu at ATH = institutions accumulating; among the most reliable single candles.

**A7 — Colour is secondary; location and wick dominate.** A long upper wick with a small body high above the prior close, deep in an uptrend, is a **rejection candle regardless of green/red** — buyers pushed up and got sold into. The green body is marginally less bearish than a red one but the *story* is the same: distribution at the highs. Bias: caution / potential short-term top. Confirm with a red follow-through day. Teaching point: never let candle colour override wick and location.

## Section B — Classic chart patterns (Q8–Q15)

**Q8.** Nifty daily. Price rallies to 24,850, pulls back to 24,300, rallies to 24,870 (marginally higher), pulls back to 24,310, rallies to 24,840 (marginally lower). The 24,300–24,310 zone has held three times. Describe the structure and the key level.

**Q9.** SBIN daily. From ₹610 it falls to ₹540, bounces to ₹575, falls to ₹541 (equal low), bounces to ₹576, and now approaches ₹575 again. Name it, bias, and the measured target if it breaks.

**Q10.** Bank Nifty, hourly. After a strong rally, price makes a high at 48,900, then a higher high 49,400, then a lower high 49,100, while each pullback low steps *down*: 48,600, 48,700, 48,500. Volume shrinks throughout. Two converging trendlines. Name it and give the bias-neutral rule.

**Q11.** TATAMOTORS daily. A sharp rally from ₹720 to ₹880 (the "pole"), then two weeks drifting sideways-down in a tight 860–840 channel on falling volume. Name it, bias, measured move.

**Q12.** Nifty daily. Price tops at 25,000, drops to 24,400, rallies to 24,700, drops to 24,100, rallies to 24,700 again, now falling. The 24,700 zone capped it twice; 24,400 then 24,100 are descending lows. What is forming and what is the trigger level?

**Q13.** USDINR daily (spot ~83.20). Long uptrend in the pair. Price makes 83.60, pulls to 83.10, spikes to 83.95, pulls to 83.05, then makes 83.55 (lower than 83.95). The middle peak is the highest. Name it and the neckline.

**Q14.** RELIANCE weekly. Two years of a broad sideways range 1,150–1,400 that gradually narrows: highs 1,400→1,370→1,350, lows 1,150→1,190→1,220, converging into an apex. Volume dries up. Name it and how to trade the resolution.

**Q15.** ADANIPORTS daily. Price gaps up 6% on news to ₹1,420, then over ten days grinds down in a rising-then-flattening pattern, forming a rounded-over top and slipping back toward the gap at ₹1,340. What pattern and what is the tell?

### Answer key B

**A8 — Double Top (roughly; "M" with a slight tilt).** Two peaks near 24,850–24,870 with a shared support ("neckline") at 24,300–24,310. It's not textbook-perfect (the peaks aren't identical) but functionally it's a double-top testing a triple support. Bias: neutral until 24,300 breaks. Trigger: short on a *close* below 24,300, measured target ≈ 24,300 − (24,850 − 24,300) = ~23,750. If 24,300 holds again, it's a range, not a top.

**A9 — Double Bottom ("W").** Two equal lows at ₹540–541 with resistance ("neckline") at ₹575–576. Bias: bullish *on confirmation.* Trigger: buy on a close above ₹576. Measured target = 576 + (576 − 540) = ~₹612. Stop below ₹540. The pattern is only *potential* until the neckline breaks — many double-bottoms fail by making a third lower low.

**A10 — Rising Wedge (bearish).** Higher highs but *decelerating* (48,900→49,400→49,100 = failing momentum) with converging lines and shrinking volume = buyers exhausting. Rising wedges typically resolve **down**. The bias-neutral rule: don't pre-empt — trade the *break* of the lower trendline (short) with target back to the wedge's origin. Volume contraction is the confirming tell.

**A11 — Bull Flag.** The ₹720→880 move is the flagpole; the tight 860–840 down-drift on falling volume is the flag. Bias: bullish continuation. Trigger: buy on breakout above the flag's upper line (~₹862) *with* a volume expansion; measured move = add the pole (₹160) to the breakout ≈ ₹1,020 area. Stop below the flag low (₹840). Falling volume in the flag is exactly what you want — it means no real selling.

**A12 — Descending Triangle (bearish bias).** Flat resistance at 24,700 (horizontal upper) with *descending* lows 24,400→24,100 (falling lower line). Descending triangles favour breakdowns. Trigger: short on a close below the most recent swing low / lower trendline (~24,100), target = height of triangle (25,000−24,100 ≈ 900) projected down ≈ 23,200. Invalidation: a decisive close back above 24,700.

**A13 — Head and Shoulders top.** Left shoulder 83.60, head 83.95 (highest), right shoulder 83.55 (lower) — three peaks, middle highest. Neckline connects the two intervening lows ~83.05–83.10. Bias: bearish reversal of the USDINR uptrend (rupee strengthening). Trigger: short USDINR on a break below ~83.05; measured target = neckline − (head − neckline) = 83.05 − (83.95−83.05) ≈ 82.15. In practice RBI intervention can blunt USDINR moves — respect that fundamental overlay.

**A14 — Symmetrical Triangle / long-term coil.** Converging highs and lows into an apex on drying volume = a massive energy build-up, direction unknown *until* it breaks. Bias: neutral; the pattern predicts *volatility expansion*, not direction. Trade the breakout: enter on a decisive weekly close beyond either boundary with volume; measured move = the triangle's widest height (₹250) projected from the breakout. Because it's weekly, the resolution can run for months.

**A15 — Rounding Top / failed gap ("island-ish" exhaustion).** A news gap-up that fails to hold and rolls over into a rounded dome heading back to fill the gap = distribution. The tell is the *gap fill risk*: unfilled gaps act like magnets. Bias: bearish. Trigger: short on a break back below the gap origin (₹1,340) — gap-fill completion often accelerates. Stop above the rounded top's high. News-gap exhaustion is a classic retail trap: the crowd buys the gap, institutions sell it.

## Section C — Multi-signal & structure reading (Q16–Q22)

**Q16.** Nifty daily. Price breaks below a well-watched 24,000 support on high volume (a "breakdown"), falls to 23,800, then over three days climbs *back above* 24,000 and closes at 24,120. What is this called and what does it flip the bias to?

**Q17.** Bank Nifty, 5-min, opening hour. First 15-min candle range 48,200–48,450. Price then breaks above 48,450, but within two candles collapses back inside and then below 48,200. Name the intraday pattern and the play.

**Q18.** DIXON (high-beta), daily. A strong uptrend, then a candle makes a new high but the RSI (from Volume III) makes a *lower* high, and the very next candle is a bearish engulfing at resistance. Combine the signals — what's the composite read?

**Q19.** Nifty weekly. Price has made higher highs and higher lows for eight months. It now pulls back to the rising 20-week EMA, prints a hammer candle exactly on the EMA, and volume rises on the bounce. Read the confluence.

**Q20.** MCX Gold daily. Price consolidates in a rectangle 71,200–72,000 for three weeks, then a candle closes at 72,050 (just above), but the next day gaps back to 71,600. Was the breakout real? What do you call the failure and what's the play?

**Q21.** ICICIBANK daily. Three pushes up in an Elliott-style sequence (wave 1, 3, 5 feel), the fifth push makes a marginal new high on clearly weaker momentum and a bearish divergence, then a sharp three-wave drop. What are you likely seeing and where's the risk line?

**Q22.** Nifty daily, choppy month. You see: a "double bottom" that fails, a "flag" that breaks down, and a "triangle" that whipsaws both ways. No pattern is paying. What is the *meta*-read, and what should a disciplined trader do?

### Answer key C

**A16 — Bear Trap / failed breakdown (bullish reversal signal).** A support break that *reclaims* the broken level traps the shorts who sold the breakdown; their forced covering fuels the move up. Bias flips to **bullish.** Trigger: long on the reclaim close above 24,000 (24,120), stop below the trap low (23,800). Failed breaks are among the highest-conviction reversals precisely because they run stops in the wrong direction. The mirror image (failed breakout) is a bull trap.

**A17 — Opening Range Breakout (ORB) failure → short.** The first-candle high (48,450) broke but couldn't hold and price fell back *below the opening range low* (48,200). This is a failed ORB, which flips to a short setup. Trigger: short below 48,200, stop back inside the range (above 48,450 or a tighter intraday level), target the next support. Opening-hour false breaks are common in Bank Nifty — the "break both sides" whipsaw traps early breakout buyers.

**A18 — Bearish divergence + engulfing = high-conviction short.** New price high with a lower RSI high = momentum not confirming the price (hidden weakness). The bearish engulfing at resistance is the *trigger candle* that turns the divergence warning into an actionable signal. Composite read: distribution; short below the engulfing low, stop above the divergent high. Two independent signals aligning (momentum + candle + location) is exactly the confluence you wait for on high-beta names like Dixon.

**A19 — Bullish trend-continuation confluence (buy the dip).** Higher-highs/higher-lows structure (uptrend) + pullback to a *rising* 20-week EMA (dynamic support) + hammer *on* the EMA + rising volume on the bounce = four factors pointing the same way. Bias: bullish continuation. Trigger: buy the hammer high, stop below the hammer low / EMA. This is the "buy strength on a pullback to a moving average" template — one of the highest-probability trend setups.

**A20 — Failed breakout / bull trap out of a rectangle.** The close at 72,050 was a marginal, unconvincing break (no volume, immediately gapped back inside) = false breakout. The play: fade it. Trigger: short on the return inside the rectangle (below 72,000), target the opposite boundary (71,200), stop above the false-break high. Rule of thumb: a breakout that isn't *held* by the next one or two candles is guilty until proven innocent, especially in commodities that whipsaw around round numbers.

**A21 — A completing five-wave advance (Elliott) with an ending diagnostic.** Marginal fifth-wave new high on weak momentum + bearish divergence + a sharp corrective drop = the impulse is likely finished and a larger correction is starting. The risk line is the *fifth-wave high* — a move back above it invalidates the "top is in" thesis. Bias: bearish/corrective. Trade the break of the fifth wave's origin, targeting a Fibonacci retracement of the whole advance. Keep it probabilistic — Elliott counts are subjective and often re-labelled.

**A22 — The meta-read: the market is in a low-quality, choppy regime; patterns are unreliable here.** When double-bottoms, flags and triangles all fail in the same window, the message is not "find a better pattern" — it is *"pattern trading has no edge in this regime."* A disciplined trader **reduces size, widens filters, or stands aside** until trending behaviour returns (e.g., a clean ADX rise, a range breakout that holds). Recognising *when not to trade* is itself a pattern-recognition skill — arguably the most profitable one. Most retail losses come from forcing pattern trades in exactly this environment.

## Scoring & what to do next

- **60–66:** Excellent. Your recognition is near-automatic. Focus your energy now on execution and risk (the next chapters), because seeing the pattern is no longer your bottleneck.
- **45–59:** Solid but leaky. You know the patterns but miss *confluence* and *failure* signals. Re-drill Sections B and C — the money is in failed patterns (bear/bull traps) and multi-signal reads.
- **Below 45:** Recognition is not yet a reflex. Do not trade live size. Go back to Volumes I–II, then re-take this quiz cold in a week. Print charts, mark them by hand.

## Interview-ready summary

If an interviewer flashes a chart and asks "what do you see?", answer in a fixed order every time: **(1) trend and structure** (higher-highs/lower-lows, or range), **(2) the specific pattern and its location** relative to that structure, **(3) the trigger and the invalidation level,** and **(4) the confluence** (volume, momentum, moving average). Then add the honest caveat: "this is a ~60% edge, not a certainty, so the trade only works because the stop is at [level] and the reward-to-risk is [ratio]." That four-part structure — see it, name it, define the risk, quantify the edge — is exactly what separates a chart-reader from someone who merely memorised pattern names. The whole point of this quiz was to make that sequence automatic.
