# US Indices TA (S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow)

No Indian trader can afford to ignore Wall Street. The single most reliable predictor of where Nifty opens tomorrow is not any Indian data point — it's what the **S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow** did overnight, and what US futures are doing at 8:30 AM IST. India does not trade in a vacuum; it trades in the gravitational field of the world's deepest, most liquid equity market. This chapter treats the three big US indices technically — their character, the levels that matter, how they lead Nifty, and how to build them into your daily process as an India-first trader.

## The instruments' character & drivers

**The S&P 500 (SPX / ES futures)** is *the* global risk barometer — 500 large-cap US companies, market-cap weighted, the benchmark against which all risk assets are measured. It is broad, relatively less volatile than the Nasdaq, and it's what people mean by "the US market." Its technicals are the cleanest and most respected of the three; institutional algorithms worldwide trade off SPX levels, which makes its support/resistance self-fulfilling.

**The Nasdaq 100 (NDX / NQ futures)** is the **growth and technology** index — Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Meta, Alphabet, Tesla. It's more volatile than the S&P, more sensitive to interest rates (long-duration growth stocks fall hardest when US yields rise), and it's the **risk-appetite gauge** — when the Nasdaq leads, risk-on is genuine; when it lags the Dow, rotation into defensives is underway. For an Indian trader, the Nasdaq matters enormously because **Indian IT (Infosys, TCS, HCL, Wipro) tracks US tech sentiment** and because the Nasdaq's mood sets the global growth-vs-value tone.

**The Dow Jones (DJIA / YM futures)** is 30 blue-chip old-economy names, **price-weighted** (a quirk — a high-priced stock moves it more than a big-market-cap one), so it's the least statistically elegant index but the most "headline" one. It's more defensive/value-tilted. When the Dow holds up while the Nasdaq falls, that's a **defensive rotation** signal.

**The relationship between the three tells a story:**

| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nasdaq leading up, all three green | Healthy risk-on, growth in favor |
| Dow up, Nasdaq down | Defensive rotation, risk appetite fading |
| Nasdaq down hardest | Rate fears / growth scare (watch US 10Y yield) |
| All three down together | Broad risk-off — brace Nifty for a gap-down |
| S&P at a key level, all watching | The whole world trades that line |

**Key drivers:** the US Federal Reserve (rate decisions, dot plots, Powell pressers), US CPI and jobs data (NFP — non-farm payrolls, first Friday of the month), US 10-year Treasury yield, and mega-cap earnings (especially Nvidia, whose results now move the entire global tech complex). The **CBOE VIX** ("the fear index") is the volatility overlay — VIX above 20 is nervous, above 30 is fear, below 15 is complacency.

## Timing — the overnight bridge to Nifty

This is the part that makes US indices actionable for an Indian trader. The clock:

- **US cash market:** 9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET = **7:00 PM - 1:30 AM IST**. So the US regular session happens overnight in India.
- **US index futures (ES/NQ/YM):** trade nearly 24 hours. This is the key. At **8:00-8:30 AM IST**, before Nifty opens at 9:15, US futures are live and reacting to Asian trade. **Where US futures sit at 8:30 AM IST is the single best predictor of Nifty's gap.**
- **GIFT Nifty** (formerly SGX Nifty) trades in that overnight window too and directly signals the Nifty gap — but *it* is heavily driven by what the US did overnight. So the causal chain is: **US close (1:30 AM) → US futures overnight → GIFT Nifty at 8:30 AM → Nifty open at 9:15 AM.**

The practical routine: at 8:00-8:30 AM IST you check (1) how the S&P/Nasdaq/Dow *closed* overnight, (2) where US futures are *now*, (3) GIFT Nifty, and (4) any US data or earnings that hit after the US close (e.g. Nvidia reports after-hours ~2:30 AM IST and can swing global futures). This gives you Nifty's opening bias before the bell.

## Reading the charts — levels & behaviour

US indices are the **most technically-respected charts in the world** because trillions of algorithmic dollars trade off them. This means:

- **Round numbers are massive.** SPX 5,000, 5,500, 6,000, 6,500; Nasdaq 18,000, 20,000; Dow 40,000, 45,000. These are global reference points; moves stall and reverse at them.
- **The 50-day and 200-day moving averages are gospel.** More than any other market, the S&P 500's 50DMA and 200DMA are watched by everyone. A break of the 200DMA is a genuine regime signal that ripples into every risk market including Nifty. The "golden cross" (50 over 200) and "death cross" get global media coverage precisely because they move flows.
- **Prior swing highs/lows and the prior day's high/low** act as clean intraday pivots on the futures.
- **VWAP on the futures** is heavily used by institutions intraday.
- **The Nasdaq leads turns.** Because it's the high-beta growth index, the Nasdaq typically tops and bottoms *before* the S&P and Dow. A Nasdaq that refuses to make a new high while the S&P does (bearish divergence between indices) is an early warning.

**Behaviour to internalize:** US indices trend more persistently than Nifty and pull back more shallowly in bull regimes ("buy the dip" genuinely works in a US uptrend). But when they break — especially below the 200DMA with the VIX spiking — the down-moves are fast and globally contagious. The S&P's character is grind-up-slowly, drop-fast, the classic equity-risk asymmetry.

## Worked example — how a US session sets up the Nifty gap

A realistic overnight sequence and how you'd read it as an India trader.

It's evening IST. The S&P 500 is sitting right on its 50-day moving average at 5,850, having pulled back from 6,000. Tonight is **US CPI at 6:00 PM IST** (8:30 AM ET) — a known, market-moving catalyst. Nifty closed the day flat, coiling, waiting on this.

**The US session unfolds:**

1. **6:00 PM IST — CPI prints softer than expected.** US inflation cooling means the Fed can ease → bullish for risk, especially rate-sensitive tech. The US 10-year yield drops 8 bps. The **Nasdaq leads** — up 1.8% — while the Dow is up a more modest 0.9% (classic: growth outperforms on falling yields). The S&P bounces hard off its 50DMA (5,850), reclaims it decisively, and closes at 5,940 near the highs. VIX falls from 18 to 15.

2. **The read:** a bullish outside session, S&P held its 50DMA (a key global support), Nasdaq-led (healthy risk-on), yields down, VIX down. This is unambiguous risk-on.

3. **Overnight into 8:30 AM IST:** US futures hold their gains, Asian markets (Nikkei, Hang Seng) open green, GIFT Nifty is trading +180 points above Nifty's previous close. **Your 8:30 AM read: Nifty gaps up ~150-180 points.**

4. **Sector overlay:** Nasdaq-led rally + falling US yields = **Indian IT tailwind.** Infosys, TCS, HCL should open strong and lead. This isn't a broad-defensive day; it's a growth/tech-favorable day. You bias your intraday longs toward IT and high-beta.

5. **The trade discipline:** you don't blindly buy the gap. A big gap-up on good news often sees a first-hour pullback (gap-fill attempt). You wait to see if Nifty holds above the previous day's high on the retest, using the strong US lead as your *bias* (long) but the Indian chart for your *entry*. The US session told you the wind direction; the Nifty chart tells you when to set sail.

**The mirror scenario:** had CPI printed *hot*, the Nasdaq would have led *down* (rate fears crush growth), the S&P would have lost its 50DMA, VIX would spike, US futures would be red, GIFT Nifty down 200 — and you'd arrive at 9:15 AM braced for a gap-down, biased short, with IT expected to underperform. Same framework, opposite conclusion.

## How to use US indices for bias & timing

- **Every morning, read the overnight US session before you form a Nifty view.** Non-negotiable for gap positioning and directional bias.
- **Use the S&P 500's key levels (50DMA, 200DMA, round numbers) as global regime markers.** If the S&P is above its 200DMA, the global risk backdrop is constructive for Nifty longs; below it, be defensive.
- **Watch the Nasdaq for Indian IT.** Nasdaq strength/weakness is a leading tell for Infosys/TCS/HCL and the Nifty IT index.
- **Watch the Nasdaq-vs-Dow relationship for the risk regime** — growth-led = aggressive; defensive rotation = de-risk.
- **Track the US 10-year yield and VIX as the two master overlays.** Rising yields + rising VIX = the environment where global equities (Nifty included) get hurt.
- **Know the US calendar:** Fed decision days, CPI (mid-month), NFP (first Friday), and mega-cap earnings (Nvidia especially). These create the overnight catalysts that gap Nifty.

## Risk notes & pitfalls

- **Correlation is not 1.0, and it breaks.** Nifty follows the US strongly on *sentiment and gaps*, but domestic factors — Budget, RBI, monsoon, Indian earnings, local politics — can override the US lead entirely on a given day. Don't trade Nifty as a pure derivative of the S&P; use the US as bias, not gospel.
- **The gap is often already priced.** By the time you see the US rally at 8:30 AM, GIFT Nifty has already gapped. You're not getting a free edge by "knowing" the US was up — everyone knows. The edge is in reading the *quality* of the move (Nasdaq-led vs Dow-led, VIX, yields) and in disciplined *entry* on the Indian chart, not in the obvious direction.
- **Gap-and-reverse traps.** A strong US session can produce a big Nifty gap that immediately fails and fills — buying the open blindly on "US was green" is how gaps trap the crowd. Let the Indian chart confirm.
- **Overnight event risk on your positions.** If you carry Indian positions overnight, you are exposed to whatever the Fed, CPI, or Nvidia does at 2 AM IST. Size overnight exposure with US catalysts in mind — a Fed night is not a night to be maximally leveraged and asleep.
- **Time-zone fatigue.** Chasing every US overnight move (staying up for the Fed at 11:30 PM-1 AM IST) burns traders out and degrades decision quality. Build a *routine* that reads the US session efficiently at 8:30 AM rather than living on Wall Street's clock.
- **Nasdaq whipsaw.** The Nasdaq is high-beta; using its overnight move to over-bias an aggressive Nifty position can backfire on a Nasdaq reversal. Respect its volatility.

## Interview-ready summary

The **S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow** are the overnight backdrop every Indian trader must read before the 9:15 open. The **S&P is the global risk barometer** with the world's most technically-respected 50DMA/200DMA and round-number levels; the **Nasdaq is the growth/tech and risk-appetite gauge** that leads turns and drives Indian IT sentiment; the **Dow is the defensive blue-chip tell**, where Dow-over-Nasdaq strength signals risk-off rotation. The causal chain runs **US close → US futures overnight → GIFT Nifty at 8:30 AM → Nifty gap at 9:15** — so US futures at 8:30 IST are the best predictor of Nifty's open. Overlay the **US 10-year yield and VIX** as master risk signals and track the **Fed / CPI / NFP / Nvidia** calendar for overnight catalysts. Use the US session for *directional bias and gap positioning*, but always confirm *entry* on the Indian chart — because correlation is strong but not perfect, the gap is already priced, and domestic factors can override Wall Street on any given day.
