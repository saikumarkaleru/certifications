# Top F&O Stocks Playbook I

Every liquid F&O name has a *personality* — a habitual way it moves, gaps, ranges and reverses that repeats across quarters. Professional traders don't treat all stocks identically; they build a mental (or written) dossier on each high-turnover name: what it respects, what it ignores, how it behaves around results, and which setups have historically paid. This chapter is the first half of that dossier, covering five of the most-traded F&O heavyweights on the NSE. Volume II continues with the next set. The prices and levels here are realistic 2026-context figures used to teach *method* — always re-anchor to live levels before you trade.

The template for each name: **character & drivers → key levels & behaviour → best setup → risk notes.**

## Reliance Industries (RELIANCE)

**Character & drivers.** Reliance is the market's largest weight and behaves like three companies stitched together — legacy oil-to-chemicals (O2C, refining margins and crude), Jio (telecom ARPU, subscriber adds, tariff hikes) and Retail (consumption). Its chart is therefore a *conglomerate composite*: it can range for months while the sub-businesses offset each other, then trend hard when one narrative (a Jio tariff hike, a retail/Jio IPO or value-unlocking event, a refining-margin super-cycle) dominates. Because of its index weight, Reliance and Nifty are joined at the hip — a Reliance breakout frequently *is* the Nifty breakout. Drivers to overlay: Singapore GRM (gross refining margins), Brent crude, telecom tariff news, and any AGM/demerger/IPO event which historically produces the biggest single-day moves.

**Key levels & behaviour.** Reliance is a classic long-base, big-breakout stock. It spends long stretches in broad rectangles (often 12-18% wide) and then resolves violently. It respects round hundreds and the 200-DMA as a structural line. Because so much passive and institutional money benchmarks to it, it is *less* prone to random whipsaw than a mid-cap — its moves are grindier and more trend-persistent once they start. Volume dries up in the ranges and explodes on the breakout, especially around AGM season.

**Best setup — the long-base breakout.** Identify the multi-month rectangle. Mark the top and bottom. Wait for a *daily close* above the top on volume clearly exceeding the range-average (ideally 1.5x+). Enter on the breakout or the first tight retest of the breakout level. Stop below the breakout level / prior minor swing. Target a measured move equal to the rectangle height projected from the breakout. Because Reliance trends persistently once it goes, trail under rising 20-DMA rather than exiting at the first target — the biggest Reliance moves run far beyond the initial measured objective, particularly when a value-unlocking catalyst is live.

**Risk notes.** The danger with Reliance is *time decay of patience* — the ranges are long and boring, and traders get chopped up trading the middle of the range or front-running the breakout. Only trade the edges. Also, because it moves the index, be aware that a Reliance move can be *caused* by index rebalancing or large passive flows rather than fundamentals; those moves can reverse. Around AGM, expect gap risk in both directions — the "value unlocking" narrative can disappoint as easily as delight, and the stock has a history of "buy the rumour, sell the news" AGM-day fades.

## HDFC Bank (HDFCBANK)

**Character & drivers.** The bellwether private bank and the largest Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty weight. Post the HDFC-HDFC Bank merger, the chart carries a heavier balance sheet, and the market spent a long period digesting merger-related NIM compression and the deposit-mobilisation challenge — which produced a rare multi-quarter *underperformance* of a blue-chip that normally led. Drivers: deposit growth vs credit growth (the CD ratio narrative), NIM trajectory, GNPA stability, and FII positioning (HDFC Bank is the single most-owned Indian stock by foreign investors, so FII flow swings hit it first).

**Key levels & behaviour.** HDFC Bank is a *low-beta, mean-reverting-then-trending* name. It respects its 200-DMA strongly — long-term investors defend it there. In the post-merger phase it built a wide multi-quarter base; the technical thesis was that a decisive close above the base top, on volume, would signal the market had made peace with merger digestion. It gaps on results but generally less violently than an NBFC — the moves are institutional and orderly. Because of massive FII ownership, its worst drawdowns coincide with FII selling waves (USDINR weakness, EM risk-off).

**Best setup — the base-breakout re-rating.** After a long consolidation, buy the decisive breakout above the base top with volume confirmation, ideally coinciding with a results print that shows the CD ratio improving and NIM stabilising. Because it is low-beta, use a *wider stop and longer holding period* than you would for a high-beta name — this is a positional trade, not a scalp. Alternatively, the *200-DMA reclaim* is a reliable swing setup: when the stock has been below the 200-DMA and then reclaims it on a weekly closing basis with improving relative strength versus Bank Nifty, it often marks the start of a multi-week leg.

**Risk notes.** The main trap is treating HDFC Bank like its old self — a stock that only went up. Post-merger it proved it *can* underperform for quarters. Respect the trend that is actually on the chart, not the reputation. Because it is the ultimate FII proxy, a sudden USDINR spike or global risk-off can override any bullish domestic setup — always check the currency and FII-flow context before committing.

## ICICI Bank (ICICIBANK)

**Character & drivers.** Through the recent cycle, ICICI Bank was the *quality private-bank outperformer* — clean asset quality, strong ROA, consistent NIMs — and its chart reflected that with a persistent, well-behaved uptrend where HDFC Bank struggled. Drivers are the classic bank set (NIM, credit growth, GNPA/NNPA, credit cost) plus its reputation as the "best-run" large private lender, which earns it a valuation premium and steady institutional accumulation.

**Key levels & behaviour.** ICICI is a *clean trender* — one of the best moving-average-respecting large caps. In its uptrend it rode the rising 50-DMA, buying every pullback to it. It makes orderly higher highs and higher lows, with results-day gaps that tend to *continue* the trend rather than reverse it (a hallmark of a stock the market genuinely believes in). Round levels matter but the moving averages matter more.

**Best setup — the trend pullback to the 50-DMA.** In an established uptrend (price above a rising 50-DMA, which is above a rising 200-DMA), wait for a pullback into the 50-DMA zone. Look for a reversal candle (hammer, bullish engulfing, or a tight inside-bar) at the average with RSI holding above ~45 (not breaking into oversold — in strong trends RSI bottoms higher). Enter on the reversal confirmation, stop below the swing low / below the 50-DMA by a small buffer, target the prior high and beyond. This is the textbook "buy the dip in the leader" trade and ICICI has been one of the most reliable large caps for it.

**Risk notes.** The trend-pullback setup fails when the *trend itself* is ending — so the disqualifier is a change in structure: a lower low, or a decisive break of the 50-DMA that price cannot reclaim within a few sessions. Don't keep buying the 50-DMA once it has been violated on volume; that is how trend-followers get destroyed at tops. Also, as a large bank, ICICI is sensitive to system-wide asset-quality scares and RBI policy — a sector-wide shock will drag even the best name.

## Infosys (INFY)

**Character & drivers.** The IT bellwether alongside TCS, and a *dollar-earnings, defensive-cyclical* name. Its chart is driven by (1) the USDINR rate — a weaker rupee is a direct tailwind to reported earnings, (2) US/Europe IT-spending sentiment and the outlook for discretionary tech budgets, (3) quarterly revenue guidance (the constant-currency growth guide and margin band), and (4) the global tech tape (Nasdaq, US software names). Infosys is a *guidance stock*: the results-day move is dominated by the forward guidance revision, not the reported quarter.

**Key levels & behaviour.** Infosys gaps hard on results — the guidance surprise produces some of the cleanest single-day trends in the large-cap universe. It is more *range-bound over long stretches* than the banks because IT-spending cycles are slow; it can spend a year in a broad band and then re-rate/de-rate sharply on a guidance inflection. It has a defensive character — in a broad market sell-off driven by domestic/financial factors, IT often holds up (and a weakening rupee in a risk-off tape adds a tailwind), making it a useful relative-strength hedge.

**Best setup — the results-guidance gap trade.** Because the guidance dominates, pre-plan both directions. Mark the pre-results range. On results, if guidance is raised and price gaps above the range top and *holds* the first 30-60 minutes on heavy volume, go long the continuation with a stop at the gap/range top. If guidance is cut and price gaps down and holds below, short. The edge is that guidance-driven gaps in Infosys tend to *trend for days-to-weeks*, not reverse, because the whole analyst community re-rates estimates in one direction. Avoid trading Infosys into results with a directional bet — the gap risk is severe and two-sided; wait for the print and trade the reaction.

**Risk notes.** The rupee is a confounding variable — a great operational quarter can be muted by an adverse currency move, or a mediocre quarter flattered by rupee weakness. Always read the constant-currency numbers, not just reported. Infosys also carries *sector-herd risk*: a TCS or Accenture guidance miss can drag Infosys down regardless of its own setup, because the market re-prices the whole IT-services outlook together.

## Tata Motors (TATAMOTORS)

**Character & drivers.** The market's favourite high-beta auto play and a *global cyclical* — its chart is dominated by JLR (Jaguar Land Rover) performance in China/UK/US, plus the domestic CV (commercial vehicle) cycle and the EV/PV narrative. It is a debt-reduction and turnaround story, which makes it *sentiment-and-momentum heavy*: when the narrative is positive (JLR margins recovering, net-debt falling, demand strong), it trends explosively; when global auto demand wobbles or JLR disappoints, it de-rates just as fast. High beta, high emotion, huge F&O volumes.

**Key levels & behaviour.** Tata Motors is a *momentum trender with violent pullbacks*. In an uptrend it makes rapid gains punctuated by sharp 10-15% shakeouts that terrify weak hands before resuming. It respects the 50-DMA loosely in strong trends but overshoots in both directions — it is *not* a clean 50-DMA name like ICICI. Round hundreds are strong psychological levels. Results and JLR monthly/quarterly volume and margin updates produce the big gaps. Because it is a retail-and-momentum favourite, it exhibits classic momentum-exhaustion signatures — parabolic runs into a blow-off, then a sharp mean reversion.

**Best setup — the momentum pullback / flag continuation.** In a confirmed uptrend, wait for a bull-flag consolidation (a tight, downward-drifting or sideways pause of 5-12% after a strong run) rather than trying to buy the vertical move. Enter on the breakout of the flag with volume, stop below the flag low, target a measured move (the height of the pole projected from the breakout). This harnesses the momentum while defining risk at the flag low. Because Tata Motors overshoots, take partial profits into strength and trail the rest — do not expect it to top gently.

**Risk notes.** This is a high-beta cyclical, so *position size must be smaller* than for a blue-chip bank. The shakeouts are brutal — a stop placed too tight inside the normal noise will get hit constantly; a stop placed at the flag/structure low respects the stock's volatility. The biggest risk is holding through a global-auto or JLR shock: a China-demand scare or a guidance cut can gap it down double digits. Never oversize, always respect the fact that the same momentum that makes it a favourite makes it a wealth-destroyer when the trend snaps. Watch global auto peers and the broad risk tape — Tata Motors falls hardest in risk-off.

## Reading these five as a set

These five names span the market's major engines: energy/telecom conglomerate (Reliance), private banking (HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank), IT/dollar-earnings (Infosys), and global auto cyclical (Tata Motors). Trading them well means matching *setup to character*:

- **Reliance and HDFC Bank** are long-base, patient breakout names — trade the edges, not the middle, and hold for the persistent move.
- **ICICI Bank** is a clean trender — buy pullbacks to the rising 50-DMA in an uptrend.
- **Infosys** is a guidance-gap name — don't predict the print, trade the reaction, and mind the rupee.
- **Tata Motors** is a high-beta momentum name — trade flags, size small, and respect the violent shakeouts.

A recurring professional discipline across all five: *never bet directionally into results.* Each of these gaps hard on its print, and the gap is two-sided. The edge is in the *reaction* — the opening-range hold or fail after the number is known — not in guessing the number. The second discipline is *context*: check the rupee for Infosys and HDFC Bank (FII proxy), Brent for Reliance, global autos for Tata Motors, and the sector index relative strength for the banks. A great single-stock setup fighting an adverse macro context is a lower-probability trade than the chart alone suggests.

## Risk notes (portfolio level)

When you run several of these simultaneously, watch your *hidden correlations*. HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank are largely the same bet (Bank Nifty direction, FII flows). Reliance and the banks together are a large-cap Nifty bet. Tata Motors and Infosys are your diversifiers — one a domestic-global cyclical, the other a dollar-earnings defensive that often moves opposite the rupee. Building a book that is *actually* diversified means not stacking three versions of the same Bank Nifty bet and calling it three positions. Aggregate by risk factor — rate/credit (banks), rupee (IT), global cyclical (autos), index weight (Reliance) — and size each factor deliberately. That factor-aware construction is what separates a professional F&O book from a retail one that is unknowingly 90% long the same thing.
