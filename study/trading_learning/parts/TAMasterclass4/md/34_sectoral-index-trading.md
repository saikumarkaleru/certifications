# Trading the Sectoral Indices

## The instruments and their character

NSE runs a family of sectoral indices that, taken together, are the anatomy of the Indian market. The headline Nifty 50 is just a market-cap-weighted blend of them, dominated by financials and IT. Trading the sectors — either directly (Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty futures/options) or as a top-down map for stock selection — is where most of the real money and most of the real information live. This chapter is a playbook: the character of each major sector, its drivers, how it behaves at key levels, and the setups that work on it.

The tradeable and watchable NSE sectoral indices, and their essential personality:

| Index | Weight of the market it carries | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank Nifty (Nifty Bank)** | The single most-traded index in India | High-beta, ~1.2–1.5× Nifty's daily range; trend-and-momentum instrument; the market's "engine" |
| **Fin Nifty** | Banks + NBFCs + insurance + AMCs | Slightly tamer than Bank Nifty, more diversified financials; heavy F&O volume on expiry |
| **Nifty IT (CNXIT)** | TCS, Infosys, HCLT, Wipro, Tech M | Global/USD-driven, earnings-gap-prone, low domestic sensitivity; USDINR and US recession fears move it |
| **Nifty Auto** | Maruti, M&M, Tata Motors, Bajaj Auto, Eicher | Cyclical; monthly sales data, rural demand, festive season, commodity input costs |
| **Nifty FMCG** | HUL, ITC, Nestlé, Britannia | Defensive; low beta; outperforms in fear/risk-off; rural + monsoon sensitive |
| **Nifty Pharma** | Sun, Cipla, Dr Reddy's, Divis | Defensive-cyclical hybrid; USFDA news, US generic pricing, USD earner |
| **Nifty Metal** | Tata Steel, Hindalco, JSW, Vedanta | Deep cyclical; global commodity prices, China demand, LME, USD; highest beta after Bank Nifty |
| **Nifty PSU Bank** | SBI, PNB, BoB, Canara | Ultra-high-beta, sentiment-driven, government-policy sensitive; violent moves |
| **Nifty Realty** | DLF, Godrej Prop, Oberoi, Lodha | Rate-sensitive, small, prone to speculative runs and equally violent drops |
| **Nifty Energy** | RIL, NTPC, Power Grid, ONGC | Crude oil + power reforms; RIL dominates so it's semi-idiosyncratic |

The key structural facts a trader must internalise:

**Bank Nifty is the beta engine.** With roughly a 1.2–1.5× amplification of Nifty's move, it leads the market both up and down. When banks fire, the market rallies; when banks stall, Nifty tops even if IT or FMCG are green. Watching Bank Nifty is half of watching Nifty.

**Sectors rotate; they do not all move together.** In a risk-on phase, high-beta cyclicals (Metal, PSU Bank, Realty, Auto) lead. In a risk-off/fear phase, defensives (FMCG, Pharma, sometimes IT as a USD hedge) outperform. Reading *which* sectors lead tells you what *kind* of market you're in — offensive or defensive — which is itself a bias signal for the whole book.

**Some sectors are domestic, some are global.** Bank Nifty, Auto, FMCG, Realty, PSU Bank are India-macro plays (RBI policy, rupee liquidity, domestic demand, budget). IT, Pharma, Metal are global plays (US growth, USDINR, LME/China). This split means IT can rally on a weak-rupee day that hurts everything else — sectors are not interchangeable.

## Drivers and key levels

Each sector has its own event calendar and its own set of levels that matter.

**Bank Nifty** trades on RBI policy (repo rate, CRR, liquidity), credit growth data, HDFC/ICICI heavyweight earnings, and bond yields. Its levels are heavily **option-driven**: because Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty are the most active options markets in the world by volume, the *max-pain* and *high-OI strikes* act as magnets and walls. On any given expiry, the strike with the fattest call OI is resistance and the fattest put OI is support — often more binding than any chart level. Round numbers (every 500 points — 50000, 50500, 51000) are psychological levels. Trade the round numbers and the OI walls together.

**Nifty IT** gaps on quarterly results (TCS reports first, sets the tone) and on USDINR. A depreciating rupee is a tailwind (they earn in dollars, report in rupees). US recession scare = IT sells off regardless of India's own health. Levels are cleaner and more technical than banks because retail-option distortion is lower; classic support/resistance and moving averages work well.

**Nifty Auto** trades on the 1st-of-month sales numbers, festive-season demand (Sept–Nov), monsoon (rural two-wheelers), and input costs (steel, aluminium — so it's inversely linked to a hot Metal index). Watch the monthly-sales day as a scheduled volatility event.

**Nifty Metal** is a pure global-cyclical proxy: LME prices, China stimulus/PMI, USD strength (commodities priced in dollars move inversely to USD). It's the highest-conviction "risk-on vs risk-off" tell after Bank Nifty. When Metal breaks out, global reflation is on; when it breaks down, growth fear is rising.

**Nifty FMCG and Pharma** are the defensives. They lead in corrections and lag in rip-roaring rallies. Rising FMCG *relative* to Nifty is an early risk-off warning even before the index cracks — a breadth/sentiment tell in itself. Pharma adds idiosyncratic USFDA-inspection risk on individual names that can whip the index.

**PSU Bank and Realty** are the momentum-junkie sectors: thin float relative to the froth, huge percentage swings, budget/policy sensitive (privatisation news, affordable-housing schemes, rate cuts). Great for trend-following when they run, brutal for mean-reversion because they overshoot both ways.

For all sectors, the universal levels are the same technical furniture — prior swing highs/lows, the 20/50/200-day EMAs, VWAP intraday, the weekly opening range — but the *catalyst* that makes those levels break differs by sector, and you must know the calendar.

## Best setups

**1. Sector rotation top-down (the strategic setup).** Weekly, rank all sectors by relative strength vs Nifty (see the RS chapter). Long the leaders' strongest stocks, avoid/short the laggards. This is the highest-probability structural edge because you're aligning with where institutional flow is going. Combine with the offensive/defensive read: if cyclicals lead, lean long and aggressive; if defensives lead, lean cautious/hedged.

**2. Bank Nifty momentum/trend intraday (the tactical bread-and-butter).** Bank Nifty's high beta and tight option spreads make it the premier intraday trending instrument. The workhorse setup: define the first 15-minute opening range; on a trend day, trade the breakout of that range in the direction of the gap and prior-day close, using VWAP as the trend filter (only longs above VWAP, only shorts below). Confirm with the OI map — a breakout *into* a big call-OI wall is likely to fail; a breakout that *clears* the wall (short-covering of call writers) accelerates. Stops are wide (Bank Nifty can retrace 150–200 points and still be in trend) so size small.

**3. Expiry-day OI setups (Fin Nifty / Bank Nifty).** On expiry, price gravitates to max-pain as option writers defend their strikes. Range-bound expiry days favour selling straddles/strangles around max-pain (defined risk via spreads); trend-day expiries (when a strong open breaks the OI structure early) favour riding the directional move as writers get squeezed. This is advanced and requires strict risk control — expiry gamma cuts both ways.

**4. Defensive-rotation hedge.** When you spot FMCG/Pharma RS rising while the index is still up (early risk-off), rotate longs from cyclicals into defensives, or hedge the book by shorting a high-beta sector (Metal, PSU Bank) against long defensives — a market-neutral pair that profits from the rotation without a directional index bet.

**5. Global-macro sector alignment.** On a sharp USDINR depreciation day, IT and Pharma get a tailwind while rate-sensitives suffer; on a China-stimulus headline, Metal gaps up. Aligning a sector trade with the fresh macro catalyst gives you a fundamental wind at your back on top of the chart setup.

## A worked example

Assume it's a Monday in mid-2025. The weekly RS scan shows **Nifty Metal** has been the top-ranked sector for three weeks, its RS line vs Nifty at a fresh high, driven by a China-stimulus reflation trade and a softening dollar. Nifty PSU Bank is second. The defensives (FMCG, Pharma) are ranked at the bottom — a textbook *offensive*, risk-on tape. Bias: lean long, favour high-beta cyclicals.

Metal index is consolidating just under a prior swing high — call it a base between 9,800 and 10,000, with the 20-day EMA rising into it. On Tuesday, a strong China PMI print hits pre-open; Metal gaps up and clears 10,000 on the open. You don't chase the gap. You wait for the 15-minute opening range to form (say 10,020–10,090), and when price breaks *above* 10,090 on expanding volume with the index also firm, you go long the *leading constituent* — not the index itself but, say, Hindalco or Tata Steel, whichever has the strongest individual RS vs the Metal index (stock leads sector leads market — three-tier alignment).

Entry on the constituent's own breakout, stop below the opening-range low (translated to the stock's chart), position sized so the stop is 1R = a fixed rupee risk you've predefined (say ₹5,000). Target 1 at the measured move of the base (roughly the base height projected up), trailing the rest under the rising 20-EMA as long as Metal's RS line keeps rising.

Two weeks later the trade has run. Then the tell appears: Metal's RS line makes a *lower high* while price grinds one more high — relative divergence — and simultaneously FMCG's RS starts curling up off the bottom. The offensive tape is quietly turning defensive. That's your cue to tighten the trail hard and take the bulk of the profit; the rotation clock has moved from Leading toward Weakening. You exit the cyclical, and the rising-defensive signal puts you on guard for the *whole book* — reduce gross exposure, don't press new high-beta longs. The sector map didn't just win you one trade; it changed your posture for the market.

## Risk notes

**Beta is a double-edged sword.** Bank Nifty, Metal, PSU Bank move 1.3–2× the broad index. Size for the *instrument's* range, not the index's. A "normal" Bank Nifty intraday swing that stops you out is a violent move in Nifty terms. Always position off the stop distance in points × the lot's value, never off "how confident I feel."

**Sector concentration is hidden single-stock risk.** Nifty Energy is really an RIL bet; a big move in one heavyweight can move the "sector" on idiosyncratic news that has nothing to do with the sector thesis. Know each index's top-weight concentration before you treat it as diversified.

**Event calendars will gap you.** Auto sales on the 1st, IT earnings in the first fortnight of each quarter, RBI policy for banks, USFDA actions for pharma, Budget day for PSU/Realty. Never carry a leveraged sector position naked into its known catalyst unless the *catalyst itself* is your thesis — and even then, size for a gap through your stop.

**Expiry gamma.** Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty expiry days can trend or pin violently. Option-selling on expiry has a lovely win rate and a fat tail that eats months of gains in one session. Define risk with spreads; never sell naked into a trend day.

**Rotation is probabilistic, not certain.** The offensive/defensive read and the RRG quadrants are edges, not guarantees. A "leading" sector can top the day you buy it; a "lagging" one can bottom. Use the sector map to *tilt the odds and the position sizing*, and let the individual price setup and stop enforce the discipline. Most retail traders who "trade the sector story" over-commit to the narrative and ignore the stop — that is how a good top-down read still produces a losing month. The map sets the direction; risk management keeps you solvent long enough to be right.
