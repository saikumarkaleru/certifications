# PSU & PSU-Bank TA

Public-sector undertakings — the government-owned banks, oil marketers, defence yards, power utilities and mining giants — trade unlike anything else on the NSE. They are the market's most **policy-sensitive, most sentiment-driven and most mean-reverting** group of stocks. A private-bank chart is a story about earnings and management execution; a PSU-bank chart is a story about a Union Budget line item, an RBI dividend, a divestment headline and the collective mood of a retail crowd that either loves or hates "sarkari" stocks that quarter. If you trade these names with the same playbook you use for HDFC Bank or Asian Paints, you will get chopped. This chapter builds the dedicated TA playbook for PSUs and PSU banks in Indian markets — their character, their levels, the setups that actually work, and the ways they punish the unwary.

## The instrument's character & drivers

Start with what a PSU actually is on the tape. The universe splits into a few behavioural families:

- **PSU banks** — SBI, Bank of Baroda, Canara, PNB, Union Bank, Indian Bank, Bank of India — tracked by the **Nifty PSU Bank index**. SBI is the 800-pound gorilla (roughly a quarter to a third of the index weight depending on the day) and behaves almost like a large private bank. The rest are high-beta, low-price, retail-heavy names that move in a herd.
- **Energy & oil PSUs** — ONGC, Oil India, Coal India, GAIL, IOC, BPCL, HPCL, Petronet. These are commodity-and-subsidy plays. Crude, LPG under-recoveries, and the excise/subsidy stance drive them more than any chart pattern.
- **Defence & capital-goods PSUs** — HAL, BEL, BDL, Mazagon Dock, Cochin Shipyard, Garden Reach, BEML. Order-book and theme stocks — the darlings of the 2023-24 bull run, and the biggest blow-off casualties.
- **Power & infra PSUs** — NTPC, Power Grid, NHPC, SJVN, REC, PFC, IRFC, RVNL, RailTel, Ircon. Yield-plus-growth or pure momentum, depending on the name.
- **Others** — BHEL, SAIL, NMDC, NLC, Container Corp, and the holding-company oddities.

The common DNA across all of them, and why TA must be adapted:

**1. Policy is the primary driver, price is the echo.** These stocks gap on news that has nothing to do with their chart — a divestment target in the Budget, an RBI record dividend to the government, a defence indigenisation order, a fuel-price freeze before elections. Your chart tells you *where* the reaction will pause; it does not predict the catalyst. Treat PSUs as **event-driven instruments with technical timing**, not pure technical instruments.

**2. They move as a pack.** When PSU banks run, PNB, Canara, Union and BoB print near-identical candles on the same days. Correlation inside the basket routinely exceeds 0.8 during trends. This is a gift for confirmation (breadth of the basket confirms the leader) and a trap for diversification (owning five PSU banks is owning one position five times).

**3. Deep, violent mean-reversion.** PSU baskets go through long dead phases (2010-2020 was largely a lost decade for PSU banks) punctuated by ferocious re-rating bursts (2021 recovery, 2023-24 defence/rail mania). Because the crowd is momentum-chasing retail, tops are sharp and bottoms are grinding. The blow-off top is the signature PSU chart event.

**4. Low absolute price = high retail participation = fat, gappy candles.** A ₹90 PNB or ₹110 IRFC attracts lakhs of small accounts. That produces overnight gaps, circuit-like intraday ranges, and indicator whipsaw. Wider stops and position-size discipline are non-negotiable.

**5. Liquidity is uneven.** SBI, Coal India, NTPC, HAL, BEL, PFC/REC, IRFC are deep and F&O-listed. But many PSUs are *not* in F&O, or have thin option chains — you cannot always hedge, and slippage on exits during a panic is real.

## Key levels & behaviour

Because policy drives the catalyst, PSU charts are unusually **level-respecting** between events — the crowd anchors hard to round numbers and prior swing points. Practical level rules:

**Round numbers are magnets and battlegrounds.** SBI at ₹600, ₹700, ₹800; PNB at ₹100; IRFC at ₹100/₹150/₹200; NTPC at ₹300/₹350; Coal India at ₹400/₹500. Retail places orders at round figures, so these become high-volume shelves. A decisive close through a big round number on volume is a genuine signal; a wick through it that closes back inside is a classic trap.

**The Nifty PSU Bank index is the regime filter.** Never trade an individual PSU bank against its index. If Nifty PSU Bank is below a falling 50-DMA, treat every long in Canara or PNB as counter-trend. Read the index first, the stock second. The same logic applies to using **Nifty Energy, Nifty PSE, and the Nifty India Defence index** as regime filters for those baskets.

**Divestment / QIP / OFS levels create hard ceilings and floors.** When the government sells a stake via OFS (offer for sale) at a stated floor price, that floor becomes a technical level for weeks — often the exact spot where the stock finds support afterwards. A QIP placement price does the same. Mark these on the chart; they are more reliable than any Fibonacci line because real institutional size transacted there.

**Gaps rarely fill quickly on genuine re-ratings but almost always fill on sentiment pops.** Learn to distinguish a *fundamental gap* (huge order win, subsidy removal, record dividend to govt) — which tends to hold and become support — from a *sentiment gap* (generic "PSU theme is hot" days) — which fills. Volume and follow-through on day two tell you which one you have.

**Behaviour around the Budget (Feb 1) and RBI dividend announcement (May).** These are the two structural events. PSU banks and PSEs routinely front-run the Budget in January (divestment hopes) and sell the news on Feb 1 regardless of content — "buy the rumour, sell the fact" is a near-mechanical pattern. The RBI surplus transfer to the government (announced May, record ₹2.1-lakh-crore-plus in recent years) periodically ignites PSU banks. Put these dates on your calendar as known volatility windows.

## Best setups

**Setup 1 — The basket-confirmed breakout (the bread-and-butter long).**
Trade the leader (SBI or Bank of Baroda) only when the *basket agrees*. Conditions:
- Nifty PSU Bank index breaks a multi-week base / prior swing high on above-average volume.
- At least 4 of 6 constituents are simultaneously above their 20-DMA and printing higher highs.
- The leader (SBI) breaks its own level with a volume expansion of 1.5x the 20-day average.
Entry on the retest of the broken level, stop below the base low, target the measured move of the base height projected from the breakout. This is the highest-probability PSU long because you are demanding internal breadth confirmation, which filters out the fake "one-stock" pops.

**Setup 2 — The mean-reversion bounce off a divestment/OFS floor.**
After a sharp sell-off into a known OFS/QIP price or a major round number, wait for a **reversal candle with a volume spike** (hammer, bullish engulfing) *and* a reclaim of the level intraday. PSU banks bounce hard from these institutional floors. Stop just below the floor (these levels break cleanly when they fail), target the first prior resistance or the declining 20-DMA. This is a counter-trend scalp/swing — keep size modest and take profits fast; PSUs do not reward greed on bounces.

**Setup 3 — The theme-momentum pullback (defence/rail/power).**
In a confirmed sector uptrend (Nifty India Defence or the rail cluster above rising 50-DMA), buy the *first or second* controlled pullback to the 20-EMA on a stock like HAL, BEL, RVNL or IRFC. Avoid buying after the stock has gone parabolic (5+ green weeks, RSI pinned above 75, price 40%+ above the 50-DMA) — that is distribution territory. The edge is buying the pause inside a trend, not chasing the vertical.

**Setup 4 — The event-fade.**
On Budget day or a big-headline day when a PSU gaps up huge and then makes a lower high in the first 60-90 minutes with fading volume, fade it back toward the prior close / gap fill. This is an experienced-trader intraday setup — the crowd buys the headline, the smart money distributes into it. Only take it with a hard stop above the day's high; a real re-rating gap will run you over.

## A worked example — a PSU-bank re-rating and its blow-off

Consider a composite, realistic Bank of Baroda scenario built from how these moves actually unfold.

BoB has spent months in a ₹230-₹260 range while Nifty PSU Bank grinds sideways below a flat 50-DMA. Nothing to do. In late January, ahead of the Budget, the PSU Bank index starts to firm — 4 of 6 constituents reclaim their 20-DMAs, index volume ticks up. On a Wednesday, BoB closes at ₹264 on volume 1.8x its 20-day average, clearing the ₹260 range top; the index simultaneously breaks its own three-month resistance. **This is Setup 1.** You wait for the retest.

Two sessions later BoB pulls back to ₹258, holds the prior resistance-turned-support intraday, and prints a bullish reversal candle. Entry ₹261, stop ₹247 (below the base low and the round ₹250). Base height was ~₹30 (₹230→₹260), so the measured target is ₹290; a secondary target sits at the round ₹300.

The RBI record-dividend headline lands in May and the whole basket ignites. BoB runs to ₹292 (first target — book a third), consolidates for a week above ₹285, then extends to ₹305 on a subsidy/dividend-fuelled crowd frenzy. Now watch the character change: RSI is pinned near 78, volume on up-days is *climbing but candles are getting smaller* (buying exhaustion), and the stock is 22% above its 50-DMA. On the day it gaps to ₹318 and closes red at ₹308 — a bearish outside day on the highest volume of the run — the blow-off signature is complete. You exit the remainder into strength near ₹305-310 rather than waiting for the reversal to confirm, because PSU tops are sharp and the first red day after a parabolic run is frequently *the* top. Over the next three weeks BoB mean-reverts to the ₹275 area (back to the 20-EMA and the breakout retest zone), where the cycle can begin again.

The lesson embedded in the example: the same stock offers a *trend-following* long (Setups 1 and 3) on the way up and a *reversion* exit discipline at the top, and both are governed by reading the basket and the volume character — not by any single indicator.

## Risk notes

**Concentration is the number-one killer.** Five PSU banks is one bet. Cap total PSU-bank exposure as if the whole basket were a single position, because on a bad day it will move as one. A common professional rule: no more than one "expression" of a theme at full size; if you want two names, halve each.

**Gap risk is structural, not occasional.** These stocks gap on policy overnight. If you cannot tolerate a 5-8% adverse gap through your stop, you are too big. For non-F&O PSUs you often *cannot* hedge the gap at all — size accordingly and never hold a large unhedged position into the Budget unless the event itself is your thesis.

**Do not fight the index regime.** The single most expensive PSU mistake is bottom-fishing individual banks while Nifty PSU Bank is in a confirmed downtrend. Wait for the basket to turn.

**Beware the "cheap" illusion.** A ₹90 stock is not cheap; it can fall to ₹60 (a 33% loss) as easily as a ₹900 stock falls to ₹600. Low price attracts undisciplined size. Position by rupee-risk (stop distance × quantity), never by share count.

**Liquidity evaporates in panics.** The bid you see in calm markets is not there when the basket dumps. Assume worse fills on exits; keep positions in the deeper names (SBI, BoB, Canara, PFC, REC, Coal India, NTPC, HAL, BEL) if you need to move size.

**The theme can die for years.** PSU re-ratings are cyclical, not permanent. When breadth deteriorates — fewer constituents making new highs even as the index holds — the theme is topping internally. Respect it and reduce.

## Interview-ready summary

- **PSUs and PSU banks are policy-driven, sentiment-amplified, mean-reverting instruments** — treat them as event stocks with technical *timing*, not as pure technical charts.
- **Read the basket first.** Nifty PSU Bank, Nifty PSE, Nifty India Defence and Nifty Energy are the regime filters; never trade a constituent against its index. Internal breadth (4-of-6 constituents aligned) confirms real moves and exposes fake one-stock pops.
- **Levels that matter:** round numbers (SBI ₹600/₹700/₹800, PNB ₹100, IRFC ₹100/₹150/₹200), OFS/QIP floors (real institutional prints), and the Budget (Feb 1) and RBI-dividend (May) volatility windows.
- **Core setups:** (1) basket-confirmed breakout in the leader, (2) mean-reversion bounce off a divestment/OFS floor, (3) theme-momentum pullback to the 20-EMA in a confirmed uptrend, (4) the event-fade on gap-and-fade days.
- **The signature risk is the blow-off top** — parabolic price, RSI pinned >75, shrinking candles on rising volume, price far above the 50-DMA — and the signature portfolio error is **concentration**: the whole basket is one bet.
- Honest odds: most retail money in PSUs is late, chasing the vertical and holding through the reversion. The edge is being early on breadth-confirmed breakouts and disciplined at exhaustion — the opposite of the crowd.
