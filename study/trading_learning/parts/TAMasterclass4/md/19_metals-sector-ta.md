# Metals Sector TA

The metals sector is the most brutally cyclical corner of the Indian market, and for the technical trader that is a gift. Steel, aluminium, copper, zinc and iron ore move in long, muscular trends driven by global demand, Chinese stimulus, the dollar and commodity super-cycles. When metals move, they move hard — Tata Steel can rally 60% in four months and give it all back in three. This chapter is a working playbook for reading and trading the Nifty Metal index and its heavyweight constituents on the chart, with India-specific levels, drivers and setups. If you learn to read one cyclical sector properly, metals is the one that pays for the education.

## The sector's character & drivers

Metals is a **price-taker** sector. Indian steel and non-ferrous producers sell into a globally priced market, so the domestic stock chart is really a leveraged bet on global commodity prices, translated through the rupee. This gives the sector three defining technical characteristics.

First, **high beta**. The Nifty Metal index routinely swings 1.5x to 2.5x the Nifty on both up and down days. On a risk-on day when Nifty is up 1%, Metal can be up 2.5-3%; on a risk-off crash it leads the fall. This means metals are excellent for momentum traders and dangerous for the complacent. Position sizing must respect that a metal stock's Average True Range (ATR) is often double that of an FMCG or IT name.

Second, **globally-driven, not domestically-driven**. The single most important external chart for a metals trader is not the Nifty — it is the **LME (London Metal Exchange) complex, Chinese steel rebar futures, and Dr. Copper**. Copper is called "Dr. Copper" because it has a PhD in economics: as the most industrially ubiquitous base metal, its price is a real-time referendum on global growth. When copper breaks out on the weekly chart, Hindustan Copper, Vedanta and Hindalco tend to follow. When Chinese authorities announce infrastructure or property stimulus, the entire Nifty Metal index gaps up the next morning.

Third, **the dollar and rupee overlay**. Commodities are priced in USD and are inversely correlated to the Dollar Index (DXY). A falling DXY is a tailwind for metals; a surging DXY is a headwind. For the Indian producer there is a second-order effect — a weak rupee raises the realised rupee price of exports and of import-parity domestic sales, which is why USDINR strength can partly cushion an Indian metal stock even when global prices soften.

The key constituents you must know by their character:

| Stock | Character | Primary driver |
|---|---|---|
| Tata Steel | The bellwether, most liquid, huge F&O interest | Global steel prices, EU operations, China |
| JSW Steel | Cleaner domestic play, capex-heavy | Domestic steel demand, spreads |
| Hindalco | Aluminium + Novelis (US auto/beverage cans) | LME aluminium, US demand |
| Vedanta | Diversified — zinc, aluminium, oil; dividend/debt story | LME complex, debt news, dividends |
| Hindustan Zinc | Zinc pure-play, high dividend, low float | LME zinc, dividend announcements |
| NMDC | Iron ore miner, PSU, price-setter domestically | Iron ore prices, PSU re-rating |
| SAIL | High-beta PSU steel, operationally levered | Steel prices, government policy |

| Hindustan Copper | Only listed copper miner, thin, explosive | Copper price, speculative flows |

## Key levels & behaviour

Because metals trend, the workhorse tools are **trend structure, moving averages, and horizontal support/resistance from prior cyclical pivots** — not oscillators used naively. In a strong metals uptrend, RSI will sit "overbought" above 70 for weeks; a trader who shorts on that signal alone gets destroyed. Treat momentum oscillators in metals as trend-confirmation and divergence tools on higher timeframes, not as mean-reversion triggers.

The 200-day EMA on the Nifty Metal index and on Tata Steel is a line institutions genuinely watch. Metals spend cyclical bull phases above a rising 200-DEMA and cyclical bears below a falling one. The **20-week EMA** is the swing-trend anchor for position trades — in the 2020-21 metals supercycle, Tata Steel rode its 20-WEMA higher for nearly a year, offering repeated pullback entries.

Behaviourally, metals **gap frequently on the open** because so much price discovery happens overnight in London and Shanghai while the NSE is shut. This creates two tradable phenomena: gap-and-go continuation on strong global cues, and gap-fade when the domestic tape disagrees with the overnight move. Metals also show **strong intra-sector correlation** — on a real sector move, Tata, JSW, SAIL, Hindalco and Vedanta all light up green together. If Tata Steel is ripping but SAIL and JSW are flat, be suspicious; the move may be stock-specific news (a block deal, an upgrade) rather than a genuine sector wave, and it is less likely to sustain.

Round numbers matter psychologically: Tata Steel at ₹100, ₹120, ₹150, ₹180; Nifty Metal at 8000, 9000, 10000. These act as magnet and battle levels because of the enormous options open interest that clusters there.

## Best setups

**Setup 1 — The sector-confirmed breakout.** The highest-probability metals trade. Wait for the Nifty Metal index to break a multi-week consolidation or a horizontal resistance on the daily chart, ideally on a day when the global cue is bullish (LME up overnight, copper strong, DXY weak). Then enter the *strongest* constituent — the one already making a fresh swing high with the cleanest structure and highest relative strength — on a breakout of its own level. You get a double confirmation: the sector wave plus the individual leader. Entry on the breakout candle close or on the first shallow pullback to the breakout level; stop below the breakout base; target measured-move (height of consolidation projected up) or the next cyclical resistance.

**Setup 2 — The 20-WEMA pullback in an established uptrend.** Once a metals bull phase is confirmed (price above rising 20- and 50-WEMA, higher highs and higher lows on the weekly), buy pullbacks into the 20-WEMA zone. This is a trend-following entry with a tight, logical stop just below the weekly moving-average cluster. It repeats several times through a cycle and is far safer than chasing extended breakouts.

**Setup 3 — The failed-breakdown reversal.** Metals over-shoot on fear. When a heavyweight like Vedanta or Tata Steel makes a fresh low on a bad global day, wick down through an obvious support, and then closes back above it (a bullish "spring" / false breakdown), that trapped-seller reversal is powerful. Confirm with the sector doing the same and with a bullish RSI divergence on the daily. Stop below the wick low.

**Setup 4 — The intermarket-triggered swing.** Trade the metal stock off the *leading* chart. When weekly LME copper or Chinese rebar breaks out before the Indian stocks fully price it, position ahead of the domestic move. This is an edge only if you actually track those external charts daily.

## A worked example

Consider a realistic Tata Steel swing sequence. The stock has spent six weeks consolidating between ₹128 and ₹138 after a prior run, coiling into a tight range with contracting volatility — a classic pre-expansion base. The 20-WEMA has flattened around ₹130 and price is holding above it. Meanwhile, on the intermarket screen, LME copper has quietly broken a multi-month down-trendline on the weekly, and Chinese authorities have announced a property-support package over the weekend.

Monday, the NSE opens with Nifty Metal gapping up 1.8% on the China cue. Tata Steel opens at ₹140, breaking the ₹138 range top on a gap. SAIL, JSW and Hindalco are all green 2%+ — the sector is moving together, confirming this is a genuine wave, not a one-stock story. The prudent entry is not the chaotic gap open; it is either (a) a break of the opening 15-minute high above ₹141 with the sector still firm, or (b) waiting for a pullback that holds above the ₹138 breakout level intraday.

Say you enter at ₹141. Your stop goes below the breakout structure at ₹135.5 (below both the range top and the intraday pullback low), risking ₹5.5 per share. The measured move projects the ₹10 range height onto the ₹138 breakout, giving a first target near ₹148, and the prior cyclical resistance sits at ₹156. You size the position so that ₹5.5 of risk equals your fixed rupee risk per trade (say 0.75% of capital). Over the next three weeks, Tata rides its rising daily 20-EMA, you trail the stop under each higher swing low, book partial at ₹148, and let a runner target ₹156. If instead the gap had faded — Tata closing back below ₹138 on day one while the sector fizzled — the failed breakout is your exit signal; you take the small loss and stand aside. That asymmetry, small controlled losses against measured-move winners, is the entire game in a high-beta sector.

## Reading the intermarket dashboard

A metals trader should keep a one-glance dashboard, updated before the open:

| Input | Bullish for metals | Bearish for metals |
|---|---|---|
| DXY (Dollar Index) | Falling / below key MA | Rising / breakout |
| LME Copper (weekly) | Uptrend / breakout | Downtrend / breakdown |
| Chinese rebar / iron ore | Rising, stimulus news | Falling, property stress |
| US 10Y & global growth tone | Reflation, growth-on | Recession fears |
| USDINR | Rupee weak (helps realisations) | Rupee sharply strong |
| Nifty Metal vs Nifty (RS) | Metal outperforming | Metal lagging |

When five of six are aligned bullish, breakouts in metal stocks have real fuel and should be traded aggressively. When the dashboard is mixed or bearish, fade rallies or stand aside — a breakout with no intermarket support is a low-quality signal prone to failure.

## Relative strength & sector rotation

Metals are a **rotation** sector — money floods in during reflationary, risk-on, early-cycle phases and abandons them in slowdowns and deflation scares. The technical tell is relative strength: plot Nifty Metal / Nifty ratio. When that ratio line is rising and breaks its own resistance, metals are the market's leadership and you want to be long the leaders. When the ratio rolls over, metals become a source of funds and even genuine-looking breakouts tend to fail. Chartink and TradingView both let you build this ratio chart; make it a permanent tab.

Within the sector, rank constituents by relative strength weekly. In a real metals bull, the leader (often JSW or Hindalco in recent cycles) makes fresh highs first; the laggards (SAIL, NMDC) catch up later. Early in the move, buy the leaders for cleanest trends; late in the move, when leaders extend and laggards start to catch a bid, that broadening can be a sign the trend is maturing — a time to tighten stops rather than add aggressively.

## Pitfalls & risk notes

**Do not fight the cycle with oscillators.** The single most common way retail traders lose in metals is shorting a raging uptrend because RSI shows "overbought," or buying a collapse because it "looks cheap." Metals overshoot in both directions; respect the trend and use structure, not oscillator extremes, for entries.

**Gap risk is real.** Overnight London/Shanghai moves mean a metal stock can gap 4-5% against you at the open. Never hold an oversized, tight-stopped intraday position into an event (Chinese data, FOMC) expecting your stop to fill at your level — gaps jump stops. Size for the gap, not just the ATR.

**Correlation kills diversification.** Holding Tata Steel, JSW and SAIL is not three positions — it is one leveraged bet on steel. On a bad day they all fall together. Count sector-correlated positions as a single risk unit when sizing.

**News-driven single-stock traps.** Vedanta and Hindustan Zinc move violently on dividend, debt, demerger and promoter news that has nothing to do with the metals cycle. A chart breakout that is really news-front-running can reverse the instant the news is out ("buy the rumour, sell the fact"). Know why a stock is moving.

**Leverage discipline.** Because metals are already high-beta, using full F&O leverage on top magnifies an already-volatile instrument into account-ending territory. Many blown retail accounts trace to leveraged metal longs bought at a cyclical top. Reduce, don't add, leverage in the most volatile sector.

## Risk notes for position sizing

Given metals' beta, standardise risk on **rupees-at-stop**, not on quantity or notional. Decide your fixed fractional risk (e.g., 0.5-1% of capital per trade). Compute quantity as (risk rupees) / (entry minus stop distance). Because the stop distance in metals is wide (ATR is large), the same rupee risk buys you a *smaller* position than in a low-beta stock — that is correct and protective. Cap total simultaneous metals exposure (all correlated names combined) at a single-sector limit, perhaps 1.5-2 risk-units, so one bad China headline cannot inflict a cluster loss across five positions at once.

## Interview-ready summary

The Metals sector on the chart is a high-beta, globally-driven, cyclical trend vehicle. Its price is set abroad — LME base metals, Chinese steel and iron ore, Dr. Copper — and translated through the dollar and rupee, so the metals trader's most important charts are intermarket, not domestic. Technically, metals reward **trend-following** (structure, 20-WEMA and 200-DEMA, relative strength versus Nifty) and punish naive oscillator mean-reversion, because they overshoot in both directions and RSI stays pinned in strong trends. The four bread-and-butter setups are the sector-confirmed breakout in the strongest constituent, the pullback to the rising 20-week EMA in an established uptrend, the failed-breakdown spring reversal, and the intermarket-triggered swing that front-runs the domestic move. The core edge is confluence: a breakout that has the sector moving together *and* a supportive intermarket dashboard *and* rising relative strength. The core dangers are overnight gaps that jump stops, correlation that turns "diversified" metal positions into one leveraged bet, single-stock news shocks in names like Vedanta, and the temptation to pile leverage onto an already-volatile sector. Respect the cycle, size on rupees-at-stop, cap correlated exposure, and let measured-move winners run against small controlled losses — that asymmetry is where metals pay.
