# Top F&O Stocks Playbook III

This is the third instalment of the single-stock playbook. Volumes earlier in this series took you through the index heavyweights — Reliance, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Infosys, TCS, SBI, Axis, L&T. Playbook III moves down the weightage ladder into the names that make F&O traders their living precisely *because* they are not the sleepy top five: the high-beta metal and PSU counters, the two-wheeler and auto ancillaries, the pharma bellwethers, the cement duopoly, and a couple of the "story" stocks that trend for months and then gap 12% on a single result. These are the counters where technical analysis earns its keep, because the moves are large, the character is distinct, and the crowd is emotional.

The rule that binds the whole chapter: **every stock has a personality, and the personality is a tradeable edge.** Tata Steel does not behave like Sun Pharma. Bajaj Finance does not behave like NTPC. If you trade all of them with the same 20-EMA-crossover template you will be chopped up in the range-bound names and shaken out of the trends. What follows is the character sheet, the levels that matter, the setups that historically pay, a worked example each, and the risk notes that keep you solvent.

All levels are indicative of the 2025-26 regime and are meant to teach *method*, not to be blindly reused — re-derive them on live charts.

## The stocks in this playbook and why they cluster

I have grouped nine liquid F&O names into four behavioural clusters. Cluster is more useful than sector for a technician, because it tells you *how the chart moves*.

| Cluster | Names | Beta vs Nifty | Character | Best regime |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High-beta cyclicals | Tata Steel, Hindalco, JSW Steel | 1.4–1.8 | Amplify index moves, news-sensitive (China, LME, INR) | Trending markets, either direction |
| PSU momentum | NTPC, Coal India, BEL | 1.0–1.6 | Long theme-driven trends, dividend support, gap-and-go | Broad bull phases |
| Defensive compounders | Sun Pharma, Divi's | 0.6–0.9 | Low-vol grind, event risk on USFDA news | Choppy / risk-off markets |
| Rate-sensitive NBFC | Bajaj Finance | 1.2–1.5 | Sharp reactions to RBI, results; strong trend persistence | Rate-cut cycles |

A trader who knows *which cluster is in favour this quarter* is already ahead of the person hunting a "good chart" in isolation. When the index is grinding up on domestic-liquidity flows, PSU momentum leads. When global growth fear hits, high-beta metals bleed first and defensives catch a bid. Read the cluster, then read the chart.

## Cluster 1 — High-beta cyclicals: Tata Steel, Hindalco, JSW Steel

### Character and drivers

Metals are the purest expression of *reflexivity* on the NSE. The price does not lead on company fundamentals; it leads on the LME price of the base metal, the CNY/USD move, China stimulus headlines, and the domestic steel spread. A technician does not need to model any of that — the chart integrates it — but you must respect that these names **gap**. A China property-stimulus rumour on a Sunday means Tata Steel opens 4% up on Monday with no intraday warning.

Practical implications:

- **Overnight risk is real.** Do not carry outsized metal positions through a weekend without a defined stop, and know that the stop may be jumped by a gap.
- **They lead the index at turns.** Metals often top and bottom a few sessions before the Nifty because global cyclical sentiment turns first. Use them as a breadth tell for the whole market.
- **Volume confirms.** A breakout in Tata Steel on 2x average delivery volume is far more trustworthy than a low-volume drift, because institutions rotate into cyclicals in size.

### Key levels and behaviour

Tata Steel (adjusted for its 10:1 split, so it trades in the ₹120–180 band) respects round-number psychology brutally: ₹130, ₹140, ₹150 act as shelf support and resistance. Hindalco, a higher absolute price (₹600–750 band), trends more cleanly and is the "cleanest chart" of the three — it whipsaws less. JSW Steel is the strongest fundamentally and tends to hold up best in a metals correction, making it the pair-trade *long* leg against a Tata Steel short in a weak tape.

### Best setups

1. **The volume-confirmed range breakout.** Metals build multi-week rectangles. Buy the close above the top of the range on above-average volume; stop below the mid-range; target a measured move equal to the range height. This is the bread-and-butter metals trade.
2. **The gap-and-go continuation.** After a fundamentally-driven gap up (China stimulus, LME spike), the first 15-minute range often continues in the gap direction. Trade the ORB (opening range breakout) only in the direction of the gap, never against it.
3. **The pair-trade mean reversion.** When JSW/Tata Steel ratio stretches two standard deviations from its 60-day mean, fade the divergence — long the laggard, short the leader — for a market-neutral bet on the spread reverting.

### Worked example — Tata Steel range breakout

Assume Tata Steel has coiled between ₹138 (support tested four times) and ₹146 (resistance tested thrice) for six weeks. LME steel is firming and the Nifty Metal index just crossed its 50-DMA. On a Tuesday, Tata Steel closes at ₹147.2 on volume 90% above its 20-day average.

- **Entry:** ₹147.5 on the confirmed daily close-above (or the next-morning follow-through above ₹147.2).
- **Stop:** ₹142.8, just below the range midpoint and the breakout candle's low — a ₹4.7 risk.
- **Target:** range height is ₹8 (146 − 138), projected from the breakout at ₹146 gives ₹154; a stretch target at the prior swing high ₹158.
- **Reward:risk:** roughly ₹6.5 to ₹10.5 reward on ₹4.7 risk = 1.4R to 2.2R. Take half off at ₹154, trail the rest under the rising 10-EMA.

Risk note: if the breakout candle prints on *below*-average volume, skip it. Metals fake out constantly on thin volume; the volume filter is not optional here.

## Cluster 2 — PSU momentum: NTPC, Coal India, BEL

### Character and drivers

The PSU re-rating of 2023-25 taught technicians a lesson: **government-owned names can trend for a year without a meaningful pullback** when the macro story (capex, defence indigenisation, energy security) is intact. These are not the value traps of the previous decade. They gap up on order-book news, pay fat dividends that put a floor under the price, and are dominated by delivery-based buying rather than intraday churn.

BEL (defence electronics) is the highest-beta of the three and the most order-flow driven. NTPC is the steadiest — a utility with a green-energy re-rating overlay. Coal India is the highest-yield and the most macro-cyclical (thermal demand, coal prices).

### Key levels and behaviour

PSU trends respect the **20-week EMA** on the weekly chart with unusual fidelity. In the strong phase, pullbacks to the 20-WEMA are buy zones, not tops. The tell that a PSU trend is *ending* is not a lower high — it is the first *weekly close below the 20-WEMA* after a year of holding it. Until that print, dips are for buying.

### Best setups

1. **The 20-WEMA pullback buy.** In an established uptrend, wait for a 3–6 week consolidation that tags the 20-WEMA, then buy the first weekly close back above the 10-WEMA. Wide stop below the 20-WEMA; long hold.
2. **The flag-pole continuation.** BEL in particular runs vertical on order news, then flags sideways for 2–3 weeks. Buy the flag breakout with a stop below the flag low.
3. **The dividend-support base.** Coal India near its dividend-yield floor (when trailing yield exceeds ~7%, price historically finds support). This is a fundamental-technical hybrid — the yield draws income buyers who defend the level.

### Worked example — NTPC 20-WEMA pullback

NTPC has trended from ₹200 to ₹360 over fourteen months, never closing a week below its 20-WEMA (currently ₹330). It corrects for five weeks to ₹332, printing a bullish hammer on the weekly chart right at the average, then closes the next week at ₹344, back above the 10-WEMA.

- **Entry:** ₹345 on the reclaim.
- **Stop:** ₹325, below the 20-WEMA and the correction low — ₹20 risk.
- **Target:** prior high ₹360 first; then measured continuation toward ₹390 if the broader market cooperates.
- **Management:** this is a position trade, not a scalp. Size it for the ₹20 stop, hold for weeks, trail under the rising 20-WEMA.

Risk note: PSU trends die *fast* when the theme breaks (a budget disappointment, a policy reversal). The wide 20-WEMA stop protects you in the trend but will hurt on the day the story ends — respect position size.

## Cluster 3 — Defensive compounders: Sun Pharma, Divi's

### Character and drivers

Pharma is the market's shock absorber. When the index is risk-off and metals are bleeding, money rotates into pharma for its dollar-earner, non-cyclical demand profile. The chart character is a **low-volatility grind** punctuated by **binary event gaps** — a USFDA observation (Form 483), an OAI classification, or a big ANDA approval can gap the stock 8% either way overnight.

Sun Pharma is the sector bellwether — the largest, most liquid, and the truest "index of the sector". Divi's is an API/CRAMS play, more sensitive to global pharma capex and China+1 sentiment, and a sharper mover.

### Key levels and behaviour

Because the base trend is a low-vol grind, the **best entries are the mean-reversion pullbacks to the rising 50-DMA**, not breakouts. Defensives reward patience and punish chasing. The event risk means you should *never* carry a full-size position into a known USFDA inspection outcome or a results date — reduce to a starter or hedge with options.

### Best setups

1. **The 50-DMA reclaim in an uptrend.** Buy pullbacks to the rising 50-DMA with a tight stop; these names respect the average.
2. **The post-event fade or follow.** After an FDA gap, let the first hour settle; if the stock reclaims the gap and holds, it usually grinds in the gap direction for days (the crowd underreacts to good news in defensives).
3. **The options-defined event play.** Ahead of a known binary, buy a defined-risk options structure (a debit spread) rather than the stock, so the gap can't blow your account.

### Worked example — Sun Pharma 50-DMA buy

Sun Pharma is in a steady uptrend, 50-DMA rising at ₹1,720. The stock pulls back over eight sessions from ₹1,800 to ₹1,725, printing a doji right at the average on light volume with the daily RSI dipping to 42.

- **Entry:** ₹1,735 on the bounce off the 50-DMA.
- **Stop:** ₹1,698, below the average and the swing low — ₹37 risk.
- **Target:** prior high ₹1,800; extended ₹1,850.
- **Reward:risk:** ~1.7R to 3R. Clean, patient, low-drama.

Risk note: check the FDA/results calendar before entering. If an inspection outcome is due within the holding window, either wait or use options. A defensive that gaps down 9% on a Form 483 is no longer defensive.

## Cluster 4 — Rate-sensitive NBFC: Bajaj Finance

### Character and drivers

Bajaj Finance deserves its own cluster. It is the market's most-watched consumer-lending proxy, extraordinarily sensitive to the RBI rate cycle, AUM growth prints, and NIM commentary. It trends *hard* — both up and down — and it does not do gentle. A results miss can take it 8–10% down in a session; a rate-cut cycle can compound it 40% over two quarters.

The stock's character is **momentum persistence**: once a trend establishes after a results reaction, it tends to run. This makes it a favourite for trend-followers and a graveyard for mean-reversion traders who try to catch the falling knife on a results day.

### Key levels and behaviour

Bajaj Finance respects its **results-gap levels** for months. The high or low of the results-day candle becomes a durable support/resistance shelf. Post-results, mark that candle's range — it is your map for the quarter. The stock also honours large round numbers given its high absolute price.

### Best setups

1. **The results-gap continuation.** After a results gap, wait one to three sessions. If the stock holds above the gap-day low (post gap-up) and breaks the gap-day high, go with it; the move often extends for weeks.
2. **The trend pullback to the 20-EMA.** In an established up-trend, buy the first shallow pullback to the 20-EMA (daily) with a stop under the prior swing low.
3. **The RBI-event straddle (options).** Ahead of a policy that could go either way, a long straddle can pay because Bajaj Finance rarely sits still after the RBI.

### Worked example — Bajaj Finance results-gap continuation

Bajaj Finance reports strong AUM growth and gaps up from ₹7,200 to ₹7,650, closing the results day at ₹7,700 (day's range ₹7,550–₹7,780). Over the next two sessions it consolidates above ₹7,600, never breaching the gap-day low.

- **Entry:** ₹7,790 on the break above the results-day high of ₹7,780.
- **Stop:** ₹7,540, below the gap-day low — ₹250 risk (respect the high absolute rupee value; size accordingly).
- **Target:** measured move of the gap (₹450) projected gives ₹8,240; trail beyond.
- **Management:** this is a high-conviction continuation only *because* the gap held. If the stock had closed the gap and printed below ₹7,550, the setup is void — do not force it.

Risk note: never trade Bajaj Finance *into* results on directional stock exposure. The gap risk is asymmetric and unhedgeable in the underlying. If you have a view, express it with defined-risk options.

## Cross-cluster execution rules

Regardless of which name you trade, four rules hold:

1. **Match the setup to the character.** Breakouts in metals and Bajaj Finance; pullbacks in pharma and PSU trends. Using a breakout template on Sun Pharma or a mean-reversion template on a metals breakout is fighting the stock's nature.
2. **Volume is the lie-detector.** Especially in cyclicals and PSUs, a move on below-average delivery volume is suspect. TradingView/Chartink delivery-volume scans separate real breakouts from traps.
3. **Position-size by rupee-risk, not lot count.** One lot of Bajaj Finance carries a vastly different rupee stop than one lot of Tata Steel. Always size so the *stop distance in rupees × lot size × lots = your fixed risk per trade* (typically ≤1% of capital).
4. **Respect the event calendar.** Results dates, RBI policy, USFDA outcomes, and budget/policy events convert a technical setup into a coin-flip. Either stand aside, reduce, or hedge with options — never carry naked full-size directional exposure into a known binary.

## Pitfalls that empty accounts in single-stock F&O

- **Chasing the gap.** The most expensive habit. Buying a metals or Bajaj Finance stock at the open after a 5% gap, right into the exhaustion, is how retail donates to the market. Let the opening range form first.
- **Averaging down on a "cheap" defensive.** Sun Pharma at ₹1,600 "looks cheap" after a Form 483 gap — until the OAI classification takes it to ₹1,450. A gap on bad news is not a discount; it is new information.
- **Trading the whole cluster as correlated.** In a metals sell-off you might short three metal names and think you have three trades. You have one trade in triple size. Correlated positions must be counted as one risk unit.
- **Ignoring liquidity in the far strikes.** These names are liquid at-the-money, but deep OTM options can have absurd spreads. Model your options fills at the mid, not the last-traded fantasy price.

## Interview-ready summary

- The core thesis of single-stock TA is that **character is edge**: metals gap and lead the index, PSUs trend for a year on the 20-WEMA, defensives grind and carry binary FDA risk, and Bajaj Finance is a momentum machine that must never be traded naked into results.
- **Match setup to character:** volume-confirmed breakouts and gap-continuations for high-beta cyclicals and NBFC; 50-DMA / 20-WEMA pullback buys for defensives and PSU trends.
- **Risk discipline is non-negotiable:** size by rupee-risk not lots, count correlated cluster positions as one unit, respect the event calendar, and prefer defined-risk options into any binary. Most retail traders lose in single-stock F&O precisely by ignoring these — the edge is not in the entry, it is in the survival.
