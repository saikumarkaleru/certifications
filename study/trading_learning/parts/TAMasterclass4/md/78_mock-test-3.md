# Mock Test III

This is a mixed, exam-style paper built to stress the *decision* muscle, not the definition muscle. Volumes I–III taught you the vocabulary; this test asks whether you can pull the right tool at the right moment when the tape is moving and the P&L is real. Every scenario is India-specific — Nifty, Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty, real NSE F&O names, MCX, USDINR, realistic 2026 levels — and every answer key explains the *why*, not just the letter.

**How to sit this paper.** Give yourself 75 minutes. Write your answer and a one-line reason for each of the 20 items before you look at any key. Score 1 mark for the right call, 1 mark for the right reason. A call that is "right" for the wrong reason is worth zero in real trading, because you cannot repeat it. Target: 30/40 to consider yourself exam-ready; below 24/40, re-read the flagged volumes before Mock Test IV.

## Section A — Breadth, Sentiment & Bias (Q1–Q6)

**Q1.** Nifty closes at a fresh all-time high of 26,180, up 0.4% on the day. But the NSE advance-decline line shows 18 advances against 32 declines among the Nifty 50, and the broader market shows 1,050 declines to 900 advances. The percentage of Nifty 500 stocks above their 50-DMA has fallen from 68% three weeks ago to 41% today, even as the index printed higher. What is the single most accurate reading, and what is your positioning bias for the next 2–3 weeks?

**Q2.** India VIX is at 11.2 — near a 12-month low. A trader argues, "VIX is low, so the market is safe; I should sell strangles aggressively for premium." State whether the logic is sound and give the one correction a professional would make.

**Q3.** The put-call ratio (PCR) by open interest on Nifty weekly options has risen from 0.8 to 1.6 over four sessions while the index fell 3%. A retail forum reads this as "extremely bullish contrarian signal — everyone is bearish, so buy." Evaluate.

**Q4.** You are tracking the NSE 52-week high/low differential. On Monday: 210 new highs, 40 new lows. By Friday: 60 new highs, 240 new lows — while the Nifty itself is down only 1.5% for the week. What breadth phenomenon is this, and what does it warn about the index level's reliability?

**Q5.** FII cash-segment data shows net selling of ₹14,000 crore over five sessions, but Nifty is flat and Bank Nifty is *up* 1.2%. DII (domestic) buying is ₹15,500 crore. What is the intermarket/flow read, and which index is structurally better supported here?

**Q6.** The Nifty Midcap 150 / Nifty 50 ratio line has been falling for six weeks and just broke a rising trendline that held since early in the year. What does a falling midcap/large-cap ratio tell you about the market's risk appetite, and how should it shift your stock-selection filter?

## Section B — Sector & Stock Playbook (Q7–Q12)

**Q7.** Bank Nifty is at 57,400. HDFC Bank (weight leader) is basing above its 200-DMA with rising volume, ICICI Bank is at an all-time high, but Kotak Mahindra Bank and IndusInd Bank are both below their 200-DMA and making lower highs. Bank Nifty itself is 2% below its record. Is the index setup healthy or hollow, and what confirmation would flip your view?

**Q8.** Nifty IT is under pressure. USDINR has fallen from 84.20 to 83.10 (rupee strengthening) over a month. TCS and Infosys are both at 3-month lows. A junior analyst says "IT is weak because of poor results." Give the *intermarket* driver he is missing and the direction it argues for.

**Q9.** Reliance Industries is consolidating in a tight ₹2,900–3,050 range for seven weeks on declining volume, sitting just under its 50-DMA. Bollinger Band width has compressed to a 12-month low. What is the highest-probability *character* read, and how do you structure a trade that does not pick a direction prematurely?

**Q10.** Tata Motors gaps up 6% on results, closing near the high of the day on volume 4× the 20-day average, clearing a six-month resistance at ₹1,020. The next two sessions hold above ₹1,020 on lighter but above-average volume. Classify the pattern and give the invalidation level.

**Q11.** Nifty Pharma is breaking to a new high while Nifty itself is stuck below resistance. Sun Pharma, Cipla and Divis are all above rising 50-DMAs. You want one long. Which selection principle picks the name, and what is the trap of simply "buying the strongest chart"?

**Q12.** Adani Enterprises shows a 9% intraday range with a long upper wick, closing in the lower third, on volume 3× average, right at a prior swing high. It is an F&O stock with a wide spread. What does the candle tell you about supply, and what execution risk is specific to this name?

## Section C — Risk & Trade Management (Q13–Q17)

**Q13.** Your account is ₹10,00,000. You risk 1% per trade. You buy Nifty 26,000 CE at ₹120 with a stop that, if hit, loses ₹40 of option premium (the underlying move to your technical stop). Lot size 75. How many lots can you take, and what is the rupee risk?

**Q14.** You are long Bank Nifty futures from 56,800. It runs to 58,200 (+1,400 points). Your original stop was 56,300 (−500). Describe a concrete trailing method that locks in profit without choking the trade, and state where the stop should sit now under that method if the last swing low is 57,600.

**Q15.** A trader has taken six losing trades in a row today, each a valid setup, each stopped out. He wants to "make it back" by doubling size on the seventh. Diagnose the two separate errors — one statistical, one psychological — and give the rule that neutralizes both.

**Q16.** You hold five open positions: long Nifty, long Bank Nifty futures, long ICICI Bank, long SBI, long Axis Bank. Each is a "different" trade. What hidden risk are you actually carrying, and what is the portfolio-level metric that would have caught it?

**Q17.** You sell a Nifty weekly 26,200/26,400 call spread (bear call) for ₹60 credit, max loss ₹140. Two days before expiry Nifty sits at 26,190 and the spread is worth ₹35. Time decay is on your side but a strong close could breach 26,200. State the management decision framework and one objective exit trigger.

## Section D — Chart-Reading & Interpretation (Q18–Q20)

**Q18.** On the Nifty daily: price made a higher high at 26,300 but RSI(14) made a lower high (72 → 64). Simultaneously MACD histogram is shrinking above zero. Two days later a bearish engulfing candle forms on rising volume. Assemble these into one thesis and one trade with a defined risk.

**Q19.** USDINR daily has formed an ascending triangle: flat resistance at 84.40 tested four times, rising support from 83.20. It breaks 84.40 with a wide-range candle. What is the measured-move target, and what does a rising USDINR simultaneously imply for Nifty IT vs Nifty FMCG (importers)?

**Q20.** MCX Gold (₹ per 10g) has rallied from 71,000 to 78,500 in a steep, near-vertical run, RSI at 84, price now 9% above its 20-DMA — the widest stretch in a year. Comex gold is flat and USDINR is stable. What is the disciplined read, and what is the *wrong* lesson most retail traders take from a chart like this?

## ANSWER KEY — Section A

**A1.** This is a classic **breadth divergence at a new high** — the index is being carried by a shrinking group of heavyweight stocks while participation collapses underneath. A fresh high on negative advance-decline, with % above 50-DMA falling from 68% to 41%, means the *average* stock topped weeks ago; the cap-weighted print is a mirage sustained by two or three mega-caps. Bias: **defensive / reduce longs, do not initiate fresh index longs, tighten stops.** This is not a short signal by itself — indices can grind higher on narrow leadership for a while — but the risk/reward of new longs is poor. Reason mark: you must name *narrowing participation*, not just "market weak."

**A2.** The logic is **half right and dangerously incomplete.** Low VIX does mean realized volatility has been low, but VIX is mean-reverting: an 11-handle is not "safe," it is the point of *maximum complacency* where premium is cheapest and a volatility spike is most likely to blow up naked short-vega positions. The professional correction: **never size short-premium off the premium alone — size off the tail.** Sell defined-risk spreads (iron condors), not naked strangles, when VIX is at the low of its range, because the asymmetry (limited gain, unlimited loss into an event) is worst exactly here.

**A3.** The contrarian read is **naïve.** A PCR jumping 0.8 → 1.6 while price falls 3% usually means genuine hedging and directional put-buying, i.e. the trend is *confirming*, not exhausting. PCR is only a useful contrarian tool at **extremes that coincide with a price reversal signal and capitulation**, not mid-decline. A rising PCR in a falling market is momentum, not a floor. You wait for PCR to spike *and* for price to stabilize/reverse before treating it as contrarian. "Everyone is bearish so buy" ignores that everyone can be right for another 3%.

**A4.** This is a **breadth-thrust reversal in the new-high/new-low line** — the H/L differential flipped from strongly positive (+170) to strongly negative (−180) while the index barely moved. It warns that the index level is **unreliable / hollow**: a handful of large caps are masking broad internal deterioration. When new lows expand rapidly under a flat index, the next index leg is far more likely down. Treat the 1.5% index resilience as a *lagging* comfort, not a signal.

**A5.** The read: **domestic institutions are absorbing foreign selling**, and the absorption is concentrated in banks — hence Bank Nifty up while FIIs sell. DII buying (₹15,500 cr) exceeding FII selling (₹14,000 cr) is net-positive flow. Structurally, **Bank Nifty is better supported** because DII money in India is disproportionately allocated to financials (large-cap banks dominate domestic MF holdings). Nifty broadly is neutral-to-supported; the relative strength sits with banks. Reason mark: identify *who is the marginal buyer* and *where their money goes*.

**A6.** A falling **midcap/large-cap ratio** signals **risk-off rotation**: money is leaving higher-beta midcaps for the safety of large caps, which is defensive behavior even inside an up-market. Breaking the multi-month rising trendline confirms the rotation is structural, not noise. It should shift your stock-selection filter toward **large-cap, liquid, index-heavyweight names** and away from midcap breakouts, which will suffer higher failure rates in this regime. Trading midcap longs against a falling ratio is fighting the flow.

## ANSWER KEY — Section B

**A7.** The setup is **hollow, not healthy.** Bank Nifty being 2% below its record while two constituents (Kotak, IndusInd) are below their 200-DMA making lower highs means the index is leaning entirely on HDFC and ICICI. Narrow leadership in a bank index is fragile because financials are highly correlated — if the leaders stall, there is no second line. **Confirmation that flips the view to healthy:** Kotak and IndusInd reclaiming their 200-DMAs *and* the Bank Nifty advance-decline (constituents above 50-DMA) improving to a majority. Until then, treat Bank Nifty strength as leader-dependent and keep stops tight.

**A8.** The missing intermarket driver is the **rupee.** IT services earn in dollars and report in rupees; a rupee strengthening from 84.20 to 83.10 (a ~1.3% appreciation) is a direct *headwind to reported margins and revenue* for TCS and Infosys, independent of operational results. The junior analyst attributed to "poor results" what is substantially a **currency translation effect.** Direction: a strengthening rupee argues *bearish* for exporters (IT, pharma) and *supportive* for importers. If you expect USDINR to keep falling, stay underweight IT.

**A9.** The character read is **volatility contraction preceding expansion** — a seven-week tight range on declining volume with Bollinger width at a 12-month low is a coiled spring; the market is storing energy and will resolve with a range-expansion move, direction unknown. Structure a trade that does not pre-guess direction: a **long straddle/strangle** (buy volatility) sized for the eventual break, *or* wait and trade the **breakout with a stop on the other side of the range** (buy above ₹3,050 / short below ₹2,900) with the opposite range boundary as invalidation. The edge is in the *expansion*, not the direction.

**A10.** This is a **breakaway gap + high-volume breakout of a six-month base**, followed by a successful **retest hold** above the breakout level. The 4× volume on the gap day confirms institutional participation; holding above ₹1,020 for two sessions on above-average volume validates the breakout rather than a bull trap. **Invalidation: a decisive close back below ₹1,020** (the breakout pivot, now support) — ideally below the low of the gap day. That close would mean the breakout failed and supply reclaimed control.

**A11.** The correct selection principle is **relative strength combined with the best base structure, not raw price height.** Among three strong names you pick the one with (a) the tightest, most orderly consolidation, (b) rising volume on up-days, and (c) the cleanest risk (nearest logical stop). The trap of "buying the strongest chart" is that the most-extended name is often the most *overbought* and offers the *worst* entry (widest stop, least room). Strongest sector, best *structure* within it — that is the professional's order of operations.

**A12.** The candle is a **rejection / distribution candle**: a long upper wick with a close in the lower third on 3× volume at a prior swing high means **supply overwhelmed demand** at resistance — buyers pushed it up, sellers slammed it back. It is a bearish reversal warning, not confirmation yet. The execution risk specific to Adani names: **high volatility, wide bid-ask spreads and gap risk** mean slippage on both entry and stop can be severe; a stop-loss order may fill far worse than its trigger. Size smaller and use the underlying's average true range, not a fixed rupee stop.

## ANSWER KEY — Section C

**A13.** Risk budget = 1% of ₹10,00,000 = **₹10,000.** Rupee risk per lot = ₹40 (premium loss at stop) × 75 (lot size) = ₹3,000 per lot. Lots = ₹10,000 ÷ ₹3,000 = **3.33, round down to 3 lots.** Rupee risk taken = 3 × ₹3,000 = **₹9,000** (under budget). Never round up — 4 lots = ₹12,000 = 1.2% risk, a breach. The discipline is: position size is an *output* of the stop distance and risk budget, never an input you choose emotionally.

**A14.** A concrete method: the **swing-low trail** (or a 2×ATR chandelier). Once the trade moves a meaningful multiple of the initial risk in your favor, you stop trailing by a fixed number and instead **trail beneath each successively higher swing low**, giving the trend room to breathe but banking structure. With the last swing low at 57,600, the **stop moves to just below 57,600 — say 57,550.** This converts a −500 risk into a locked ~+750 minimum (57,550 vs 56,800 entry) while leaving upside open. The error to avoid is a tight fixed trail that chokes a trending future on normal noise.

**A15.** Statistical error: **six losses in a row from valid setups is fully expected variance**, not evidence the method is broken. With a 45% win-rate strategy, a streak of six losers occurs routinely over a career — the sample is too small to conclude anything, and there is *no* mathematical reason the seventh is "due" to win (gambler's fallacy). Psychological error: **revenge trading / loss-chasing** — doubling size to "make it back" is emotional, and it maximizes size exactly when judgment is worst. The rule that neutralizes both: a **hard daily loss limit** (e.g. stop trading at −3R or −3% for the day), size-locked, no exceptions. It caps the tail and removes the decision from a tilted brain.

**A16.** The hidden risk is **correlation / concentration.** Long Nifty, long Bank Nifty, and three large banks (ICICI, SBI, Axis) are *not* five different trades — they are effectively **one leveraged bet on Indian financials and the broad market**, which are highly correlated. In a bank-sector down day every position bleeds together; your true risk is several times what you think. The portfolio metric that catches it: **beta-weighted / correlation-adjusted net exposure** (sum positions in delta-equivalent index terms). It would show, say, "4.5× effective long Bank Nifty," prompting you to cut overlap.

**A17.** Framework: for a credit spread near expiry the questions are (1) *how much of the max profit is already captured?* and (2) *how close is the short strike?* Here you sold for ₹60, it's worth ₹35, so you've captured ₹25 of ₹60 (~42%) with two days left, and Nifty at 26,190 is **only 10 points under the short strike of 26,200** — uncomfortably close. Decision: the remaining ₹35 of premium does not justify sitting through breach risk into expiry with the short strike essentially at-the-money. Objective exit trigger: **buy the spread back (close) if Nifty closes above 26,200**, or if the spread's value rises back toward your entry credit (₹55–60), whichever first — take the partial profit or the small loss before it becomes the ₹140 max loss.

## ANSWER KEY — Section D

**A18.** Thesis: this is a **triple bearish confluence at a high** — (1) bearish RSI divergence (higher price high, lower RSI high = weakening momentum), (2) shrinking MACD histogram above zero (the up-thrust is losing steam), and (3) a bearish engulfing candle on rising volume (supply took control with participation). Together they signal a probable short-term top. Trade: **short/exit longs on a break below the engulfing candle's low**, stop above 26,300 (the divergent high), target the prior consolidation or the 20-DMA. Defined risk = high (26,300) minus entry; only take it if reward ≥ 1.5× that risk. Divergence alone is not a signal — the engulfing candle is the *trigger*.

**A19.** Measured move: triangle height = resistance (84.40) − support at the point the pattern began (~83.20) = **1.20 rupees**, projected from the 84.40 breakout → **target ≈ 85.60.** Intermarket implication of a rising USDINR (rupee weakening): **bullish for Nifty IT** (exporters earn dollars, gain on translation) and a **headwind for importers/FMCG** with dollar-denominated input costs and for oil-sensitive names. So a USDINR breakout is a signal to *rotate toward IT and pharma and away from import-heavy consumption names* — a clean cross-asset trade idea.

**A20.** Disciplined read: MCX Gold is **climactically overbought and over-extended** — RSI 84 and price 9% above the 20-DMA (a one-year stretch extreme) with Comex flat and USDINR stable means the move is **momentum/sentiment-driven with no fresh fundamental fuel**, so mean-reversion risk is high. The professional does *not* short a strong trend blindly, but does **stop chasing, tighten trailing stops, and refuse fresh longs at the extreme** — wait for a pullback to the 20-DMA or a consolidation. The *wrong* lesson most retail traders take: "it's going vertical, it must keep going — buy more." Chasing a parabolic 84-RSI move is where the worst risk/reward entries and the largest drawdowns are created. The chart is a warning, not an invitation.

**Interview-ready summary.** The through-line of Mock III is that *the index price is the least reliable single number on your screen.* Breadth, flows, sentiment, correlation and the tape underneath tell you whether a price is trustworthy. Every professional edge in this paper came from refusing to take a number at face value — a new high with collapsing breadth, a low VIX that hides tail risk, five "different" trades that are one bet, a parabolic chart that begs to be chased. Master the habit of asking "what is *underneath* this print?" and you have most of what separates the survivors from the statistics.
