# Infra & Capital Goods TA

Infrastructure and capital goods is the sector that best captures the Indian capex super-cycle story of the 2020s — roads, railways, power transmission, defence indigenisation, industrial machinery, EPC (engineering-procurement-construction) contractors and the equipment makers who feed them. On the chart these names — L&T, Siemens, ABB, Cummins, Thermax, BHEL, RVNL, IRCON, KEC, KEI, Polycab, Kalpataru, GR Infra, PNC Infratech, HG Infra — behave as **order-book momentum stocks**: they trend hard and long when the capex narrative is on, and they give back years of gains in months when it turns. This chapter is the dedicated playbook: what drives them, the levels and behaviour that matter, the setups that work, a worked example, and the risk notes that keep you solvent when a beloved theme rolls over.

## The instrument's character & drivers

Capital goods and infra is a heterogeneous bucket, and lumping it together is the first mistake. Separate the families:

- **Diversified engineering & capital goods** — L&T (the bellwether and Nifty heavyweight), Siemens, ABB, Cummins India, Thermax, CG Power, Bharat Forge. These are quality, institutionally-owned compounders. They trend cleanly and respect technical levels well.
- **EPC / construction contractors** — L&T (again), KEC International, Kalpataru Projects, NCC, Larsen's peers, GR Infra, PNC Infratech, HG Infra, Dilip Buildcon. Balance-sheet-heavy, working-capital-intensive, order-book stories. More volatile, more debt-sensitive.
- **Railways** — RVNL, IRCON, RailTel, Titagarh, Texmaco, Jupiter Wagons, plus the financiers IRFC. A pure theme cluster that went vertical in 2023-24.
- **Power capex & transmission** — BHEL, Siemens Energy peers, KEC, KEI Industries, Polycab, Hitachi Energy, Transformers & Rectifiers, GE Vernova T&D. Beneficiaries of the transmission and power-equipment upcycle.
- **Cables, wires & industrial products** — Polycab, KEI, Havells, Finolex, RR Kabel, Cummins, SKF, Timken, AIA Engineering, Grindwell. Consumer-adjacent industrials with cleaner charts.

What they share, and why the TA is distinctive:

**1. Order books are the fundamental fuel; charts front-run the order flow.** These stocks re-rate on order-inflow announcements, budget capex allocations, and the government's infrastructure pipeline. The chart often anticipates the order news — accumulation shows up as a tightening base before the announcement. Read bases as accumulation ahead of catalysts.

**2. They are high-beta to the capex regime.** In a capex-on macro (rising government capex, private capex revival, strong PMI), the whole bucket trends together for quarters. In a capex-off phase or a broad risk-off, they fall harder than the Nifty because much of the move was multiple-expansion, not just earnings.

**3. The theme runs in long, clean trends — then blows off.** Unlike PSU banks (sharp reversion), quality capital-goods names like L&T, Siemens and ABB trend for *years* with orderly pullbacks to the 50-DMA. The junior, retail-loved names (RVNL, railway wagons, small EPC) go parabolic and top violently. Match your setup to which family you are in.

**4. Nifty Capital Market of this space: the indices.** Use **Nifty Infrastructure, Nifty Capital Goods (and the broader Nifty India Manufacturing / Nifty CPSE)** as the regime filter, plus the **Nifty India Defence** and **rail cluster** for the theme sub-groups. The Bank Nifty / Nifty relationship matters too — capex stocks need financing, so a healthy PSU/lending environment supports them.

**5. Liquidity and F&O coverage vary.** L&T, Siemens, ABB, BHEL, Polycab, KEI, Cummins, RVNL, IRFC are liquid and mostly F&O-listed. Many mid-cap EPC and wagon names are cash-only, thinly traded, and gap violently — treat them as small, un-hedgeable positions.

## Key levels & behaviour

**L&T is the sector's compass.** As the largest, most liquid, index-heavy name, L&T sets the tone. If L&T is trending above a rising 50-DMA and making higher highs, the capex regime is *on* and pullbacks in the junior names are buyable. If L&T breaks its trend, treat the whole bucket as suspect regardless of how exciting the small-cap rail chart looks. Read L&T first.

**Order-win gaps become support.** When a capital-goods name gaps up on a large order announcement (an L&T mega-order, a BEL/BHEL/RVNL contract), that gap frequently holds and the gap-fill level becomes support for weeks. This is the opposite of a sentiment gap — real order flow anchors price. Mark the gap edges.

**Round numbers and prior all-time highs.** L&T ₹3,000/₹3,500/₹4,000; Siemens and ABB at psychological thousands; Polycab and KEI at round levels. Because much of this bucket has been in *price discovery* (repeated all-time highs during the 2023-24 run), the relevant resistance is often "no overhead supply" — the stock is above all prior trading, and pullbacks find support at prior breakout shelves and the 20/50-DMA rather than at old tops.

**Bases before budgets.** These stocks tighten into the Union Budget (Feb 1) on capex-allocation hopes. A multi-week ascending base into late January that breaks out with volume is a recurring, tradeable pattern. Conversely, "sell the budget" reversion happens in the over-extended junior names when the capex number disappoints expectations.

**Volume is the truth-teller in EPC/rail names.** Because the retail-heavy juniors gap on news, the *quality* of a breakout is in the volume and the follow-through candle. A breakout on 2x+ volume that holds day two is real; a spike-and-fade on a single euphoric candle is distribution.

## Best setups

**Setup 1 — The stage-2 trend pullback (core, for quality names).**
In a confirmed uptrend (price above a rising 50-DMA, 50 above 200, Nifty Capital Goods index trending up), buy L&T / Siemens / ABB / Cummins / Polycab on a controlled pullback to the 20- or 50-EMA that prints a reversal candle on declining down-volume. Stop below the 50-EMA / the pullback swing low. This is the highest-quality-of-life setup in the sector: these names trend for quarters and reward patience. Add on subsequent higher-low pullbacks; trail behind the 50-DMA.

**Setup 2 — The order-win gap-hold breakout.**
When a liquid capital-goods name gaps up on a genuine large-order announcement and *holds* the gap for two to three sessions (higher low above the gap), enter on the tight consolidation break with a stop below the gap-fill. You are buying confirmed institutional order flow with a defined risk to the gap edge. Target the measured move of the consolidation or the next round number.

**Setup 3 — The ascending-base breakout into a capex catalyst.**
Identify a multi-week ascending base (higher lows, flat top) forming ahead of the Budget or a known ordering cycle. Enter on the volume-backed break of the base top; stop below the last higher low. This front-runs the order/allocation news the chart is already discounting. Works best in L&T, KEC, KEI, Polycab, and the diversified engineering names — the ones with clean bases rather than parabolic spikes.

**Setup 4 — The blow-off exit discipline (for the junior/rail/EPC names).**
This is a *risk* setup as much as a trade. When a retail-favourite (RVNL, a wagon maker, a small EPC) has gone parabolic — 6+ green weeks, price 40-60% above the 50-DMA, RSI pinned above 80, and volume climbing while candle bodies shrink — you exit into strength rather than trailing a stop. Optionally, experienced traders fade the first bearish outside day on record volume with a hard stop above the high. The junior names top like PSU banks: sharply, on the first distribution day.

## A worked example — an order-book breakout and the trend that followed

Take a composite, realistic KEC International-style setup (a transmission/EPC contractor) during a capex-on regime.

The macro backdrop: Nifty Capital Goods is trending above a rising 50-DMA, and L&T — the compass — is making higher highs above ₹3,500 with orderly pullbacks. Regime is *on*, so junior names are buyable on strength. KEC has spent seven weeks building an **ascending base** between ₹740 and ₹800 — higher lows at ₹745, ₹758, ₹770, with a flat ceiling near ₹800. Volume has been drying up into the apex (classic accumulation). **This is Setup 3.**

In the third week of January, ahead of the Budget, KEC announces a large transmission order and gaps to ₹815, closing the day at ₹822 on volume 2.4x its 20-day average — clearing the ₹800 base top decisively. The next two sessions it holds ₹810-825 (a higher low above the gap), which now overlaps Setup 2 (order-win gap-hold). You enter the tight consolidation break at ₹826 with a stop at ₹788 (below the gap-fill and the base top). Base height was ₹60 (₹740→₹800), so the measured target is ₹860; the next round number is ₹900.

KEC reaches ₹862 within two weeks (first target — book a third, trail the rest behind the 20-EMA). The capex theme stays hot; the stock pulls back once to the rising 20-EMA near ₹840 on light volume — a textbook Setup 1 add — before extending to ₹905. You raise the trailing stop behind each higher low. Over the next two months, in the confirmed uptrend, KEC grinds to ₹960 with two more buyable 50-EMA pullbacks, and you keep the core position trailing behind the 50-DMA rather than trying to pick the top — because *quality* capital-goods names in a capex-on regime trend far longer than they "should."

The trade only ends when the character changes: L&T (the compass) breaks its 50-DMA, Nifty Capital Goods rolls over, and KEC loses its 50-EMA on expanding down-volume. That regime break — not a price target — is the exit for the trailed core. The lesson: in this sector you enter on a base/order catalyst, you *add* on trend pullbacks, and you exit on **regime change read through the bellwether and the index**, not on a fixed number.

## Risk notes

**Multiple-expansion cuts both ways.** Much of a capex-cycle rally is the market paying a higher P/E for the same earnings on order-book optimism. When sentiment turns, the multiple compresses fast — these stocks fall harder than the index because you are unwinding hope, not just earnings. Never assume a "great order book" protects the chart.

**EPC balance sheets carry debt and working-capital risk.** Construction contractors can look cheap and technically strong right up to a receivables/execution shock. Keep positions in the debt-heavy EPC names smaller and stops tighter than in the asset-light engineering compounders.

**The junior names gap and can't be hedged.** Small-cap rail, wagon and EPC stocks are cash-only, thin, and gap violently on news. Size them as speculative positions you can lose 20-30% of overnight, and never carry large unhedged size into results or the Budget.

**Concentration across the theme.** Owning L&T, KEC, KEI, RVNL and a wagon maker feels diversified but is one capex bet. On a capex-off day they move together. Cap total sector exposure and prefer one high-quality expression at size plus smaller satellites.

**Do not chase the vertical.** The single most expensive infra/capital-goods mistake is buying the junior names after a 5-6 week parabola because "the theme is structural." Structural themes still have 40-50% drawdowns. Buy pullbacks in a confirmed trend; do not buy exhaustion.

**Regime is everything — read the bellwether.** If L&T and Nifty Capital Goods roll over, exit or hedge regardless of any single stock's chart. The bucket is a beta-to-capex machine; when the regime turns, individual "good charts" do not save you.

## Interview-ready summary

- **Infra & capital goods are order-book momentum stocks** — they front-run order inflows and budget capex; the chart discounts the pipeline before the news prints.
- **Separate the families:** quality engineering compounders (L&T, Siemens, ABB, Cummins) trend for *years* with orderly pullbacks; junior/rail/EPC names (RVNL, wagons, small EPC) go parabolic and top violently. Match the setup to the family.
- **L&T is the compass and Nifty Capital Goods / Nifty Infrastructure is the regime filter** — read them first; individual "good charts" do not survive a regime break.
- **Levels that matter:** order-win gaps that become support, ascending bases into the Budget, prior breakout shelves and the 20/50-DMA in price-discovery names, and volume as the truth-teller on junior breakouts.
- **Core setups:** (1) stage-2 trend pullback to the 20/50-EMA in quality names, (2) order-win gap-hold breakout, (3) ascending-base breakout into a capex catalyst, (4) blow-off exit discipline on the parabolic juniors.
- **Signature risks:** multiple-compression on the downturn (they fall harder than the Nifty), debt/working-capital shocks in EPC names, un-hedgeable gaps in the thin juniors, and hidden concentration across a single capex theme.
- Honest odds: the capex super-cycle is real but not a one-way ticket — most retail losses come from chasing parabolic junior names late and holding through 40-50% drawdowns. The edge is buying breadth- and bellwether-confirmed pullbacks and respecting regime change.
