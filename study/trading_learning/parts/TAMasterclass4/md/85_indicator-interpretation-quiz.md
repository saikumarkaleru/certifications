# Indicator-Interpretation Quiz

Patterns tell you *what* the price is doing. Indicators tell you *how* it is doing it — the momentum, the participation, the exhaustion, the volatility. Volume III drilled the mechanics of every indicator, from RSI and MACD to the encyclopedia oscillators. This chapter drills the harder skill: **reading indicators in context, together, and knowing when they lie.**

The single biggest mistake retail traders make with indicators is treating them as signals in isolation — "RSI crossed 70, sell." That is not interpretation; it is superstition. A real interpreter asks: *What is the trend? What is the indicator saying relative to that trend? Do the indicators agree with each other? Is this a signal or a trap?* An overbought RSI in a raging bull market is a feature, not a warning — it can stay overbought for weeks while the crowd shorts into strength and gets run over.

Below are 24 scenarios, India-first, each with the numbers you need. Cover the answer, write your read (**bias + action + why**), then check. Score 1 point each for correct bias, correct action, and correct *reasoning*. 72/72 is perfect. The reasoning point is the one that matters — anyone can guess a direction.

## Ground rules for indicator reading

1. **Trend first, indicator second.** Always establish the trend before interpreting any oscillator. The same RSI reading means opposite things in an uptrend vs a range.
2. **Divergence > absolute level.** A momentum indicator making a lower high while price makes a higher high (bearish divergence) is far more informative than any fixed threshold.
3. **Confluence beats single signals.** Two indicators of *different types* (a momentum oscillator + a volume indicator) agreeing is worth ten of the same type agreeing.
4. **Indicators lag; they confirm, they don't predict.** Moving-average crossovers, MACD signal-line crosses — all lag price. Use them to confirm structure, not to front-run it.
5. **Context kills thresholds.** 70/30 on RSI, 80/20 on Stochastic, 0-line on MACD — none of these are mechanical buy/sell buttons.

## Section A — Momentum oscillators (Q1–Q8)

**Q1.** Nifty daily, strong 6-week uptrend. RSI(14) = 78, has been above 70 for nine sessions. Price still making higher highs. A retail tip says "RSI overbought, short." Your read?

**Q2.** SBIN daily, downtrend. Price makes a new low at ₹538. RSI(14) makes a *higher* low (was 22 at the prior price low, now 31 at the new price low). Read it.

**Q3.** Bank Nifty, hourly, in a tight range. Stochastic(14,3,3) %K crosses above %D at 18 (below 20), then again a "sell" cross at 85. How do you use Stochastic here vs in a trend?

**Q4.** RELIANCE daily. MACD line crosses above the signal line while both are still *below* zero, after a long downtrend. Volume is picking up. What kind of signal is this and how much weight?

**Q5.** TATAMOTORS daily uptrend. MACD histogram has been positive but the bars are *shrinking* for five sessions even as price grinds to new highs. Price hasn't turned down yet. What is this and what does it warn?

**Q6.** INFY daily. RSI(14) = 52, MACD flat near zero, price chopping in a ₹40 range for three weeks. A momentum breakout system keeps giving small losses. Diagnose.

**Q7.** DIXON (high-beta) daily. RSI(14) hits 84 intraday, then price closes red with a bearish engulfing, and the *next* day RSI drops through 70 from above. Combine the momentum + candle. Action?

**Q8.** Nifty daily. RSI(14) has a bullish divergence (price lower low, RSI higher low) AND the RSI itself breaks above a small downtrend line drawn on the RSI panel. Two RSI signals stack. Weight?

### Answer key A

**A1 — Overbought in an uptrend is strength, not a sell.** In a strong trend, RSI can hold above 70 for weeks; "overbought" simply means momentum is powerful. Shorting a trending market because RSI > 70 is the classic retail suicide trade. Bias: bullish/hold. Action: stay long, trail stops; do NOT short. The only time to act on overbought is when it's accompanied by *bearish divergence* or a structural break — neither is present. Reasoning: threshold ≠ signal; trend context dominates.

**A2 — Bullish (positive) divergence in a downtrend.** Price made a lower low but momentum made a higher low = selling pressure is weakening. Bias: potential bullish reversal *brewing.* Action: don't buy yet — divergence is a warning, not a trigger. Wait for price confirmation (a break of a minor swing high or a bullish candle). Reasoning: divergence signals loss of momentum, but momentum can diverge for a long time before price turns; confirmation controls the risk.

**A3 — Stochastic is a *range* tool.** In a range, buy the oversold %K/%D cross near the range low and sell the overbought cross near the range high — exactly what these two crosses offer. Bias: range-fade. Action: the 18-cross is a long toward range top; the 85-cross is a short toward range bottom. But the moment the range breaks into a trend, Stochastic will pin at extremes and its crosses become death — so only trust it while the range holds. Reasoning: match the tool to the regime.

**A4 — A bullish MACD crossover below zero = early reversal signal, moderate weight.** A cross below the zero line after a downtrend suggests momentum is turning up but the larger trend is still down — it's an *early* countertrend signal, riskier than a cross above zero. Rising volume adds weight. Action: a starter long with a tight stop, or wait for MACD to cross zero for confirmation. Reasoning: signal-line crosses below zero catch bottoms early but have more false positives.

**A5 — Shrinking MACD histogram = momentum divergence / fading thrust.** New price highs but shrinking histogram bars mean each up-push has less force — a leading warning of a stall even before price rolls over. Bias: caution; uptrend maturing. Action: tighten stops, take partial profits; don't blindly short (price hasn't confirmed). Reasoning: the histogram is the derivative of momentum — it weakens *before* price, making it a useful early-warning gauge.

**A6 — No-trend regime; momentum indicators are useless here.** RSI at 52, MACD at zero, three-week chop = the market has no directional energy. Momentum breakout systems bleed via whipsaws in exactly this environment. Diagnosis: wrong tool for the regime. Action: stand aside or switch to range tactics (fade the edges with Stochastic/Bollinger), reduce size. Reasoning: indicators can't manufacture a trend that isn't there — recognising the flat regime is the skill.

**A7 — Momentum exhaustion + reversal candle = short trigger.** RSI spiking to 84 then a bearish engulfing then RSI cutting back below 70 = the momentum thrust is spent and price is confirming. On a high-beta name like Dixon these unwind fast. Action: short below the engulfing low, stop above the RSI-84 high, target a mean-reversion to the moving average. Reasoning: the RSI cross *back through* 70 is the confirmation that overbought has become a genuine turn — level + candle + momentum all align.

**A8 — Two stacked RSI signals = high conviction (but same-family caveat).** Bullish divergence *plus* an RSI-trendline break is strong because the second confirms the first. Weight: high for a momentum entry. Action: buy on price confirmation, stop below the divergence low. Caveat: both signals come from the *same* indicator (RSI), so it's still one "vote" of information — ideally add a different-family confirmation (volume/OBV) before sizing up. Reasoning: confluence within one indicator is good; confluence across indicator families is better.

## Section B — Trend, volatility & volume indicators (Q9–Q16)

**Q9.** Nifty daily. ADX(14) = 15 and falling; +DI and −DI are tangled together crossing repeatedly. What regime, and which strategies work / fail?

**Q10.** Bank Nifty daily. ADX rises from 18 to 34 over two weeks with +DI clearly above −DI. Price trending up. What does the rising ADX tell you and how do you trade it?

**Q11.** RELIANCE daily. Price rides the upper Bollinger Band (20,2) for six sessions, tagging it repeatedly, bands widening. A "sell — price at upper band" tip arrives. Read it.

**Q12.** TATASTEEL daily. Bollinger Bands (20,2) contract to their tightest in six months (a "squeeze"); price coils in a ₹6 range. What does the squeeze predict and how do you position?

**Q13.** Nifty daily. Price closes above the 50-DMA and the 50-DMA crosses above the 200-DMA (a "golden cross"). It's widely reported in the press. How tradeable, and what's the catch?

**Q14.** SBIN daily. On-Balance Volume (OBV) makes a new high *before* price breaks out of a range. Then price breaks out. Interpret the sequence.

**Q15.** MCX Crude, daily. Price rallies to a new high but OBV makes a lower high (volume not confirming). Then a big red day comes on huge volume. Interpret.

**Q16.** Bank Nifty, 5-min intraday. VWAP is rising; price pulls back to VWAP from above and holds, with a bullish candle. What is the institutional read and the intraday play?

### Answer key B

**A9 — Non-trending / choppy regime (ADX < 20).** Low, falling ADX with tangled DI lines = no trend. Trend-following (moving-average crosses, breakouts) will get chopped up; mean-reversion / range strategies (Bollinger fades, Stochastic) work better. Action: fade extremes or stand aside; never run breakout systems here. Reasoning: ADX measures trend *strength* regardless of direction — below 20 is the "do not trend-trade" zone.

**A10 — Rising ADX with +DI on top = strengthening uptrend; ride it.** ADX climbing 18→34 confirms the trend is gaining strength and is real, not noise. Action: trend-follow — buy pullbacks, trail stops, let winners run; avoid counter-trend shorts. Note: ADX doesn't give direction (the DI relationship does) — +DI above −DI = up. Reasoning: this is the ideal environment for trend systems; ADX confirms the pattern-based trend read.

**A11 — Riding the upper band in an uptrend is strength.** In a strong trend price "walks the band" — repeated tags of the upper Bollinger with widening bands = powerful momentum, not a sell signal. Selling because "price hit the upper band" is the Bollinger version of the RSI-overbought mistake. Action: hold/trail; a short is only justified on a *close back inside* the band plus a lower high. Reasoning: bands are volatility envelopes, not fixed reversal lines; context (trend) rules.

**A12 — A squeeze predicts a volatility *expansion* (direction unknown).** The tightest bands in six months = compressed energy about to release into a large move, but the squeeze itself is directionless. Action: don't guess — set alerts above and below the coil, trade the breakout with volume confirmation, size the stop to the coil's opposite side. Reasoning: low volatility mean-reverts to high volatility; the squeeze times *when*, price action times *which way*.

**A13 — Golden cross: real but lagging, and crowded.** A 50/200 DMA golden cross confirms a durable trend change and has a decent long-term record, but it's a *lagging* signal — by the time it prints, much of the initial move is done — and its wide reporting means it's often already priced. Action: use it as a regime filter (favour longs) rather than a precise entry; don't chase the announcement. Catch: in choppy markets golden/death crosses whipsaw repeatedly. Reasoning: lagging confirmation, not a timing trigger.

**A14 — OBV leading price = bullish accumulation ahead of the breakout.** OBV making a new high before price means volume (smart money) is accumulating before the move is visible in price — a leading confirmation. When price then breaks out, it's a *higher-quality* breakout. Action: the pre-breakout OBV high is your green light to trust the breakout and buy it. Reasoning: volume often leads price; OBV divergence in the bullish direction pre-stages the move.

**A15 — Bearish volume divergence confirmed by a distribution day.** New price high on falling OBV = the rally isn't backed by volume (weak hands / no real buying), and the big red high-volume day is institutional distribution confirming the top. Action: bias bearish; short below the distribution-day low, stop above the divergent high. Reasoning: price up + volume down = unsustainable; the high-volume reversal day is the confirmation that turns divergence into a signal.

**A16 — Price holding VWAP from above = institutions defending; intraday long.** Rising VWAP with a pullback that holds VWAP and bounces = large players using VWAP as their reference support (they're net buyers on the day). Action: long off the VWAP hold, stop just below VWAP, target the prior high / next resistance. Reasoning: VWAP is the institutional benchmark; price respecting it from above signals accumulation and gives a low-risk intraday entry with a tight, logical stop.

## Section C — Multi-indicator confluence & traps (Q17–Q24)

**Q17.** Nifty daily. RSI bullish divergence + MACD bullish cross + OBV turning up + price reclaiming the 50-DMA — all in the same week after a correction. Rate this and act.

**Q18.** Bank Nifty daily. RSI = 75 (overbought), but ADX = 38 rising and price walking the upper Bollinger. Two "signals" conflict. Resolve it.

**Q19.** RELIANCE daily. MACD gives a bullish cross but ADX = 14 and Bollinger Bands are flat/narrow. Should you trust the MACD cross? Why or why not?

**Q20.** TATAMOTORS daily. A trader stacks RSI, Stochastic, CCI, and Williams %R — all four say "overbought." He calls it "four-indicator confluence." Critique this.

**Q21.** Nifty daily. Price gaps up 1.5%, RSI jumps to 71, MACD histogram spikes — but the gap is on flat/below-average volume and OBV doesn't confirm. Read the quality of the move.

**Q22.** SBIN daily. Everything is bullish (RSI, MACD, OBV, above all MAs) and price is up 40% in two months. A beginner asks "when do the indicators tell me to sell?" What's the honest answer?

**Q23.** USDINR daily. Technicals flash a clean bullish MACD cross and RSI breakout, but the RBI is known to be actively selling dollars near 83.50 to defend the rupee. How do you weight the indicators?

**Q24.** Bank Nifty, expiry-day, 5-min. All your intraday indicators whipsaw violently — RSI spikes 30→70→35 within an hour, MACD flips repeatedly. What's happening and what should you do?

### Answer key C

**A17 — Four-family confluence = high-conviction long.** Momentum (RSI), trend-momentum (MACD), volume (OBV), and trend structure (50-DMA reclaim) are *four different families* all aligning after a correction — this is textbook high-quality confluence. Action: buy on the 50-DMA reclaim, stop below the divergence low, size normally-to-larger. Reasoning: agreement *across* indicator families (not four momentum clones) is what makes confluence meaningful — each adds independent information.

**A18 — No real conflict; trend wins.** RSI 75 "overbought" *plus* ADX 38 rising *plus* walking the upper band all describe **the same thing**: a powerful uptrend. The "conflict" is illusory — the overbought reading is a symptom of strength, and the other two confirm the trend is real. Action: stay long, don't fade. Reasoning: when a level-based signal (overbought) conflicts with strong trend/volatility confirmation, trend context overrides the threshold every time.

**A19 — Distrust the MACD cross in a flat, low-ADX, narrow-band regime.** A bullish MACD cross with ADX 14 and flat bands means there's no trend to sustain the signal — it's likely a whipsaw. Action: skip it or demand a Bollinger breakout / ADX rise first. Reasoning: momentum-cross signals only pay in trending regimes; in dead-flat conditions they generate small, repeated losses. The *regime filter* (ADX/bands) overrides the momentum signal.

**A20 — Fake confluence: they're all the same measurement.** RSI, Stochastic, CCI, and Williams %R are *all momentum oscillators* — they're mathematically near-cousins and will almost always agree. Stacking them is one vote counted four times, not four independent confirmations. Critique: real confluence needs *different families* (momentum + volume + trend + volatility). Reasoning: redundancy masquerading as confirmation is a classic false-confidence trap that leads to oversized, over-confident trades.

**A21 — Low-quality, suspect move (a possible gap-fade setup).** Momentum spiking on a gap that lacks volume and OBV confirmation = a hollow move, likely a gap driven by overnight sentiment rather than real buying. Action: distrust the RSI/MACD pop; watch for a gap-fill / fade, especially if price stalls. Reasoning: momentum indicators react to price change regardless of *quality* — volume (OBV) is the quality check, and here it's failing. Price up without volume is fragile.

**A22 — Honest answer: indicators don't ring a bell at the top.** In a strong trend, momentum indicators stay pinned bullish for a long time and will NOT hand you a clean "sell" — waiting for them to turn bearish means giving back a chunk of the move. The real sell discipline comes from **price structure and stops** (a break of the trend, a lower low, a trailing stop hit), not from an oscillator. Reasoning: indicators confirm trends; *exits* are a risk-management decision (next chapters), not an indicator decision. This is one of the most important truths in the book.

**A23 — Down-weight the technicals heavily; the fundamental overlay dominates.** In USDINR, active RBI intervention near a known level can cap or reverse a technically bullish setup regardless of what MACD and RSI say. Action: respect the intervention zone — reduce size, expect the up-move to stall near 83.50, don't trust the breakout until it clears with force. Reasoning: technicals assume a free market; a central bank actively defending a level is a fundamental force that overrides chart signals. Know your instrument's drivers.

**A24 — Expiry-day noise; indicators are unreliable — stand down or trade price only.** Bank Nifty expiry brings extreme option-driven, mean-reverting volatility; oscillators whipsaw because price is being yanked by gamma/hedging, not trend. Action: drastically reduce size or avoid indicator-based signals entirely — trade only clean price levels (VWAP, prior day's high/low) if at all, with wide stops or small size. Reasoning: no indicator can filter structural expiry noise; recognising the *regime* (and respecting it) is the only edge. Most expiry-day retail blowups come from trusting indicators that are just reflecting noise.

## Scoring & what to do next

- **66–72:** You interpret indicators like a professional — trend-first, divergence-aware, confluence-across-families, trap-conscious. Your remaining edge is execution and risk.
- **48–65:** Good mechanics, but you're still occasionally fooled by thresholds (overbought/oversold) and same-family fake-confluence. Re-drill Sections B and C.
- **Below 48:** You're treating indicators as mechanical buy/sell buttons. That will lose money. Re-read Volume III, then internalise the five ground rules at the top of this chapter before re-taking.

## Interview-ready summary

When an interviewer asks "how do you use indicators?", never answer with a threshold ("I sell when RSI hits 70"). Answer with the framework: **"I read indicators in trend context, I weight divergence over absolute level, I look for confluence across *different families* — momentum, volume, volatility, trend — and I treat any single-indicator threshold as a symptom, not a signal."** Then give the killer nuance: *"Indicators confirm and describe; they lag price and they can't call tops in a strong trend — so I use them to build conviction and time entries, but my exits come from price structure and stops, not from an oscillator."* That answer demonstrates you understand indicators as a *measurement layer* over price rather than a magic signal generator — which is precisely the maturity that separates a professional from someone who bought a course on RSI. The overbought-in-an-uptrend trap (Q1, Q11, Q18, A22) is the single most important idea in this chapter; if you internalise nothing else, internalise that.
