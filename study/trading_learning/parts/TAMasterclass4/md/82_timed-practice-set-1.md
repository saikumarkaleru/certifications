# Timed Practice Set I

Reading a chart in the calm of a book is easy. Reading it in ninety seconds, with money on the line and a spread widening against you, is the actual skill. This chapter is a **timed practice set** — a simulated exam that forces you to commit to a decision under a clock, exactly as the live market forces you. Twenty scenarios, each written as a snapshot of a real Indian instrument at a specific moment, followed by a *decision prompt*. You get a suggested time budget per question. Commit to an answer — direction, entry, stop, target, and size logic — *before* you read the key.

## How to run this set

- **Total time: 40 minutes for 20 questions** (average 2 minutes each). Use a phone timer.
- For each scenario, write down four things: **bias** (long/short/no-trade), **trigger** (the price that confirms), **stop** (where you're wrong), and **why** (the one-line thesis). If you can't state all four inside the time box, that itself is data: the setup isn't clean enough to trade.
- Score yourself on the **reasoning**, not just the label. The answer key marks the *process* points. A "no-trade" that's correctly justified scores full marks — knowing when *not* to trade is half the edge.
- **Grading band:** 16–20 correct-process = tournament-ready; 11–15 = solid, keep drilling; below 11 = re-read Volumes I–III on the flagged topics before risking capital.

All levels are realistic for 2026 Indian markets. Assume standard NSE cash/F&O and MCX conditions, and that you can size to a fixed rupee risk.

## The scenarios

**Q1 — Nifty, daily.** Index has run from 24,000 to 26,300 over eight weeks with no pullback deeper than 2%. RSI(14) is 74. Today it closes at 26,300, an inside day (range inside yesterday's). India VIX 11. *Decision: do you initiate a fresh swing long here?*

**Q2 — Bank Nifty, 15-min, intraday.** Opens at 57,800, sells to 57,400 in the first hour, then builds a series of higher lows back to 57,750 by noon on rising volume, and is now pressing the 57,800 opening level. *Decision: long, short, or wait — and on what trigger?*

**Q3 — Reliance, daily.** Base between ₹1,380–₹1,440 for six weeks. Today closes at ₹1,458 on volume 2.3× the 20-day average, clearing the range. *Decision: trade it? Entry, stop, first target.*

**Q4 — USDINR, daily.** Rupee has weakened from 86.0 to 88.4 over a month. Today prints a bearish engulfing (for USDINR — i.e., dollar reverses down) at 88.4 after an RBI-intervention news headline. *Decision: what does this mean for your IT-sector longs?*

**Q5 — Nifty weekly options, expiry Thursday.** It's Wednesday afternoon. Spot 25,850. 25,800 put and 25,900 call both have very heavy OI. India VIX 12, flat. *Decision: what's the honest expiry-day expectation, and would you sell a strangle?*

**Q6 — Tata Steel, daily.** Downtrend, price ₹135, below a falling 50-DMA (₹148) and 200-DMA (₹160). Today a hammer on high volume at ₹135 near a prior 2024 support shelf. *Decision: is this a buy?*

**Q7 — Fin Nifty, 5-min, intraday.** Choppy, overlapping candles all morning in a 120-point band, VIX low. *Decision: what's your default posture in this tape?*

**Q8 — HDFC Bank, daily.** Uptrend intact, price ₹1,720, pulls back three days to touch its rising 20-DMA at ₹1,700, RSI cools from 68 to 52, no break of the last swing low (₹1,690). *Decision: trade the pullback? Entry/stop.*

**Q9 — MCX Gold, daily.** Rallies with a widening gap between price and the 20-DMA (price 6% above the mean). Today a shooting star on the highest volume in a month. *Decision: what does this warn, and what do you do if long?*

**Q10 — Nifty, daily.** New closing high 26,400 but the % of Nifty 500 above 200-DMA has slid from 75% to 44%, and the advance-decline line is making lower highs. *Decision: does this change how you manage existing longs?*

**Q11 — A midcap IT name, daily, into results.** You're long from lower. Results are tonight. Position is up 1.5R. The stock has moved ±9% on the last two results. Your stop is 2% away. *Decision: what does honest risk management require?*

**Q12 — Bank Nifty, daily.** Breaks below a well-defined 57,000 support on volume, falls to 56,300, then two days later closes back *above* 57,000. *Decision: name the pattern and the play.*

**Q13 — Infosys, daily.** Higher high in price at ₹1,650 but RSI makes a distinctly lower high vs its previous peak; price still above rising 50-DMA. *Decision: short the divergence now?*

**Q14 — Nifty, gap open.** Opens +1.1% gap up on strong US cues, then in the first 30 minutes fills the entire gap and trades red. *Decision: read and intraday bias?*

**Q15 — A PSU bank, daily.** Sector (PSU Bank index) is the day's strongest, up 3%. Your chosen name is up only 0.6% and below its 50-DMA while sector peers break out. *Decision: buy the laggard for catch-up?*

**Q16 — USDINR + Nifty, intermarket.** Rupee gaps weaker to 88.9 (disorderly), global risk-off, DXY up, US yields up. Nifty opens −0.8%. *Decision: which sectors do you avoid, which might hold up?*

**Q17 — A stock, position management.** Long at ₹1,000, stop ₹960, target ₹1,120. Price runs to ₹1,090 (2.25R) then stalls for three sessions in a tight flag just below ₹1,100 on declining volume. *Decision: what do you do?*

**Q18 — Nifty, VIX spike.** Sharp 2.5% down day, India VIX jumps 11 → 19. Next day Nifty opens flat, VIX ticks down to 17, price holds the prior day's low. *Decision: what's the setup and the trigger?*

**Q19 — Sizing.** You want to short a stock at ₹800 with a stop at ₹832 (above a failed-breakout high). Account risk budget for the trade: ₹12,000. *Decision: position size, and one risk caveat.*

**Q20 — Meta-decision.** You've taken 4 losers in a row today, all valid setups, all stopped by the same 2% adverse. You're now eyeing a fifth. *Decision: take it or stop? Justify technically and psychologically.*

## Answer key and reasoning

**A1 — No fresh long (or tiny size at most).** Eight weeks up, no meaningful pullback, RSI 74, VIX at complacent lows — this is a *late, extended* trend. Chasing here means poor reward-to-risk: your stop must go below a distant swing low, and you're buying near the top of the range with no pullback to lean on. **Full marks:** recognise "extended trend, wait for a pullback to the rising 20-DMA or a higher low." Buying strength here isn't wrong in principle, but the entry location is bad. *Trend up, but no edge at this price.*

**A2 — Long on a break/hold above 57,800, stop below the last higher low (~57,650).** The intraday structure — higher lows on rising volume reclaiming the opening level — is a bullish reversal-of-the-morning-dip. The **trigger is acceptance above 57,800** (the reference level), not anticipation. Target the day's earlier high / measured move. **Full marks:** wait for the trigger; don't front-run the level; stop under structure.

**A3 — Buy the breakout. Entry ₹1,458 (or a small pullback to ₹1,445), stop below ₹1,438 (back inside the range), first target ~₹1,520** (range height ≈ ₹60 projected). Six-week base + close above range on **2.3× volume** is a textbook high-quality breakout — volume confirms real demand. **Full marks:** identify volume confirmation as the key; stop just back inside the broken range; measured-move target.

**A4 — Trim/tighten IT longs; the currency tailwind is pausing.** A bearish engulfing on USDINR after RBI intervention suggests the rupee's slide (which had been *helping* IT margins) may pause or reverse. It doesn't kill the IT thesis (demand matters more than currency), but it removes a tailwind. **Full marks:** don't panic-sell IT, but recognise the intermarket signal and manage risk / tighten stops on positions that were partly a rupee-weakness play.

**A5 — Expect pinning pressure toward the heavy-OI strikes; do NOT casually sell a naked strangle.** With spot between two heavy-OI strikes and flat VIX, expiry often sees range-bound "pinning." *But* the honest answer flags the tail: a naked short strangle has **unlimited/large risk** and any late-day news can blow through a strike. **Full marks:** acknowledge pinning tendency *and* the asymmetric risk of premium selling; if selling, define it (iron condor) or size tiny. Calling Max Pain a certainty = wrong.

**A6 — Not yet a buy; wait for confirmation.** A hammer at support in a downtrend is a *potential* bottom, but catching a falling knife below both falling MAs is low-probability. The disciplined play: **wait for a follow-through close and ideally a reclaim of a near-term level / the 50-DMA to start turning.** **Full marks:** respect the downtrend, demand confirmation, don't anticipate. A hammer alone in a strong downtrend fails often.

**A7 — Default posture: stand aside / trade the range edges only, small.** Choppy overlapping candles in a low-VIX band = **no trend, no edge for momentum**. Forcing directional trades in chop is how intraday accounts bleed via slippage and false breaks. **Full marks:** recognise the regime as untradeable for trend-following; if anything, fade the extremes of the range with tight stops and small size, or simply wait for a range break with volume.

**A8 — Long the pullback. Entry near ₹1,705–₹1,710 on signs of the bounce, stop below ₹1,688 (under the swing low and the 20-DMA).** This is a **healthy pullback in an uptrend**: three days down to the rising 20-DMA, RSI reset to the low-50s (not oversold, just cooled), swing-low structure intact. Classic buy-the-dip in a trend. **Full marks:** identify it as a continuation pullback, not a reversal; enter near support with a structure stop; favourable R:R because the stop is close.

**A9 — Warns of short-term exhaustion; if long, tighten stop / book partial.** Price stretched 6% above the 20-DMA (over-extended) + a **shooting star on the highest volume of the month** = potential blow-off / distribution. It's not a reason to *short* an uptrend, but it's a strong reason to **protect profits**: trail up, book part, don't add. **Full marks:** distinguish "manage the long" from "initiate a short." Over-extension + reversal candle + volume climax = exhaustion risk.

**A10 — Yes: tighten stops, stop adding, favour only the strongest names.** Price making highs while **breadth deteriorates** (fewer stocks above 200-DMA, A-D line lower highs) is a late-cycle warning: the index is narrowing. It's *not* a short signal (timing is unknown), but it changes **management** — de-risk gradually, don't chase. **Full marks:** correct read of breadth divergence as a management/defense signal, not a reversal trigger.

**A11 — Reduce size before the event, or hedge; your 2% stop is an illusion here.** With a ±9% results history and a 2% stop, an adverse gap **jumps your stop** — real risk is the gap, not the stop. Honest management: **book part / cut size** so a realistic bad gap costs no more than intended, or hold a defined-risk hedge. **Full marks:** recognise gap risk defeats the stop; size to the *gap*, not the stop; being up 1.5R is a good reason to protect it. Holding full size into a binary event on a 2% stop is the error.

**A12 — Failed breakdown / spring (bear trap). Play: long on the reclaim of 57,000, stop below the ₹56,300 low, target back toward the range top.** A breakdown that reverses back *above* the broken support traps the shorts; failed moves fuel fast moves the other way (Wyckoff spring). **Full marks:** name it, and note that the reclaim is the trigger with the trap-low as the stop.

**A13 — Do NOT short yet.** RSI bearish divergence is a *warning*, not a trigger. Price is still above a rising 50-DMA — the trend is up. Shorting a divergence with no *price* confirmation (no broken swing low / trendline) is fighting the trend. **Full marks:** treat divergence as a heads-up to tighten longs; short only on price confirmation (structure break). Shorting on the oscillator alone = wrong.

**A14 — Bearish reversal read; a filled gap-up that turns red is a failure/trap.** The market rejected the higher open (gap fill + red) — buyers who chased the gap are now offside. Intraday bias tilts **short/weak**, especially if it breaks the opening range low. **Full marks:** identify the failed gap as a bearish tell; trigger = break of opening range low, stop above the day's high.

**A15 — No, don't buy the laggard.** In a sector ripping day, your name is **underperforming and below its 50-DMA** — that's *relative weakness*, a red flag, not a coiled-spring bargain. Buy the *leaders* breaking out, not the laggard hoping it plays catch-up. **Full marks:** relative-strength principle — weakness on a strong sector day usually means something specific is wrong with that name.

**A16 — Avoid rate-sensitive/high-beta and USD-debt/importer names; defensives and exporters may hold better.** Disorderly rupee weakness + global risk-off (DXY up, yields up) = FII-outflow risk. **Avoid:** midcaps, high-USD-debt names, aviation, OMCs, richly-valued growth. **Relatively resilient:** IT/pharma (dollar earners), quality FMCG defensives. **Full marks:** connect the macro/intermarket picture to sector rotation; note it's *relative* resilience — in a risk-off gap most things fall, defensives fall less.

**A17 — Trail the stop up under the flag / book partial; the move is maturing.** At 2.25R with a stall just below the target on **declining volume**, the momentum is fading. Professional move: **lock in gains** — raise the stop to protect ~1.5–2R, or scale out half and let a runner try for ₹1,120. **Full marks:** don't give back 2R hoping for the last bit; trail or scale. Doing nothing with no plan = the error.

**A18 — Setup: potential capitulation low / mean-reversion bounce. Trigger: hold of prior day's low + VIX rolling over.** A sharp down day with a **VIX spike** is often near-term capitulation; the tradeable signal is when **fear stops rising** (VIX ticks down) and **price holds the low**. Trigger = a reclaim of the prior day's intraday resistance / a higher low forming, stop below the capitulation low. **Full marks:** don't short the spike (late); wait for VIX-turn + price-hold as the long trigger.

**A19 — Position size: risk per share = ₹832 − ₹800 = ₹32; ₹12,000 ÷ ₹32 = ~375 shares.** Caveat: this is a **short with a defined stop above a failed-breakout high** — good structure — but shorting has **borrow/squeeze risk** and the stop can gap on news; consider that the ₹832 stop may slip. **Full marks:** correct arithmetic (₹32 risk/share → ~375 shares) *and* a genuine short-side risk caveat (gap/squeeze).

**A20 — Stop for the day.** Four valid setups all failing the same way is a signal the **market regime doesn't suit your system today** (e.g., chop/whipsaw), and psychologically you're now at high risk of **revenge trading** and over-sizing. Even with valid setups, when the environment is punishing your edge, the professional move is to **step away, preserve capital and mental state**, and review. **Full marks:** cite both the *technical* read (regime mismatch, edge not working today) and the *psychological* read (loss-streak → tilt risk). Taking the fifth "because the setup is valid" is the classic tilt trap — process discipline includes knowing when the whole session is a no-go.

## Debrief: what this set is really testing

Notice the pattern across the twenty. Barely any question was answered with a naked "buy" or "sell." The professional answers cluster around a few disciplines: **wait for the trigger** (Q1, Q2, Q6, Q13, Q18), **let volume/breadth confirm** (Q3, Q10), **respect the trend and relative strength** (Q8, Q13, Q15), **manage risk around gaps and events** (Q11, Q16, Q19), and **protect the mind and the capital when the edge isn't working** (Q7, Q20). That is not an accident. Chart-reading exams — and live trading — reward *restraint and process* far more than boldness. The trader who can articulate a clean bias-trigger-stop-why in ninety seconds, and who can also say "no-trade, here's why," is the one whose equity curve survives.

Run this set again in a week without reading the key first. Track your correct-*process* score over three attempts. Rising scores mean the reads are becoming reflexive; that reflex, built under a clock, is what you actually take into the market.
