# Chart-Reading Drills III

This is the capstone. Drills I built single-chart reads; Drills II layered on multi-timeframe, index-stock alignment, option-chain context, and management. Drills III puts it all together under exam conditions. The first section is a **timed mock test** — fifteen mixed questions you should attempt in one sitting, clock running, no peeking — that simulates the pressure of a trading-desk assessment or a real session's decision density. The second section is a set of **full end-to-end trade scenarios**: you plan the trade from blank chart to closed position, and the key walks the whole lifecycle. Everything draws on material from Volumes I–III; nothing new is introduced.

Set a 30-minute timer for the mock test. Write your answers — bias, entry, stop, target, invalidation — before checking any key. Then score honestly against the rubric at the end. A reminder that has run through all three drill chapters, and that no amount of skill repeals: the F&O statistics in India are brutal, and most retail traders lose. These drills sharpen the minority who treat the market as a game of probabilities and risk control, not prediction.

## Section 1 — Timed Mock Test (30 minutes, 15 questions)

### MT1. Opening read
Nifty closed Friday at 24,300 near the top of a two-week range (23,900–24,350). Monday gaps up to open at 24,380 — clearing the range high — and the first 5-minute candle closes strong at 24,400 on heavy volume. India VIX is calm at 11. State your opening bias in one line and the single level that invalidates it.

### MT2. Candle at a level
On the daily, ICICI Bank prints a hammer with a long lower wick exactly at its rising 50-DMA (1,180), closing at 1,205 after dipping to 1,178, on above-average volume. Bullish or bearish, and why does *location* make this hammer high-quality?

### MT3. Indicator conflict
Bank Nifty's daily MACD is bullish (above signal, above zero) but the daily RSI shows a clear bearish divergence at the recent high. Which do you weight, and what does the conflict tell you to *do* rather than predict?

### MT4. Volume tell
A stock breaks above a well-defined resistance at 500 but on *below-average* volume, and the breakout candle has a long upper wick closing back at 501. Is this a trustworthy breakout? What is the likely next move?

### MT5. Intraday structure
By 11 a.m. Nifty has made three higher lows (24,050 → 24,080 → 24,120) and is pressing against intraday resistance at 24,180 for a third time. Volume is rising into the level. What is the higher-probability resolution and your trigger?

### MT6. Options context
It's expiry. Nifty at 24,140. Call OI heaviest at 24,200, put OI heaviest at 24,000, and through the afternoon the 24,200 calls are *adding* OI aggressively while price stalls at 24,150. What does the call writing at 24,200 imply for the rest of the day?

### MT7. Relative strength
Nifty IT index is up 1.5% and leading; within it, TCS is up 0.8% but Persistent Systems is up 3.2%, both breaking out. If you want maximum participation in the sector move and accept higher risk, which do you choose and why?

### MT8. Stop placement
You go long SBI at 645 on a breakout. The last swing low is 638; ATR(14) is 6. Where do you place your stop to respect both structure and noise, and what is your per-share risk?

### MT9. Trend exhaustion
After a 9-session vertical run with no meaningful pullback, Nifty prints a doji at a new high on the highest volume of the move, followed the next day by a bearish engulfing. What is the message, and what is the *action* (not prediction)?

### MT10. Range day tactics
ADX on the 15-minute is 14 and flat; Nifty has oscillated between 24,100 and 24,180 for three hours, reversing cleanly at each edge. How do you trade this, and what is the cardinal error?

### MT11. Gap fill logic
Reliance gaps *down* 1.5% to open at 2,850, below yesterday's low of 2,880, then spends the morning grinding back up toward 2,880. What is the significance of 2,880, and what does a *rejection* there tell you versus a *reclaim*?

### MT12. Breadth check
Nifty is up 0.4% but the advance-decline is strongly *positive* (roughly 3:1 advancers), small- and mid-caps are outperforming, and sector participation is broad. How does this breadth reading affect confidence in the up-move versus a narrow, heavyweight-only rally?

### MT13. Position sizing
Your account risk per trade is fixed at ₹5,000. A Nifty setup has a 25-point stop distance. Nifty lot size is 75. Roughly how many lots can you take, and what is the discipline this enforces?

### MT14. The failed setup
You entered a long on a flag breakout; price broke out, then immediately fell back *below* the flag low and is now below your entry, VWAP has rolled over, and the index turned red. Your stop is 8 points away. Do you wait for the stop or act, and on what principle?

### MT15. End-of-day decision
It's 3:00 p.m. Your intraday Nifty long is up +1.2R but momentum has died, price is drifting sideways below VWAP, and you don't carry intraday positions overnight. What do you do in the last 20 minutes and why?

## Section 2 — Full trade scenarios

### TS1. The swing long, start to finish
Nifty daily is in an uptrend above a rising 20-DMA (23,900). It pulls back four sessions to the 20-DMA, and today prints a bullish engulfing off it on rising volume, while the hourly has just broken its minor down-trendline. Walk the *entire* trade: entry, stop, first target, trail, and the three things that would make you exit early. Assume a 3–7 day horizon.

### TS2. The intraday short, start to finish
Bank Nifty gaps up but immediately fails — the first 15-minute candle is a bearish engulfing that closes below the prior day close, price is below VWAP by 9:45, and the index (Nifty) is also weak. Plan the full intraday short: trigger, stop, targets, management on a trend-down day, and the invalidation.

### TS3. The breakout that you must not chase
Adani Enterprises has coiled in a 6-week base between 2,900 and 3,100. It breaks 3,100 on 4x volume and closes at 3,180. You missed the breakout candle. Describe the *disciplined* way to still participate without chasing, including the exact "second-mouse" entry and where you'd abandon the idea.

## Answer Key — Section 1

### MT1. Bias & invalidation
**Bullish** — a gap that clears a two-week range high and holds on heavy volume, with calm VIX, is a range-breakout in the trend's favour. **Invalidation:** a fall back *below the old range high (24,350)* — if price re-enters the range, the breakout has failed and the gap is filling. That single level is your line: above it, bullish; below it, thesis dead.

### MT2. Hammer at the 50-DMA
**Bullish.** A hammer is a reversal candle showing sellers pushed price down but buyers overwhelmed them into the close (long lower wick, close near high). **Location makes it high-quality:** it printed *exactly at the rising 50-DMA* — a well-watched dynamic support in an uptrend — on above-average volume. The same hammer in mid-air means little; at confluence support with volume, it marks buyers defending a level institutions respect. Buy above the hammer high, stop below its low (1,178).

### MT3. MACD vs RSI conflict
**Weight the divergence as a caution, don't blindly follow MACD.** MACD is a trend/lagging tool confirming the up-move; the RSI bearish divergence warns momentum is fading beneath the surface. The conflict does not tell you to *predict* a top — it tells you to *act defensively*: tighten stops on longs, stop adding, reduce size, and demand structural confirmation (a lower low / trendline break) before shorting. Conflicting indicators = lower conviction = smaller size and tighter risk, not a coin-flip bet.

### MT4. Weak-volume breakout
**Not trustworthy.** A break above resistance on *below-average* volume with a long upper wick and a close right at the level shows no demand conviction — the breakout lacks the fuel to sustain. Likely next move: a *failure back into the range* (a bull trap), with price falling below 500 and stopping out breakout buyers. Volume is the witness to a breakout; without it, treat the break as suspect and either stand aside or wait for a *volume-confirmed* retest.

### MT5. Third test with rising volume
**Higher-probability resolution: a breakout above 24,180.** Three rising higher lows into a resistance, with volume *increasing* into the level, is a classic *ascending-triangle-style* compression where buyers are stepping up more aggressively each time — pressure builds against the ceiling. **Trigger:** a 5-minute close above 24,180 on a volume surge; enter the break (or the immediate retest-hold), stop below the last higher low (24,120). Caveat: a *third* touch that *fails* with volume drying up would flip the read, so honour the trigger, don't anticipate it.

### MT6. Call writing at 24,200 on expiry
**Implies 24,200 acts as a strong ceiling / pin for the day.** Aggressive call OI *addition* at 24,200 means writers are confident price won't close above it and are selling that strike; their hedging and the max-pain dynamic pull price toward/under it. For the rest of an expiry day this argues *against* a sustained push through 24,200 — range-to-fade behaviour is more likely than a clean breakout. Only heavy *unwinding* of that call OI (writers covering) plus a volume surge would signal the ceiling is breaking.

### MT7. Max participation, higher risk
**Choose Persistent Systems.** It's the higher-beta, stronger relative performer (+3.2% vs TCS +0.8%) leading the sector move — for *maximum participation* in a sector rally with accepted higher risk, the leading high-beta name delivers the most. The trade-off (accepted here) is a wider range and harder reversal if the sector move fails. TCS offers a steadier, lower-variance ride; the question specifies max participation and higher risk tolerance, so the leader wins.

### MT8. Structure-plus-noise stop
**Place the stop below the swing low *plus* a noise buffer:** swing low 638 minus ~1× ATR (6) ≈ **632**. This respects structure (below the swing) *and* volatility (beyond the noise), so a normal wick won't take you out. **Per-share risk = 645 − 632 = ₹13.** Then size the position so total rupee-risk equals your fixed per-trade risk. Placing the stop *right at* 638 would sit in the obvious stop cluster and invite a hunt.

### MT9. Vertical run, doji, engulfing
**Message: exhaustion / distribution after a climactic run** — a doji (indecision) at a new high on the *highest volume of the move* signals buyers meeting heavy supply, and the next-day bearish engulfing confirms sellers seized control. **Action (not prediction):** for *longs*, book profits / tighten trailing stops aggressively and stop adding; you don't need to *short* the first reversal against a strong trend, but you do protect gains. If you want a short, wait for a *lower low* to confirm the change of character. Protect first, hunt reversals second.

### MT10. Range day
**Trade it as a range: fade the edges toward the middle** — sell the 24,180 test with a reversal candle, buy the 24,100 test, target the opposite edge or the midpoint, stops just beyond each edge. Or, given ADX 14 and low directionality, *stand aside*. **Cardinal error:** trading it like a *trend* day — chasing "breakouts" at the range edges that immediately fail, getting chopped to death on both sides. In a confirmed range you fade extremes; you do not chase breaks until ADX rises and a genuine expansion begins.

### MT11. Gap-down, prior low as resistance
**2,880 (yesterday's low) is now overhead resistance** by the principle of polarity — the gap-down opened below it, so trapped longs and the broken level sit above. **A *rejection* at 2,880** (price stalls and turns down) confirms the gap-down's bearishness — sellers defend the old support-turned-resistance; stay short / avoid longs. **A *reclaim* of 2,880** (a decisive move back above on volume) signals a *failed gap-down / bullish reversal* — the gap is filling and trapped shorts must cover, opening a long toward the prior close. The level is the referee; watch how price behaves *at* it.

### MT12. Broad breadth
**Broad breadth raises confidence substantially.** A 3:1 advance-decline, mid/small-cap outperformance, and wide sector participation mean the *average* stock is rising — the move is *healthy and broad*, not a narrow heavyweight levitation. Broad rallies are more durable and less prone to sudden reversal than narrow ones. This is the opposite of the fragile new-high-on-poor-breadth case; here you can trust dips as buyable and give trends more room. Breadth is the market's internal health check — broad is bullish, narrow is fragile.

### MT13. Position sizing math
**Risk ₹5,000 ÷ (25 points × ₹1/point × 75 per lot) = 5,000 ÷ 1,875 ≈ 2.67, so ~2 lots** (round *down* — never up — to stay within risk). **The discipline:** position size is *derived from* stop distance and fixed rupee-risk, not chosen by conviction or greed. Wider stops force smaller size; tighter stops allow more — but rupee-risk stays constant. This single formula is what keeps one bad trade from becoming an account-ending event, and it's the habit that separates survivors from the blow-up statistics.

### MT14. Failed flag breakout
**Act now — don't wait for the stop.** The setup is *confirmed failed*: price broke out then fell back *below the flag low* (breakout negated), VWAP rolled over, and the index turned red — three independent confirmations the thesis is wrong. **Principle:** a *time/thesis stop* — when the reason you entered is demonstrably invalidated, you exit at market rather than donating the last 8 points to a trade you *know* is broken. The price stop is your *maximum* loss, not your *target* loss; a confirmed-failed setup is cut immediately. Waiting to be maximally wrong is not discipline, it's stubbornness.

### MT15. End-of-day exit
**Close the position (scale out or exit fully) in the last 20 minutes.** You don't hold overnight, momentum has died, and price is drifting below VWAP with no continuation — there is no reason to risk the +1.2R gain into the close for a trade going nowhere. Book it. Holding a dead intraday winner into the last minutes exposes you to close-auction volatility and a give-back for zero expected upside. The discipline: *exit when the reason to be in is gone*, and never carry an intraday position past your stated horizon out of hope. A booked +1.2R is a good trade; let it be one.

## Answer Key — Section 2

### TS1 — The swing long, full lifecycle
**Setup validity:** daily uptrend + pullback to rising 20-DMA + bullish engulfing on volume + hourly down-trendline break = multi-timeframe aligned long (daily bias, daily-candle setup, hourly trigger). High quality.

- **Entry:** buy on a break above the bullish engulfing candle's high (confirming the reversal), or on the hourly resumption. Say entry ~24,050 area.
- **Stop:** below the engulfing candle's low / just under the 20-DMA (23,880), placed *beyond* noise — this is where the thesis (20-DMA holds) is proven wrong.
- **First target:** the prior swing high / recent range top; book a partial there (one-third to one-half) to de-risk.
- **Trail:** after the partial, move the stop to break-even, then trail under the rising daily 20-DMA or under each new daily higher-low, letting the swing run with the trend.
- **Three early-exit triggers:** (1) a daily *close back below the 20-DMA* (support failed); (2) a bearish reversal candle at a major overhead resistance on high volume (supply appearing); (3) the *index/broader market* rolling over decisively against the position (the tide turning). Any of these means the edge is gone — cut or tighten hard. This is how a planned swing long is run from blank chart to close: aligned entry, structural stop, partial-and-trail, and pre-defined invalidations.

### TS2 — The intraday short, full lifecycle
**Setup validity:** failed gap-up + bearish engulfing first 15-min candle closing below prior close + price below VWAP by 9:45 + Nifty also weak = aligned intraday short (index and instrument agree, VWAP confirms sell control). A failed gap is a high-conviction reversal because it traps gap-chasing longs.

- **Trigger:** short on a break below the first 15-minute candle's low (or a lower-high rejection under VWAP), confirming sellers have control.
- **Stop:** above VWAP / above the failed-gap high — that's where the bearish thesis (below-VWAP, failed gap) breaks. Use a buffer beyond the obvious high.
- **Targets:** prior day low, then the next support / the day's measured extension; book partials into supports.
- **Management on a trend-down day:** if it develops into a trend day (lower highs, price riding under the 9-EMA and VWAP, no higher highs), *don't scalp* — trail the stop above the declining 9-EMA / each lower-high and let it run; add small on failed rallies back to VWAP.
- **Invalidation:** a decisive *reclaim of VWAP* that holds, or a higher-high on the 5-minute structure — the failed-gap short is over; cover. This is the mirror image of the swing long: aligned trigger, structural stop above VWAP, partial-and-trail on the trend, VWAP reclaim as the line.

### TS3 — The breakout you must not chase
**Situation:** valid base breakout (6-week base, 4x volume, strong close) but you missed the breakout candle and price is extended at 3,180, ~2.5% above the breakout level. Chasing here means a *huge* stop (back below 3,100) and buying into the extended move — poor R:R.

- **Disciplined participation — the second-mouse entry:** do *not* market-buy at 3,180. Wait for the near-inevitable *pullback / retest* of the breakout level (3,100). The high-quality entry is a *hold* of 3,100 — price returns, tests the old resistance now as support, and turns up on *lighter* volume (profit-taking, not distribution) with a bullish reversal candle. Buy that retest-hold.
- **Stop:** just below the retest low / below 3,080 — a *tight* stop, which is the whole advantage of the second-mouse entry versus chasing (much better R:R).
- **Target:** measured move = base height (3,100 − 2,900 = 200) added to breakout = ~3,300, plus trailing beyond if momentum persists.
- **When to abandon the idea:** if price *never* pulls back and simply runs away, you *let it go* — missing a trade costs nothing; chasing an extended move risks capital. And if the "retest" *fails* — price cuts *back below 3,100 and keeps going* on volume — the breakout was false; abandon the long entirely (potential bull trap). The professional's rule: it's better to miss a runaway than to chase it, and the retest offers a defined-risk way back in for the disciplined.

## Scoring rubric and final word

**Mock test (Section 1):** score 1 point per question where your *process* matched — correct bias reasoning, a stop placed at the thesis-invalidation (not the obvious cluster), a defined target, and a stated invalidation. **12–15:** desk-ready reading and risk discipline. **8–11:** solid, but re-drill the categories you missed (candles/location, indicators, options/breadth, or management). **Below 8:** go back to Drills I and II before trading live size.

**Full scenarios (Section 2):** score yourself on whether you planned the *entire lifecycle* — not just the entry, but the stop logic, the partial-and-trail plan, and the pre-defined early exits. Most losing traders can find an entry; few plan the exit *before* they need it. That planning, done in the calm before the trade, is what survives contact with the fear and greed of a live position.

The through-line of all three drill chapters: **location beats signal, confirmation beats prediction, the higher timeframe rules, breadth and the option chain reveal the crowd, and management is where edge becomes money.** Above all — position size derived from a real stop, and the humility to take the no-trade, cut the confirmed-failed setup, and stand aside in chop. The market will always offer another setup; your job is to still be there, with capital and composure intact, when the good one comes. Most won't be. The disciplined few, who drill these reads until they're reflexive and who never let one trade threaten the account, give themselves the only edge that compounds: survival.
