# Global Cues for India (Deep)

An Indian trading day does not begin at 9:15 am. It begins the previous evening when Wall Street opens, continues through the night as US markets close and futures drift, and hardens into a bias by the time Asia and GIFT Nifty are trading at dawn. The "global cue" is the single most-cited phrase on every Indian pre-market show, yet most retail traders use it as a vague mood word rather than a structured, tradable input. This chapter builds a rigorous framework for converting overnight global information into a ranked, actionable India plan — the gap, the sectors, and the invalidations — before the opening bell.

## What it is & the logic

A "global cue" is any price or event from a market that trades while India sleeps and that carries information about risk appetite, the dollar, rates, or a specific Indian-relevant sector. The logic is one of *information timing*: India is in a time zone where it inherits a full US session and a partial Asian session before it opens. That inherited information is already priced into GIFT Nifty (the reliable pre-market proxy since the SGX Nifty transition to NSE IX/GIFT City), into the rupee's offshore NDF, and into ADRs of Indian companies listed in the US.

The chain of overnight information that shapes the Indian open, in the order it arrives:

1. **US session (India evening–night):** S&P 500, Nasdaq, US10Y, DXY, VIX, and crude/gold set the global risk tone. A Nasdaq rout on a tech-earnings miss is a direct cue for Indian IT.
2. **US close & futures (India late night):** After-hours moves in US index futures and in Indian ADRs (Infosys, Wipro, HDFC Bank, ICICI on the NYSE) refine the tone.
3. **Asian open (India early morning):** Nikkei, Hang Seng, Kospi, and Chinese indices show how *Asia* is digesting the US lead — a critical filter, because if Asia shrugs off a weak US close, India often does too.
4. **GIFT Nifty & USDINR NDF (India pre-open):** the direct, quantified verdict — GIFT Nifty's premium/discount to the previous Nifty close *is* the expected gap.

## Construction & reading the pre-market checklist

The professional's pre-open routine is a fixed checklist, run the same way every morning so nothing is missed:

| # | Input | What to read | India implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GIFT Nifty vs prev close | Gap size & direction | Expected Nifty open |
| 2 | S&P / Nasdaq overnight | Broad + tech risk tone | Index & IT bias |
| 3 | US10Y & DXY | Rate/dollar pressure | FPI-flow & rupee bias |
| 4 | Brent crude | Oil shock | OMC/paints/aviation vs upstream |
| 5 | Asian indices (live) | US-lead confirmation/rejection | Confidence multiplier |
| 6 | USDINR NDF | Currency verdict | Rupee & FPI read |
| 7 | Indian ADRs | Stock-specific overnight moves | IT & bank name gaps |
| 8 | Scheduled events today | Fed minutes, US CPI, RBI, expiry | Event-risk sizing |

The synthesis rule: **a cue is high-conviction only when the layers agree.** A weak US close *confirmed* by weak Asia *confirmed* by a GIFT Nifty discount *confirmed* by a soft rupee is a genuine gap-down you can trade. A weak US close *rejected* by firm Asia and a flat GIFT Nifty is noise — the market has already decided not to care, and fading the "obvious" gap-down is often the trade.

## Reading the gap: the four gap types

The overnight cue expresses itself as an *opening gap*, and the tradable question is always what happens *after* the gap. Four archetypes recur on the Nifty:

1. **Gap-and-go (continuation):** Strong global cue, broad-based, confirmed across layers, into a trending market. The gap holds and extends. Tactic: trade in the gap's direction; the first 15-minute range break confirms.
2. **Gap-and-fill (fade):** A gap driven by a *single* overstated cue that Asia and the rupee do not confirm, or a gap into a strong opposing level. Price fills the gap back to the prior close. Tactic: fade the gap toward the previous close, especially when GIFT premium contradicts the US lead.
3. **Gap-and-trap (reversal):** A gap-up on good news that immediately reverses because it was "buy the rumour, sell the news" — classic on Budget day and post-results. Tactic: the failure of the opening high is the short trigger.
4. **Exhaustion gap:** A large gap in the direction of an already-extended trend, on emotional news. Often marks a local top/bottom. Tactic: watch for immediate rejection; do not chase.

The first 15–30 minutes — the opening range — is where the gap type reveals itself. India-specific nuance: the 9:15 open is preceded by a 9:00–9:08 pre-open call auction, so the "gap" is already partly discovered; the real information is whether the *first 15-minute candle* holds or rejects the opening level.

## Worked India example

Overnight into an Indian Friday in 2026:

- S&P 500 closed −1.4%, Nasdaq −2.1% on a mega-cap tech earnings miss.
- US10Y ticked *down* to 4.10% (risk-off flight to bonds), DXY up modestly to 104.0.
- Brent flat at $79.
- Asian open: Nikkei −1.6%, Hang Seng −1.1% — Asia is *confirming* the weak US lead.
- GIFT Nifty: trading at a 180-point *discount* to the previous Nifty close.
- USDINR NDF: 85.05, mildly weaker.
- Infosys ADR closed −3.2% overnight; TCS unaffected (no US listing but sympathy expected).
- Event: no major domestic data today.

**The synthesis:**

- The layers *agree*: weak US, weak Asia, GIFT discount, soft rupee. This is a **confirmed gap-down**, not noise. Bias: Nifty opens ~180 points lower and is under genuine pressure.
- The *character* of the selloff matters: it is **tech-led**, and US10Y actually *fell*. So this is not a rate shock — it is a growth/tech-sentiment shock. That means IT is the epicentre, but rate-sensitive domestics (banks, autos) may be *relatively* insulated and could even see rotation buying.
- The ADR read: Infosys −3.2% overnight means the IT gap-down is stock-specific-confirmed, not just index sympathy.

**The ranked plan:**

1. *Highest conviction short:* IT largecaps (Infosys, and TCS/HCLTech on sympathy). Two confirmations — Nasdaq rout + Infosys ADR. Enter on failure of the opening 15-minute high.
2. *Index bias:* Nifty gap-down; classify the first candle. If the opening range *breaks down*, it is gap-and-go — press shorts to the next support. If the first 15 minutes holds *above* the open and the rupee stops weakening, suspect gap-and-fill — the tech-led selloff may be too narrow to drag the whole index, and a rotation into banks/autos could lift Nifty back toward the prior close.
3. *Rotation long candidate:* private banks/autos, funded by IT weakness, *only if* breadth confirms after 9:45.
4. *Invalidation:* if GIFT's discount narrows sharply into the open and Asia bounces, the gap-down is being rejected — stand down on shorts and watch for the fill.
5. *Event guard:* it's a Friday with no domestic event, so no oversized event-risk premium; normal sizing.

This is the difference between "global cues weak, so I'll short" (crude) and a *structured, sector-ranked, invalidation-bounded* plan built from six independent overnight inputs.

## How to use it for bias & timing

- **Run the checklist every single day, identically.** Consistency is the edge; a fixed routine prevents you from cherry-picking the one cue that confirms your bias.
- **Weight confirmation over any single input.** One dramatic cue is a hypothesis; four agreeing layers is a plan. The most expensive gaps to trade are the *unconfirmed* ones.
- **Let the cue pick the sector, not just the direction.** A tech-led US selloff is an *IT* trade first and an index trade second. An oil-led shock is an OMC trade first. Global cues are sharpest at the sector level.
- **Size for event days.** On US-CPI mornings, FOMC-result days, RBI-policy days, and expiry, the overnight cue can be violently overwritten intraday — trade smaller and demand the opening range confirm before committing.
- **Use the first 15 minutes as the referee.** The gap sets the hypothesis; the opening-range behaviour confirms which of the four gap types you are in. Never fully commit before the market has spoken for 15 minutes on an event-sensitive open.

## Pitfalls

- **"Global cues weak, so sell" is not analysis.** The whole edge is in *whether the layers confirm* and *what character* the move has. A weak-but-unconfirmed cue often produces a gap-and-fill that traps the crowd short at the open.
- **Over-anchoring on the US.** India is increasingly driven by *domestic* flows (SIPs) and its own earnings/politics. There are frequent sessions where a weak US close is simply ignored because the domestic bid is strong. The US is a cue, not a command.
- **ADR moves can mislead.** Indian ADRs are thin after hours; a −3% ADR print on low volume may not carry to the local stock. Confirm with GIFT and pre-open, don't trade the ADR gap blindly.
- **GIFT Nifty premium/discount includes cost-of-carry.** A small premium is normal (financing); only *changes* in the premium/discount versus fair value are information. Don't mistake structural carry for a bullish cue.
- **Chasing the gap.** Entering at 9:15 on the gap itself, before the opening range confirms, is the most common way to get caught in a gap-and-trap reversal. Let the first candle referee.
- **Event blindness.** Forgetting that a US CPI print or FOMC decision lands *tonight* means yesterday's cue is worthless — the market is coiled and waiting, and last night's move will be overwritten. Always check the calendar first.
- **Correlation drift.** The India–US correlation itself waxes and wanes; in strong domestic bull phases India decouples for weeks. Re-verify that the relationship is currently *active* before leaning on it.

## Interview-ready summary

An Indian trading day is shaped overnight, and a "global cue" is any price or event — US indices, US10Y, DXY, crude, gold, VIX, Asian markets, Indian ADRs, GIFT Nifty, the USDINR NDF — that carries risk-appetite, rate, dollar, or sector information into the Indian open. The professional runs a fixed eight-point pre-market checklist and applies one synthesis rule: **a cue is high-conviction only when the layers agree** — a weak US close confirmed by weak Asia, a GIFT-Nifty discount, and a soft rupee is a genuine, tradable gap-down, whereas the same US close *rejected* by firm Asia and flat GIFT is noise you often fade. The cue expresses itself as an opening gap that resolves into one of four archetypes — gap-and-go, gap-and-fill, gap-and-trap, exhaustion — and the first 15-minute opening range is the referee that tells you which. Crucially, let the cue's *character* pick the sector: a tech-led Nasdaq rout is an IT short first (confirmed by the Infosys ADR), an oil shock is an OMC trade first, a rate shock routes growth-vs-value. The honest caveats: "cues weak, so sell" is not analysis, India increasingly decouples on strong domestic SIP flows, ADRs are thin and can mislead, GIFT premium is partly cost-of-carry, chasing the gap before the opening range confirms invites the gap-and-trap, and an event landing tonight overwrites last night's cue — so check the calendar, demand confirmation, and let the first candle speak before you commit.
