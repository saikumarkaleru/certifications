# Three White Soldiers, Three Black Crows & Deliberation

## What it is & why it works

Three White Soldiers and Three Black Crows are **three-candle trend-establishment / reversal patterns** built from *sequential, same-direction* candles — three strong days marching the same way. Three White Soldiers is three consecutive long green candles, each opening within the prior body and closing near its high, that signals a durable shift *from bearishness to bullishness*. Three Black Crows is the bearish mirror — three long red candles closing near their lows — signalling a shift from bullishness to bearishness. **Deliberation** (and its close relative, the **Advance Block**) are the *warning* variants: the same three-soldier shape, but with each successive candle *losing power*, telling you the advance is running out of steam even as it continues.

These patterns matter because they are about **persistence of conviction**, not a single dramatic bar. One big candle can be a spike, a fat-finger, or a news pop. *Three* strong same-direction candles in a row, each building on the last, is hard to fake — it requires sustained participation across multiple sessions. That is why Three White Soldiers appearing after a long downtrend or a base is treated as a genuine trend-change signal: it takes real, broad demand to print three consecutive strong-closing green days.

The behavioural engine is **momentum with follow-through**. In Three White Soldiers:
- Each candle **opens within the prior candle's body** (buyers accept slightly lower prices at the open — no runaway gap) …
- … and **closes near its high**, above the prior close (buyers dominate the whole session and finish strong).
- Repeated three times, this is the footprint of steady accumulation: dips are bought, every session ends bullish, and shorts are progressively squeezed.

The genius of **Deliberation** and the **Advance Block** is that they read the *rate of change of that conviction*. The pattern still marches up, but the second and especially the third candle have **smaller bodies and/or long upper wicks** — buyers are getting tired, sellers are pushing back into the close. It says: "the trend is still up, but the last buyers are arriving; distribution may be starting." It is an *internal* weakening, invisible if you only look at the direction, obvious if you look at candle *quality*.

In Indian markets these show up constantly. Three White Soldiers off a Nifty base after a correction, or in a beaten-down PSU/banking name that suddenly finds sustained buying, is a classic momentum-ignition signal. Three Black Crows after an over-extended midcap rally is a warning that distribution has begun. And the Advance Block near a resistance zone — three up-days but each weaker — is the tell that a breakout is more likely to fail than fly.

## The mechanics

### Three White Soldiers (bullish reversal / continuation)
| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Context | Best after a downtrend, a base, or a pullback within an uptrend |
| Three candles | Three consecutive **long green** real bodies |
| Opens | Each candle opens **within** (or near the top of) the prior candle's body — *not* a big gap up |
| Closes | Each closes **near its high** and **above the prior close** — small upper wicks |
| Strength cues | Bodies roughly equal or growing; small wicks; rising or steady volume; no exhaustion |

### Three Black Crows (bearish reversal / continuation)
| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Context | Best after an uptrend or at a top |
| Three candles | Three consecutive **long red** real bodies |
| Opens | Each opens **within** the prior candle's body (ideally in its lower half) |
| Closes | Each closes **near its low** and **below the prior close** — small lower wicks |
| Strength cues | Full bodies, tiny wicks, steady/rising volume; a "staircase down" |

### The weakening cousins — Deliberation & Advance Block
| Pattern | Shape | Message |
|---|---|---|
| **Advance Block** | Three green candles advancing, but each **body gets smaller** and **upper wicks get longer** | Buyers tiring into resistance; up-move losing thrust — *caution / potential top* |
| **Deliberation (Stalled)** | Two strong green candles, then a **third small candle (often a spinning top/star) near the top**, sometimes gapping up slightly | Momentum has stalled; the trend "hesitates" — reversal risk rising |

The distinction is subtle but crucial: **Three White Soldiers = healthy, each candle strong; Advance Block / Deliberation = the same march but visibly running out of gas.** One is a green light, the other an amber light on the *same* road.

### Quality checklist (what separates the real thing from a trap)
| Good Three White Soldiers | Suspicious (looks like it, isn't) |
|---|---|
| Bodies similar/growing | Third body much smaller (→ Advance Block) |
| Small upper wicks | Long upper wicks (sellers capping) |
| Opens inside prior body | Big gaps between candles (over-extension/exhaustion) |
| Steady/rising volume | Volume fading each day |
| Not already over-extended | Prints after a huge run (climax risk) |

### Timeframe note (India)
On **daily** charts these are trend-quality signals worth acting on. On **weekly** charts, three white soldiers after a bear phase is a strong bottoming/turn signal. Intraday (15-min), three strong same-colour candles can mark the start of a trend day — useful, but confirm with volume/VWAP because intraday sequences reverse quickly. Because Indian cash trades continuously, the "opens within prior body" rule is usually satisfied naturally (no intraday gaps); the bigger risk is the *over-extension* fake after a gap-up open on the daily.

## Reading it — a worked India example

**Three White Soldiers on State Bank of India (SBIN, daily) — a base breakout.**

**Phase 1 — the base.** SBIN has been dead money, chopping in a **₹770–800** range for six weeks after a decline. Volume is low, RSI is flat near 45, sentiment indifferent. Price is coiling.

**Phase 2 — the three soldiers.**
- **Day 1:** SBIN opens ₹788, buyers step in all day, closes **₹806** near the high — a long green body that pokes above the range top. Volume ticks up 1.3×.
- **Day 2:** opens ₹804 (inside day-1's body — no gap), closes **₹821** near its high. Small upper wick. Volume 1.5×.
- **Day 3:** opens ₹819, closes **₹836** near the high. Three long green bodies, each opening within the prior and closing strong; volume now ~1.7× the 20-day average.

This is a clean **Three White Soldiers** emerging from a base — the bodies are similar-to-growing, wicks are small, and volume is *expanding*, which is exactly what you want. It marks the ignition of a new uptrend, not a spike.

**Phase 3 — follow-through.** SBIN consolidates one day (a small inside candle — healthy digestion, not a crow), then advances to **₹860**, later **₹885**, over the following two weeks. The soldiers correctly flagged the trend change.

Now the **cautionary mirror — an Advance Block on a midcap (say, a hot railway PSU) into resistance.** The stock has already run 40% and approaches a major supply zone at **₹640**.
- **Day 1:** long green, close ₹612, strong.
- **Day 2:** green, but the body is *smaller* and there is a **long upper wick** — it tagged ₹628 and closed ₹620.
- **Day 3:** green again, but the body is *smaller still*, closes ₹627 with an upper wick to ₹636, right under the ₹640 resistance, and volume is *fading*.

Three up-days — but this is an **Advance Block**, not soldiers: each candle weaker, wicks longer, volume declining, into resistance. The message is the opposite of the SBIN case: buyers are exhausting themselves at the wall. Sure enough, the next session prints a bearish engulfing and the stock rolls over to ₹590. The *shape* was bullish; the *quality* was a warning.

## Trading it

### Three White Soldiers long (SBIN example)
- **Entry trigger:** These patterns are already three candles in, so avoid chasing the third close blindly. Best entries: (a) buy the **first minor pullback / inside day** after the pattern (e.g., buy the break of the small consolidation candle's high, ~₹838), or (b) enter on the third candle's close (~₹836) only if it is *not* over-extended. Chasing a big third candle far from support is the main way to overpay.
- **Stop-loss:** below the **low of the pattern** (day-1 low ≈ ₹786) for a positional stop, or tighter under the last swing low / the pullback candle's low (~₹818) for an aggressive entry. From an ₹838 entry with a ₹818 stop, risk ≈ ₹20.
- **Target / measured move:** the pattern often runs at least the height of the three soldiers projected from the breakout. Soldiers spanned ₹786→₹836 (~₹50). Project from the ~₹800 base top → first target **₹850–860**, then the next resistance **₹885**. Book partial at ₹860, trail the rest under the rising 20-DMA or under each higher swing low. Move stop to breakeven after a close above ₹850.

### Three Black Crows short / long exit
On an over-extended stock or index, three black crows (three strong red closes) is a cue to **exit longs and consider shorts / puts**. Entry: short on the third crow's close or on a pullback that fails at a broken level (e.g., a retest of the first crow's open that gets sold). Stop above the pattern's high. Target the prior demand shelf or a measured move equal to the crows' height. Caveat: by the third crow the move is often *already extended to the downside*, so short-sellers frequently do better waiting for a small bounce than chasing the third red candle — otherwise you sell into an oversold spring.

### Advance Block / Deliberation — the *defensive* play
These are not entries in the trend's direction — they are **exit/hedge signals**:
- If you are **long** and see an Advance Block into resistance (weakening bodies, longer upper wicks, fading volume), **tighten stops, book partials, or hedge with puts** — do not add.
- For **option sellers**, an Advance Block at a Call-OI wall is a cue to write call spreads: the up-thrust is losing fuel even though price is still rising.
- A short entry is only justified *after confirmation* — e.g., a bearish candle that closes below the block's last swing low — not on the block itself.

### Scenarios
1. **Healthy soldiers → pullback → continuation:** the ideal; buy the pullback, trail.
2. **Soldiers → immediate stall (turns into deliberation):** if the fourth candle is a tiny spinning top with a long upper wick, treat it as an amber light — tighten stops, don't add.
3. **Crows into oversold:** if three crows arrive already deep and stretched from the 20-DMA, expect a bounce; short only on a lower-high retest, not the third crow.

## Confluence

- **Location / structure:** Three White Soldiers *breaking out of a base or reclaiming a level* is far stronger than soldiers in mid-air. Three Black Crows *breaking down from a top or distribution range* is the reliable version. Location decides quality.
- **Volume:** the single most important filter. Real soldiers/crows carry **expanding volume**; fading volume across the three candles is the fingerprint of an Advance Block / exhaustion, not a healthy trend.
- **Moving averages:** soldiers reclaiming the 50-/200-DMA, or crows breaking below them, confirm the regime change. Soldiers off a rising 20-DMA in an existing uptrend are a continuation buy.
- **RSI/MACD:** soldiers pushing RSI up through 60 with MACD crossing above zero support a genuine trend start; soldiers into RSI > 75 warn of over-extension (climax risk). A *bearish divergence* accompanying an Advance Block is a strong distribution tell.
- **Option-chain / OI (India edge):**
  - *Three White Soldiers on Nifty/Bank Nifty:* strongest when accompanied by **short-covering** (futures OI *falling* as price rises — trapped bears fuelling the march) or by **Put writers adding OI** below (building a demand floor). Soldiers that punch *through* a heavy Call-OI strike, forcing call writers to unwind, can trigger a gamma-driven acceleration.
  - *Three Black Crows:* most reliable with **fresh short additions** (OI up, price down) and **Call writers holding/adding** at the strikes above — supply confirmed by positioning.
  - *Advance Block:* if the block stalls right at the **highest Call-OI strike** with call writers *adding* OI, the option market is telling you the same thing the candles are — the ceiling is real.

The rule of thumb: **three same-colour candles + expanding volume + a level being reclaimed/broken + OI confirmation** = a trustworthy trend signal. Drop the volume or the level and you are often looking at exhaustion, not ignition.

## Pitfalls & false signals

1. **Over-extension / climax.** The classic trap. Three white soldiers that appear *after* a stock has already run hard, with big bodies and gaps, are often the *end* of the move (a buying climax), not the start. Chasing the third candle far from support is how traders buy the top. Filter: is the pattern *starting* a move from a base, or *ending* an extended run?

2. **Confusing soldiers with an Advance Block.** If bodies shrink and upper wicks lengthen across the three candles, it is *not* healthy soldiers — it is distribution wearing a bullish costume. Look at candle *quality*, not just colour and direction.

3. **Fading volume.** Three green candles on *declining* volume is weak participation; the move lacks fuel. Demand expanding or at least steady volume.

4. **Crows into oversold.** By the third black crow the move is often stretched; shorting there sells into a likely bounce. Wait for a retest/lower-high.

5. **Gaps between the candles.** Big gaps violate the "opens within prior body" logic and signal emotional over-extension rather than steady accumulation/distribution — lower reliability.

6. **Ignoring the higher timeframe & events.** Three soldiers into a weekly resistance, or right before results/RBI policy, can be reversed instantly. Respect the bigger frame and the calendar.

**How pros filter:** they grade candle *quality* (body size trend, wick length) not just direction; they insist on expanding volume; they check whether the pattern *initiates* a move from a base/level versus *terminating* an extended run; they read the OI to see if positioning agrees; and they treat the Advance Block / Deliberation as an explicit *warning to defend*, not a trigger to add. Entry is usually on the *pullback* after soldiers (or the *bounce* after crows), never a blind chase of the third candle.

## Interview-ready summary

"Three White Soldiers and Three Black Crows are three-candle momentum patterns built from consecutive same-direction candles. **Three White Soldiers** — three long green bodies, each opening *within* the prior body and closing near its high — signals a durable shift to bullishness; it's most trustworthy emerging from a base with *expanding volume*. **Three Black Crows** is the mirror: three long red closes near their lows, marking distribution and a shift to bearishness. The key is *candle quality*: healthy soldiers have similar-or-growing bodies, small upper wicks, and rising volume. When the bodies instead *shrink* and *upper wicks lengthen* as price still advances, it's not soldiers — it's an **Advance Block**, and if the third candle stalls into a tiny spinning top it's **Deliberation**; both are *warnings* that the up-move is exhausting, cues to tighten stops or hedge, not to add. You don't chase the third candle — you buy the first pullback after soldiers (stop below the pattern low) or short the failed bounce after crows, targeting a measured move equal to the pattern's height. The biggest trap is over-extension: soldiers after a long run can be a buying climax, not ignition. In Indian F&O, soldiers backed by short-covering or put-writing (and crows backed by fresh short additions) are the high-conviction versions, and an Advance Block stalling at the heaviest Call-OI strike is the option chain confirming the candles."
