# Marubozu, Spinning Tops & Continuation Candles

## What it is & why it works

Most candlestick education is obsessed with reversals — the hammers, the engulfings, the stars that promise a turn. But the majority of trading days are not turning points; they are the *middle* of moves, or moments of indecision that resolve into more of the same. This chapter is about reading those candles: the ones that tell you a trend is healthy and should be pressed (Marubozu), the ones that tell you the market has gone neutral and you should wait (Spinning Tops), and the small "resting" candles that appear *within* a trend without ending it (continuation candles like Rising/Falling Three Methods).

A **Marubozu** ("shaved head" / "bald" in Japanese) is a candle with a full real body and *no wicks* — or wicks so tiny they don't matter. A bullish Marubozu opens at its low and closes at its high: buyers were in control from the first tick to the last, and there was no point in the session where sellers pushed price back. A bearish Marubozu opens at its high and closes at its low: relentless one-way selling. It is the purest single-candle expression of conviction. Where a Doji says "the two sides fought to a draw," a Marubozu says "one side never showed up."

A **Spinning Top** is almost the opposite: a small real body with *long wicks on both sides*. Price ranged widely during the period, poking high and low, but closed near where it opened. Buyers and sellers both had their moment, and neither won. It is a candle of *indecision*. A Spinning Top's colour barely matters; what matters is the small body sandwiched between two shadows. (Its close cousin, the Doji, is the limiting case where the body shrinks to a line.)

**Continuation candles** are small-bodied candles that appear during a trend and represent a *pause, not a reversal*. The classic formalisation is the **Rising Three Methods** (bullish) and **Falling Three Methods** (bearish): a long trend candle, then a cluster of small counter-coloured candles that stay inside the first candle's range, then another long trend candle in the original direction that closes beyond the first. It is the market catching its breath before continuing.

Why does this family "work"? Because trend-following is, statistically, the more reliable edge for most retail traders, and these candles help you *stay with a trend* rather than constantly hunting for its end. A Marubozu in the direction of the trend is a green light to hold or add; a Spinning Top after a strong run is a yellow light to tighten stops; a Three Methods pattern is a "the trend is just resting" signal that keeps you from bailing out of a good position on the first small pullback. The behavioural truth underneath is that trends persist longer than reversal-hunters expect, and the losing side capitulates gradually — these candles are the texture of that persistence.

## The mechanics

**Marubozu**
| Feature | Bullish Marubozu | Bearish Marubozu |
|---|---|---|
| Open | At (or extremely near) the low | At (or extremely near) the high |
| Close | At (or extremely near) the high | At (or extremely near) the low |
| Wicks | None / negligible on both ends | None / negligible on both ends |
| Message | Full-session buyer control | Full-session seller control |
| Body size | Should be large relative to recent candles (an ATR-sized or bigger range) | Same |

Purists distinguish an *Opening Marubozu* (no wick at the open end only) and a *Closing Marubozu* (no wick at the close end only) from the *full Marubozu* (no wick either end). The most powerful is the full Marubozu; a Closing Marubozu in the trend direction is still a strong hold signal because it means the period ended at its extreme.

**Spinning Top**
| Feature | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Real body | Small, centred |
| Upper wick | Long |
| Lower wick | Long (roughly comparable to the upper) |
| Colour | Largely irrelevant |
| Message | Indecision / balance; momentum stalling |

If the wicks are lopsided — a long lower wick with a small body near the top — it stops being a neutral Spinning Top and becomes a **Hammer / Hanging Man** (directional). Symmetry of the wicks is what makes it "spinning."

**Rising / Falling Three Methods**
| Step | Rising Three Methods (bullish continuation) |
|---|---|
| Candle 1 | Long bullish candle |
| Candles 2–4 | A group (typically three) of small candles, usually bearish, that trade *within* Candle 1's high-low range and drift gently lower |
| Final candle | Long bullish candle that closes *above* Candle 1's close (ideally at a new high) |
| Meaning | Shallow pullback failed to reverse; trend resumes |

Falling Three Methods is the exact mirror: a long red candle, a small upward drift contained within it, then a long red candle to new lows. The essential test for *any* continuation pattern is that the pause **holds inside the trend candle's range** — the moment the small candles break decisively out of that range against the trend, it is no longer a continuation, it's a potential reversal.

A practical, timeframe-agnostic point: "long" and "small" are always *relative to recent range*. Compare each candle's body/range to the **Average True Range (ATR)** of the last 14 periods. A Marubozu worth respecting is one whose range is ~1.5x ATR or more with no wicks; a Spinning Top is a candle whose body is a small fraction of ATR with wicks eating the rest. This ATR-normalisation is what separates a genuine conviction candle from a candle that only looks big because you're zoomed in.

## Reading it — a worked Nifty example

Trace a Nifty up-move across roughly two weeks and read the candles as a sequence — this is where the family shines, because they are context, not standalone triggers.

**Phase 1 — the ignition Marubozu.** Nifty has been coiling in a tight 150-point range around 24,000 for several sessions (lots of small bodies, no direction). Then on a strong global cue it opens at 24,050 and simply runs, closing at 24,290 right at the high with essentially no wicks — a bullish full Marubozu, range ~240 points against a recent ATR near 150. The message is unambiguous: the range is resolved *up*, and buyers controlled every minute. A trader who was flat now has a clean directional bias; a trader who was already long holds with confidence. Volume is well above the 20-day average, confirming real participation rather than a thin drift.

**Phase 2 — the pause (Three Methods).** Over the next three sessions Nifty does *not* extend hard. Instead it prints three small red candles: 24,240, then 24,210, then 24,225 closes — each a modest body, each staying *inside* the 24,050–24,290 range of the ignition Marubozu. Reversal-hunters start calling a top. But note what is *not* happening: price is not breaking back below 24,050, and the pullback is orderly and low-volume (profit-taking, not distribution). This is the middle of a Rising Three Methods. The correct action is patience, not panic — the trend is resting.

**Phase 3 — the resolution candle.** The fifth session gaps up and closes strong at 24,410, a long bullish candle clearing the ignition candle's high. The Rising Three Methods is complete. The shallow pause held, and the trend has resumed with authority. Anyone who sold the three small red candles as a "reversal" is now chasing.

**Phase 4 — the warning Spinning Top.** The move extends further to about 24,650 over the next few days, but now momentum is visibly slowing. Then a candle prints that ranges from 24,720 down to 24,540 but closes at 24,610 — a small body with long wicks on both sides, right after an extended run. This Spinning Top is the market's first sign of two-sided fighting after a one-way move. It is *not* a sell signal by itself, but it is a clear instruction to tighten stops and stop adding. If the very next candle is a bearish Marubozu closing at its low, the indecision has resolved *down* and the up-move is likely over.

The teaching point across all four phases: no single candle made the trade. The Marubozu gave direction and conviction; the Three Methods kept us in through a scary-looking pause; the Spinning Top warned that the party was ending. Reading the *sequence* is the skill.

## Trading it

These candles are less about a standalone entry trigger and more about *bias and management* — but they generate concrete trades too.

**Marubozu — breakout / continuation entry.** A Marubozu that resolves a consolidation (Phase 1 above) is a legitimate breakout entry. Enter on a break of the Marubozu's high (bullish) or low (bearish) on the next candle, or aggressively on the close of the Marubozu itself. Stop goes below the *midpoint or low* of the Marubozu — because a genuine conviction candle should not be fully retraced quickly. For the Nifty case: enter above 24,290, stop below ~24,170 (midpoint) or 24,050 (low, wider). Target the measured range projected from the prior consolidation, or the next resistance / high-OI call strike. A Marubozu also makes an excellent *trailing reference* while in a trade: as long as strong-bodied candles keep printing in your direction, hold; the appearance of wicks and shrinking bodies is your cue to trail tighter.

**Rising/Falling Three Methods — add or hold.** This is primarily a *hold-and-add* pattern, not a fresh entry. If you are already long from the ignition Marubozu, the Three Methods pause is where you resist the urge to exit, and optionally *add* on the resolution candle's break of the pattern high (24,290 → new highs). Stop for the add sits below the *low of the pause* (the lowest of the three small candles), which is a tight, well-defined level. Risk is small relative to the fresh trend leg, which is what makes pyramiding into Three Methods attractive.

**Spinning Top — management, not entry.** Never enter *on* a Spinning Top; it is indecision by definition and could break either way. Its trade value is defensive: after a strong run, a Spinning Top says "move your stop up to the recent swing low and stop adding." It can also be a *conditional* setup — a Spinning Top at a major resistance level becomes a short trigger *only if* the next candle breaks its low; a Spinning Top at support becomes a long trigger only if the next candle breaks its high. In other words, you trade the *resolution* of the indecision, not the indecision itself.

**Scenarios.**
- *Continuation works:* Marubozu → shallow Three Methods → resolution → hold and trail under swing lows. Textbook trend ride.
- *Continuation fails:* the "pause" candles break *below* the ignition Marubozu's low. The Three Methods is invalidated — this is now a potential reversal (a failed continuation is a strong signal in itself). Exit longs; the failed pattern often precedes a sharp move the other way.
- *Indecision resolves against you:* you're long, a Spinning Top prints, and the next candle is a bearish Marubozu to a new low. Your tightened stop takes you out near the top with most of the gain intact — exactly why the Spinning Top warning mattered.

## Confluence

**Volume is the truth serum for the Marubozu.** A bullish Marubozu on volume 1.5–2x the 20-day average is genuine institutional buying; the same shape on light volume can be a thin-tape squeeze that reverses. Always pair the Marubozu with volume — conviction candle *plus* conviction volume is the combination that trends.

**Support/resistance and the range breakout.** A Marubozu that breaks a well-defined consolidation, a horizontal resistance, or a trendline is far more tradeable than one in open trend. The Nifty ignition Marubozu mattered *because* it resolved a multi-day range. Location turns a candle into a setup.

**Moving averages for trend context.** In an uptrend defined by price above a rising 20/50-EMA, a Falling Three Methods that pulls back to the 20-EMA and holds is a beautiful continuation-buy — the moving average catches the pause and the candle confirms the resume. Continuation patterns are best traded *with* the higher-timeframe trend, never against it.

**Option chain / OI (India).** A bullish Marubozu breakout in Bank Nifty gains conviction if it is accompanied by aggressive **put writing** at and below the breakout strike (writers backing the move) and **short covering** in calls overhead. A Spinning Top forming right at a strike with the **highest call OI** (a known resistance wall) is a strong "don't chase, expect rejection" signal. When a Three Methods pause holds and the resolution candle fires, watch for OI to shift — fresh call *unwinding* above confirms the path is clearing.

**ATR for calibration.** Use ATR not just to grade candle size but to *set stops*. A Marubozu-breakout stop placed at 1–1.5x ATR below entry respects normal noise; a stop at the candle midpoint is often close to that ATR distance anyway, which is a nice cross-check that your risk is neither too tight nor too loose.

## Pitfalls & false signals

**The exhaustion Marubozu.** The single biggest trap: a huge Marubozu that appears at the *end* of an extended move rather than the start. After a long run, a giant one-way candle can be *climactic* — the last, most emotional participants piling in — and it can mark the exact top or bottom (a "buying/selling climax"). The tell is context: a Marubozu breaking *out of* a base is ignition; a Marubozu appearing after price is already stretched far from its moving average, into resistance, on a news spike, is more likely exhaustion. Same candle, opposite meaning, decided by *where* it prints.

**Chasing the Marubozu close.** Because a Marubozu closes at its extreme, buying the close means buying the *worst* price of the session with your stop necessarily far away. Entering on a small pullback or a break of the next candle usually gives far better risk-reward. Discipline over FOMO.

**Mistaking indecision for a signal.** Traders often assign a bullish/bearish meaning to a Spinning Top's colour. Don't. A Spinning Top is *neutral by construction*; its predictive content is "wait." Acting on the body colour of an indecision candle is reading tea leaves.

**Continuation vs reversal confusion.** The Three Methods pause and a reversal both start with small counter-trend candles. The discriminator is strict: continuation requires the pause to *stay inside the prior trend candle's range* and resume in the original direction. If the small candles break out against the trend, it is not a continuation — treat a *failed* continuation as a reversal warning, because trapped continuation-buyers become sellers.

**Timeframe and zoom illusions.** A candle that looks like a decisive Marubozu on a 1-minute chart may be a trivial wiggle on the daily. Always grade candle significance against ATR and prefer higher-timeframe signals. Low-liquidity mid-cap stocks also print "Marubozu" candles that are really just wide bid-ask spread and one big order — meaningless without volume behind them.

**Event candles.** A Marubozu formed purely on a Budget-day, RBI-policy, or earnings gap is an *event* candle, not a technical one. It reflects a one-time repricing, and the follow-through is driven by fundamentals, not the candle. Weight these far less, and never assume a news Marubozu implies a tradable technical trend by itself.

## Interview-ready summary

"This family reads the *middle* of moves rather than the turns. A Marubozu is a full-bodied candle with no wicks — one side controlled the entire session; in the trend's direction it's a conviction/continuation signal, and I'll trade a Marubozu that breaks a consolidation, entering on the break with a stop at the candle's midpoint. A Spinning Top is a small body with long wicks on both sides — pure indecision; I never enter *on* it, I use it to tighten stops and I only act when the *next* candle resolves the indecision. Continuation patterns like Rising/Falling Three Methods are a long trend candle, a shallow pause that stays inside its range, then another trend candle to new extremes — they keep me in a good trend through a scary-looking pullback and give me a tight, well-defined add. The universal caveats: grade every candle's size against ATR, demand volume behind a Marubozu, and mind context — a big one-way candle breaking *out of* a base is ignition, but the same candle after an extended, stretched move can be *exhaustion* and mark the top. In Nifty and Bank Nifty I confirm with the option chain — put writing backing a bullish Marubozu breakout, a call wall warning me a Spinning Top will be rejected. Read the *sequence*, not the single candle, and it's probabilities managed with tight risk, never certainty."
