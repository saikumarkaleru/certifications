# Head & Shoulders (and Inverse)

## What it is & why it works

The Head & Shoulders is the most famous reversal pattern in technical analysis, and unlike much of the pattern-recognition folklore, it has a genuine structural logic that survives scrutiny. A standard (topping) Head & Shoulders forms at the end of an uptrend and consists of three consecutive peaks: a **left shoulder**, a taller **head**, and a **right shoulder** that is roughly the same height as the left. The lows between these peaks define a **neckline**. When price closes below that neckline, the pattern is said to complete, and the market has signalled that the balance of buyers and sellers has flipped from accumulation to distribution.

The **inverse Head & Shoulders** is the mirror image at the bottom of a downtrend: a left shoulder trough, a deeper head trough, a right shoulder trough, and a neckline connecting the two intervening highs. A close above the neckline signals a bottom.

Why does it work? Because it is a visual record of a trend running out of buyers. Consider the topping version in an uptrend. Price makes a new high (left shoulder), pulls back, then rallies to an even higher high on strong momentum (the head). So far this is textbook uptrend behaviour — higher highs, higher lows. The tell comes on the third rally: buyers push price up again but **fail to exceed the head**, making a lower high (right shoulder). That single failure to make a higher high is the market whispering that demand is exhausted. The neckline break is the confirmation — the point at which the sequence of higher lows is also broken, so both pillars of the uptrend (higher highs AND higher lows) have collapsed. In Dow Theory terms, the trend structure has reversed.

The pattern also encodes a **distribution story**. Smart money that accumulated lower is unloading into the strength of the head and the right-shoulder rally. Volume typically confirms this: heavy on the advance to the head, noticeably lighter on the right-shoulder rally (fewer committed buyers), and expanding on the neckline break (sellers taking control). In the Indian context — where retail participation is heavy and momentum chasing is common — the right shoulder often represents the last cohort of latecomers buying the "dip," only to be trapped when the neckline gives way.

It is essential to hold this honestly: a Head & Shoulders is a **probability tool**, not a prophecy. Many potential H&S patterns fail — the right shoulder morphs into a new leg up, or the neckline break turns out to be a bear trap. What makes the pattern tradeable is not certainty but an asymmetric, definable risk: a clear invalidation level (above the right shoulder / head) and a measurable target (the neckline-to-head distance projected). That structure lets you size a trade where you lose a little when wrong and make a multiple when right.

## The mechanics

**Anatomy of the topping pattern:**

| Component | Description | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Left shoulder | First peak after a sustained advance, with a pullback | Normal uptrend high |
| Head | Higher peak than left shoulder | Momentum climax; often on high volume |
| Right shoulder | Peak lower than the head, ideally near left-shoulder height | Failure to make new high — demand fading |
| Neckline | Line joining the two reaction lows (troughs) | The trigger line; break = confirmation |
| Volume | Ideally declining left→head→right; surges on break | Confirms distribution |

**Neckline construction.** Draw a line connecting the low after the left shoulder to the low after the head. This neckline can be horizontal, up-sloping, or down-sloping. A **down-sloping neckline** is more bearish (each reaction low is weaker); an **up-sloping neckline** is slightly less reliable and delays the signal. For the inverse pattern, the neckline joins the two intervening highs.

**The measured move (price target).** Measure the vertical distance from the top of the **head** to the neckline (at the point directly below the head). Project that same distance **downward from the point where price breaks the neckline**. Formula:

- Target = Neckline breakpoint − (Head price − Neckline price below head)

For the inverse pattern, add the distance upward from the breakout point.

**Confirmation rules (to filter noise):**

1. **Close beyond the neckline**, not just an intrabar poke. On the daily timeframe, a daily close is the standard; stricter traders wait for a second close or a weekly close for index-level positions.
2. **A filter buffer** — many use a 1–3% penetration for indices, or 0.5×ATR, to avoid whipsaws exactly at the line.
3. **Volume expansion** on the break adds weight (though downside breaks can succeed on average volume; upside breaks in inverse patterns should show clear volume).
4. **The throwback / pullback:** after breaking, price frequently returns to retest the neckline (now resistance for a top, support for a bottom). A failed retest that resumes the move is the highest-probability entry.

**Symmetry guidelines (not rigid rules):**

- The two shoulders should be roughly symmetric in height and in time. Grossly lopsided shoulders weaken the read.
- The right shoulder should not exceed the head — if it does, the pattern is invalidated.
- Time symmetry: a pattern that takes weeks to months is more significant than one built over a few hours on a 5-minute chart.

**Timeframe scaling.** The pattern is fractal — it appears on 5-minute intraday charts for scalping Bank Nifty and on weekly charts for multi-month position trades. The larger the timeframe, the larger and more reliable the signal, and the larger the target.

## Reading it — a worked India example

Let us walk a realistic **Nifty 50** topping Head & Shoulders on the daily chart, with representative levels.

**Phase 1 — the left shoulder.** Nifty has been in a strong uptrend and rallies to **24,800**, a local high, on healthy volume. Momentum is stretched; RSI is near 72. Price pulls back to **24,200** over a week as profit-taking sets in. This first peak-and-pullback is the left shoulder. Nothing bearish yet — it looks like a normal uptrend breather.

**Phase 2 — the head.** Buyers return and drive Nifty to a fresh all-time high of **25,300**, exceeding the left shoulder by 500 points. This is the head. Crucially, note the internals: the advance to 25,300 comes on **lower volume than the left-shoulder rally**, and RSI makes a **lower high (~68) despite the higher price** — a classic bearish divergence. Price then falls back to **24,150**, near the prior reaction low. Connecting 24,200 and 24,150 gives us a **near-horizontal neckline around 24,150–24,200**.

**Phase 3 — the right shoulder.** The market rallies a third time but stalls at **24,750**, failing to reach the head's 25,300. This lower high is the right shoulder. Volume on this rally is visibly thin — the weakest of the three advances. Now the structure is complete in outline: 24,800 / 25,300 / 24,750 with a neckline at ~24,150. RSI on this bounce barely reaches 55, confirming waning momentum.

**Phase 4 — the neckline break.** Nifty rolls over from 24,750 and, ten sessions later, **closes at 23,980**, decisively below the 24,150 neckline (about 0.7% penetration) on the heaviest down-volume in weeks. The pattern is confirmed.

**The measured move.** Head = 25,300; neckline below head ≈ 24,150. Distance = **1,150 points**. Break point ≈ 24,150. Target = 24,150 − 1,150 = **23,000**.

**Phase 5 — the throwback.** Three sessions after the break, Nifty bounces back to **24,120**, kissing the underside of the neckline, then fails and turns down again on the intraday reversal. This retest-and-fail is the textbook low-risk short entry. Over the next three weeks Nifty grinds down to **23,050**, essentially hitting the measured target, before the pattern's work is done.

The same logic in reverse builds an **inverse H&S on Bank Nifty** at a market bottom: left shoulder low **48,500**, head low **47,200**, right shoulder low **48,600**, neckline (joining the intervening highs) at **49,800**. A daily close above 49,800 targets 49,800 + (49,800 − 47,200) = **52,400**.

## Trading it

**Entry.** There are three entry styles with a risk/reward trade-off:

1. **Break entry (aggressive):** short on the daily close below the neckline (23,980 in our Nifty example). Best reward but exposed to a false break.
2. **Retest entry (preferred):** wait for the throwback to the neckline underside (~24,120) and enter on the reversal candle. Tighter stop, higher win rate, but you occasionally miss trades that never retest.
3. **Right-shoulder entry (anticipatory):** for experienced traders, short into the right-shoulder rally when it shows a reversal candle below the head, e.g., near 24,700. Best price but the pattern is not yet confirmed — smallest size.

**Stop-loss.** The logical invalidation is a close **above the right shoulder** (24,750) for a conservative stop, or above the neckline retest high for an aggressive stop. If you entered on the break at 23,980, a stop above the right shoulder (24,750) risks ~770 points; a stop above the retest high (~24,200) risks only ~220 points on a retest entry — illustrating why the retest entry gives far superior reward-to-risk.

**Target.** Primary target is the measured move (23,000). Scale out: book half at a nearby structural support or at 50% of the projected move, trail the rest. In our example, book partial at 23,500 (a prior consolidation zone) and let the remainder run to 23,000.

**Scenario A — clean success.** Retest entry at 24,120, stop at 24,260 (140 points risk), target 23,000 (1,120 points reward) = **8:1 reward-to-risk**. Even at a 45% win rate this is strongly positive expectancy.

**Scenario B — the retest overshoots.** Price breaks the neckline, then throws back and briefly trades **above** the neckline to 24,300 before failing. If your stop was tight (24,200) you were stopped out, yet the pattern still played out down to 23,000. The lesson: place the stop beyond the noise (a swing high or an ATR buffer), not exactly at the line. A stop at 24,400 would have survived.

**Scenario C — outright failure.** Nifty breaks the neckline to 23,980, then reverses hard and closes back above the right shoulder at 24,800. The pattern has failed; a failed H&S often produces a sharp squeeze because trapped shorts must cover. Exit immediately on the close above the right shoulder and consider that failed bearish patterns can become bullish signals.

**Management.** Once price moves halfway to target, trail the stop to breakeven, then below each lower high the downtrend prints. For index positions, a break back above the neckline after the move has begun is a warning to tighten.

## Confluence

A Head & Shoulders is far more tradeable when it aligns with independent evidence:

- **Volume signature:** declining volume left→head→right and a volume surge on the break is the classic confirmation. In Indian stocks, watch delivery volumes too — distribution shows up as high-volume down days with rising delivery percentage.
- **Momentum divergence:** bearish RSI/MACD divergence between the left shoulder/head and the right shoulder (as in our Nifty example, RSI 72 → 68 → 55) is powerful confirmation that the reversal has teeth.
- **Moving averages:** if the neckline break coincides with price losing the 50-DMA and the 50-DMA rolling over toward the 200-DMA (an approaching death cross), conviction rises.
- **Fibonacci / structure:** a neckline that sits on a prior support shelf or a key Fib retracement of the whole prior advance strengthens the level.
- **Option-chain / OI (the India edge):** at the neckline break level, check the option chain. If the neckline (say 24,150) coincides with a strike that has heavy **Put OI unwinding** and rising **Call OI** just above the right shoulder, it confirms that option writers expect a cap and a fall. A shift in **max pain** downward and a rising **Put-Call Ratio that then rolls over** align with distribution. For the inverse pattern, heavy Put writing at the neckline support and Call OI unwinding above confirm the bottom. Watching where the largest OI walls sit tells you where the "real" neckline defence is.
- **Sector/breadth confluence:** a Nifty H&S is more trustworthy if Bank Nifty and the advance-decline line are simultaneously weakening. A topping pattern in the index while breadth is still expanding is suspect.

The highest-probability trade is a neckline break that occurs **with** bearish divergence, **on** rising volume, **at** a prior support that also shows Call-writer defence overhead. That is four independent tools pointing the same way.

## Pitfalls & false signals

1. **Pattern-hunting bias.** Traders "see" a Head & Shoulders in every three-bump squiggle. Insist on the full checklist: a prior trend to reverse, three clear peaks with the head highest, roughly symmetric shoulders, and a definable neckline. Without a preceding uptrend, a topping H&S is meaningless — there is no trend to reverse.

2. **The un-broken neckline.** The pattern is **not complete until the neckline closes are broken.** A perfect-looking H&S that never breaks its neckline is not a trade — many resolve upward instead. Do not pre-empt the break with a full position.

3. **Whipsaw breaks.** Price pokes below the neckline intrabar, triggers stops, and reverses. Defences: require a daily (or weekly) close, use a 1–3% or ATR filter, and prefer the retest entry, which naturally filters false breaks.

4. **Right shoulder exceeding the head.** If the right shoulder rallies above the head, the higher-high sequence resumes and the pattern is void. Some traders redraw a bigger pattern; better to step aside until structure clarifies.

5. **Up-sloping neckline traps.** A steeply up-sloping neckline can produce a "break" that is still above the head's reaction low — weak signal. Horizontal or down-sloping necklines are more reliable for tops.

6. **News override.** A powerful macro event — an RBI surprise, a Budget shock, a global risk-off — can invalidate any pattern in one gap. TA gives structure, not immunity from fundamentals. Size accordingly and respect stops.

7. **The measured move is a guide, not a guarantee.** Targets are hit often but not always; price can overshoot or fall short. Always book partials and manage rather than marrying the projected level.

Pros filter the pattern by demanding **confluence and confirmation**, trading the **retest** for better risk-reward, sizing so a single failed pattern is survivable, and — critically — respecting that a failed H&S (a close back above the right shoulder) is itself a strong reversal signal to be traded in the opposite direction.

## Interview-ready summary

"A Head & Shoulders is a reversal pattern: three peaks — left shoulder, higher head, lower right shoulder — with a neckline joining the two intervening lows. It works because the lower right-shoulder high is the market failing to make a new high, and the neckline break confirms the higher-lows are gone too, so the uptrend structure is fully broken. I confirm with a daily close beyond the neckline plus a filter, look for declining volume into the right shoulder and a volume surge on the break, and prize bearish momentum divergence. My target is the head-to-neckline distance projected from the break point. I prefer entering on the throwback retest of the neckline for a tight stop and superior reward-to-risk, place my stop beyond the right shoulder, and scale out at structure. The inverse version is the mirror at a bottom. In Indian markets I cross-check with the option chain — Call writing above the right shoulder and Put unwinding at the neckline confirm distribution. And I hold it honestly: it's a probability setup with a defined invalidation, not a certainty; a close back above the right shoulder means the pattern failed and I'm out, or I flip."
