# TA Philosophy & Dow Theory

## What it is & why it works

Technical analysis (TA) is the study of price and volume — the recorded footprints of every buyer and seller who has acted on a security — to estimate the *probability* of the next move. It is not fortune-telling, and it is not a claim that a chart "knows" the future. It is a disciplined framework for reading crowd behaviour and managing risk, built on a single premise: **all known information, and all opinion about that information, is already expressed in price.** Earnings, RBI policy, an FII sell programme, a promoter's confidence, retail euphoria — every one of these ends the same way, as an order that moves the tape. TA reads the *result* of the news rather than the news itself.

The reason it works — imperfectly, probabilistically — is human behaviour. Markets are made of people (and the algorithms people build), and people are not random. They anchor to round numbers (Nifty 25,000; Reliance ₹1,500). They chase what is going up and panic-sell what is falling. They remember the price where they were trapped and sell there to "get out even," creating resistance. They feel regret at missing a move and buy the first dip, creating support. These reflexes repeat across decades and across instruments because the wiring of fear and greed does not change. That repetition is why patterns recur — not because a triangle has magical properties, but because a triangle is a *picture of a recurring negotiation* between two crowds.

The intellectual honesty a serious analyst must carry: TA gives **edges, not certainties.** A textbook-perfect double bottom on Bank Nifty might have, say, a 60% hit rate with a favourable reward-to-risk. That is a superb edge — and it still means four out of ten times you are wrong. TA's real product is therefore not prediction but *asymmetry*: entering where you can define a small, precise risk against a larger, reasoned reward, and being right often enough that the maths compounds. Anyone who sells TA as certainty is selling a lie; anyone who dismisses TA as astrology has never sized a position by a stop.

Charles Dow — co-founder of Dow Jones and the *Wall Street Journal*, writing editorials around 1900–1902 — never used the term "Dow Theory." It was assembled from his essays by William Hamilton, Robert Rhea, and later E. George Schaefer and Richard Russell. Yet it remains the philosophical bedrock of everything that follows in this book: trend, confirmation, volume, and the idea that markets move in identifiable phases driven by identifiable crowds. Master Dow Theory and every later tool — moving averages, structure, Elliott waves, Wyckoff — reveals itself as a refinement of the same skeleton.

## The mechanics

Dow Theory rests on six tenets. Learn them as a connected argument, not a list.

**1. The averages discount everything.** Price already contains the sum of all hopes, fears, and facts. This is TA's founding axiom. In an Indian context, the Nifty 50 and Nifty Next 50, or Nifty and Bank Nifty read together, are the modern "averages."

**2. The market has three trends — nested like waves within tides.**

| Trend | Duration | Dow's analogy | Modern name | Who trades it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Months to years | The tide | Bull/bear market | Investors, positional |
| Secondary | Weeks to ~3 months | The wave | Correction/pullback | Swing traders |
| Minor | Days to ~3 weeks | The ripple | Noise/short swings | Intraday, scalpers |

The primary trend is the one that matters; secondary reactions retrace roughly one-third to two-thirds of the prior primary swing and are the traps that shake out weak hands; minor trends are largely noise and are where over-trading kills accounts.

**3. Primary trends have three phases.** This is the single most useful part of Dow Theory for a working trader.

- *Bull market — Accumulation:* the smart, patient money buys from disgusted sellers at the end of a bear market, when news is still terrible and sentiment is dead. Price grinds sideways in a base.
- *Bull market — Public participation (markup):* the trend becomes visible, technicians and trend-followers pile in, earnings improve, price rises steadily. This is the longest, most tradeable phase.
- *Bull market — Excess/distribution:* the public is euphoric, IPOs flood the market, taxi drivers give tips, valuations detach. The smart money that accumulated at the bottom now *distributes* (sells) into that euphoria.

The bear market mirrors this: distribution, panic/public participation on the downside, and despair (the bottoming accumulation of the next cycle). Recognising which phase you are in tells you whether to be aggressive or defensive.

**4. The averages must confirm each other.** Dow watched the Industrials *and* the Railroads. His logic was economic: if industry is truly booming, factories produce goods, and railroads must be busy carrying them — so both averages should make new highs together. A new high in one, unconfirmed by the other, was suspect. The modern Indian equivalent: **a Nifty breakout should be confirmed by Bank Nifty** (banks are the circulatory system of the economy and the market's largest weight), and ideally by breadth. A Nifty new high while Bank Nifty lags and advance-decline deteriorates is a *non-confirmation* — a warning that the move rests on a few heavyweights.

**5. Volume must confirm the trend.** Volume should expand in the direction of the primary trend and contract on counter-trend reactions. In a bull market, up-days should carry heavier volume than down-days. Rising price on shrinking volume is a red flag — advance without conviction.

**6. A trend is assumed in force until a clear reversal is signalled.** This is the Newtonian core: a trend in motion stays in motion. Do not anticipate reversals; wait for the market to *prove* the change by breaking structure. This tenet is the direct ancestor of "the trend is your friend" and of modern break-of-structure analysis.

The reversal signal itself, in Dow's terms, is defined by the **succession of peaks and troughs.** An uptrend is a series of higher highs and higher lows. The uptrend is intact while each rally exceeds the prior peak and each reaction holds above the prior trough. It is threatened when a rally *fails* to exceed the prior peak, and it is broken when the subsequent reaction *falls below* the prior trough. This peak-trough language is the seed of the entire "market structure" chapter that follows.

## Reading it — a worked India example

Consider the Nifty 50 through the COVID cycle and its aftermath — a textbook Dow sequence at large scale, which every serious Indian trader lived through.

*Distribution & the bear (Jan–Mar 2020).* Nifty topped near 12,430 in January 2020 with sentiment buoyant. As COVID fears spread, the index broke its rising structure: the February rally failed to make a new high, and price then sliced below prior troughs — Dow's classic reversal signal. Volume *expanded* violently on the down-days, confirming the new primary downtrend. The crash bottomed around **7,511 on 24 March 2020**, a fall of roughly 40% in weeks — the "despair" phase, news uniformly apocalyptic, retail liquidating.

*Accumulation (late March–May 2020).* At the low, the smart money began buying from panicked sellers. Note the psychology Dow described: you *cannot* accumulate at the bottom unless the news is terrible, because only terrible news produces sellers cheap enough to buy from. FIIs had sold, but domestic institutions and patient value buyers absorbed stock. Price carved a base off 7,511.

*Public participation / markup (mid-2020 through Oct 2021).* The trend turned. Nifty made a higher high above the initial bounce, then a higher low, then another higher high — the peak-trough sequence flipped bullish. Trend-followers and, eventually, a flood of new demat accounts (the "public") joined. The index ran from ~7,500 to about **18,600 by October 2021.** Crucially, **Bank Nifty confirmed** — financials, having been crushed hardest, recovered and made their own new highs, satisfying tenet 4. Up-volume dominated.

*Confirmation and non-confirmation in practice.* Through 2021 the two averages moved together — a healthy, confirmed bull. Contrast late 2021–2022: as global rates rose and FIIs sold heavily, Nifty's attempt to hold highs saw Bank Nifty and broader breadth wobble; the primary trend went into a long secondary correction (a "wave" against the "tide") that retraced a chunk of the advance before the primary uptrend reasserted itself into 2023–2024, printing fresh records above 22,000 and later 25,000.

Reading it phase-by-phase, a Dow-literate trader in 2020 would have: (a) respected the confirmed downtrend and not "caught the knife" in February–March, (b) watched for the *structural* reversal — a higher high after a higher low off 7,511 — before turning bullish, (c) ridden the confirmed markup with the primary trend, and (d) grown cautious only when confirmation and volume began to fail, not merely because the index "felt high."

## Trading it

Dow Theory is a *positional and swing* framework more than an intraday one, but its logic scales down. Here is how to trade the philosophy concretely.

**Entry.** Do not anticipate — confirm. Buy when the primary trend is proven up: a fresh higher high that closes above the prior peak, ideally after a higher low held. On the Nifty 7,511 example, the actionable signal was the *first higher high confirmed by a preceding higher low*, not the exact bottom tick.

**Stop.** Your invalidation is structural: place the stop below the most recent higher low (the prior trough). If price falls below that trough, tenet 6 says the trend is no longer proven up and you must be out. This gives a *defined, small, logical* risk — the essence of TA's asymmetry. Never use a random percentage; use the structure the market itself drew.

**Target / management.** Because primary trends run months, the target is not a fixed number but *the trend's own continuation*. Trail the stop under each successive higher low as the uptrend prints new peaks and troughs. You exit not on a price target but when the structure breaks — a lower high followed by a break below the prior low.

**Scenarios.**

- *Trend-continuation (base case):* Nifty makes HH, pulls back into a secondary reaction that holds above the prior HL on lighter volume, then resumes. Add on the resumption; trail the stop up.
- *Secondary-correction shakeout:* a sharp two-to-eight-week drop retraces ~50% of the last primary swing but does *not* break the prior major trough. Weak hands sell; the Dow trader recognises a "wave against the tide" and holds, because the structural low is intact.
- *Genuine reversal:* the rally fails to make a new high (lower high) *and* the next reaction breaks the prior trough, on expanding volume, ideally unconfirmed/deteriorating breadth. Now exit longs and consider the new primary downtrend.

Position sizing follows from the stop: risk a fixed fraction (say 1%) of capital, and size = risk ÷ (entry − stop). A wide structural stop means a smaller position; a tight one allows more — the market dictates size, not your conviction.

## Confluence

Dow Theory is the *skeleton*; confluence adds the muscle. Combine it with:

- **Volume (tenet 5 made concrete):** demand new highs on rising volume and count corrections as healthy only when volume dries up. A breakout to a new primary high on falling volume is a Dow non-confirmation waiting to become a failure.
- **Breadth / market internals:** the modern extension of "averages confirming each other." Watch the Nifty advance-decline line, the percentage of stocks above their 200-DMA, and whether Bank Nifty and Nifty Next 50 confirm the headline index. A Nifty high carried by three heavyweights while breadth rolls over is precisely Dow's warning.
- **Inter-market confirmation:** Nifty ↔ Bank Nifty is the cleanest Indian pair. In a truly healthy bull, financials lead or at least confirm; when Bank Nifty diverges bearishly at a Nifty high, respect it.
- **Option-chain / OI (the 2026 refinement Dow never had):** at a suspected distribution top, watch for aggressive call writing building at and above the spot strike, a falling Put-Call Ratio failing to support price, and unwinding of longs in Nifty/Bank Nifty futures (falling open interest on up-moves = short-covering rally, not fresh buying — a weak, un-Dow-like advance). Conversely, accumulation phases often show heavy put writing establishing a floor. Rising price on rising futures OI is the option-chain analogue of Dow's "volume confirms trend."
- **Moving-average trend filter:** the 200-DMA is the mechanical proxy for Dow's primary trend. Trade longs with the primary (above a rising 200-DMA), shorts against it — a simple filter that keeps you on the right side of the tide.

The higher-probability setup is a confluence stack: primary uptrend intact (structure) + Bank Nifty confirming (inter-market) + breakout on rising volume/futures OI (tenets 5 & option-chain) + healthy breadth. When all agree, you have a genuine edge; when they disagree, you have a warning.

## Pitfalls & false signals

**It is a lagging, philosophical framework — not a timing tool.** Dow Theory confirms a trend change only *after* it is well underway; you will never catch the exact top or bottom, and trying to will bankrupt you. Its critics rightly note that by the time both averages confirm, a large part of the move is gone. Accept this: Dow keeps you on the right side of the *big* move, not on the perfect entry.

**Whipsaws in sideways markets.** In a range-bound, trendless tape (long stretches of a consolidating Nifty), the peak-trough logic generates false reversal signals — a "lower low" that immediately reverses. Filter with volume, breadth, and by demanding that structural breaks *close* beyond the level, not merely wick through it.

**Subjectivity of "the trend."** Two analysts can define the relevant peaks and troughs differently and reach opposite conclusions. Discipline this by fixing your timeframe first (are you trading the tide, the wave, or the ripple?) and only marking *significant* swing points, not every squiggle.

**Ignoring non-confirmation because the index is rising.** The most expensive Dow error: a heavyweight-driven Nifty makes new highs while Bank Nifty, breadth, and volume all deteriorate, and traders cheer the number. Pros treat that as distribution in progress and reduce risk into strength — exactly when it feels wrong to do so.

**Over-fitting the phase model.** Not every top is textbook distribution; sometimes trends end fast and ugly. Use the phase model as a lens, not a straitjacket, and always defer to price and structure over your narrative.

## Interview-ready summary

Technical analysis rests on one axiom — price discounts everything — and works because markets are made of people whose fear and greed repeat, making behaviour, and therefore patterns, recurrent. TA's honest product is *probabilistic edge and defined risk*, not certainty. Dow Theory is its philosophical foundation, and I explain it in six tenets: the averages discount everything; markets move in three nested trends (primary tide, secondary wave, minor ripple); primary bull trends run through accumulation, public participation, and distribution (bears mirror this into despair); the averages must confirm each other (in India, Nifty and Bank Nifty); volume must confirm the trend; and a trend is assumed intact until price breaks its succession of higher highs and higher lows. The 2020 Nifty cycle — distribution at 12,430, panic to 7,511, accumulation, then a confirmed markup to 18,600 with Bank Nifty confirming and up-volume dominating — is a clean, lived illustration. In practice I trade *with* the confirmed primary trend, enter on structural confirmation rather than anticipation, stop below the last higher low, trail with structure, and treat non-confirmation between the averages, failing volume, weakening breadth, and unwinding futures OI as my earliest warnings. Dow lags by design; it keeps me on the right side of the big move rather than at the perfect tick — and that, sized properly, is enough.
