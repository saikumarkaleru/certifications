# Morning Star, Evening Star & Abandoned Baby

## What it is & why it works

The Morning Star and Evening Star are **three-candle reversal patterns** — the most reliable of the classical candlestick reversals precisely because they require three sessions to build, not one or two. They tell a complete three-act story: **trend, indecision, reversal.** The Morning Star marks a bottom (the "star" that appears before dawn, before price rises); the Evening Star marks a top (the star before nightfall, before price falls). The **Abandoned Baby** is the rarest, most powerful variant of each, where the middle candle is a doji *gapped away* from both neighbours on either side — an "island" of indecision left stranded.

Why does three candles beat two? Because a reversal is not an event, it is a *process*: exhaustion of the old trend, a pause where neither side can push, then a decisive commitment by the new side. A two-candle engulfing compresses this into a single bar and is therefore quicker but noisier. The star pattern insists you *watch the transfer of control happen in stages*, which filters out a lot of false reversals.

Walk through the **Morning Star** as three acts:
- **Candle 1 (the trend):** a long red body. The downtrend is intact and, if anything, accelerating — capitulation, bad news, sellers fully in control, everyone bearish.
- **Candle 2 (the star):** a *small* body, often gapping down from candle 1. This is the emotional low. The small range says the selling has run out of force — sellers pushed to a new low but couldn't expand the range, and buyers began to nibble. It can be red or green; colour is secondary. Its smallness is the message: **equilibrium at the extreme.**
- **Candle 3 (the reversal):** a long green body that closes *well into* candle 1's body (ideally past its midpoint). Buyers have now seized control decisively. The traders who sold candle 1 and the shorts who piled in are trapped, and their covering fuels the advance.

The **Evening Star** is the exact mirror at a top: long green trend candle, a small-bodied star (often gapping up — the euphoric blow-off), then a long red candle plunging back into the first body. It is one of the most trustworthy topping signals in the book, especially on higher timeframes.

The behavioural engine is **exhaustion → balance → commitment**. The star is the fulcrum where the crowd's conviction is maximal in the old direction but momentum has quietly died. In India this is vivid around events — a stock gaps up on results into an evening star and rolls over as the "buy the rumour, sell the news" crowd exits; or the Nifty capitulates on a global scare, prints a doji star, and reverses as value buyers and short-coverers step in.

## The mechanics

### Morning Star (bullish, at a bottom)
| Candle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Long **red** body; downtrend in progress |
| 2 (star) | **Small** body (any colour); ideally gaps *down* from candle 1's body; a doji here = **Morning Doji Star** (stronger) |
| 3 | Long **green** body that closes **above the midpoint of candle 1's body** (deeper penetration = stronger) |
| Confirmation | Candle 3 volume ≥ candles 1–2; close near candle 3's high |

### Evening Star (bearish, at a top)
| Candle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Long **green** body; uptrend in progress |
| 2 (star) | **Small** body; ideally gaps *up*; doji = **Evening Doji Star** (stronger) |
| 3 | Long **red** body closing **below the midpoint of candle 1's body** |
| Confirmation | Volume expansion on candle 3; close near candle 3's low |

The **midpoint penetration rule** is the single most important mechanic. A "star" whose third candle only nibbles a little into the first body is weak; one that closes past the midpoint (or fully engulfs it) is a strong reversal. The deeper candle 3 eats into candle 1, the more the pattern resembles an engulfing bolted onto a pause — the best of both.

### Abandoned Baby (the rare, high-conviction variant)
| Feature | Bullish Abandoned Baby | Bearish Abandoned Baby |
|---|---|---|
| Candle 1 | Long red (downtrend) | Long green (uptrend) |
| Candle 2 | **Doji** that **gaps down** — its *entire range* (including wicks) is below candle 1's range | **Doji** that **gaps up** — its entire range is above candle 1's range |
| Candle 3 | Gaps **up** away from the doji; long green | Gaps **down** away from the doji; long red |
| Key | Doji is "abandoned" — isolated by gaps on **both** sides | Same, at a top |

The defining feature of the Abandoned Baby is the **double gap that isolates the doji**, including the shadows — a true island reversal in a single candle. It is the candlestick cousin of the Western "island reversal." It is genuinely rare in liquid Indian indices during regular hours because continuous trading tends to fill gaps, but it shows up in **single stocks around gap-up/gap-down events** (results, block deals, sector news) and on **daily charts where the overnight gap does the isolating**.

### Timeframe & the gap caveat for India
On **daily** and **weekly** charts these patterns are most reliable. Indian cash equities and index futures trade continuously through the session, so intraday "gaps" between candles rarely happen — meaning the *classic gapped star* mostly appears as **overnight gaps on the daily chart**. Intraday (5/15-min), the star's middle candle usually just *touches* rather than gaps, which is acceptable; purists relax the gap requirement intraday and rely on the small-body + midpoint-penetration logic.

## Reading it — a worked India example

**Evening Star on Nifty 50 (daily), a topping sequence.**

**Phase 1 — the trend (candle 1).** Nifty has run from 24,100 to **25,300** over three weeks, a strong, well-liked uptrend. The session in focus is a long green candle: opens 25,050, closes **25,280** near its high — maximum bullishness, headlines calling for "25,500 next."

**Phase 2 — the star (candle 2).** The next morning, buoyed by a strong US close, Nifty **gaps up** and opens 25,340, tags a new high at 25,388… and then goes nowhere. It closes at **25,355** — a tiny body sitting *above* candle 1, essentially a doji-like star. The range is small; the gap-up euphoria met immediate supply. This is the fulcrum: the bulls made their new high but could not expand the range. Under the surface, in the option chain, **Call writers begin adding OI at 25,400 and 25,500**, and the highest Call OI wall firms up at 25,400 — smart money is selling upside.

**Phase 3 — the reversal (candle 3).** The following session opens 25,330 and sellers take command all day; it closes at **25,110** — a long red body that closes *below the midpoint of candle 1's body* (midpoint ≈ 25,165). Volume is elevated; Nifty futures see fresh short additions (OI up, price down). The three candles together — long green, small star above it, long red plunging back in — are a textbook **Evening Star**, and because the star gapped up and the whole thing sits at a Call-OI ceiling, it is a high-conviction top.

**What follows.** Nifty slides to 24,800 within four sessions, then to **24,450**, giving back the last leg of the rally. The trader who read the Evening Star at 25,110 either exited longs, went short the future, or bought puts near the close of candle 3.

Now a quick **Morning Star on a stock (Infosys, daily)** for the bullish mirror. Infosys falls from ₹1,560 to a support shelf near **₹1,455** on IT-sector weakness. Candle 1: long red, close ₹1,458. Candle 2: gaps down, opens ₹1,449, prints a low of ₹1,442, but closes ₹1,452 — a small body, a doji star at the extreme (a **Morning Doji Star**). Candle 3: opens ₹1,456 and rallies all day to close ₹1,498 — past the midpoint of candle 1's body — on above-average volume. The Morning Star signals the bottom; Infosys grinds back to ₹1,540 over two weeks.

## Trading it

### Evening Star short (Nifty example)
- **Entry trigger:** (a) *Aggressive:* short the Nifty future / buy a put on the close of candle 3 at ~25,110. (b) *Conservative:* sell-stop below candle 3's low (25,090) to require follow-through. Options traders favour a **bear put spread** (buy 25,100 put, sell 24,700 put) to define risk and cheapen the trade against theta.
- **Stop-loss:** Above the *high of the star* (candle 2's high, 25,388) — say **25,420**. That high is the pattern's invalidation; a close above it means the euphoria was real and the top failed. Risk ≈ 300 points from a 25,110 entry.
- **Target / measured move:** First target the last consolidation / demand shelf at **24,800** (≈310 pts, ~1R), then the base of the prior leg at **24,450** (≈660 pts, >2R). A measured-move estimate projects the height of the reversal leg down from the breakdown. Management: book a third at 24,800, trail the rest under lower swing highs; move stop to breakeven after a close below 25,000.

### Morning Star long (Infosys example)
- **Entry:** buy on candle-3 close (₹1,498) or on a buy-stop above candle-3 high; conservative traders wait for a higher close the next day.
- **Stop:** below the *low of the star* — ₹1,442, so a stop at **₹1,435**. Risk ≈ ₹63.
- **Target:** prior swing high ₹1,540 (first), then ₹1,560. Trail under the rising 20-DMA.

### Abandoned Baby
Trade it like the star but with **tighter conviction and a wider stop** (the double gap makes the pattern larger). Entry on candle-3 confirmation; stop beyond the isolated doji's extreme. Because it is rare and strong, it often precedes a sharper move — but beware the gap being *filled*: if price closes back into the gap against you, the island has been "re-connected" and the signal is void.

### Scenarios
1. **Clean reversal:** trail to measured-move targets.
2. **Star, then failure:** candle 3 forms but the *next* candle reclaims the star's high (evening star) — exit; the top failed, momentum resumed.
3. **Shallow candle 3:** if candle 3 doesn't reach candle 1's midpoint, treat the pattern as *weak* — either skip it or wait for one more confirming candle.

## Confluence

Star patterns are strong on their own but become premium setups when stacked:

- **Location:** Evening Stars at resistance / prior highs / round numbers (Nifty 25,000, 25,500) and Morning Stars at support / prior lows / the 200-DMA are the ones to trust.
- **Fibonacci & measured moves:** an Evening Star at the 1.618 extension of a leg, or a Morning Star at the 61.8% retracement of a larger uptrend, adds a structural reason.
- **RSI/MACD divergence:** an Evening Star with a *bearish RSI divergence* (price makes a higher high on candle 2, RSI a lower high) is one of the most reliable tops you can find. Same for Morning Star + bullish divergence at a bottom.
- **Volume:** candle 3 should carry conviction — expanding volume validates the transfer of control.
- **Option-chain / OI (the India edge):**
  - *Evening Star:* most powerful when candle 2's new high tags a strike carrying the **highest Call OI** (a supply ceiling) and you see **Call writers adding OI** as price stalls, plus **fresh futures shorts** (OI up, price down) on candle 3. The option writers' positioning corroborates the candle.
  - *Morning Star:* strongest at a strike with the **highest Put OI** (a demand floor). A Morning Star followed by **short-covering** (futures OI falling as price rises) is the cleanest version — trapped shorts do the lifting.
  - *PCR / IV:* a spike in IV into candle 2 (fear/greed peak) that then deflates on candle 3 supports the reversal read; falling IV after a Morning Star is classic post-capitulation relief.

The discipline: **three-candle pattern + a real level + a divergence or OI-wall + volume.** That combination turns a good pattern into a high-probability trade.

## Pitfalls & false signals

1. **The midpoint rule ignored.** A "star" whose third candle barely dents the first body is not a real star — it's a pause. Weak candle 3 = weak signal. Insist on midpoint penetration (deeper is better).

2. **Star in a choppy range.** Like all reversals, these need a genuine prior trend. In a sideways Nifty, evening/morning "stars" print at range edges and mostly just oscillate — location and trend context are mandatory.

3. **Chasing gap euphoria without candle 3.** An evening star's candle 2 gap-up can *keep going* the next day. Never short on the star alone; wait for candle 3 to close red past the midpoint.

4. **Abandoned Baby gap-fill.** The island's power evaporates if the gap is filled. Indian intraday gaps fill often; don't over-trust an intraday "abandoned baby."

5. **Event risk overriding the pattern.** A morning star the day before a results announcement or RBI policy can be blown away by the event. Respect the calendar; size down or wait.

6. **Higher-timeframe conflict.** A daily evening star inside a strong weekly uptrend is frequently just a pullback for the bulls, not a major top. Always check the frame above and size accordingly.

**How pros filter:** demand the midpoint penetration and closing strength on candle 3; require a real support/resistance location; add a divergence or an OI-wall confirmation; expand volume on the reversal candle; and align (or consciously fade) the higher timeframe. They set the stop at the star's extreme — the one price that logically invalidates the whole three-act story — and size the position to that distance.

## Interview-ready summary

"Morning and Evening Stars are three-candle reversal patterns that tell a story of *trend → indecision → reversal*. A **Morning Star** (bottom) is a long red candle, then a small-bodied 'star' (often gapping down — the doji version is stronger), then a long green candle that closes past the midpoint of the first body, marking demand seizing control. An **Evening Star** is the mirror at a top: long green, small star (often gapping up in euphoria), then a long red candle plunging back below the first body's midpoint. The **Abandoned Baby** is the rare, most powerful variant — the middle doji is isolated by gaps on *both* sides, including its wicks: a true island reversal. The critical mechanic is *midpoint penetration by the third candle* — the deeper, the stronger — plus volume confirmation. You enter on the third candle's close (or a stop beyond it), place the stop at the star's extreme, and target the prior structure or a measured move. In Indian F&O they're most reliable when the star's high sits at the heaviest Call-OI strike (evening star) or its low at the heaviest Put-OI strike (morning star), confirmed by fresh shorts or short-covering in the futures OI. Three candles make them slower but more trustworthy than a two-candle engulfing."
