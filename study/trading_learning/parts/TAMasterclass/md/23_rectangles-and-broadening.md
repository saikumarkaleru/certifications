# Rectangles & Broadening Formations

## What it is & why it works

Rectangles and broadening formations are two sides of the same coin — both describe price oscillating between two boundaries — but they are geometric and psychological opposites, and treating them the same is a costly mistake.

A **rectangle** (also called a trading range, box, or consolidation) forms when price bounces repeatedly between a roughly **horizontal resistance** and a roughly **horizontal support**, producing parallel, flat boundaries. The market is in equilibrium: at the top, sellers reliably step in; at the bottom, buyers reliably step in. Nothing is expanding — the range is *stable*. A rectangle represents a **truce**, a pause where supply and demand are momentarily balanced while the market digests a prior move or accumulates/distributes before the next one. Volatility is *contracting or steady*, and the boundaries are being *defended*.

A **broadening formation** (or megaphone) is the inverse: price makes **higher highs *and* lower lows**, so the two boundaries *diverge* — an expanding, megaphone shape opening to the right. Each swing overshoots the previous one in both directions. This is the fingerprint of **rising volatility, emotional extremes, and loss of control**. Broadening tops especially form after big rallies when the crowd is euphoric and jumpy — every dip gets bought aggressively (new higher high) and every rip gets sold in panic (new lower low), with the amplitude *growing*. It is instability, not equilibrium.

Why do they work as trading tools?

- The **rectangle** works because ranges are self-reinforcing: as long as participants believe support and resistance will hold, they fade the edges (buy support, sell resistance), which *keeps* the range intact — until one edge breaks on real conviction, at which point the trapped fade-traders fuel a directional move. Rectangles give you two clean, repeatable edges to trade *and* a well-defined breakout to trade.
- The **broadening formation** works as a *warning*. Expanding swings mean the market has lost its equilibrium and is being driven by emotion; they frequently precede trend reversals or violent, hard-to-hold moves. They're brutally difficult to trade cleanly (which is the point — recognise them so you *reduce risk* rather than get chopped), but a broadening top after a mature bull run is a genuine distribution signal.

In Indian markets, rectangles are everywhere: Nifty coiling in a 300-point box for two weeks ahead of a Budget or an RBI policy; a stock like ITC ranging between ₹440 and ₹470 for months as a defensive base. Broadening formations show up around euphoric blow-off tops and around major event uncertainty — the pre-election chop of 2024, or a stock whipsawing violently on conflicting news flow.

## The mechanics — construction, rules and settings

**Rectangle construction.** You need **at least two touches on each boundary** — two swing highs defining a horizontal resistance and two swing lows defining a horizontal support — ideally four to six total reaction points. The boundaries needn't be to-the-tick perfect; treat them as *zones* a few points wide. The more touches, the more significant the range, but also the more likely the eventual break is decisive.

| Feature | Rectangle | Broadening formation |
|---|---|---|
| Upper boundary | Flat (horizontal) | Rising (higher highs) |
| Lower boundary | Flat (horizontal) | Falling (lower lows) |
| Shape | Parallel box | Diverging megaphone |
| Volatility | Steady / contracting | Expanding |
| Psychology | Equilibrium, balance | Emotion, instability |
| Volume | Often declining in range | Often erratic / rising |
| Best trade | Range-fade + breakout | Reduce risk; fade extremes cautiously |
| Reliability | High (clean edges) | Low (whipsaw-prone) |

**Volume in a rectangle.** Classic behaviour: volume *dries up* as the range matures (participants lose interest in the chop), then *surges* on the genuine breakout. A breakout on a volume spike, especially with a wide-range candle closing well beyond the boundary, is trustworthy; a quiet drift through the edge is suspect.

**Rectangle breakout target — measured move.** Measure the **height of the box** (resistance − support) and project it from the breakout point in the break direction. A ₹300-tall Nifty box that breaks up at 24,500 projects to 24,800. Rectangles are *neutral* patterns — they can break either way — so you let the break tell you the direction rather than predicting it, though the prior trend gives a bias (a rectangle within an uptrend more often breaks up, acting as continuation).

**Broadening construction.** You need the diverging structure: at least two ascending highs and two descending lows, forming five reference points in the classic "five-point broadening top" (high–low–higher high–lower low–higher high, then break). Because the boundaries diverge, there is **no tidy measured-move target** — the pattern is about volatility and reversal risk, not a clean projection. The most tradeable version is the **five-point pattern**, where a failure to make a *sixth* new extreme signals the reversal.

**Timeframe and duration.** Both are scale-invariant. Rectangles on the daily can last weeks to months (great for positional traders and for selling option premium); on the 5-min they last an hour. Broadening formations tend to be shorter-lived and are more common near tops than bottoms.

**Settings for the trader.** For range trading, oscillators tuned to the range help: **RSI** and **Stochastics** oscillate reliably between overbought at resistance and oversold at support *while the range holds* — they're excellent range tools and terrible trend tools, so they suit rectangles perfectly. **Bollinger Bands** contracting (a "squeeze") often overlays a maturing rectangle and warns a breakout is near; Bands *expanding* violently overlays a broadening formation.

## Reading it — a worked India example, phase by phase

**Rectangle on Nifty (daily), phase by phase.**

**Phase 1 — the range forms.** After a rally, Nifty stalls and starts oscillating. Over three weeks it prints:

- Resistance touches: 24,780, 24,810, 24,795 → a resistance *zone* around **24,800**.
- Support touches: 24,510, 24,490, 24,520 → a support *zone* around **24,500**.

Box height ≈ **300 points**. Volume tapers through the range; daily RSI swings from ~65 at the top to ~38 at the bottom without reaching true extremes — the market is balanced, waiting (in reality, waiting on an upcoming RBI policy).

**Phase 2 — the range-fade trades (optional income while you wait).** A disciplined trader can fade the edges: buy near 24,520 with a stop below 24,470, sell near 24,780 with a stop above 24,830. Each fade offers ~250 points of range for ~60 points of risk. Two or three of these can be booked *while* the market decides — but you must respect that the *first* failed fade is often the breakout, so stops are sacred.

**Phase 3 — the option-chain context.** As the range persists, the monthly option chain shows a fat **straddle premium collapsing** (implied volatility bleeding out as the market coils) and heavy OI at both 24,500 (puts) and 24,800 (calls) — participants are *selling both edges*, effectively betting the range holds. **Max pain** sits near 24,650, mid-range. This is a signature range environment; premium sellers are in control until something breaks.

**Phase 4 — the breakout.** RBI surprises with a dovish tilt. Nifty gaps and a daily candle closes at **24,920**, decisively above the 24,800 zone, on volume ~1.6× the 20-day average — a genuine breakout. The 24,800 call writers are now trapped short and scramble to cover, adding fuel.

**Phase 5 — the measured move.** Target = breakout (24,800) + box height (300) = **25,100**, reached within four sessions. A pullback to *retest* 24,800 (now support) holds cleanly on the way — the classic breakout-retest that confirms the box has flipped from resistance to support.

**Broadening formation on a euphoric midcap (weekly).** Imagine a hot midcap that's tripled: it prints a high at ₹900, dips to ₹780, spikes to a *higher* high ₹960, craters to a *lower* low ₹720, then rockets to ₹1,010. Each swing is bigger than the last — a megaphone. Volume is erratic and rising; RSI is diverging (lower highs vs price's higher highs). When the stock then *fails* to make a sixth higher high — topping at ₹990 and rolling over through the lower rising line — the broadening top confirms a reversal, and the stock unwinds hard because the late, emotional buyers are all offside.

## Trading it — entries, stops, targets, management

**Rectangle — two distinct playbooks:**

*1. Range-fade (while the box holds):*
- **Entry:** buy the support zone / sell the resistance zone, ideally on a *reversal candle* (hammer at support, shooting star at resistance) or an oscillator turn (Stochastic curling up from oversold at support).
- **Stop:** just beyond the zone — below support for longs, above resistance for shorts. Tight, because a clean break beyond the zone *invalidates* the fade.
- **Target:** the opposite edge (or two-thirds of the way, to be safe).
- **Management:** never average into a losing fade at the edge — that's how ranges bankrupt fade-traders when they finally break.

*2. Breakout (when the box gives way):*
- **Entry:** on a *close* beyond the boundary on expanding volume, or — safer — on the **retest** of the broken boundary holding as new support/resistance.
- **Stop:** back inside the box, beyond the boundary (e.g., for the Nifty long, stop below 24,760).
- **Target:** measured move = box height projected from the break (25,100 above).
- **Management:** trail behind swing points; take partial at the measured-move target, let a runner ride if the breakout has trend character.

**Broadening formation — trade defensively:**
- The honest primary use is **risk reduction**: recognise the megaphone, tighten stops, cut size, and *don't* trade the middle (the chop will shred you).
- The tradeable edge is the **five-point reversal**: after the fifth point (a new extreme that *fails* to extend), enter *against* the last swing when price breaks the opposite rising/falling line. For a broadening *top*: short the failure of the higher high, stop above that failed high, target the pattern's earlier lows or a full retrace of the prior rally.
- Alternatively, **fade the extremes** — but only with a reversal-candle trigger and a hard stop, accepting a lower hit-rate. Given expanding volatility, position size *smaller* than in a rectangle for the same rupee risk.
- **Never** trade breakouts *out of* a broadening formation the way you would a rectangle — the expanding boundaries mean a "breakout" is often just the next overshoot that reverses.

## Confluence — stacking the odds

**For rectangles:**
- **Volume:** dry-up in the range, surge on the break. Non-negotiable for trusting a breakout. For NSE stocks, check delivery volume on the breakout day.
- **Oscillators:** RSI/Stochastic reaching overbought at resistance and oversold at support *confirms* the range is intact for fading; a **failure to reach oversold** on a dip to support (RSI holding higher) warns the box is about to break *up* — a subtle, powerful tell.
- **Bollinger squeeze:** bands pinching over a maturing rectangle flags an imminent breakout; the first band expansion often coincides with the real break.
- **Option chain / OI:** heavy OI stacked at both edges with collapsing IV = premium sellers expect the range to hold; watch for **OI unwinding at one edge** as the early sign that side will give way. A breakout that traps a fat wall of writers accelerates fastest.
- **Prior trend & higher timeframe:** a rectangle within an uptrend is a *continuation* base — bias the breakout upward. Alignment with the higher-timeframe trend raises breakout odds.

**For broadening formations:**
- **Momentum divergence:** almost always present at a broadening top — price higher highs, RSI/MACD lower highs. This is the core confirmer of the reversal.
- **Volume:** erratic and often climactic on the final push — a blow-off spike into the fifth point that then can't be sustained.
- **Breadth (for indices):** a broadening Nifty top with *deteriorating* advance-decline and fewer stocks above their 200-DMA is a classic distribution signature.
- **Sentiment / IV:** rising India VIX alongside the expanding swings confirms the fear-greed instability the pattern embodies.

The unifying idea: **rectangles are traded *with* the expectation of order** (edges hold, then a clean break), so confluence is about confirming the break is real. **Broadening formations are traded *with* respect for disorder** — confluence is about confirming a *reversal* and controlling risk, never about a tidy target.

## Pitfalls & false signals

- **Rectangle false breakouts ("bull/bear traps").** The single biggest range hazard: price closes just beyond the edge, sucks in breakout traders, then snaps back inside. Filters: demand a *close* beyond the zone (not a wick), require a volume surge, and strongly prefer the **retest** entry. A break that can't hold the boundary on the retest is a trap — reverse or stand aside.
- **Fading the edge that's actually breaking.** Range-faders die on the day the range ends. The discipline: a tight stop just beyond the zone, and *never* adding to a losing fade. The first fade that fails is your signal the regime changed.
- **Treating a broadening formation like a rectangle.** Fatal. The diverging boundaries mean there is no reliable "support" or "resistance" to lean on — each level gets overshot. Range-fading a megaphone with a rectangle mindset gets you run over on the next larger swing.
- **Broadening whipsaw.** These patterns are *designed* by market emotion to trap both bulls and bears. If you must trade them, trade *smaller*, wait for the five-point failure, and accept a lower win-rate. Many pros simply **stand aside** in a clear megaphone — recognising it to *avoid* it is a valid, profitable use.
- **No target on broadening.** Don't invent a measured move for a diverging pattern; there isn't a dependable one. Manage by structure (prior swings) and trail.
- **Event risk.** Both patterns dissolve around scheduled shocks — Budget, RBI, Fed, elections, big earnings. A rectangle *into* an event often breaks *on* the event (which is fine if you're positioned for the break), but pre-event fades are dangerous. Broadening formations frequently *are* the market's expression of event uncertainty; expect them to resolve violently when the news lands.
- **Over-fitting boundaries.** On noisy Indian intraday charts it's easy to draw a box or a megaphone that isn't really there. Require the touches, treat boundaries as zones, and if the structure needs constant redrawing, it isn't a pattern.

## Interview-ready summary

"Rectangles and broadening formations are opposites. A **rectangle** is price oscillating between a *flat* support and a *flat* resistance — parallel boundaries, an equilibrium where buyers defend the bottom and sellers defend the top. It's a high-quality pattern: I can fade the edges while it holds, using RSI or Stochastics which oscillate beautifully in a range, and I can trade the breakout — a *close* beyond the boundary on a volume surge, targeting the box height projected from the break, ideally entering on the retest. On Nifty I read the option chain: fat OI at both edges with collapsing IV means premium sellers expect the range to hold, and OI unwinding at one edge warns that side will break.

A **broadening formation** or megaphone is the inverse — *higher highs and lower lows*, diverging boundaries, expanding volatility. It's the fingerprint of emotion and instability, and it usually appears at euphoric tops as a distribution warning, typically with bearish momentum divergence. There's no clean measured-move target, and it's whipsaw-prone, so I mostly use it to *reduce risk* — cut size, tighten stops, avoid the middle — and if I trade it, I wait for the five-point failure and short the failed higher high. The one-line distinction: a rectangle is *balance you can lean on*; a broadening formation is *disorder you must respect*. And as always, these are probabilities managed with defined stops, not certainties."
