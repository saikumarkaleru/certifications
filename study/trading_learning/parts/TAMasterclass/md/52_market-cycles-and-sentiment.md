# Market Cycles & Sentiment

## What it is & why it works

Markets do not move in straight lines; they breathe. Price expands and contracts, trends and ranges, in a rhythm driven not by fundamentals alone but by the collective emotional state of participants — the swing between greed and fear, hope and capitulation. **Market-cycle analysis** is the study of that rhythm at its highest level: the recurring four-stage arc every liquid market travels — accumulation, markup, distribution, markdown — and the **sentiment** (crowd psychology) that both drives and betrays each stage. Where a chart pattern is a paragraph and a candle a word, the cycle is the whole story. It is the frame that tells you *which chapter you're in* and therefore which tactics apply.

Why does it work? Because human psychology is the one market input that has not changed in three hundred years. The same emotions that inflated tulips, railways, dot-coms, and crypto inflate every Indian bull run and deflate every bear market. Fear and greed are hard-wired, they are contagious, and they push the crowd to do the wrong thing at the extremes — to buy most aggressively at the top (when risk is highest) and to sell in panic at the bottom (when risk is lowest). Because the crowd is largest and most confident precisely when it is most wrong, **sentiment is a contrarian tool at extremes**. That is the deep logic behind "be fearful when others are greedy." Cycle analysis operationalises Wyckoff's accumulation/distribution at the market-wide level and marries it to measurable sentiment.

For an Indian trader, cycle-awareness is the difference between strategies that work and strategies that quietly bankrupt you. Trend-following and buy-the-dip mint money in markup (2020–2021, 2023–2024 legs of Nifty) and destroy accounts in markdown. Mean-reversion and range-selling thrive in accumulation/distribution and get run over in a trending markup. Knowing the stage tells you whether to press trends or fade them, whether to be aggressive or defensive, whether the "cheap" stock is a bargain or a falling knife. Sentiment and cycle analysis is regime detection — the meta-skill that governs every other tool in this book.

Honesty check: cycles are real but *not periodic clockwork*. They vary in length and amplitude; you cannot set a calendar by them. The value is in *stage identification and probabilistic tilt*, not precise timing.

## The mechanics

**The four stages** (the market-wide analogue of Wyckoff):

| Stage | Price behaviour | Sentiment | Smart money | Right tactics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Accumulation | Sideways base after a decline; flat 200-DMA | Despair, apathy, disbelief | Buying quietly | Build longs on tests; patience |
| 2. Markup | Higher highs/lows; rising MAs | Hope → optimism → greed | Holding, adding | Trend-follow, buy dips |
| 3. Distribution | Choppy ceiling near highs | Euphoria, "new paradigm" | Selling into strength | Take profit, tighten stops, hedge |
| 4. Markdown | Lower highs/lows; falling MAs | Anxiety → fear → capitulation | Sold; waiting | Short rallies, cash, protect capital |

The **sentiment cycle** overlays a well-known emotional curve: *disbelief → hope → optimism → belief → thrill → euphoria (top) → complacency → anxiety → denial → fear → desperation → panic → capitulation → despondency (bottom) → depression → hope*. The two emotional inflection points that matter most are **euphoria** (maximum financial risk, minimum perceived risk — the top) and **capitulation/despondency** (maximum opportunity, minimum perceived opportunity — the bottom).

**Measurable sentiment tools (the ones that matter in India):**

| Tool | What it reads | Contrarian signal |
|---|---|---|
| India VIX | Expected 30-day Nifty volatility ("fear gauge") | Extreme lows (<11–12) = complacency/top risk; spikes (>25–30) = panic/bottom zone |
| Put-Call Ratio (PCR, OI) | Puts vs calls outstanding | Very high PCR (>1.5) = excess fear (contrarian bullish); very low (<0.7) = complacency |
| Advance-Decline / breadth | % of stocks participating | Narrowing breadth in markup = late-cycle distribution warning |
| % stocks above 200-DMA | Market-wide trend health | <20% = washed-out; >90% = overheated |
| FII/DII flows | Institutional positioning | Persistent FII selling + retail buying = late/topping tell |
| IPO/SME frenzy, margin debt, retail account openings | Speculative excess | Manic new-issue market & leverage = euphoria stage |
| Fear & Greed style composites | Aggregated sentiment | Extreme Greed / Extreme Fear = fade zones |

**Cross-checking against structure.** Confirm the stage with objective price tools: the **200-DMA slope** (flat = accumulation/distribution, up = markup, down = markdown), the sequence of swing highs/lows (Dow Theory), and volume character (rising on markup up-legs, climactic at tops/bottoms). Sentiment tells you *how extreme*; structure tells you *which way* — you need both, because sentiment can stay extreme for a long time.

## Reading it — a worked Nifty example

Trace a stylised full Nifty cycle with levels and sentiment readings.

**Accumulation (Stage 1).** Nifty has fallen from 18,600 to 15,200 over months. Now it goes quiet, chopping 15,200–16,000 for eight weeks. The 200-DMA flattens. India VIX, which spiked to 28 during the fall, drifts to 16. News is grim, retail is disgusted, SIP inflows wobble, IPO market is dead. PCR is elevated (~1.4) from residual fear. *Despair.* This apathy — nobody wants stocks — is the accumulation fingerprint. Smart money is quietly buying the tests of 15,200.

**Markup (Stage 2).** Nifty breaks 16,000 on rising volume; the 200-DMA turns up. Higher highs and higher lows print: 16,400 → 17,200 → 18,000. Sentiment climbs the wall of worry — *disbelief* ("dead-cat bounce") gives way to *hope* then *optimism*. Breadth is broad; % of stocks above 200-DMA rises past 60%. VIX sits in a comfortable 12–15. Buy-the-dip works beautifully. This is the money-making stage; you press trends and hold.

**Distribution (Stage 3) — the danger zone.** Nifty reaches 19,800–20,000 and stalls. Now watch the *divergences*: price chops sideways near the highs for weeks, but **breadth narrows** (only a handful of heavyweights lead, % above 200-DMA slips even as the index holds), India **VIX drops to 10–11** (extreme complacency), **PCR sinks below 0.8** (everyone's short puts, nobody hedges), the **IPO/SME market goes manic**, retail account openings spike, leverage builds, and **FIIs quietly sell while DIIs/retail absorb**. Sentiment is *euphoria* — "India's decade," "20,000 is just the start." Every classical distribution tell (Wyckoff BC/UTAD) shows up on the tape. Maximum financial risk wears the mask of minimum perceived risk.

**Markdown (Stage 4).** Nifty breaks the range, loses the 20-DMA then the 50-DMA, prints its first lower-low, and the 200-DMA rolls over: 19,600 → 18,400 → 17,000. Sentiment descends — *anxiety → denial ("healthy correction") → fear → panic*. India VIX spikes to 22, then 30 on a capitulation day. PCR rockets above 1.6 as the crowd finally buys puts (too late). % above 200-DMA collapses under 20%. The capitulation day — a wide-spread, huge-volume down-bar with VIX >30 and PCR extreme — marks *despondency*, and the cycle prepares to seed a new accumulation near 15,500–16,000.

The lesson lives in the transitions: the euphoria at 20,000 (VIX 10, PCR 0.7, IPO mania) *screamed* late-cycle even as price looked strongest; the panic at 16,000 (VIX 30, PCR 1.6, capitulation volume) *screamed* opportunity even as price looked worst.

## Trading it

Cycle analysis is a **regime filter** more than a single entry trigger, but it produces concrete actions.

**Stage-appropriate strategy selection.**
- *Accumulation:* accumulate quality longs on tests of the base (buy near 15,200 with stop below the base). Position, don't trade. Mean-reversion works.
- *Markup:* trend-follow. Buy pullbacks to the 20/50-DMA, hold, trail stops under higher-lows. Ignore overbought signals — trends stay overbought.
- *Distribution:* shift to defense. Take profits into strength, tighten stops, buy portfolio hedges (Nifty puts) *while VIX is cheap* (10–11 — hedging is on sale exactly when nobody wants it). Reduce leverage. Start looking for shorts only once markdown structure confirms.
- *Markdown:* short rallies to falling MAs, hold cash, protect capital. Do *not* buy dips — the dip-buying reflex from markup is what destroys accounts here. Wait for capitulation + accumulation signs before turning long.

**Contrarian extremes as timing.** Fade sentiment only at *extremes with structural confirmation*:
- VIX >30 + PCR >1.6 + capitulation volume + a bullish structure signal (e.g. a Wyckoff spring / SMC sell-side sweep) = high-probability *long* entry near the bottom.
- VIX <11 + PCR <0.75 + breadth divergence + a distribution/UTAD = high-probability *defensive/short* setup near the top.

**Sizing and management.** Size *up* when the cycle stage and your setup agree (a trend-following long in confirmed markup); size *down* or stand aside when they conflict (a countertrend short in a raging markup). Never let a contrarian conviction override structure: if you're leaning long into panic, your stop goes below the capitulation low; if it breaks, the cycle hasn't turned yet — exit.

**Scenario — the seductive dip.** In distribution, Nifty falls from 20,000 to 19,300 and the markup-conditioned crowd (and you) want to "buy the dip." Cycle discipline says: check breadth (narrowing), VIX (was 10, now rising), structure (first lower-high forming). If those flash distribution/markdown, that dip is a *sell rally*, not a buy. This single reframe — refusing to buy dips once the stage has turned — is the highest-value application of cycle analysis.

## Confluence

Cycle/sentiment analysis is a *top-down frame* that supercharges every other tool.

**Wyckoff.** Accumulation and distribution stages *are* Wyckoff at the index level; use the schematic to time the stage transitions the sentiment gauges warn about.

**Moving-average structure.** The 200-DMA slope and the 20/50/200 stack objectively confirm the stage — pair the emotional read with the mechanical one.

**Breadth.** Advance-decline line, % above 200-DMA, and new-highs/new-lows are the *hard data* behind "narrowing participation" in late-cycle distribution — the most reliable non-price top warning.

**Option chain (India-central).** India VIX and PCR are your primary quantitative sentiment feed; overlay them with max-pain and OI shifts. Extreme cheap VIX in a stalling market = buy hedges; VIX spike + PCR extreme + put unwinding starting = capitulation forming.

**Intermarket & flows.** FII/DII cash flows, USD-INR, US 10-year yields, and crude contextualise the stage — persistent FII outflows with a rising dollar reinforce a markdown thesis.

**Classical patterns & candles.** A head-and-shoulders top or a monthly shooting star *at* a euphoric extreme (VIX 10) is far more reliable than the same pattern mid-markup — the cycle stage weights the signal.

## Pitfalls & false signals

**Cycles aren't clockwork.** The single biggest error is expecting fixed periodicity — "we're due for a crash." Stages vary wildly in length; a markup can run for years past "overvalued." Trade the *evidence of transition*, never the calendar.

**Early is wrong.** Sentiment extremes can get *more* extreme. VIX at 11 can sit at 11 for months; an overbought market can keep rising. Being "right too early" on a contrarian short in markup is indistinguishable from being wrong — you lose money either way. **Wait for structure to confirm the turn** before acting on a sentiment extreme; sentiment tells you a move is *possible*, structure tells you it has *begun*.

**Single-indicator sentiment.** No one gauge is sufficient. A high PCR alone, or a low VIX alone, gives false signals constantly. Demand a *cluster* of extremes (VIX + PCR + breadth + froth indicators) before calling a top or bottom.

**Narrative capture.** Every top has a compelling story ("India's structural bull decade"); every bottom has a terrifying one ("this time the system breaks"). The more seductive the narrative and the more universal the agreement, the closer you likely are to the turn. Notice when *everyone* — cab drivers, WhatsApp groups, your barber — is talking stocks: that's euphoria data.

**Your own psychology.** You are part of the crowd. The hardest trade — buying capitulation, selling euphoria — feels physically wrong precisely because your emotions are synced with the crowd's. Cycle analysis only pays if you can act against your own gut at the extremes, which is why pre-defined rules and stops matter more here than anywhere.

**How pros filter.** They never fight the primary trend on sentiment alone; they use extremes to *lean* and structure to *trigger*; they demand multi-indicator confirmation; and they respect that identifying the stage is probabilistic — sizing up when cycle, structure, and sentiment agree, and standing aside when they conflict.

## Interview-ready summary

"Markets move in a four-stage cycle — accumulation, markup, distribution, markdown — driven by the crowd's swing between fear and greed, and because the crowd is largest and most confident exactly when it's most wrong, sentiment is a contrarian tool at the extremes. Accumulation is apathy and a flat 200-DMA after a decline; markup is higher highs, broad breadth, and rising optimism; distribution is a choppy ceiling with euphoria, complacent low VIX, narrowing breadth, and FIIs selling to retail; markdown is lower lows, fear, and finally capitulation. I read the stage with structure — the 200-DMA slope and the swing sequence — and gauge the emotion with India VIX, PCR, breadth like the percent of stocks above their 200-DMA, and froth signals like IPO mania and margin debt. The payoff is regime selection: trend-follow and buy dips in markup, but turn defensive, take profits, and buy cheap hedges in distribution, and short rallies while holding cash in markdown — refusing the dip-buying reflex that kills accounts. At a Nifty top near 20,000, VIX at 10 and PCR under 0.8 with narrowing breadth flag euphoria even as price looks strongest; at a bottom near 16,000, VIX at 30 with capitulation volume flags opportunity even as it looks worst. The discipline is that cycles aren't clockwork and sentiment extremes can stretch — so I lean with sentiment but only trigger when structure confirms the turn, always with a stop, because this is probability and regime, not prophecy."
