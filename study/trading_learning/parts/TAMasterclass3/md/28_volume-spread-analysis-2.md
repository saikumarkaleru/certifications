# Volume Spread Analysis (VSA) II

VSA I taught you to read a single bar as a sentence — spread, close and volume combined to reveal whether professional money was buying or selling. VSA II teaches you to read the paragraphs and chapters: how those individual bars link into a *campaign*. Smart money does not accumulate a large position in one bar; it works over days or weeks inside a trading range, shaking out weak holders, testing for remaining supply, and finally marking the price up (or down). This chapter maps the full accumulation and distribution structure, drills the marquee reversal patterns — the **spring** and the **upthrust** — and gives complete India trade plans with rupee levels, stops and targets. It closes with a campaign checklist you can run down before committing capital.

## What it is and the logic

A VSA campaign is the life-cycle of a professional operation. In an **accumulation** campaign, large operators quietly buy stock from a fearful or bored public inside a sideways range, then mark price up. In a **distribution** campaign, they quietly sell into an excited public inside a sideways range, then mark price down. The Wyckoff schematic gives the skeleton; VSA supplies the bar-by-bar flesh — the specific spread/close/volume signatures that tell you which phase you are in.

The reason campaigns exist is liquidity. To buy 3,00,000 shares of a mid-cap or 50,000 lots of a Nifty future, the operator needs sellers. The best time to find eager sellers is after a decline, when the public is frightened and dumping. So accumulation happens at the *end* of a downtrend, inside a range, where the operator absorbs the last panic supply (the selling climax), then repeatedly *tests* to confirm sellers are gone, and only then marks up. Distribution is the mirror: at the end of an uptrend, the operator finds eager buyers in the crowd's greed, feeds stock out during a buying climax and subsequent no-demand rallies, then marks down.

The whole method rests on one asymmetry the crowd never internalises: **the low is made on the day of maximum bad news and maximum volume, and the high is made on the day of maximum good news and maximum volume.** VSA campaigns are the structural expression of that asymmetry.

## Construction: the accumulation and distribution schematics

### Accumulation (bottom) — the sequence

| Phase / event | VSA name | Bar signature | What is happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — stopping the decline | Preliminary Support (PS) | Wide down-bar closing off low, high volume | First professional buying appears |
| A — the climax | Selling Climax (SC) | Very wide down-bar, new low, close upper third, ultra-high volume | Panic supply absorbed by big buyers |
| A — the bounce | Automatic Rally (AR) | Sharp up-move, supply temporarily exhausted | Defines the top of the range |
| B — building the cause | Secondary Test (ST) | Return toward SC low on *lower* volume, narrow spread | Confirms supply is drying up |
| C — the shakeout | Spring / Shakeout | Quick dip below range support, then close back inside on... it depends on volume | Final stop-hunt to grab the last weak sellers |
| C — proof | Test after Spring | Narrow down-bar, low volume, close up | No supply left — the confirmation |
| D — markup begins | Sign of Strength (SOS) | Wide up-bar, close high, expanding volume | Demand overwhelms — trend up starts |
| D — pullback | Last Point of Support (LPS) | Higher low on low volume | Final low-risk entry before markup |

### Distribution (top) — the mirror sequence

| Phase / event | VSA name | Bar signature | What is happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Preliminary Supply (PSY) | Wide up-bar, close off high, high volume | First professional selling |
| A | Buying Climax (BC) | Very wide up-bar, new high, close off top, ultra-high volume | Public buying frenzy absorbed by sellers |
| A | Automatic Reaction (AR) | Sharp drop | Defines bottom of the range |
| B | Secondary Test (ST) | Rally toward BC high on lower volume | Confirms demand fading |
| C | Upthrust (UT / UTAD) | Spike to new high then close near low, high volume | Final bull trap above range |
| C | Test after UT | Narrow up-bar, low volume (No Demand) | No demand left — confirmation |
| D | Sign of Weakness (SOW) | Wide down-bar, close low, expanding volume | Supply overwhelms — markdown begins |
| D | pullback | Last Point of Supply (LPSY) | Lower high on low volume | Final low-risk short entry |

### The two marquee patterns

**The Spring.** A spring is a deliberate push of price *below* an established support level, designed to trigger the stop-losses and breakout-short orders resting there, immediately followed by a snap back inside the range. It is the accumulation range's final act. The tell is the *reaction to the break*: if price breaks support on high volume and *cannot* return, that is genuine weakness; but if it breaks support and springs back with the down-move showing *low volume* (no supply) or the recovery showing strong demand, the break was a shakeout. Springs are graded:

- **Spring #1 (weak)** — break on high volume, wide spread; supply is still present, needs more testing.
- **Spring #2 (moderate)** — break on average volume, quick recovery.
- **Spring #3 (strong / textbook)** — break on *low* volume with immediate close back inside; no supply, ready for markup.

**The Upthrust (UTAD — Upthrust After Distribution).** The exact mirror: price is pushed *above* resistance to trigger breakout-buy stops, then collapses back inside. High volume on the thrust with a close near the low, followed by a no-demand test, confirms distribution. The UTAD is the single most reliable short trigger in the VSA vocabulary because it combines a failed breakout, a stop-run, and confirmed absence of demand.

## Worked India example (levels and ₹)

**Long campaign — Tata Motors, daily (illustrative levels around a ₹620 base).**

Tata Motors declines from ₹720 to ₹630 over three weeks. Then:

- **PS/SC (Day 0):** a plunge to ₹612 intraday on 8.5 crore shares (3× the 20-day average), closing at ₹641 in the upper third of a very wide bar. Selling climax logged. Range low ≈ ₹612.
- **AR (Days 1–4):** rally to ₹672, defining range top.
- **ST (Days 8–10):** drift back to ₹628 on volume down to 2 crore — well below the climax. Secondary test confirms supply thinning. The range is now ₹612–₹672.
- **Spring (Day 16):** an intraday jab to ₹605 — a clean break below the ₹612 range support, tripping stops — but volume is a modest 3 crore (not the crescendo you'd expect on a genuine breakdown) and it closes back at ₹626, inside the range. **Spring #3.** The break found no fresh supply.
- **Test (Day 17):** narrow down-bar, dips to ₹619, volume just 1.4 crore, closes at ₹631. No Supply confirmation.
- **SOS / entry (Day 18):** wide up-bar closes at ₹655 on 5 crore, expanding volume. Markup underway.

**The trade.** Enter on the SOS confirmation or on the LPS pullback. Aggressive entry: ₹632 on Day 17's close (just above the spring). Conservative: ₹648 on the Day 18 break of range mid. Stop below the spring low with buffer — ₹600 (under ₹605). Risk from the aggressive entry ≈ ₹32. Targets: range top ₹672 (first, ~1.2R), then a measured move — range height ₹672 − ₹612 = ₹60 projected from breakout ≈ ₹732, and the prior swing ₹720. Scale out one-third at ₹672, one-third near ₹720, trail the rest under rising swing lows. Tata Motors ran to ₹735 over the following month; the spring located the exact low.

**Short campaign — Bank Nifty futures, illustrative near a 53,000 top.**

- **BC:** wide up-bar to 53,240, close 53,010, on the heaviest volume in two months. Buying climax; range top ≈ 53,240.
- **AR:** drop to 52,100; range bottom.
- **ST:** rally to 53,050 on notably lighter volume. Demand fading.
- **UTAD:** a spike to 53,310 — marginal new high above resistance, triggering breakout longs — then a collapse to close at 52,780 on high volume. Textbook upthrust after distribution.
- **No-Demand test:** narrow up-bar to 52,950 on the lowest volume of the sequence. Confirmed.
- **SOW / entry:** wide down-bar closes at 52,150 on expanding volume.

**The trade.** Short the SOW break or the LPSY lower-high. Entry ≈ 52,700 on the UTAD-day close, or 52,150 on the SOW. Stop above the upthrust high 53,340 (risk from 52,700 ≈ 640 points). Targets: range bottom 52,100 (first), measured move (range 1,140 from breakdown ≈ 50,960), then the prior consolidation floor ≈ 50,500 (>3R). In F&O terms, this is where you roll from long/neutral to buying puts or shorting futures; the upthrust-plus-no-demand pair is your green light to press the short.

## How to trade it — entry, stop, target, management

1. **Identify the range and label the phase.** Draw the SC/BC extreme and the AR extreme — those are your range boundaries. Everything inside is the "cause" being built.
2. **Wait for phase C.** Do not front-run. The spring/upthrust and its confirming test are where risk is lowest and edge highest, because the stop-run has already happened and the level is now defined.
3. **Entry choices (risk-ranked):**
   - *Aggressive:* on the close of the spring/upthrust bar back inside the range.
   - *Standard:* on the confirming No Supply / No Demand test.
   - *Conservative:* on the LPS/LPSY pullback after the first SOS/SOW, accepting a worse price for higher confirmation.
4. **Stop:** just beyond the spring low / upthrust high plus an ATR buffer. This is the whole point of the spring — it defines a tight, logical invalidation.
5. **Targets:** range top/bottom first, then the measured move (range height projected from the breakout), then prior structural swings.
6. **Management:** scale at the range boundary, trail behind the developing markup's swing points. If price re-enters the range and closes back on the *wrong* side after your breakout, the campaign read has failed — exit; a valid spring does not get reclaimed.

## Confluence

- **Volume profile:** accumulation ranges build a fat High-Volume Node; a spring that dips below the node's value area low and snaps back is doubly confirmed.
- **Wyckoff phases:** VSA and Wyckoff are the same tradition — use the phase labels (A–E) as scaffolding and VSA bars as the trigger detail.
- **Breadth & VIX:** a selling climax on a day India VIX spikes above ~20 and advance-declines are washed out is a genuine market-wide capitulation, not a single-stock quirk.
- **F&O positioning:** distribution tops often coincide with extreme call-side OI and complacent low VIX; accumulation bottoms with heavy put OI and elevated VIX. The upthrust/spring plus an OI unwind is a powerful two-method agreement.
- **Higher timeframe trend:** best campaigns fire *with* the higher-timeframe backdrop — a daily accumulation inside a weekly uptrend pullback is the A+ setup.

## Pitfalls

- **Forcing the schematic.** Not every base is textbook accumulation; some are just distribution that keeps going lower. Require the *volume evidence* (climax, drying tests, low-volume spring) — if the down-tests keep arriving on *rising* volume, supply is not exhausted; stand aside.
- **The failed spring.** A "spring" that breaks support and keeps falling on expanding volume was never a spring — it was a genuine breakdown. Volume on the break is the arbiter. Honour the stop instantly.
- **Expiry and rollover distortion (India-specific).** Bank Nifty and Nifty volumes are wildly inflated on weekly/monthly expiry and during rollover. A "climax" volume on expiry Thursday can be settlement mechanics, not conviction. Use continuous or consistent near-month futures and mentally discount expiry bars.
- **Index has no spot volume.** Always run VSA campaigns on *futures* volume for indices; spot "volume" is meaningless.
- **Impatience in Phase B.** The cause-building phase can drag for weeks and is boring by design (it is meant to shake out the impatient). Entering mid-range before the spring/upthrust is the most common way to get chopped.
- **News overrides.** A campaign read can be blown up by an exogenous event (RBI, Budget, global shock). VSA reads positioning, not information you don't have; size accordingly and respect stops.

## Campaign checklist and interview-ready summary

**Pre-trade checklist:**

1. Is there a clear climax (SC or BC) with ultra-high volume and a close against the move? 
2. Is there an AR defining the range, and at least one secondary test on *lower* volume? 
3. Has phase C arrived — a spring (long) or upthrust (short) — and did the break happen on *low/moderate* volume with a snap back inside? 
4. Did a confirming test (No Supply / No Demand) follow? 
5. Is the setup aligned with higher-timeframe trend, S/R, breadth and OI? 
6. Is the stop logical and tight (just beyond the spring/upthrust extreme) and the reward at least ~2–3× to first structure?

**Summary.** VSA II assembles single bars into campaigns. Accumulation runs Preliminary Support → Selling Climax → Automatic Rally → Secondary Test → **Spring** → Test → Sign of Strength → markup; distribution runs the mirror sequence ending in an **Upthrust (UTAD)** and markdown. The two highest-probability triggers are the spring (a low-volume false breakdown that traps sellers) and the upthrust (a high-volume false breakout that traps buyers), each confirmed by a low-volume no-supply/no-demand test. The spring/upthrust defines a tight, logical stop, making the reward-to-risk exceptional. In Indian markets, run campaigns on index *futures* volume, discount expiry-day distortion, confirm with breadth/VIX/OI, and above all let the *volume on the break* decide whether a spring is a shakeout or a genuine breakdown — a reclaimed spring is a failed read, and you exit at once.
