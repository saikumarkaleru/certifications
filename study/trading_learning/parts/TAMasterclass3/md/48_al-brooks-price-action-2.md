# Al Brooks Price Action II

Chapter I built the alphabet: the bar, the signal bar, the always-in concept, and the trend-from-the-open day classification. This chapter builds sentences and paragraphs — the actual tradable setups Al Brooks reads off a naked 5-minute chart, with precise entries, stops, targets, and management, all worked on Nifty and Bank Nifty. The named setups are few but they recur every single session: **H1/H2 and L1/L2** (the with-trend pullback entries), **double tops and bottoms**, **wedges and three-pushes**, **final flags**, and the **breakout / measured-move / channel** trend template. Master these and you can trade almost any intraday chart without an indicator.

We keep faith with Brooks' probabilistic honesty: no setup is better than roughly 60% in the right context and many are near coin-flips executed with an edge only because reward exceeds risk. The edge is in selection and management, not prediction.

## The always-in framework and the H/L counting system

Everything hangs on the always-in direction — the answer to "if forced to hold a position right now with no exit, long or short?" Brooks then counts pullbacks against that direction.

In a **bull** move, after any one-bar (or larger) pullback, the first bar whose high is exceeded is an **H1** (High 1). If price pulls back again and a second bar's high is exceeded, that is an **H2**. In a **bear** move the mirror images are **L1** and **L2**. The count resets when the trend makes a new extreme.

| Setup | Context | Entry | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | Strong bull trend / trend-from-open | Buy 1 tick above signal bar high, after first pullback | First pullback in a strong trend rarely gives a second chance |
| **H2** | Bull trend, normal/weaker, or after EMA test | Buy above the second pullback's signal bar | Two-legged pullbacks are the market's default; second leg flushes weak longs |
| **L1** | Strong bear trend | Sell 1 tick below signal bar low, first pullback | Mirror of H1 |
| **L2** | Bear trend, or rally into resistance | Sell below second pullback signal bar | Mirror of H2; the classic short in a bear |

The single most important practical rule Brooks teaches: **in a trading range, take H2/L2, not H1/L1.** Because ranges chop, the first attempt usually fails; the second attempt (a second entry) filters out noise. In a *strong* trend, H1 is fine because there is no second chance. So your read of trend-vs-range decides whether you take the first or second signal — a beautifully simple decision tree.

## Setup 1 — H2 buy in a Nifty bull trend (worked example)

Nifty futures, 5-minute. Suppose after a gap-up open Nifty trends from 24,240 to 24,340 by 10:15 a.m., riding above a rising 20-EMA (~24,300 by now). A pullback begins:

- **10:20 bar:** Bear bar, dips to 24,318, closes 24,322. (Leg 1 down.)
- **10:25 bar:** Small bull bar — this exceeds nothing important; a minor H1 attempt forms but let's say it fails, the next bar is bearish.
- **10:30 bar:** Second bear leg, dips to 24,305, tags the rising EMA, closes 24,312 with a tail below (buyers stepped in at the EMA). This is a textbook **H2 signal bar**: second pullback leg, EMA test, bull tail.
- **10:35 bar:** Trades above 24,312's high (24,319). Buy-stop fills at 24,320.

Trade plan:
- **Entry:** 24,320.
- **Stop:** below the signal bar low, ~24,303 (below the EMA test). Risk ≈ 17 points.
- **Target 1:** the prior swing high 24,340 (scalp), booking half.
- **Target 2 (runner):** a measured move. The prior leg 24,240→24,340 = 100 points; from the ~24,305 pullback low a measured move projects ~24,405. Trail the runner under successive higher lows or the EMA.

On Nifty futures (lot 25 as an illustration; verify the current lot), 17 points risk = ₹425/lot; a 60-point runner = ₹1,500/lot — a ~3.5R outcome on the runner portion. Even at a 50% hit rate on the runner, the expectancy is strongly positive, which is the whole point: Brooks setups are managed to asymmetry.

## Setup 2 — L2 short at resistance (Bank Nifty, expiry-flavoured)

Bank Nifty, 5-minute, a range day. Suppose Bank Nifty ranges 51,800–52,050 through the morning, with 52,000 being the highest-OI call strike (option-writer resistance). Price rallies into 52,040:

- First rally to 52,045 stalls, prints a bear bar (L1 attempt), pulls back to 51,990.
- Second rally to 52,050 prints a bear reversal bar with a long upper tail — sellers defending the call wall. This is the **L2 signal bar** at range resistance with OI confluence.
- Sell-stop 1 tick below its low (say 52,010) fills the short.

Plan:
- **Stop:** above 52,055 (above the second push and the round number). Risk ≈ 45 points — Bank Nifty is wider; size accordingly.
- **Target:** the opposite range edge / VWAP near 51,820, roughly a 190-point move — better than 4R.
- **Management:** because it is a range, book most at the middle (VWAP) and keep a small runner only if a bear trend bar breaks the range low with follow-through.

This is Brooks' "fade the range edge, especially the second attempt, with confluence" — and the OI wall is the India-first upgrade to pure price action.

## Setup 3 — Double top / double bottom (the two-legged reversal)

A double top in Brooks' language is not the textbook M with a perfect neckline; it is *two attempts to break out that both fail at roughly the same price*, with the second often a lower high or a bull-trap spike-and-fail. It signals the always-in is flipping to short.

Worked: Nifty rallies to 24,400, pulls back to 24,360, rallies again to 24,405 (marginally higher, a "double top bull trap"), and prints a bear signal bar. Short below the signal bar low; stop above 24,410; first target the pullback low 24,360, runner to a measured move down. The double bottom is the mirror — two failed attempts to break down, common at prior-day lows and high-OI put strikes, giving a long.

Brooks stresses these often form the *ends* of trends and the *edges* of ranges, and that a double top/bottom is really just an L2/H2 dressed up — the second entry, again.

## Setup 4 — Wedges and three-pushes

A **wedge** is three pushes in the trend direction with diminishing momentum, each push making a new extreme but with the trend-channel lines converging (overshoot-and-reverse). Brooks treats the third push's reversal bar as a high-quality counter-trend signal — one of the few counter-trend trades he endorses, because a wedge is an exhaustion pattern.

Worked (Bank Nifty selloff): three pushes down to 51,600, 51,520, 51,470, each smaller, the third overshooting a lower trend-channel line and printing a strong bull reversal bar. Buy above that bar; stop below 51,465; target the start of the third push (~51,520) as a scalp and the wedge's origin (~51,600+) as the swing. Wedge reversals often at least return to the second push — a reliable measured objective.

The three-push wedge is ubiquitous on Nifty around 2:45–3:15 p.m. when a trending day makes its final exhaustion push before the close.

## Setup 5 — Final flag and the trend's end

A **final flag** is the last pullback/pause of a trend before it reverses: after a long trend, price forms a tight flag, breaks out one more time, and that breakout immediately fails. Brooks trades the *failure of the final-flag breakout* as a reversal. The tell is a small, tight consolidation late in an extended trend, a weak breakout (small bars, no follow-through), and then a reversal bar.

Worked: Nifty trends up all afternoon to 24,470, forms a 4-bar tight flag near 24,465, pokes to 24,478, and reverses hard. Short the failure below the flag; the target is a measured move back to the flag's origin. This is how Brooks catches the top of a trend day without predicting it — he waits for the final flag to *fail*.

## The trend template: breakout → channel → measured move

Brooks' model of a trend is three phases you can trade differently:

1. **Breakout:** strong trend bars, often with a gap, low pullback frequency. Trade with-trend on any tiny pause (H1/L1); do not fade.
2. **Channel:** the trend continues but slopes gentler with deeper pullbacks; H2/L2 entries dominate, and the channel's trend-line/trend-channel-line define entries and profit-taking.
3. **Measured-move target / climax:** the channel reaches a measured objective (often the breakout leg's height projected from the channel's start), then either a final flag or a climax (a huge trend bar on high volume) ends it, flipping the always-in.

The measured move is Brooks' primary target discipline: **project the height of the first leg from the start of the pullback** (or from a mid-trend gap). On Nifty this repeatedly nails intraday swing targets to within a handful of points, which is why it doubles as a profit-taking rule.

## How to trade it: entries, stops, targets, management

**Entry.** Always a stop order one tick beyond the signal bar in the trade direction. This guarantees the market moved your way before you were filled; you never predict, you react.

**Stop.** Initial stop one tick beyond the signal bar's opposite extreme. Brooks also references an *actual* stop (what you'll truly honour) versus the theoretical one — for scalps he may use a wider "structural" stop below the swing but manage the position actively so the true risk is smaller.

**Targets and scaling.** Brooks typically scalps part (a fixed target ≈ the risk, i.e., 1R, or the prior swing) and swings a runner to a measured move. On Nifty a common template: book half at +1R or the prior swing, move the stop to breakeven, trail the rest under higher lows / the 20-EMA.

**Trade management by day type.** On a strong trend-from-the-open day, hold runners aggressively and re-enter on every pullback (add-on H2s). On a trading-range day, take profits quickly at the opposite edge and do not hope for trends. Matching management to the Chapter I day classification is where most of the money is made or lost.

**The "second entry" as a rescue.** If an H1 stops you out, the resulting deeper pullback often sets up the H2 — the very stop-out fuels the better trade. Brooks encourages re-entry on the second signal rather than revenge-avoidance.

## Confluence

- **EMA + structure:** best longs are H2s that test a rising 20-EMA; best shorts are L2s that rally to a falling EMA.
- **Measured moves lining up with prior-day levels and round numbers** (Nifty 24,000/24,500; Bank Nifty 52,000/52,500).
- **VWAP:** intraday, VWAP behaves as a Brooks magnet and mean; L2s that fail at VWAP from below and H2s that hold VWAP from above are high quality.
- **Option OI walls:** highest-OI call = resistance for L2 shorts; highest-OI put = support for H2 longs; max-pain as an end-of-day magnet, especially on expiry.
- **Second-attempt everywhere:** double tops/bottoms, wedges (third push), and final-flag failures are all "second/last attempt fails" — the single most reliable Brooks meta-pattern.

## Pitfalls

- **Taking H1/L1 in a range.** The commonest killer. In chop, wait for the second entry.
- **Counter-trend trading outside of wedges/climaxes/final flags.** Brooks is a with-trend trader; fading a strong trend without an exhaustion signal is how accounts die.
- **Ignoring signal-bar quality.** A doji or opposing-tail signal bar cuts the win rate sharply; skip it or demand the next one.
- **Not honouring the always-in flip.** Two strong opposite trend bars flip the always-in; holding the old side is stubbornness, not conviction.
- **Over-scaling in Bank Nifty.** Its points are 3–4× Nifty's; a "small" 45-point stop is real money. Size to fixed rupee risk, not to point count.
- **Chasing the breakout instead of the pullback.** Buying the big breakout bar's high often fills you at the top before a pullback; Brooks prefers to buy the *first pullback after* the breakout (the H1), not the breakout bar itself.
- **Fighting the last hour.** Expiry-day gamma and the 3:00–3:20 p.m. positioning push can invalidate mid-day structure; reduce size unless a clean setup with OI confluence appears.

## Interview-ready summary

Brooks' tradable method rests on the always-in direction and a pullback-counting system: with-trend entries are H1/H2 (bull) and L1/L2 (bear), taken on stop orders one tick beyond a graded signal bar, with stops beyond the signal bar's opposite extreme. The governing rule is take H1/L1 in strong trends but only H2/L2 (second entries) in trading ranges, because most first attempts in a range fail. Reversals are traded only at exhaustion — double tops/bottoms (really second-attempt failures), three-push wedges, final-flag breakout failures, and climaxes — never as naked counter-trend fades. Trends are modelled as breakout → channel → measured-move, and the measured move (first leg's height projected from the pullback) is both the target discipline and the trend's likely end. Management scales out part at ~1R or the prior swing and trails a runner to the measured move, with aggressiveness matched to the day type from Chapter I. On Nifty and Bank Nifty, the pure price action is upgraded with India-first confluence — VWAP, prior-day levels, round numbers, and option OI walls (highest-OI call/put strikes, max pain) — turning Brooks' bar reading into a complete, honest, indicator-light intraday system.
