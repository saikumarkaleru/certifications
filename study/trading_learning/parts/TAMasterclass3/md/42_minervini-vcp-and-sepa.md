# Minervini VCP & SEPA

Mark Minervini turned a five-figure account into millions and won the U.S. Investing Championship with a return north of 150% in a single year, and he did it running one specific pattern inside one specific methodology. The pattern is the **Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP)**; the methodology that surrounds it is **SEPA — the Specific Entry Point Analysis**. Both descend from the CAN SLIM / William O'Neil school but Minervini codified the chart mechanics with unusual precision. For the Indian swing and position trader, VCP+SEPA is a superb momentum framework because it demands strength, precision, and tight risk — three things that keep you alive through the chop that NSE mid-caps love to serve up. This chapter treats it as a complete named system: the idea, exact rules, an India example in rupees, edge/cost notes, F&O adaptation, and the traps.

## Origin & idea

Minervini's central belief is that **the biggest, fastest gains come from strong stocks that get stronger** — you are not bottom-fishing value, you are buying leadership at the precise moment it resumes an established uptrend with minimal risk. SEPA is the full filter stack: it insists on strong fundamentals (earnings and sales acceleration), a confirmed Stage 2 uptrend (Minervini uses Weinstein's stage language explicitly), leadership relative strength, and *then* a low-risk technical entry point.

The VCP is that entry point. Its logic is beautifully simple. As a stock consolidates after a run, each successive pullback within the base gets **shallower** and **volume dries up**. A first correction might be 25% deep; the next 12%; the next 6%. This progressive tightening — the "contractions" — signals that sellers are being exhausted and the float is passing into strong hands who refuse to sell cheaply. When the last, tightest contraction resolves upward on a volume expansion, supply has been wrung out and the stock can move with little overhead resistance. Minervini's phrase is that the stock reaches a point of **"the line of least resistance."**

Think of it as a coiling spring: volatility compresses, then releases directionally. It is Wyckoff accumulation and a Weinstein Stage 1-to-2 handoff, but drawn with a magnifying glass on the last few weeks of the base.

## Exact rules

### The pre-conditions (SEPA gatekeepers)

Before a VCP even qualifies, Minervini's **Trend Template** must be satisfied. Adapted to Indian daily charts:

| # | Trend Template condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Price is above the 150-day and 200-day moving averages |
| 2 | The 150-day MA is above the 200-day MA |
| 3 | The 200-day MA is trending up (rising for at least ~1 month) |
| 4 | The 50-day MA is above both the 150- and 200-day MAs |
| 5 | Price is above the 50-day MA |
| 6 | Price is at least ~25–30% above its 52-week low |
| 7 | Price is within ~25% of its 52-week high |
| 8 | Relative Strength vs Nifty is high (top quartile of the market) |

Only stocks passing the Trend Template are Stage 2 leaders worth hunting a VCP in. On Chartink you can encode conditions 1–7 directly; condition 8 needs a relative-strength ranking.

### The VCP structure

| Feature | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| Number of contractions | Typically 2 to 6 (Minervini calls them "T" — e.g. "3T") |
| Depth progression | Each contraction shallower than the last (e.g. 25% → 12% → 6%) |
| Final contraction | Tight — often 3–10% peak-to-trough |
| Volume | Declines through the base; dries up in the final contraction |
| Duration | Weeks to several months; longer, deeper bases after bigger runs |
| Pivot | The high of the final tight contraction = buy trigger |

Minervini annotates a base like "**VCP 18w 3T 28/12/5**": an 18-week base, three contractions of 28%, 12% and 5%. The final 5% contraction on dried-up volume is the coil.

### The entry — the pivot buy point

The **pivot** is the resistance line across the top of the final contraction. You buy as price pushes through the pivot on a **volume expansion** (Minervini wants volume clearly above average — ideally well over the 50-day average). The tightest part of the base often forms a small handle or a sideways cheat area; the pivot is the ceiling of that.

### Exit and risk rules

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Initial stop | Below the low of the final contraction, tuned so risk is small |
| Max loss per trade | ~1.25–2.5% of the *stock price* from pivot; keep account risk ≤ ~0.5–1% |
| Batting-average discipline | Minervini expects ~50% win rate; edge comes from big winners, tiny losers |
| Profit-taking | Sell partial into strength; trail the rest under the 50-day MA |
| Sell rule | Cut instantly if it fails and breaks the stop — no averaging down, ever |

Minervini's asymmetry is the whole game: keep losses around 5–8% max (often far tighter on a good VCP), let winners run to 20–100%+. A 50% win rate with 3:1 average win/loss is enormously profitable.

## Worked India example (levels & ₹)

Model a leadership mid-cap, **Stock ABC** on NSE, after a strong prior advance from ₹300 to ₹520.

**Trend Template check:** ABC at ₹500 sits above its 50-DMA (₹470), 150-DMA (₹430) and 200-DMA (₹400); 50 > 150 > 200 and the 200-DMA is rising; it's ~66% above its 52-week low (₹300) and ~4% below its 52-week high (₹520); RS vs Nifty is in the top decile. All conditions pass — ABC is a Stage 2 leader.

**The VCP forms over 11 weeks (3 contractions):**

- **C1:** ABC falls from ₹520 to ₹416 — a **20%** pullback — on heavy volume as early buyers take profits.
- **C2:** it recovers to ₹505, then dips to ₹454 — a **10%** contraction — on lighter volume.
- **C3:** it firms to ₹500 and eases to ₹477 — a tight **4.6%** contraction — on volume that has dried up to roughly half the 50-day average.

Annotation: **VCP 11w 3T 20/10/5**. The pivot is the ceiling of C3 ≈ **₹500**.

**Entry:** ABC pushes through ₹500 and closes at ₹506 on volume ~2x its 50-day average. You buy the pivot at **₹500**.

**Stop:** just below the C3 low of ₹477 — place it at **₹474**. Risk per share = ₹26, i.e. **5.2%** from the pivot. (On a tighter VCP you'd get 2–3%; this is the wide end.)

**Position size:** ₹8,00,000 account, risking 0.75% (₹6,000). Quantity = ₹6,000 ÷ ₹26 = **230 shares** (round to 225). Capital deployed = 225 × ₹500 = ₹1,12,500 (14% of the account — sensible concentration for a leader).

**Management:** ABC advances. You sell one-third (75 shares) into strength at ₹560 (+12%), banking ₹4,500 and reducing risk. You trail the remaining 150 shares under the rising 50-DMA. Over the next two months ABC runs to ₹640 before closing below its 50-DMA at ₹610, where you exit the balance.

- Tranche 1: 75 × (₹560 − ₹500) = ₹4,500
- Tranche 2: 150 × (₹610 − ₹500) = ₹16,500
- **Total ≈ ₹21,000 gross** against an initial defined risk of ₹6,000 — roughly **3.5:1 realised**, on a base you sized to lose ₹6,000 if wrong.

Had ABC failed the pivot and hit ₹474 instead, you'd have lost ₹5,850 and moved on. That asymmetry, repeated across many trades, is SEPA.

## Backtest / edge notes & realistic costs

**Where the edge comes from.** VCP works because it fuses three real, separately-documented effects: the **momentum / relative-strength anomaly** (past winners keep winning over 3–12 months), **volatility compression preceding expansion** (low-vol regimes are unstable and break directionally), and **behavioural supply exhaustion** in a tightening base. None of these is folklore.

**Honest win-rate reality.** Do not expect a high hit rate. In choppy or corrective markets VCP breakouts fail frequently — false breakouts ("shakeouts above the pivot" that reverse) are common on NSE mid-caps where a single operator or a block deal can spike volume. The edge is **not** in being right often; it is in the payoff ratio and ruthless stops. A trader who honours 5% stops and lets winners run 25%+ is profitable at a 40–50% hit rate; a trader who widens stops "to give it room" destroys the entire mathematical edge.

**Realistic costs (NSE delivery equity, 2026).** Per round trip you carry: brokerage (₹0 to ~0.03% depending on broker), STT (0.1% on buy + 0.1% on sell for delivery), exchange transaction charges (~0.00297%), SEBI + stamp (~0.015% buy), and GST (18% on brokerage + txn charges). On a ₹1,12,500 position, expect roughly **₹250–₹400 round-trip** — trivial against a ₹21,000 gain but material if you overtrade tight bases for 2% pops. VCP is designed for a handful of high-conviction position trades, not day-trading; keep frequency low so costs stay negligible.

**Market-regime dependence.** Like all momentum systems, VCP shines in trending, risk-on markets (think a broad Nifty Stage 2) and gets chopped up in rangebound or bearish tapes. Minervini himself goes largely to cash when breakouts stop working — a market filter (are your last several breakouts succeeding? is Nifty above a rising 50-DMA?) is part of the system, not optional.

## Adaptations for NSE / F&O

- **Liquidity screen.** Restrict the universe to liquid names — Nifty 500 constituents, decent daily turnover — so your stop actually fills near where you set it. Illiquid small-caps gap through stops.
- **Circuit filters.** Indian stocks carry 5/10/20% circuit limits; a VCP breakout that hits an upper circuit can't be chased. Prefer names in the F&O list or large caps where circuits rarely bind intraday.
- **Gap risk & overnight.** VCP is a swing/position method held overnight, so respect India's frequent gap opens around results and global cues. Size for gap risk; never bet the account on one leader.
- **Expressing it in F&O.** For F&O-eligible leaders you can substitute a **bull call spread** for the equity position to cap risk and cost — buy the near-ATM call at the pivot, sell a higher-strike call near your first target. This defines risk precisely (matching Minervini's philosophy) and sidesteps the STT drag on large delivery positions, at the cost of theta and a capped upside. For pure momentum purists the cash-market position with a hard stop remains cleaner because it lets the biggest winners run uncapped.
- **Index VCPs.** Nifty and Bank Nifty themselves form VCP-like coils before big directional moves; a tight, low-volatility Bank Nifty consolidation resolving on volume is tradeable via futures or spreads with the same pivot/stop logic.

## Pitfalls

- **Buying before the pivot.** Anticipating the breakout inside the base gets you chopped in the final contraction. Wait for the pivot to be taken on volume.
- **No volume, no trade.** A pivot break on weak volume is the classic Indian bull-trap. Demand the volume expansion.
- **Widening the stop.** The instant you "give it room," you break the asymmetry that makes the whole method profitable. Stop is defined by the base, size is defined by the stop.
- **Ignoring the Trend Template.** Drawing a "VCP" on a Stage 4 downtrending stock is just pattern-hallucination. VCP is only valid inside a confirmed Stage 2 uptrend with real relative strength.
- **Sloppy contraction reading.** Not every wiggle is a contraction. The pattern needs *progressive* tightening with *declining* volume — if the last pullback is deeper than the previous one, it isn't a VCP.
- **Fighting the market.** Forcing VCP trades when breakouts across your watchlist are all failing means the market regime is wrong. Fewer, better trades — or cash.
- **Overtrading tiny bases.** Chasing 2% pops on marginal setups lets costs and slippage eat you. Reserve capital for genuine, well-formed VCPs in true leaders.

## Interview-ready summary

Minervini's **VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern)** is a momentum entry pattern: a leadership stock in a confirmed Stage 2 uptrend consolidates in a series of **progressively shallower pullbacks on progressively lighter volume** (e.g. 20% → 10% → 5%), coiling like a spring until the final tight contraction resolves upward through a **pivot** on a **volume expansion**. It sits inside **SEPA (Specific Entry Point Analysis)**, whose gatekeeper is the **Trend Template** — price above rising 50/150/200-day MAs stacked in order, within 25% of the 52-week high, high relative strength versus Nifty. You buy the pivot, stop just below the final contraction's low (keeping account risk under ~1%), sell partials into strength, and trail the rest under the 50-DMA. The edge is not a high win rate — it's **asymmetry**: tiny, disciplined losses against 20–100% winners at roughly a 50% hit rate. In Indian markets, screen for liquidity, respect circuits and gaps, use a market-regime filter, and optionally express F&O-eligible setups as bull call spreads to define risk while keeping the pivot-and-stop logic intact.
