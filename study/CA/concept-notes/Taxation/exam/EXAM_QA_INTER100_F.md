### Q1. Ch: Basic Concepts — Surcharge and Marginal Relief for an Individual (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Aravind, aged 45 years, a resident individual who has **opted out of the default tax regime** under section 115BAC(6) (i.e. he is taxed under the optional old regime), has a total income of ₹51,00,000 for A.Y. 2025-26. Compute his tax liability and clearly show the **marginal relief**, if any.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Total income (old regime) | 51,00,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Income-tax on total income of ₹51,00,000 (old regime slab rates)**

| Slab | Rate | Tax (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | Nil | 0 |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% | 12,500 |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% | 1,00,000 |
| Above ₹10,00,000 (on ₹41,00,000) | 30% | 12,30,000 |
| **Income-tax** | | **13,42,500** |

Since total income exceeds ₹50 lakh but does not exceed ₹1 crore, **surcharge @ 10%** is attracted.
Surcharge = 10% × ₹13,42,500 = **₹1,34,250**. Tax plus surcharge = ₹13,42,500 + ₹1,34,250 = **₹14,76,750**.

**WN-2: Marginal relief u/s computation (income exceeding ₹50 lakh)**

Tax on ₹50,00,000 (no surcharge, as income does not exceed ₹50 lakh) = ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + 30% × ₹40,00,000 = **₹13,12,500**.

Marginal relief ensures that tax + surcharge on income exceeding ₹50 lakh does **not exceed** (tax on ₹50 lakh) **plus** the amount of income exceeding ₹50 lakh.

- Increase in income over ₹50 lakh = ₹51,00,000 − ₹50,00,000 = ₹1,00,000
- Permissible ceiling of (tax + surcharge) = ₹13,12,500 + ₹1,00,000 = **₹14,12,500**
- Actual (tax + surcharge) = ₹14,76,750
- Excess = ₹14,76,750 − ₹14,12,500 = **₹64,250 = Marginal Relief**

Surcharge after marginal relief = ₹1,34,250 − ₹64,250 = **₹70,000**.

**Statement Showing Tax Liability of Mr. Aravind for A.Y. 2025-26**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Income-tax (WN-1) | 13,42,500 |
| Add: Surcharge @ 10% | 1,34,250 |
| Less: Marginal relief (WN-2) | (64,250) |
| Tax and surcharge | 14,12,500 |
| Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% | 56,500 |
| **Total tax liability** | **14,69,000** |

**Answer:** Tax liability = **₹14,69,000**; marginal relief allowed = **₹64,250**.

*(Full-marks tip: the examiner rewards showing that surcharge is levied on income-tax (not on income) and computing marginal relief as the excess of "(tax+surcharge) on actual income" over "(tax on ₹50 lakh + income above ₹50 lakh)". Common deduction: computing cess before granting marginal relief, or applying surcharge on total income.)*

### Q2. Ch: Basic Concepts — Surcharge and Marginal Relief for a Firm (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** M/s Vasudha & Associates, a partnership firm, has a total income of ₹1,02,00,000 for A.Y. 2025-26. Compute the firm's tax liability, showing marginal relief separately.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Total income of the firm | 1,02,00,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Income-tax on the firm**

A firm is taxed at a flat rate of **30%**.
Income-tax = 30% × ₹1,02,00,000 = **₹30,60,000**.

Since total income exceeds ₹1 crore, **surcharge @ 12%** is attracted.
Surcharge = 12% × ₹30,60,000 = **₹3,67,200**. Tax + surcharge = ₹34,27,200.

**WN-2: Marginal relief (income exceeding ₹1 crore)**

Tax on ₹1,00,00,000 (no surcharge) = 30% × ₹1,00,00,000 = **₹30,00,000**.

- Increase in income over ₹1 crore = ₹2,00,000
- Permissible ceiling of (tax + surcharge) = ₹30,00,000 + ₹2,00,000 = **₹32,00,000**
- Actual (tax + surcharge) = ₹34,27,200
- Excess = ₹34,27,200 − ₹32,00,000 = **₹2,27,200 = Marginal Relief**

Surcharge after marginal relief = ₹3,67,200 − ₹2,27,200 = **₹1,40,000**.

**Statement Showing Tax Liability of M/s Vasudha & Associates for A.Y. 2025-26**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Income-tax @ 30% | 30,60,000 |
| Add: Surcharge @ 12% | 3,67,200 |
| Less: Marginal relief | (2,27,200) |
| Tax and surcharge | 32,00,000 |
| Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% | 1,28,000 |
| **Total tax liability** | **33,28,000** |

**Answer:** Tax liability of the firm = **₹33,28,000**; marginal relief = **₹2,27,200**.

*(Full-marks tip: the surcharge threshold for a firm is a single slab — 12% only when income exceeds ₹1 crore. Common deduction: applying the individual's 10%/15% slabs to a firm, or forgetting that marginal relief for a firm is anchored at the ₹1 crore threshold, not ₹50 lakh.)*

### Q3. Ch: Basic Concepts — Capital Receipt vs Revenue Receipt (Marks: 4) [Application]
**Question:** State, with reasons and the relevant test, whether each of the following is a **capital receipt** or a **revenue receipt**:
(i) ₹10,00,000 received by a firm as compensation for premature termination of one of its many distribution agency contracts, its business continuing thereafter.
(ii) ₹25,00,000 received by a manufacturer under a non-compete agreement, agreeing not to carry on any competing business for 5 years.
(iii) ₹2,00,000 received as a subsidy from the Government to reimburse a portion of electricity charges incurred in running the factory.
(iv) Sale proceeds of ₹5,00,000 from disposal of a discarded plant.

**Answer:**

The distinction between capital and revenue receipts is governed by judicial tests, the primary one being the **"fixed capital vs circulating capital"** test and the **"loss of source vs loss of income" (structure of the business) test** laid down by the courts. A receipt that impairs the **profit-making apparatus/structure** of the business is capital; a receipt arising in the ordinary course of trading, i.e. from **circulating capital**, is revenue.

| Item | Nature | Reason / Test applied |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Compensation for termination of one agency | **Revenue receipt** | The agency was one of several and its loss did not impair the profit-making structure; it is a normal incident of business (fits the *Kettlewell Bullen* / ordinary-course test). |
| (ii) Non-compete consideration | **Capital receipt** in character, but **specifically taxable** as business income u/s 28(va) | Received for a restrictive covenant sterilising the source; capital in nature, yet the statute deems such sums chargeable to tax. |
| (iii) Subsidy to reimburse electricity (a revenue cost) | **Revenue receipt** | Under the "purpose test", a subsidy that reimburses a **revenue expense** of running the business is revenue; it supplements trading receipts. |
| (iv) Sale of discarded plant | **Capital receipt** | Plant is a fixed capital asset; its realisation is on capital account (may attract capital gains / balancing under other provisions). |

**Conclusion:** The character is decided by whether the receipt relates to the **framework/source** (capital) or to the **carrying on** of business (revenue), subject to specific charging provisions such as section 28(va) that may tax a capital receipt.

*(Full-marks tip: state the governing test for each item, not just the label. Section 28(va) fetches full marks on the non-compete item — merely calling it "capital" loses marks because the statute expressly taxes it.)*

### Q4. Ch: Basic Concepts — Capital Expenditure vs Revenue Expenditure (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Classify each of the following as **capital** or **revenue** expenditure for a manufacturing company, giving the underlying test:
(i) ₹6,00,000 spent on replacing worn-out parts of an existing machine, restoring it to working condition (no increase in capacity).
(ii) ₹15,00,000 lump-sum paid to acquire technical know-how giving an enduring advantage to the business.
(iii) ₹3,00,000 paid to shift the factory to a new location for commercial convenience.
(iv) ₹4,00,000 paid as expenditure to overhaul and increase the installed capacity of an existing machine.
(v) ₹1,20,000 annual licence fee for use of a software renewed every year.

**Answer:**

The tests distinguishing capital from revenue expenditure are (a) the **"enduring benefit" test** (*Atherton*) — expenditure bringing into existence an asset or advantage of enduring benefit is capital; (b) the **"once and for all" test**; and (c) whether the outlay is incurred on **acquiring/improving** the profit-making apparatus (capital) or on **running** it and preserving it (revenue).

| Item | Nature | Test / Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Replacing worn-out parts, no added capacity | **Revenue** | Current repairs preserving an existing asset; no new asset or enduring advantage. |
| (ii) Lump-sum technical know-how of enduring benefit | **Capital** | Enduring-benefit test — an advantage of a lasting nature to the business framework. |
| (iii) Factory-shifting for commercial convenience | **Revenue** | Held to be revenue where the shift is for commercial expediency and no new asset/advantage results. |
| (iv) Overhaul that increases installed capacity | **Capital** | Improvement adds to the earning capacity/profit-making apparatus. |
| (v) Annual software licence fee, renewed yearly | **Revenue** | Recurring outlay for use, not acquisition; no enduring benefit (right lapses each year). |

**Conclusion:** Outlays that **augment or acquire** the profit-earning apparatus with an enduring benefit are capital; those that **maintain or operate** it are revenue, irrespective of the quantum involved.

*(Full-marks tip: quantum is irrelevant — a large repair can be revenue and a small acquisition can be capital. State the enduring-benefit / profit-making-apparatus test explicitly for each item.)*

### Q5. Ch: Basic Concepts — Charge of Tax, Rounding-off and Previous Year (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Answer the following with reference to the Income-tax Act, 1961:
(i) On what income is tax charged and at what rate? Cite the section.
(ii) How are the **total income** and the **tax payable** rounded off? Cite the sections.
(iii) State the **previous year** in the case of a business **newly set up** during the financial year.

**Answer:**

**(i) Charge of income-tax — Section 4.** Income-tax is charged for an assessment year in respect of the **total income of the previous year** of every person at the rate(s) prescribed by the relevant Finance Act. Where the Act provides for tax on income of a period other than the previous year (e.g. sections 172, 174, 174A, 175, 176) or for deduction/collection at source, tax is charged accordingly.

**(ii) Rounding off.**
- **Section 288A** — the amount of **total income** computed shall be rounded off to the nearest multiple of **₹10** (ignore paise and any part below ₹5; round up ₹5 or more).
- **Section 288B** — the amount of **tax, interest, penalty, fine or any other sum payable/refundable** shall be rounded off to the nearest multiple of **₹10** in the same manner.

**(iii) Previous year for a newly set-up business — Section 3.** The "previous year" is the financial year immediately preceding the assessment year. However, where a business or profession is **newly set up** (or a source of income newly comes into existence) during the financial year, the previous year is the period **beginning with the date of setting up** of the business/profession (or the date the source came into existence) **and ending on 31st March** of that financial year.

**Conclusion:** Tax is charged u/s 4 on the previous year's total income; total income is rounded off u/s 288A and tax payable u/s 288B to the nearest ₹10; and for a new business the first previous year runs from the date of setting up to the following 31st March.

*(Full-marks tip: cite 288A for income and 288B for tax separately — many students quote only one. For the new-business point, the previous year cannot exceed 12 months and always ends on 31st March.)*

### Q6. Ch: Residential Status — Indian Citizen Leaving India for Employment (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Suresh, an Indian citizen, had never left India until the previous year 2024-25. On **20th September 2024** he left India for the **first time** to take up **employment** in Qatar and remained there for the rest of the year. Determine his residential status for A.Y. 2025-26, giving the relevant section and the number of days of his stay in India.

| Month (P.Y. 2024-25) | Days in India |
|---|---|
| April 2024 | 30 |
| May 2024 | 31 |
| June 2024 | 30 |
| July 2024 | 31 |
| August 2024 | 31 |
| 1–20 September 2024 | 20 |
| **Total** | **173** |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Stay in India during P.Y. 2024-25** = 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 + 20 = **173 days** (he was outside India from 21st September 2024 to 31st March 2025).

**WN-2: Applicable basic conditions [Section 6(1)]**
An individual is resident if he satisfies **either** basic condition:
(a) in India for **≥ 182 days** in the previous year; **or**
(b) in India for **≥ 60 days** in the previous year **and ≥ 365 days** in the four preceding previous years.

**Explanation 1(a) to section 6(1):** In the case of an **Indian citizen who leaves India in the previous year for the purpose of employment** outside India, condition (b) does **not apply** — the **60-day period is substituted by 182 days**. Hence only condition (a) is to be tested.

**WN-3: Application to facts**
Mr. Suresh, an Indian citizen leaving India for employment, is resident only if he is in India for **≥ 182 days** in P.Y. 2024-25. His stay = **173 days < 182 days**. Therefore he does **not** satisfy the (only applicable) basic condition.

**Conclusion:** Mr. Suresh is a **Non-Resident (NR)** for A.Y. 2025-26.

**Answer:** **Non-Resident**, because as an Indian citizen leaving for employment his stay of **173 days** falls short of the extended threshold of 182 days under Explanation 1(a) to section 6(1).

*(Full-marks tip: the examiner wants the explicit statement that the 60-day condition is replaced by 182 days for an Indian citizen leaving for employment. Merely computing 173 days and concluding "resident/non-resident" without citing Explanation 1(a) loses marks.)*

### Q7. Ch: Residential Status — Effect of Non-Resident Status on Foreign Income (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Continuing from Q6, Mr. Suresh is a **Non-Resident** for A.Y. 2025-26. During P.Y. 2024-25 he had the following incomes. Determine, giving reasons under section 5(2), the amount **chargeable to tax in India**.

| # | Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Salary for April–September 2024 for services rendered in India, received in India | 6,00,000 |
| 2 | Salary from Qatar employer for services in Qatar, received in Qatar | 12,00,000 |
| 3 | Interest on savings bank account with SBI, Mumbai | 40,000 |
| 4 | Interest on a fixed deposit in a Dubai bank, received there | 1,50,000 |
| 5 | Rent from a house property situated in Hyderabad, received in Qatar | 3,60,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Scope of total income of a Non-Resident [Section 5(2)]**
For a non-resident, total income includes income which:
(a) is **received or deemed to be received** in India; **or**
(b) **accrues or arises or is deemed to accrue or arise** in India.
**Foreign income** (income that neither accrues/arises in India nor is received in India) is **not taxable** in the hands of a non-resident.

**WN-2: Item-wise analysis**

| # | Income | Taxable in India? | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Indian salary (services in India, received in India) | **Yes – ₹6,00,000** | Accrues in India (services rendered here) and received in India. |
| 2 | Qatar salary (services & receipt in Qatar) | **No** | Foreign income — neither accrues in India nor received in India; salary accrues where services are rendered. |
| 3 | SBI savings interest | **Yes – ₹40,000** | Accrues and is received in India. |
| 4 | Dubai FD interest, received in Dubai | **No** | Foreign income — accrues and received outside India. |
| 5 | Rent of Hyderabad house (received in Qatar) | **Yes – ₹3,60,000 (gross)** | Income from house property **situated in India** deemed to accrue/arise in India, irrespective of place of receipt. |

**Statement of Income Chargeable to Tax in India (Non-Resident)**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Salary earned and received in India | 6,00,000 |
| Interest on SBI savings account | 40,000 |
| Rent of house property in India (before deductions under "Income from house property") | 3,60,000 |
| **Total India-taxable income (before deductions)** | **10,00,000** |

**Conclusion:** The **Qatar salary (₹12,00,000)** and **Dubai interest (₹1,50,000)** — being foreign income — are **not chargeable** in India because Mr. Suresh is a non-resident. Only income received or accruing in India, aggregating **₹10,00,000** (before head-wise deductions), is taxable.

*(Full-marks tip: the decisive point is that place of accrual of salary is where services are rendered, and Indian-situated property income is deemed to accrue in India regardless of place of receipt. Losing the reasoning on items 2 and 5 is the common error.)*

### Q8. Ch: Residential Status — Deemed Resident under Section 6(1A) (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Rohit is an **Indian citizen** who manages his investments and a consultancy that is set up in India. During P.Y. 2024-25 he stayed in India for **only 90 days**, spending the rest of the year in various countries; he is **not liable to tax** in any other country by reason of domicile, residence or any similar criterion. His income for the year (other than income from foreign sources) is **₹18,00,000** and his stay in India in the four preceding years aggregates 340 days. Determine his residential status for A.Y. 2025-26.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Test under section 6(1)**
- Condition (a): ≥182 days in India — **not satisfied** (only 90 days).
- Condition (b): ≥60 days in the P.Y. **and** ≥365 days in the four preceding P.Y.s — he has 90 days (≥60) but only **340 days (<365)** in the four preceding years — **not satisfied**.

Hence Mr. Rohit is **not** a resident under the ordinary tests of section 6(1).

**WN-2: Deemed residence under section 6(1A)**
An **individual who is a citizen of India** shall be **deemed to be resident in India** in a previous year if:
(i) his **total income, other than income from foreign sources, exceeds ₹15,00,000**; **and**
(ii) he is **not liable to tax** in any other country/territory by reason of domicile, residence or any other criterion of similar nature; **and**
(iii) he is not a resident under section 6(1) for that year.

**WN-3: Application**
- Income other than foreign sources = **₹18,00,000 > ₹15,00,000** ✓
- Not liable to tax in any other country ✓
- Not resident under section 6(1) ✓

All conditions of section 6(1A) are satisfied, so Mr. Rohit is a **deemed resident**.

**WN-4: Type of residence [Section 6(6)(d)]**
A person who is a resident by virtue of section 6(1A) is treated as **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**.

**Conclusion:** Mr. Rohit is a **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** for A.Y. 2025-26 under section 6(1A) read with section 6(6)(d).

**Answer:** **RNOR** (deemed resident u/s 6(1A)).

*(Full-marks tip: section 6(1A) applies only to an Indian citizen, only when income (other than foreign-source income) exceeds ₹15 lakh, and only when he is not tax-liable abroad and is not otherwise resident u/s 6(1). Any student who forgets that such a deemed resident is automatically RNOR loses the concluding mark.)*

### Q9. Ch: Residential Status — Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (Marks: 4) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Kiran, after living abroad for several years, returned to India and was **present in India for 200 days** during P.Y. 2024-25, thereby satisfying the basic conditions of section 6(1). His stay in India during the **seven preceding previous years** aggregated **700 days**, and he was **non-resident in 6** of the 10 preceding previous years. Determine whether he is Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR) or Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) for A.Y. 2025-26.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Additional conditions for "ordinarily resident" [Section 6(6)(a)]**
A resident individual is **Resident and Ordinarily Resident** only if he satisfies **both**:
- Condition (I): **Resident** in India in **at least 2** out of the **10** previous years preceding the relevant P.Y.; **and**
- Condition (II): In India for **730 days or more** during the **7** previous years preceding the relevant P.Y.

If **either** additional condition is **not** satisfied, the individual is **RNOR**.

**WN-2: Application to facts**
- Condition (I): He was non-resident in **6** of the 10 preceding years, i.e. **resident in 4** of the 10 — this satisfies "resident in at least 2 of 10" ✓
- Condition (II): Stay in the **7** preceding years = **700 days < 730 days** — **not satisfied** ✗

Since Condition (II) is not satisfied, he fails the test for "ordinarily resident".

**Conclusion:** Mr. Kiran is **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** for A.Y. 2025-26.

**Answer:** **RNOR**, because although he is resident (200 days) and satisfies the "resident in ≥2 of 10 years" test, his 700-day stay in the seven preceding years falls short of the 730-day requirement.

*(Full-marks tip: both additional conditions are cumulative for ROR — failing even one makes the person RNOR. State the exact thresholds "2 out of 10 years" and "730 days in 7 years".)*

### Q10. Ch: Residential Status — Residential Status of HUF, Firm and Company (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain how the **residential status** of (i) a Hindu Undivided Family (HUF), (ii) a firm/association of persons, and (iii) a company is determined under the Income-tax Act, 1961.

**Answer:**

**(i) Hindu Undivided Family — Section 6(2).** An HUF is **resident** in India in any previous year in every case **except** where the **control and management of its affairs is situated wholly outside India** during that year. Thus, even partial control and management in India makes the HUF resident. Further, a resident HUF is **Resident and Ordinarily Resident** only if its **karta** satisfies **both** the additional conditions of section 6(6) (resident in ≥2 of 10 preceding years **and** in India for ≥730 days in the 7 preceding years); otherwise the HUF is **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident**.

**(ii) Firm / Association of Persons — Section 6(2) and 6(4).** A firm or AOP is **resident** in India in any previous year except where the **control and management of its affairs is situated wholly outside India** during that year. Under **section 6(4)**, every other person (including a firm/AOP) is resident unless control and management is wholly outside India. A firm/AOP is **either resident or non-resident** — the RNOR concept does **not** apply to them.

**(iii) Company — Section 6(3).** A company is resident in India in any previous year if:
(a) it is an **Indian company**; **or**
(b) its **Place of Effective Management (POEM)** — the place where **key management and commercial decisions** necessary for the conduct of the business as a whole are, in substance, made — is **in India** in that year.

| Person | Resident unless… | RNOR possible? |
|---|---|---|
| HUF | control & management **wholly** outside India | Yes (test on karta) |
| Firm / AOP | control & management **wholly** outside India | No |
| Company | not Indian **and** POEM outside India | No |

**Conclusion:** For HUF, firm and AOP the touchstone is where **control and management** lies (wholly outside → non-resident), while for a company it is **incorporation in India or POEM in India**; only HUF (among these) can be RNOR.

*(Full-marks tip: use the word "wholly" for HUF/firm/AOP — even a fraction of control in India makes them resident. For a company, define POEM in the statutory language. Stating that RNOR applies only to individuals and HUFs earns the distinguishing mark.)*

### Q11. Ch: Income under Salaries — Gratuity, Leave Encashment and Pension of a Retiring Employee (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Girish retired on **31st December 2024** from Zenith Ltd. (a private company **not covered** by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972) after **30 years and 7 months** of service. His salary for the last 10 months (unchanged throughout) was: **Basic pay ₹48,000 p.m.** and **Dearness Allowance ₹20,000 p.m.** (fully forming part of retirement benefits as per terms of employment). On retirement he received the following. Compute the amount **taxable under the head "Salaries"** in respect of these retirement benefits for A.Y. 2025-26.

| # | Retirement benefit | Details |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gratuity | ₹12,00,000 received |
| 2 | Leave encashment | ₹9,00,000; leave entitlement 30 days for each completed year of service; leave availed during service 420 days |
| 3 | Uncommuted pension | ₹15,000 p.m. from 1 Jan 2025 |
| 4 | Commuted pension | Commuted 40% of pension on 1 Feb 2025 and received ₹6,00,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-0: "Salary" for computing exemptions** = Basic + DA forming part of retirement benefits + commission (% of turnover). Here = ₹48,000 + ₹20,000 = **₹68,000 p.m.** Average salary of the last 10 months = **₹68,000**. Completed years of service = **30** (ignore 7 months).

**WN-1: Gratuity — Section 10(10)(iii) (not covered by Gratuity Act)**
Exempt = **least** of:
| Limit | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| (a) Notified monetary limit | 20,00,000 |
| (b) Actual gratuity received | 12,00,000 |
| (c) ½ month's avg. salary × completed years = ½ × 68,000 × 30 | 10,20,000 |
Least = **₹10,20,000**. **Taxable gratuity = 12,00,000 − 10,20,000 = ₹1,80,000.**

**WN-2: Leave encashment — Section 10(10AA)(ii) (non-Government employee)**
Cash equivalent of leave to credit: entitlement 30 days per completed year (max 30 days/year permitted) = 30 × 30 = 900 days; less leave availed 420 days = **480 days = 16 months**.
Exempt = **least** of:
| Limit | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| (a) Notified monetary limit | 25,00,000 |
| (b) Actual leave encashment received | 9,00,000 |
| (c) 10 months' average salary = 10 × 68,000 | 6,80,000 |
| (d) Cash equivalent of leave to credit = 16 × 68,000 | 10,88,000 |
Least = **₹6,80,000**. **Taxable leave encashment = 9,00,000 − 6,80,000 = ₹2,20,000.**

**WN-3: Uncommuted (monthly) pension — fully taxable**
- January 2025 (before commutation): ₹15,000
- February & March 2025: after commuting 40%, pension reduces to 60% = ₹9,000 p.m. → ₹9,000 × 2 = ₹18,000
**Taxable uncommuted pension = 15,000 + 18,000 = ₹33,000.**

**WN-4: Commuted pension — Section 10(10A)(ii)**
As Mr. Girish **also receives gratuity**, exemption = **1/3 of the full value** of the commuted pension.

### Q12. Ch: Income under Salaries — Taxability of Gratuity across Employee Categories (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the taxability of **gratuity** received by (i) a Government employee, (ii) a non-Government employee covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, and (iii) a non-Government employee not covered by that Act, under section 10(10).

**Answer:**

Gratuity is a retirement benefit chargeable under the head "Salaries", with exemption governed by **section 10(10)**.

**(i) Government employee — Section 10(10)(i).** Death-cum-retirement gratuity received by an employee of the Central/State Government, local authority is **wholly exempt** without any monetary ceiling.

**(ii) Non-Government employee covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 — Section 10(10)(ii).** Exempt = **least** of:
- Notified limit ₹20,00,000;
- Actual gratuity received;
- **15/26 × last drawn salary × number of completed years of service (or part thereof exceeding 6 months)**.
Here "salary" = **basic + dearness allowance** (whether or not it forms part of retirement benefits), and the divisor is **26** (working days in a month).

**(iii) Non-Government employee not covered by the Act — Section 10(10)(iii).** Exempt = **least** of:
- Notified limit ₹20,00,000;
- Actual gratuity received;
- **½ month's average salary of the last 10 months × completed years of service** (part of a year is ignored).
Here "salary" = **basic + DA forming part of retirement benefits + commission as a fixed % of turnover**.

| Category | Section | Key exemption limit |
|---|---|---|
| Government employee | 10(10)(i) | Fully exempt |
| Covered by Gratuity Act | 10(10)(ii) | 15/26 × last salary × yrs; part-year >6 months counts |
| Not covered by Act | 10(10)(iii) | ½ × avg. 10-month salary × completed yrs |

**Conclusion:** Government gratuity is fully exempt; for others the exemption is the least of three limits, differing in the salary definition, the multiplier and the treatment of part-years.

*(Full-marks tip: highlight the two key differences between (ii) and (iii) — the "15/26 × last drawn salary" vs "½ × average 10-month salary" formula, and part-year over 6 months counting only under the Gratuity Act. The ₹20 lakh overall cap applies cumulatively to all gratuities received.)*

### Q14. Ch: Income under Salaries — Pension: Commuted vs Uncommuted (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between **commuted** and **uncommuted** pension and explain their taxability under section 10(10A) for Government and non-Government employees.

**Answer:**

**Meaning.** **Uncommuted pension** is the **periodic (monthly) pension** received by a retired employee. **Commuted pension** is a **lump sum** received in lieu of surrendering (commuting) the whole or a part of the future periodic pension.

**Taxability — Uncommuted pension.** Uncommuted (monthly) pension is **fully taxable** as salary for **both** Government and non-Government employees.

 - If the employee **also receives gratuity**: exempt = **1/3 of the full value** of the pension which he is entitled to receive.
 - If the employee does **not** receive gratuity: exempt = **1/2 of the full value** of the pension.

| Type | Government employee | Non-Government employee |
|---|---|---|
| Uncommuted pension | Fully taxable | Fully taxable |
| Commuted pension | Fully exempt | Exempt 1/3 (if gratuity also received) or 1/2 (if no gratuity) of full value |

**Conclusion:** Monthly pension is always taxable; commuted pension is fully exempt for Government employees, and for others is exempt to the extent of 1/3 (with gratuity) or 1/2 (without gratuity) of its full value.

### Q16. Ch: Income under Salaries — Profits in Lieu of Salary (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** What is meant by **"profits in lieu of salary"** under section 17(3)? State the items included and any specific exclusion.

**Answer:**

Under **section 17(1)(iv)**, "salary" includes any **profits in lieu of or in addition to any salary or wages**, which is defined in **section 17(3)** to include:

**(i)** The amount of any **compensation** due to or received by an assessee from his employer or former employer **in connection with the termination** of his employment or **modification** of the terms and conditions relating thereto.

**(ii)** Any **payment** (other than specified exempt receipts) due to or received by an assessee from an employer/former employer, or from a **provident or other fund** (to the extent it does not consist of the employee's own contributions or interest thereon), including any sum received under a **Keyman insurance policy** (including bonus thereon).

**Conclusion:** Profits in lieu of salary broadly cover termination compensation, taxable fund payments and pre-/post-employment receipts, but **exclude** amounts that are separately exempt under section 10.

### Q18. Ch: Income from House Property — Composite Letting of Building with Amenities (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Mr. Anand owns a commercial building at Hyderabad which he lets out to a firm together with air-conditioning plant, lifts, furniture and a captive power back-up. The lease deed provides a single consolidated rent of ₹1,50,000 per month covering both the building and the amenities/facilities. State, with reasons and reference to the relevant statutory provisions, under which head of income the rent is chargeable to tax. Would your answer change if the lease deed had shown ₹90,000 as rent for the building and ₹60,000 separately as charges for the amenities?

**Answer:**
The chargeability of composite rent depends on whether the letting of the building is **separable** from the letting of the other assets/amenities, applying the tests laid down under sections 22, 56(2)(ii) and 56(2)(iii) of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

Where a building is let out along with plant, machinery, furniture or amenities, the rent so received is called **composite rent**. The treatment is as follows:

| Situation | Head of income | Statutory basis |
|---|---|---|
| Letting of building and amenities are **inseparable** (i.e., the tenant would not accept one without the other and a single indivisible rent is charged) | Entire composite rent is taxable under **"Profits and Gains of Business or Profession"** or **"Income from Other Sources"** (as the case may be) — **not** under house property | Section 56(2)(iii); the building income is *not* assessable under section 22 |
| Letting of building and amenities are **separable** | Rent for the **building** is taxable under **"Income from House Property" (Sec. 22)**, and the rent/charges attributable to amenities, plant, furniture etc. is taxable under **"Profits and Gains of Business or Profession"** or **"Income from Other Sources" [Sec. 56(2)(ii)/(iii)]** | Sections 22 and 56(2)(ii)/(iii) |

**Application to the facts:** Here a **single consolidated rent** of ₹1,50,000 p.m. is charged for the building together with the air-conditioning plant, lifts, furniture and power back-up, and these amenities are so intrinsically connected with the enjoyment of the building that the letting is composite and **inseparable**. Since the letting of the building is not separable from the letting of the plant, machinery and amenities, the **entire rent of ₹18,00,000** for the year is chargeable under the head **"Income from Other Sources"** under section 56(2)(iii), and **no deduction under section 24 (standard deduction / interest) is available**; only the actual amenity-related expenses, repairs, depreciation etc. under sections 30 to 38 are deductible.

**If the rent is bifurcated** (₹90,000 for the building and ₹60,000 for amenities): the two lettings become **separable**. The rent attributable to the building (₹90,000 × 12 = ₹10,80,000, subject to the GAV/municipal-value tests) would be taxable under **"Income from House Property"** with the benefit of the 30% standard deduction under section 24(a) and interest under section 24(b); the ₹60,000 × 12 = ₹7,20,000 for amenities would be taxable under **"Income from Other Sources"** (or PGBP), after allowing the related expenses and depreciation.

**Conclusion:** The consolidated inseparable rent of ₹18,00,000 is taxable under "Income from Other Sources" (Sec. 56(2)(iii)); on bifurcation, the building portion shifts to "Income from House Property" and only the amenity portion remains under "Other Sources".

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the crisp separable/inseparable test and the correct section (56(2)(iii) for inseparable). A common deduction is wrongly taxing the whole composite rent under house property, or forgetting that once inseparable, Section 24 standard deduction is lost.)*

### Q21. Ch: Income from House Property — More than Two Self-Occupied Houses (Deemed Let-Out) (Marks: 4) [Application]

The treatment of more than two self-occupied houses is governed by **section 23(4)** read with sections 23(2), 24(a) and 24(b).

Under **section 23(2)**, where a house (or part) is in the occupation of the owner for his own residence and is not actually let, its Annual Value is taken as **Nil**. ### Q25. Ch: Income from House Property — Deemed Ownership and Income Taxable under Other Heads (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** "The legal owner is not always the person chargeable under the head Income from House Property." In the light of this statement, explain the concept of **deemed ownership** under section 27 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, and give any two situations where the annual value of a building is **not** chargeable under the head "Income from House Property".

**Answer:**
Ordinarily, under **section 22**, the annual value of a property consisting of buildings or lands appurtenant thereto, of which the assessee is the **owner**, is chargeable under the head "Income from House Property". However, **section 27** creates the concept of **deemed ownership**, whereby certain persons who are not the legal owners are nevertheless treated as owners and made chargeable.

**Deemed owners under section 27** include:

| Clause | Person deemed to be the owner |
|---|---|
| Sec. 27(i) | An individual who **transfers a house property to his/her spouse** (otherwise than for adequate consideration or under an agreement to live apart) or to a **minor child** (not being a married daughter), without adequate consideration |
| Sec. 27(ii) | The **holder of an impartible estate** in respect of the property comprised in the estate |
| Sec. 27(iii) | A **member of a co-operative society, company or AOP** to whom a building is allotted or leased under a house-building scheme |
| Sec. 27(iiia)/(iiib) | A person in possession of a property under **section 53A of the Transfer of Property Act** (part performance), and a person acquiring rights under a lease for **not less than 12 years** |

**Situations where annual value is NOT charged under house property:**
1. **Property used by the owner for his own business or profession** — under **section 22 itself**, the annual value of property occupied for the purpose of any business/profession carried on by the owner (the profits of which are chargeable to tax) is **not** charged under house property.
2. **Property held as stock-in-trade** by a builder/dealer — the annual value of such property (used for the business of letting is different) is generally treated under PGBP; further, under **section 23(5)**, where property held as stock-in-trade is not let during the year, its annual value is Nil for **two years** from the end of the financial year of the completion certificate.
3. Income from letting of a building **inseparable** from plant/machinery — taxable under PGBP or Other Sources [Sec. 56(2)(iii)], not house property.

**Conclusion:** Section 27 extends the charge under house property to deemed owners, while sections 22 and 23(5)/56(2)(iii) carve out situations where the annual value escapes charge under house property and is dealt with under other heads.

### Q26. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — Cash Basis vs Mercantile Basis (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the significance of the **method of accounting** under section 145 for computing income under the head "Profits and Gains of Business or Profession". Distinguish between the **cash basis** and the **mercantile basis** of accounting, and state the extent to which an assessee is free to choose a method. Also mention the role of the notified Income Computation and Disclosure Standards (ICDS).

**Answer:**
Under **section 145(1)**, income chargeable under the head "Profits and Gains of Business or Profession" or "Income from Other Sources" shall be computed in accordance with **either the cash or the mercantile system of accounting** regularly employed by the assessee. Thus the assessee is free to choose, but the method must be **regularly and consistently followed**; a hybrid method is not permitted.

| Basis | Cash system | Mercantile (accrual) system |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition of income | Income recorded only **when actually received** | Income recorded **when it accrues/is earned**, whether or not received |
| Recognition of expense | Expense recorded only **when actually paid** | Expense recorded **when the liability is incurred**, whether or not paid |
| Outstanding/prepaid items | **Ignored** (no debtors/creditors, outstanding or prepaid entries) | **Accounted for** (debtors, creditors, outstanding, prepaid, provisions all recognised) |
| Typical users | Professionals (doctors, lawyers) | Businesses, companies |

**Freedom of choice and its limits:** The choice under section 145(1) rests with the assessee, but:
(i) The method must be **regularly employed**; and
(ii) Under **section 145(3)**, if the Assessing Officer is **not satisfied** about the correctness or completeness of the accounts, or where the method has not been regularly followed, or income has not been computed as per the notified ICDS, he may make a **best judgment assessment under section 144**.

**Role of ICDS [Sec. 145(2)]:** The Central Government has notified **ten Income Computation and Disclosure Standards (ICDS)** applicable to assessees following the **mercantile system** for computing income under PGBP/Other Sources. ICDS override the accounting policies only for **computation of taxable income** (not for maintaining books) and apply irrespective of the accounting standards followed for financial reporting. ICDS do **not** apply to an assessee following the **cash basis**.

**Conclusion:** Section 145 permits either the cash or mercantile method (consistently followed), but income of a mercantile-basis assessee must further conform to the notified ICDS, failing which the AO may resort to best-judgment assessment under section 144.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the section 145(1)/(2)/(3) structure and the point that ICDS apply only to the mercantile basis. Common deduction: saying a hybrid method is allowed, or omitting the consequences under section 145(3)/144.)*

### Q30. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — Certain Deductions Only on Actual Payment (Sec. 43B) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the scheme of **section 43B** of the Income-tax Act, 1961, listing the categories of expenses covered. Also explain the special rule introduced in respect of amounts payable to **micro and small enterprises** and its interaction with section 43B.

**Answer:**
**Section 43B** provides that certain deductions, though otherwise allowable, shall be allowed **only in the previous year in which the sum is actually paid**, irrespective of the year in which the liability was incurred under the mercantile system.

**Categories of expenses covered by section 43B:**

| Clause | Nature of payment |
|---|---|
| (a) | Any **tax, duty, cess or fee** (e.g., GST, customs duty) payable under any law |
| (b) | **Employer's contribution** to any provident fund, superannuation fund, gratuity fund or other welfare fund |
| (c) | **Bonus or commission** payable to employees for services rendered |
| (d)/(e) | **Interest on any loan/borrowing** from a public financial institution, scheduled bank, NBFC, etc. |
| (f) | Sum payable in lieu of **leave encashment** to employees |
| (g) | Sum payable to the **Indian Railways** for use of railway assets |

**General rule and its proviso:** The above sums are deductible in the year of **actual payment**. However, the **proviso** allows the deduction on **accrual basis** if the amount is actually paid **on or before the due date of filing the return under section 139(1)** (and proof of payment is furnished).

**Special rule — clause (h) for micro and small enterprises [inserted w.e.f. A.Y. 2024-25]:**
Section 43B(h) provides that any sum payable by the assessee to a **micro or small enterprise** beyond the time limit specified in **section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006** (i.e., the agreed period not exceeding 45 days, or 15 days where there is no agreement) shall be allowed as a deduction **only in the year of actual payment**. **Conclusion:** Section 43B enforces the "pay-and-claim" principle for statutory and specified dues; and for dues to micro/small enterprises under clause (h), the stricter MSMED-Act time limit (not the return due date) governs allowability.

### Q34. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — Presumptive Taxation: Sections 44AD vs 44ADA (Marks: 6) [Theory]
**Question:** Compare and contrast the presumptive taxation schemes under **section 44AD** (eligible business) and **section 44ADA** (eligible profession). Explain the enhanced turnover/receipt thresholds and the consequence of declaring income **lower** than the presumptive rate. Also state the effect of **opting out** of section 44AD once opted in.

**Answer:**
Sections 44AD and 44ADA provide **presumptive taxation** schemes to relieve small businesses and professionals from the burden of maintaining detailed books and getting them audited.

| Basis | Section 44AD (eligible business) | Section 44ADA (eligible profession) |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible assessee | Resident **individual, HUF or partnership firm** (not LLP) | Resident **individual or partnership firm** (not LLP) carrying on a profession referred to in section 44AA(1) |
| Eligible activity | Any business **except** plying/hiring goods carriages (covered by 44AE), agency business, and commission/brokerage | Notified professions — legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, etc. |
| Threshold | Turnover up to **₹2 crore** (₹**3 crore** where cash receipts ≤ 5% of turnover) | Gross receipts up to **₹50 lakh** (₹**75 lakh** where cash receipts ≤ 5% of gross receipts) |
| Presumptive rate | **8%** of turnover (**6%** for receipts through banking/digital modes) | **50%** of gross receipts |
| Deductions | All deductions u/s 30–38, **including depreciation, deemed already allowed**; WDV reduced accordingly | Same — all deductions deemed allowed |

**Declaring income lower than the presumptive rate:** An assessee who claims that his income is **lower** than the presumptive rate (8%/6% or 50%) **and** whose total income **exceeds the basic exemption limit** is required to **maintain books of account under section 44AA** and get them **audited under section 44AB**.

**Effect of opting out of section 44AD [Sec. 44AD(4)/(5)]:** Where an eligible assessee declares profit under section 44AD for a previous year but **does not** declare presumptive income for any of the **five consecutive assessment years** succeeding it, he shall **not be eligible** to claim the benefit of section 44AD for **five assessment years** subsequent to the year in which he opted out; and, if during that period his total income exceeds the basic exemption limit, he must maintain books u/s 44AA and get them audited u/s 44AB. **This "lock-in / five-year debarment" applies to section 44AD only — there is no corresponding provision in section 44ADA.**

**Conclusion:** Both schemes ease compliance, but 44AD applies to business at 8%/6% with a ₹2/₹3 crore ceiling and carries a five-year opt-out debarment, whereas 44ADA applies to professionals at 50% with a ₹50/₹75 lakh ceiling and has no such debarment.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the enhanced ₹3 crore / ₹75 lakh thresholds (5% cash condition), the 6% digital rate, and the section 44AD(4) five-year lock-in that has NO 44ADA counterpart. Common deduction: quoting only the old ₹2 crore/₹50 lakh limits or applying the lock-in to 44ADA.)*

### Q37. Ch: Capital Gains — Sec 54F: conditions, restrictions & withdrawal (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the conditions to be satisfied for claiming exemption under Section 54F, the circumstances in which the exemption is **withdrawn**, and the treatment of the unutilised amount deposited in the Capital Gains Account Scheme.

**Answer:** Section 54F grants exemption from long-term capital gain arising on transfer of **any long-term capital asset, other than a residential house**, where the net consideration is invested in a residential house in India.

**Conditions to be satisfied:**
1. The assessee is an **individual or HUF** (Sec 54F(1)).
2. The asset transferred is a **long-term** capital asset **not being a residential house**.
3. The assessee purchases **one** residential house in India within **1 year before or 2 years after** the date of transfer, or **constructs** one within **3 years** after the date of transfer.
4. On the date of transfer, the assessee does **not own more than one** residential house (other than the new house), and
5. The assessee does not, within **2 years**, purchase, or within **3 years**, construct, **any other** residential house (Sec 54F(2)).

**Quantum:** If the net consideration is fully invested, the **entire** capital gain is exempt; if only part is invested, exemption is **proportionate** = Capital Gain × (Amount invested ÷ Net consideration).

**Withdrawal of exemption:** The exemption is withdrawn and the amount earlier exempted is **taxed as LTCG in the year of default** if —

| Trigger (Section) | Consequence |
|---|---|
| New house **transferred within 3 years** of purchase/construction (Sec 54F(3)) | Earlier-exempted gain becomes LTCG of the year of transfer of the new house |
| Assessee **purchases another house within 2 years / constructs within 3 years** (Sec 54F(2)) | Earlier-exempted gain becomes LTCG of the year in which the other house is acquired/constructed |

**Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS):** The amount not utilised for purchase/construction **before the due date of filing return u/s 139(1)** must be deposited in the CGAS to claim exemption. If the deposited amount is **not utilised** within the specified period (2/3 years), the **unutilised amount** is charged as **LTCG of the previous year in which the period of 3 years expires**.

**Conclusion:** Sec 54F exemption is conditional and carries a lock-in; violation of the ownership or lock-in conditions results in the exempted gain being taxed in the year of default.

### Q43. Ch: Clubbing & Set-off — Carry forward: business loss vs unabsorbed depreciation priority (Marks: 6) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between **carry forward and set-off of business loss (Sec 72)** and **unabsorbed depreciation (Sec 32(2))**, and state the **order of priority** in which brought-forward business loss, current depreciation and unabsorbed depreciation are set off against business income.

**Answer:** Both a brought-forward business loss and unabsorbed depreciation reduce future profits, but the statutory conditions differ materially.

| Basis | Business loss — Sec 72 | Unabsorbed depreciation — Sec 32(2) |
|---|---|---|
| Set-off against | Only **profits and gains of business/profession** in the carry-forward years | Income under **any head** (except salaries) |
| Maximum carry-forward period | **8 assessment years** immediately succeeding the year of loss | **No time limit** — indefinite |
| Continuity of business | Business need not be continued (post-amendment for most; loss set-off does not require same business) | Business need not be continued |
| Return filing condition | Loss must be **determined in a return filed u/s 139(3) within due date u/s 139(1)** | **No** such condition — can be carried forward even if return is belated |
| Nature | A real loss | A notional/statutory allowance treated as current-year depreciation of the following year |

**Order of set-off against current business income (as laid down in *CIT v. Mother India Refrigeration Industries*):**
1. **Current year depreciation** (Sec 32(1)) — first charge;
2. **Brought-forward business loss** (Sec 72) — next, because it can be carried forward only for 8 years and hence should be exhausted first; then
3. **Unabsorbed depreciation** (Sec 32(2)) — last, as it has no time limit and can be carried forward indefinitely.

**Conclusion:** Setting off brought-forward business loss **before** unabsorbed depreciation preserves the taxpayer's ability to use the time-barred loss within the 8-year window while retaining the indefinitely-carriable depreciation for later years.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the correct **priority order** (current depreciation → b/f business loss → unabsorbed depreciation) with the rationale that the time-limited item is exhausted first, and the "any head except salary" feature of Sec 32(2). Common deduction: applying the 8-year limit to unabsorbed depreciation.)*

### Q49. Ch: Clubbing & Set-off — Speculation loss & capital loss carry forward (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the provisions relating to carry forward and set-off of (a) **speculation business loss (Sec 73)** and (b) **capital losses (Sec 74)**, stating the permitted set-off and carry-forward periods.

**Answer:**

**(a) Speculation business loss — Sec 73:** A speculation business is deemed distinct and separate. Losses of a speculation business have a **restricted** treatment:
- **Set-off:** Loss from a speculation business can be set off **only against profits of another speculation business** in the same year (Sec 73(1)); it cannot be set off against any other business income or any other head.
- **Carry forward:** Unabsorbed speculation loss may be carried forward and set off **only against speculation profits** of subsequent years, for a maximum of **4 assessment years** immediately succeeding the year of the loss (Sec 73(2)/(4)).
- Continuation of the speculation business is **not** required, but the return must be filed within the due date u/s 139(1) to carry forward.

**(b) Capital losses — Sec 74:**

| Type of loss | Set-off in the same year (Sec 70/71) | Carry forward & set-off (Sec 74) |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-term capital loss (STCL)** | Against STCG or LTCG (not against other heads) | Against STCG or LTCG of subsequent years |
| **Long-term capital loss (LTCL)** | Only against **LTCG** | Only against **LTCG** of subsequent years |

- **Carry-forward period:** Capital losses may be carried forward for **8 assessment years** immediately succeeding the year of loss.
- Carry forward is allowed only if the return of loss is filed within the due date u/s 139(1) (Sec 80).

**Conclusion:** Speculation loss and long-term capital loss are the most "ring-fenced" losses — the former usable only against speculation profits (4 years) and the latter only against LTCG (8 years) — whereas STCL enjoys the wider set-off against any capital gain.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the precise carry-forward periods — **4 years** for speculation loss, **8 years** for capital loss — and the rule that **LTCL can be set off only against LTCG**. Common deduction: allowing STCL/LTCL set-off against other heads.)*

### Q60. Ch: Advance Tax / TDS / TCS — Section 194Q vs. Section 206C(1H) (Marks: 5) [Application]

### Q68. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Best-judgement assessment (Sec. 144) & faceless assessment (Sec. 144B) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the circumstances in which the Assessing Officer may make a **best-judgement assessment** under Section 144, and the safeguard available to the assessee. Briefly note the concept of faceless assessment under Section 144B.

**Answer:** **Best-judgement assessment [Sec. 144]:** The Assessing Officer is empowered to make an assessment **to the best of his judgement**, after taking into account all relevant material he has gathered, in the following circumstances:

1. the assessee **fails to make the return** required u/s 139(1) and has not filed a belated or updated return u/s 139(4)/139(8A); or
2. the assessee **fails to comply with all the terms of a notice u/s 142(1)** (calling for return/accounts/information) or with a **direction for special audit u/s 142(2A)**; or
3. the assessee, having filed a return, **fails to comply with all the terms of a notice u/s 143(2)** (scrutiny).

In such a case the AO determines the total income/loss and the sum payable on the basis of his best judgement.

**Safeguard (opportunity of being heard):** Before completing a best-judgement assessment, the AO must give the assessee an **opportunity of being heard by serving a show-cause notice** as to why the assessment should not be completed to the best of judgement [proviso to Sec. 144(1)]. **Faceless assessment [Sec. 144B]:** To eliminate the personal interface between the assessee and the Assessing Officer, assessments u/s 143(3) and 144 are to be made in a **faceless manner** through the **National Faceless Assessment Centre (NFAC)**, with automated allocation of cases, team-based assessment, dynamic jurisdiction, and communication only through electronic mode. The assessee is given an opportunity to respond to notices/draft assessment order electronically before the order is finalised.

**Conclusion:** A best-judgement assessment u/s 144 may be made on failure to file the return or to comply with notices u/s 142(1)/143(2), subject to a mandatory hearing; such assessments are now generally conducted through the faceless mechanism u/s 144B.

| B | Mixed supply (highest rate) | 3,000 | 28% | 840 |
| | **Total GST payable** | | | **11,640** |

**Answer:** Case A is a **composite supply** taxed at 18% → **₹10,800**; Case B is a **mixed supply** taxed at 28% → **₹840**. Total GST payable = **₹11,640**.

### Q73. Ch: GST – Charge & RCM — Forward charge vs reverse charge (Sec 9(3)/9(4)) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between **forward charge** and **reverse charge** under GST. State the two statutory sources of reverse charge under Section 9 of the CGST Act, 2017 and explain whether a person who is required to pay tax under reverse charge is compulsorily required to register.

**Answer:**
Under GST, "reverse charge" is defined in **Section 2(98)** as the liability to pay tax by the **recipient** of the supply of goods or services instead of the supplier, under Section 9(3)/9(4) of the CGST Act or Section 5(3)/5(4) of the IGST Act.

| Basis | Forward charge | Reverse charge |
|-------|----------------|----------------|
| Who pays tax | The **supplier** collects and pays GST | The **recipient** pays GST directly to the Government |
| Statutory basis | Section 9(1) – the default charging mechanism | Section 9(3) and Section 9(4) |
| Invoice | Supplier issues a tax invoice charging GST | Recipient issues a self-invoice under Section 31(3)(f) where supplier is unregistered |
| Payment of tax | Can be paid using ITC or cash | Must be paid in **cash**; cannot be discharged through ITC |

**Two statutory sources of reverse charge under Section 9:**
1. **Section 9(3):** Government notifies specific categories of goods/services (e.g., GTA services, legal services by an advocate, sponsorship, services of a director to the company) on which the recipient pays tax.
2. **Section 9(4):** Tax on supply of specified goods/services by an **unregistered supplier** to a specified registered recipient (notified classes, e.g. promoters in real estate) is payable by the recipient.

**Registration:** As per **Section 24(iii)**, a person required to pay tax under reverse charge is **compulsorily required to register**, irrespective of the threshold limit under Section 22.

**Conclusion:** Forward charge is the default (supplier pays); reverse charge (Section 9(3)/9(4)) shifts the liability to the recipient, who must register compulsorily and discharge the tax in cash.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite Section 2(98), 9(3), 9(4) and Section 24(iii) together, and mention that RCM liability is payable in cash, not through ITC. Missing the compulsory-registration point is the most common deduction.)*

### Q76. Ch: GST – Charge & RCM — Tax paid by Electronic Commerce Operator (Sec 9(5)) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the special provision under **Section 9(5)** of the CGST Act, 2017 regarding tax payable by an electronic commerce operator (ECO). Give any two examples of notified services and state whether such an ECO is required to register.

**Answer:**
**Section 9(5)** of the CGST Act, 2017 empowers the Government to notify categories of **services** the supply of which through an **electronic commerce operator (ECO)** shall be taxed as if the **ECO is the supplier liable to pay tax**. In such cases, all provisions of the Act apply to the ECO as though it were the person liable for paying tax in relation to the supply of such services — even though the actual service is provided by another person through the ECO's platform.

**Two examples of notified services under Section 9(5):**
1. Transportation of passengers by radio-taxi, motorcab, maxicab, motorcycle or omnibus (e.g. cab-aggregator services).
2. Providing accommodation in hotels, inns, guest houses, etc., where the person supplying such service through the ECO is **not liable to register** under Section 22(1) (i.e. below the threshold).
(Also covered: housekeeping services such as plumbing/carpentry where the supplier is unregistered; restaurant services supplied through the ECO other than at specified premises.)

**Registration:** Where an ECO is liable to pay tax under Section 9(5), it must obtain registration; further, if the ECO does not have a physical presence in the taxable territory, a person representing it (or an appointed person) is liable to pay tax. The individual supplier providing the notified service through the ECO need not pay tax on that supply.

**Conclusion:** Under Section 9(5) the tax liability on notified services supplied through an ECO shifts wholly to the ECO, which is treated as the deemed supplier for all purposes.

*(Full-marks tip: Emphasise that Section 9(5) applies only to notified **services** and that the ECO is the deemed supplier — distinct from TCS under Section 52. Give at least two correct notified examples.)*

### Q84. Ch: GST – Time & Value of Supply — Value of supply and post-supply discount (Rule 27/Sec 15(3)) (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** (a) Where the consideration for a supply is **not wholly in money**, state briefly the sequence for determining the value of supply under Rule 27 of the CGST Rules, 2017. (b) Then compute the value of supply: A new machine having a list price of ₹5,00,000 is supplied by Delta Ltd. in exchange for an old machine plus money; the customer pays ₹4,20,000 in cash and gives an old machine whose open market value is ₹90,000. The open market value of the new machine is not ascertainable, but a machine of like kind and quality is sold for ₹5,05,000. (c) Also state the condition under Section 15(3)(b) for a **post-supply discount** to be excluded from value.

**Answer:**

**(a) Rule 27 – consideration not wholly in money.** The value is determined in the following sequence:
(i) the **open market value (OMV)** of the supply;
(ii) if OMV is not available, the **sum of consideration in money plus the money equivalent** of the non-monetary consideration, if such amount is known at the time of supply;
(iii) if the value is still not determinable, the value of supply of **goods/services of like kind and quality**;
(iv) if none of the above, the sum of money consideration plus money equivalent determined under **Rule 30 or Rule 31**.

**(b) Computation:**

**WN-1:** OMV of the new machine is not ascertainable, so Rule 27(a) fails. Under Rule 27(b), value = money consideration + money equivalent of non-monetary consideration, **if known** = ₹4,20,000 (cash) + ₹90,000 (OMV of old machine) = **₹5,10,000**. Since this amount is ascertainable at the time of supply, Rule 27(b) applies and there is no need to go to the "like kind and quality" value of ₹5,05,000.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|-------------|------------|
| Cash consideration | 4,20,000 |
| Add: Money value of old machine (OMV) | 90,000 |
| **Value of supply (Rule 27(b))** | **5,10,000** |

**Answer (b):** Value of supply = **₹5,10,000**.

**(c) Post-supply discount [Sec 15(3)(b)]:** A discount given **after the supply** is excluded from the value only if — (i) it is established in terms of an **agreement entered into at or before the time of supply** and is specifically linked to relevant invoices; **and** (ii) the **input tax credit** attributable to the discount has been **reversed by the recipient**. If either condition fails, the discount is not deductible.

**Conclusion:** Value under Rule 27(b) is ₹5,10,000; a post-supply discount is excludible only on satisfying both conditions of Section 15(3)(b).

*(Full-marks tip: In Rule 27, jump to (b) only after OMV is unavailable, and stop there if the money-equivalent is known — do not go to like-kind value. For post-supply discounts, both the pre-agreement link and the ITC-reversal condition are mandatory; stating only one costs marks.)*

### Q89. Ch: GST – Registration — Persons Liable to be Registered (Sec 22 & 24) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the provisions relating to persons liable to be registered under the CGST Act, 2017, distinguishing the **threshold-based liability under section 22** from the **compulsory registration under section 24**.

**Answer:** Registration under GST is governed principally by sections 22 to 24 of the CGST Act, 2017.

**Liability based on aggregate turnover (Section 22):** Every supplier is liable to be registered in the State or Union territory (other than a special category State) from where he makes a taxable supply of goods or services or both, if his **aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds ₹20 lakh**. The threshold is **₹10 lakh** for the specified special category States (viz. Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura). By virtue of the enhanced threshold notification, a person **engaged exclusively in supply of goods** enjoys a threshold of **₹40 lakh** in the specified States, provided he is not making inter-State supplies, is not liable to compulsory registration and does not supply the specified goods (ice cream, pan masala, tobacco, fly ash bricks, etc.). Aggregate turnover is computed on an **all-India basis for persons having the same PAN**, and once liable, registration is required in **every State** from where a taxable supply is made. A person already registered under the earlier law and persons acquiring a business as a going concern are also liable u/s 22.

**Compulsory registration irrespective of turnover (Section 24):** Notwithstanding section 22, the following persons must obtain registration even if their turnover is below the threshold: persons making any **inter-State taxable supply** (of goods; inter-State supply of services up to the threshold is exempt from compulsory registration by notification), **casual taxable persons** and **non-resident taxable persons** making taxable supply, persons required to pay tax under **reverse charge**, persons required to pay tax u/s 9(5) (e-commerce specified services), persons required to **deduct tax (TDS) u/s 51** or **collect tax (TCS) u/s 52**, **input service distributors**, persons who supply through an **e-commerce operator** required to collect TCS, an **e-commerce operator**, persons supplying **online information database access and retrieval (OIDAR) services** from outside India to an unregistered person in India, and such other persons as may be notified.

| Basis | Section 22 | Section 24 |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Aggregate turnover exceeds threshold | Specified categories, irrespective of turnover |
| Threshold relevance | Central to liability | Not relevant — mandatory even below threshold |
| Nature | General rule | Overriding ("notwithstanding") special rule |

**Conclusion:** A person becomes liable to register when he crosses the section 22 threshold; independently, persons falling in any category listed in section 24 must register compulsorily even if below that threshold, section 24 overriding section 22.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner looks for the word "notwithstanding" and the point that section 24 overrides the turnover threshold. Mention the ₹40 lakh goods-only threshold and its conditions, and that inter-State supply of services up to the threshold is exempt from compulsory registration.)*

### Q91. Ch: GST – Registration — Compulsory Registration (Sec 24) — Application (Marks: 4) [Application]

### Q92. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Contents & Time-limit of Tax Invoice (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the essential particulars that a **tax invoice** must contain under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017, and the **time-limit** within which a tax invoice is to be issued under section 31 of the CGST Act, 2017 in the case of supply of goods and supply of services.

**Answer:** A registered person supplying taxable goods or services must issue a **tax invoice** under section 31 read with Rule 46.

**Contents of a tax invoice (Rule 46):** A tax invoice shall, inter alia, contain — (a) name, address and GSTIN of the supplier; (b) a **consecutive serial number** (not exceeding sixteen characters) unique for a financial year; (c) date of its issue; (d) name, address and GSTIN/UIN of the recipient, if registered; (e) where the recipient is unregistered and the value of supply is ₹50,000 or more, the name and address of the recipient and the address of delivery with the name of the State; (f) **HSN code** of goods or services; (g) description of goods or services; (h) quantity (for goods) and unit; (i) total value of supply; (j) **taxable value** after adjusting any discount; (k) **rate and amount of tax** (CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST, cess) charged; (l) place of supply along with the name of the State in case of inter-State supply; (m) address of delivery where it differs from place of supply; (n) whether tax is payable on **reverse charge** basis; and (o) **signature or digital signature** of the supplier or his authorised representative.

**Time-limit for issue of invoice (Section 31):**

| Nature of supply | Time-limit for issue of tax invoice |
|---|---|
| Supply of goods (involving movement) | Before or at the time of **removal** of goods for supply to the recipient |
| Supply of goods (no movement) | Before or at the time of **delivery** or making available to the recipient |
| Supply of services (general) | Within **30 days** from the date of supply of service |
| Supply of services by banks, NBFCs, insurers | Within **45 days** from the date of supply of service |
| Continuous supply of goods (successive statements/payments) | On or before the date of issue of each statement or receipt of each payment |
| Continuous supply of services (due date ascertainable) | On or before the **due date of payment** |

**Conclusion:** A tax invoice must carry the Rule 46 particulars and be issued within the time-limits of section 31 — for goods, before/at removal or delivery; for services, within 30 days (45 days for banks/NBFCs/insurers).

### Q93. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Order of Utilisation of ITC (Sec 49/49A/49B, Rule 88A) (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** M/s Meridian Ltd., a registered person, has the following output tax liability and input tax credit for the month of December, 2025. Determine the **manner of utilisation of input tax credit** and the **net GST payable in cash**, applying section 49(5) read with sections 49A, 49B and Rule 88A of the CGST Rules, 2017, so as to minimise the cash outflow.

| Particulars | IGST (₹) | CGST (₹) | SGST (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output tax liability | 1,00,000 | 80,000 | 80,000 |
| ITC available | 1,50,000 | 50,000 | 40,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Rules of set-off:** (a) IGST credit must be **fully utilised first** — towards IGST liability, and the balance towards CGST and SGST in any order/proportion [Sec 49A, Rule 88A]. (b) Only after IGST credit is exhausted can CGST credit and SGST credit be used. (c) CGST credit cannot be set off against SGST liability and vice versa [Sec 49(5)(c) & (d)]. To minimise cash, the residual IGST credit is applied to the SGST liability (since SGST credit alone is insufficient).

**WN-2 — Set-off working:**
- IGST liability ₹1,00,000 paid from IGST credit → IGST credit balance = 1,50,000 − 1,00,000 = **₹50,000**.
- Residual IGST credit ₹50,000 applied to **SGST** liability → SGST liability remaining = 80,000 − 50,000 = ₹30,000; paid from SGST credit ₹30,000 → SGST credit balance = 40,000 − 30,000 = **₹10,000 (carried forward)**.
- CGST liability ₹80,000: no IGST credit left; CGST credit ₹50,000 utilised → cash payable = 80,000 − 50,000 = **₹30,000**.

**Statement Showing Utilisation of ITC and Net GST Payable in Cash**

| Liability | Paid from IGST credit | Paid from CGST credit | Paid from SGST credit | Paid in cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IGST 1,00,000 | 1,00,000 | — | — | — |
| CGST 80,000 | — | 50,000 | — | 30,000 |
| SGST 80,000 | 50,000 | — | 30,000 | — |
| **Total** | **1,50,000** | **50,000** | **30,000** | **30,000** |

ITC carried forward: SGST **₹10,000** (CGST credit cannot be used against SGST, hence SGST credit ₹10,000 remains unutilised).

**Answer:** Net GST payable in cash = **₹30,000 (CGST)**; SGST credit of **₹10,000** is carried forward.

*(Full-marks tip: State the mandatory sequence — IGST credit first and in full. The rewarded insight is that CGST credit and SGST credit are ring-fenced, so ₹10,000 SGST credit is stranded and must be carried forward, not adjusted against CGST.)*

### Q95. Ch: GST – Input Tax Credit — Conditions for Availing ITC (Sec 16) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions that a registered person must satisfy under section 16 of the CGST Act, 2017 to be entitled to take input tax credit. Also state the consequence if the recipient fails to pay the supplier within the prescribed time.

**Answer:** Under section 16(1), every registered person is entitled to take credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business, subject to the conditions in section 16(2):
- **(a)** He is in possession of a **tax invoice or debit note** issued by a registered supplier (or other prescribed tax-paying document).
- **(aa)** The **details of the invoice/debit note have been furnished by the supplier** in his statement of outward supplies (GSTR-1) and **communicated to the recipient** (i.e., reflected in GSTR-2B).
- **(b)** He has **received the goods or services** or both (including receipt by a third person on his direction — "bill-to-ship-to").
- **(ba)** The credit **has not been restricted** in the details communicated to him under section 38.
- **(c)** The **tax charged has actually been paid** to the Government by the supplier, in cash or through utilisation of admissible ITC.
- **(d)** He has **furnished the return** under section 39.

Further, under section 16(4), ITC in respect of an invoice/debit note cannot be taken after the **30th November** following the end of the financial year to which it relates, or the date of furnishing the annual return, whichever is earlier.

**Consequence of non-payment to supplier (2nd & 3rd proviso to Sec 16(2)):** Where the recipient fails to pay the supplier the **value of supply along with tax** within **180 days** from the date of the invoice, an amount equal to the ITC availed shall be **added to his output tax liability along with interest**. The credit can be **re-availed** when the payment is subsequently made (this condition does not apply to supplies on which tax is payable under reverse charge).

**Conclusion:** ITC is available only on cumulative satisfaction of all conditions in section 16(2) within the time-limit of section 16(4); failure to pay the supplier within 180 days triggers reversal with interest, with re-credit on later payment.

*(Full-marks tip: List all conditions including the newer clauses (aa) and (ba) and the section 16(4) time-limit (30th November). The 180-day proviso with interest and re-availment is the part most often forgotten.)*

### Q97. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Time-limit for Issue of Invoice in Special Cases (Marks: 4) [Theory]

### Q100. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Returns: Forms, Due Dates & Consequences of Non-filing (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the returns to be furnished by a normal registered person under the CGST Act, 2017 — the **statement of outward supplies (GSTR-1)** and the **monthly return (GSTR-3B)** — stating the relevant forms, due dates and the **consequences of not furnishing** a return.

**Answer:** Every normal registered person is required to furnish periodic returns under sections 37 and 39 of the CGST Act, 2017.

**Statement of outward supplies — Form GSTR-1 (Section 37):** The details of outward supplies of goods or services effected during a tax period must be furnished in **Form GSTR-1**. For a person filing monthly, the due date is the **11th of the succeeding month**; a person opting for the **QRMP scheme** (aggregate turnover up to ₹5 crore) files GSTR-1 quarterly by the **13th of the month following the quarter** (with an optional IFF for the first two months). A return under section 37 cannot be furnished after **30th November** following the end of the relevant financial year, or the date of furnishing the annual return, whichever is earlier. GSTR-1 of the supplier populates the recipient's **GSTR-2B** and thereby drives ITC eligibility u/s 16(2)(aa).

**Monthly return — Form GSTR-3B (Section 39):** Every normal registered person must furnish a monthly return in **Form GSTR-3B** summarising outward supplies, ITC claimed and tax payable, and **discharge the tax** by the due date. The due date is the **20th of the succeeding month** for monthly filers; for QRMP filers it is the **22nd or 24th** of the month following the quarter (based on the State), with tax paid monthly through Form PMT-06 for the first two months.

**Consequences of non-furnishing:**
- **Late fee** under section 47 for delay in furnishing GSTR-1/GSTR-3B (prescribed per day, subject to a cap linked to turnover).
- **Interest** @ 18% p.a. under section 50 on the tax paid belatedly (on the net cash liability where the return is filed late).
- A registered person **cannot furnish GSTR-1** for a tax period if the GSTR-1 of a previous period is pending, and **cannot furnish GSTR-3B** unless the GSTR-1 for that period is filed (sequential filing).
- Continued default can lead to **restriction/blocking of e-way bill generation**, and eventually **cancellation of registration** under section 29.

**Conclusion:** A normal taxpayer files GSTR-1 (outward supplies) by the 11th and GSTR-3B (summary return with payment) by the 20th of the succeeding month (or the QRMP quarterly dates); non-filing attracts late fee u/s 47, interest u/s 50, sequential-filing restrictions and potential cancellation of registration.

*(Full-marks tip: State the exact form numbers and due dates and mention the sequential-filing bar (no GSTR-3B without GSTR-1, no current GSTR-1 with a prior one pending) — these procedural points, plus late fee (Sec 47) and interest (Sec 50), carry the marks.)*
