### Q1. Ch: Basic Concepts — Interplay of Rebate u/s 87A and Alternate Minimum Tax (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Aravind, a resident individual aged 45, has **opted out** of the default tax regime and is assessed under the **optional (old) regime** for A.Y. 2025-26. His Gross Total Income is ₹22,80,000, out of which he has claimed a deduction of ₹18,00,000 under section 80-IB (eligible industrial undertaking). His remaining total income after the deduction is ₹4,80,000. Assuming he has no other deduction under Chapter VI-A, compute his tax liability for A.Y. 2025-26, and state the amount of tax credit, if any, available to be carried forward.

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Computation of Total Income and normal tax (regular provisions)**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Gross Total Income | 22,80,000 |
| Less: Deduction u/s 80-IB | (18,00,000) |
| **Total Income** | **4,80,000** |

Tax on ₹4,80,000 (old regime): 5% of (4,80,000 − 2,50,000) = ₹11,500.
Since Mr. Aravind is a resident individual whose total income does not exceed ₹5,00,000, **rebate u/s 87A** = lower of ₹11,500 or ₹12,500 = **₹11,500**.
Tax after rebate = 11,500 − 11,500 = **Nil**.

**WN-2 — Computation of Adjusted Total Income (ATI) and AMT u/s 115JC**

Since he is claiming a deduction under Part C of Chapter VI-A (section 80-IB), the provisions of Alternate Minimum Tax apply. Adjusted Total Income is computed by adding back such deduction to the total income [section 115JC(2)].

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Total Income (regular) | 4,80,000 |
| Add: Deduction claimed u/s 80-IB (Part C, Chapter VI-A) | 18,00,000 |
| **Adjusted Total Income (ATI)** | **22,80,000** |

As ATI (₹22,80,000) exceeds ₹20,00,000, AMT provisions are attracted [section 115JEE].
AMT = 18.5% of ATI = 18.5% × 22,80,000 = ₹4,21,800.
Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% = ₹16,872.
**AMT liability = ₹4,38,672.**

**Main Solution — Statement of Tax Payable for A.Y. 2025-26**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Tax on total income under regular provisions (after rebate 87A) | Nil |
| AMT u/s 115JC (incl. cess) | 4,38,672 |
| **Tax payable = higher of the two** | **4,38,672** |

**Tax credit u/s 115JD** = AMT paid − regular income-tax = 4,38,672 − 0 = **₹4,38,672**, to be carried forward and set off in a subsequent year in which regular tax exceeds AMT, up to **15 assessment years**.

**Answer:** Tax payable for A.Y. 2025-26 = **₹4,38,672**; AMT credit carried forward u/s 115JD = **₹4,38,672**.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the insight that the section 87A rebate reduces regular tax to Nil but has no effect on AMT, which is computed on ATI — the two operate on different bases. Deduction was lost if a student forgot to add back 80-IB, or wrongly restricted AMT to cases where total income (not ATI) exceeds ₹20 lakh.)*

### Q2. Ch: Basic Concepts — Rounding-off u/s 288A and 288B (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the provisions relating to rounding-off of total income and of the amount of tax under the Income-tax Act, 1961, and illustrate each with a numerical example.

**Answer:**
The Income-tax Act provides two distinct rounding-off provisions, one for income and one for tax/refund.

**Rounding-off of Total Income — Section 288A:** The amount of total income computed in accordance with the provisions of the Act shall be **rounded off to the nearest multiple of ten rupees**. For this purpose, any part of a rupee consisting of paise is first ignored; thereafter, if the last figure of the amount so arrived at is **five rupees or more**, the amount is increased to the next higher multiple of ten, and if it is **less than five rupees**, it is reduced to the next lower multiple of ten.

*Illustration:* Total income of ₹6,45,676.80 is first reduced to ₹6,45,676 (ignoring paise); the last figure being 6 (i.e., 5 or more), it is rounded **up** to **₹6,45,680**.

**Rounding-off of Tax, etc. — Section 288B:** Any amount payable, and the amount of refund due, under the provisions of the Act shall be **rounded off to the nearest multiple of ten rupees** using the same mechanism as above. This is applied to the **final tax/refund figure**, and not to each component (such as cess or surcharge) separately.

*Illustration:* Tax and cess computed at ₹43,867.20 is rounded to **₹43,870** (paise ignored to ₹43,867; last figure 7 being 5 or more, rounded up).

**Distinction:**

| Basis | Section 288A | Section 288B |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Total income | Tax payable / refund due |
| Multiple | Nearest ₹10 | Nearest ₹10 |
| Stage of application | Once, on final total income | Once, on final tax/refund |

**Conclusion:** Total income is rounded off under section 288A and the resultant tax or refund under section 288B, both to the nearest multiple of ten rupees.

*(Full-marks tip: State the "5-or-more up / less-than-5 down" rule precisely and give a worked figure for each section. A common deduction is applying section 288B component-wise instead of to the final figure.)*

### Q3. Ch: Basic Concepts — Surcharge and Marginal Relief (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Bhaskar, a resident individual aged 52, is assessed under the optional (old) regime for A.Y. 2025-26 with a total income of **₹50,50,000**. Compute his tax liability, clearly showing the working for marginal relief, if any.

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Income-tax on ₹50,50,000 (before surcharge)**

| Slab | Rate | Tax (₹) |
|---|---|---:|
| Up to 2,50,000 | Nil | 0 |
| 2,50,001 – 5,00,000 | 5% | 12,500 |
| 5,00,001 – 10,00,000 | 20% | 1,00,000 |
| Above 10,00,000 (on 40,50,000) | 30% | 12,15,000 |
| **Income-tax** | | **13,27,500** |

Since total income exceeds ₹50 lakh but not ₹1 crore, **surcharge @ 10%** applies = 10% × 13,27,500 = ₹1,32,750.

**WN-2 — Marginal Relief**
Marginal relief ensures that the tax + surcharge on income exceeding ₹50 lakh does not exceed the (tax on ₹50 lakh) **plus** the income in excess of ₹50 lakh.

- Tax on ₹50,00,000 = 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 30% × 40,00,000 = ₹13,12,500.
- Income above ₹50,00,000 = ₹50,000.
- Ceiling = 13,12,500 + 50,000 = ₹13,62,500.
- Tax + surcharge without relief = 13,27,500 + 1,32,750 = ₹14,60,250.
- **Marginal relief** = 14,60,250 − 13,62,500 = **₹97,750**.
- Surcharge after relief = 1,32,750 − 97,750 = **₹35,000**.

**Main Solution — Statement of Tax Liability**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Income-tax | 13,27,500 |
| Add: Surcharge (net of marginal relief) | 35,000 |
| Tax + Surcharge | 13,62,500 |
| Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% | 54,500 |
| **Total Tax Liability (rounded u/s 288B)** | **14,17,000** |

**Answer:** Tax liability for A.Y. 2025-26 = **₹14,17,000** (after marginal relief of ₹97,750).

*(Full-marks tip: Marginal relief is computed on tax **plus** surcharge, and cess is added only **after** giving relief. A frequent error is computing cess before marginal relief or forgetting that relief itself is measured against "tax on ₹50 lakh + excess income".)*

### Q4. Ch: Basic Concepts — Diversion of Income vs. Application of Income (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between **diversion of income by an overriding title** and **application of income**, and explain the tax consequence of each with the help of decided case law.

**Answer:**
The distinction determines whether an amount is taxable in the hands of the person who earns it, and rests on the point at which the diversion takes effect.

**Diversion of income by overriding title:** Where, by virtue of an obligation, income is diverted **before it reaches the assessee** — such that the assessee never becomes the owner of that portion — it is a case of diversion by an overriding title and is **not taxable** in the assessee's hands. The controlling test laid down in **CIT v. Sitaldas Tirathdas (SC)** is whether the obligation is one that fastens on the **source of income itself** or is merely a charge on income already earned. In **Raja Bejoy Singh Dudhuria v. CIT (PC)**, a maintenance allowance charged on the estate by a decree of court was held to be a diversion at source and hence not the assessee's income.

**Application of income:** Where the income first accrues or arises to the assessee and is **thereafter applied** to discharge an obligation, it is a mere application of income; the assessee is fully taxable on it, and the subsequent spending is immaterial. In **Sitaldas Tirathdas**, maintenance paid to the wife and children under a decree, being an obligation to spend the assessee's own income, was held to be application and therefore taxable.

**Distinction:**

| Basis | Diversion by overriding title | Application of income |
|---|---|---|
| Point of diversion | Before income reaches the assessee | After income has accrued to the assessee |
| Charge on | Source of income | Income already earned |
| Taxability | Not taxable in assessee's hands | Fully taxable in assessee's hands |

**Conclusion:** Only income diverted at source by an overriding title escapes tax; an obligation discharged out of income already earned is an application of income and remains taxable.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner looks for the Sitaldas Tirathdas "source vs. income earned" test and at least one correctly attributed case. Merely defining the two terms without the controlling test loses marks.)*

### Q5. Ch: Basic Concepts — Applicability of AMT under the Default Regime u/s 115BAC (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Ms. Charu, a resident individual, is being assessed for A.Y. 2025-26 under the **default tax regime (section 115BAC)**. Her total income is ₹35,00,000 after claiming deduction of ₹6,00,000 under **section 80JJAA** and ₹1,80,000 under **section 80CCD(2)** (employer's NPS contribution). She wants to know whether Alternate Minimum Tax under section 115JC will be attracted, given that her adjusted total income would exceed ₹20 lakh. Advise her.

**Answer:**
The applicability of AMT is governed not only by the adjusted total income threshold but also by the tax regime under which the assessee is taxed.

**Position under section 115BAC:** Where an individual's income is chargeable to tax under the default regime of section 115BAC(1A), the sub-section itself provides that the provisions of **sections 115JC to 115JF (Alternate Minimum Tax) shall not apply**. Consequently, an assessee taxed under the default regime is **wholly outside the AMT net**, irrespective of the level of adjusted total income and irrespective of the deductions claimed.

**Application to the deductions claimed:** Under section 115BAC, most Chapter VI-A deductions are not available, but **section 80JJAA** (additional employee cost) and **section 80CCD(2)** (employer's contribution to NPS) **continue to be allowable**. Even though the add-back of the ₹6,00,000 section 80JJAA deduction would notionally take adjusted total income above ₹20 lakh, the **exclusion clause in section 115BAC overrides** the AMT machinery entirely. Hence no computation of adjusted total income under section 115JC(2) is required at all.

**Conclusion:** Since Ms. Charu is assessed under the default regime of section 115BAC, **AMT under section 115JC is not attracted**, notwithstanding that her adjusted total income would have exceeded ₹20,00,000. AMT would become relevant only if she had validly **opted out** of the default regime into the optional (old) regime.

*(Full-marks tip: The trap is the ₹20 lakh figure — an examiner rewards the candidate who spots that the regime, not the threshold, is decisive, and who correctly notes 80JJAA/80CCD(2) survive under 115BAC.)*

### Q6. Ch: Residential Status — Deemed Resident u/s 6(1A) [NOR consequence] (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Dinesh, an **Indian citizen**, is a self-employed consultant based in Dubai (U.A.E.), where there is no personal income tax and he is not liable to tax by reason of his residence. During the P.Y. 2024-25 he was present in India for only **100 days**. His income for the year comprises: profession income earned in India ₹18,00,000; income from a business controlled from Dubai ₹9,00,000 (received in Dubai). He was a non-resident in each of the preceding 10 years. Determine his residential status for A.Y. 2025-26 and state the consequence.

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Test of ordinary residence u/s 6(1)**
Mr. Dinesh was in India for only 100 days in P.Y. 2024-25 — less than 182 days — and being an Indian citizen who is a visiting person, the 60-day condition in section 6(1)(c) does not apply to him in the ordinary way. Hence he is **not a resident under section 6(1)**.

**WN-2 — Deemed residence u/s 6(1A)**
Section 6(1A) deems an individual to be resident in India if **all** the following are satisfied:

| Condition | Position of Mr. Dinesh | Satisfied? |
|---|---|---|
| He is an **Indian citizen** | Yes | ✓ |
| Total income (other than income from foreign sources) exceeds **₹15,00,000** | Indian profession income ₹18,00,000 > ₹15,00,000 | ✓ |
| He is **not liable to tax in any other country** by reason of domicile/residence | Not liable to tax in U.A.E. | ✓ |
| He is **not** already a resident under 6(1) | Correct — he is not | ✓ |

All conditions are met, so Mr. Dinesh is a **deemed resident** under section 6(1A).

**WN-3 — Type of residence**
An individual who is a resident only by virtue of section 6(1A) is always treated as **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** under section **6(6)(d)**.

**Answer:** Mr. Dinesh is a **deemed resident**, and being resident solely under section 6(1A), his status is **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)** for A.Y. 2025-26. As an RNOR, the Dubai business income of ₹9,00,000 (received outside India, from a business **controlled from Dubai**) is **not taxable** in India, whereas the Indian profession income of ₹18,00,000 is taxable.

*(Full-marks tip: All four limbs of section 6(1A) — including "not already resident under 6(1)" and "income other than from foreign sources" — must be tested. The examiner specifically rewards stating that a 6(1A) deemed resident is automatically RNOR under 6(6)(d).)*

### Q7. Ch: Residential Status — RNOR determination u/s 6(6) [returning NRI] (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Elango, an Indian citizen who had settled abroad, returned to India during the P.Y. 2024-25 and was present in India for **200 days**. His stay in India during the seven preceding previous years and his residential status in the ten preceding previous years are given below. Determine his residential status for A.Y. 2025-26.

| Previous Year | Days in India |
|---|---:|
| 2023-24 | 40 |
| 2022-23 | 60 |
| 2021-22 | 70 |
| 2020-21 | 90 |
| 2019-20 | 120 |
| 2018-19 | 130 |
| 2017-18 | 150 |

He was a **non-resident in 8 of the 10** immediately preceding previous years.

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Resident or Non-resident u/s 6(1)**
Mr. Elango was in India for 200 days in P.Y. 2024-25, which is 182 days or more. He therefore satisfies the basic condition in section **6(1)(a)** and is a **Resident** for A.Y. 2025-26.

**WN-2 — Ordinarily Resident or Not Ordinarily Resident u/s 6(6)**
A resident individual is **Not Ordinarily Resident** if he satisfies **either** of the following:

*Condition (a):* Non-resident in India in **9 out of the 10** preceding previous years.
He was non-resident in only **8** of the last 10 years — condition (a) is **not** satisfied.

*Condition (b):* Present in India for **729 days or less** during the **7** preceding previous years.

| Previous Year | Days |
|---|---:|
| 2023-24 | 40 |
| 2022-23 | 60 |
| 2021-22 | 70 |
| 2020-21 | 90 |
| 2019-20 | 120 |
| 2018-19 | 130 |
| 2017-18 | 150 |
| **Total (7 years)** | **660** |

Total stay of **660 days** is **≤ 729 days** — condition (b) **is** satisfied.

**Answer:** Since Mr. Elango is a resident who satisfies condition (b) of section 6(6) (≤ 729 days in the seven preceding years), his residential status for A.Y. 2025-26 is **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**.

*(Full-marks tip: Both limbs of section 6(6) are alternatives — satisfying either one makes the assessee RNOR. Marks are lost by treating them as cumulative, or by testing "9 out of 10" against the wrong count.)*

### Q8. Ch: Residential Status — Scope of Total Income u/s 5 for RNOR (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Farhan is a **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident** in India for A.Y. 2025-26. From the following particulars, compute the income chargeable to tax in India, giving reasons for the treatment of each item.

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| (i) Income from a profession set up in India, received in Canada | 4,00,000 |
| (ii) Profit from a business in Germany, **controlled from India**, received in Germany | 5,00,000 |
| (iii) Profit from a business in Sri Lanka, controlled from Sri Lanka, ₹2,00,000 of which was **received in India** | 3,50,000 |
| (iv) Rent from a house property in London, received in London | 2,80,000 |
| (v) Dividend from a foreign company, received in India | 1,20,000 |
| (vi) Past untaxed foreign income brought into India during the year | 6,00,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN — Scope of total income for an RNOR [section 5(1)]**
For an RNOR, the following are taxable: (a) income received or deemed to be received in India; (b) income accruing or arising or deemed to accrue or arise in India; and (c) income accruing/arising **outside** India **only if** it is derived from a business **controlled in** or a profession **set up in** India.

**Main Solution — Statement showing Income chargeable to tax in India**

| Particulars | Taxable (₹) | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| (i) Profession set up in India, received in Canada | 4,00,000 | Profession set up in India — taxable for RNOR even though received abroad |
| (ii) Germany business **controlled from India**, received abroad | 5,00,000 | Business controlled from India — taxable for RNOR |
| (iii) Sri Lanka business (controlled abroad): ₹2,00,000 received in India | 2,00,000 | Income received in India is taxable; balance ₹1,50,000 accrues & is received abroad from a business not controlled from India — not taxable |
| (iv) Rent of London house, received in London | Nil | Accrues and received outside India; not from Indian business/profession — not taxable |
| (v) Dividend from foreign company, **received in India** | 1,20,000 | Income received in India — taxable |
| (vi) Past untaxed foreign income remitted to India | Nil | Mere remittance of past income is not "receipt" of income of the year — not taxable |
| **Total Income chargeable to tax in India** | **12,20,000** | |

**Answer:** Income chargeable to tax in India in the hands of Mr. Farhan (RNOR) = **₹12,20,000**.

*(Full-marks tip: For an RNOR the decisive tests are "received in India" and "business controlled from / profession set up in India". Two classic traps are taxing the whole Sri Lanka profit rather than only the ₹2,00,000 received in India, and taxing the remittance of past income — a reason must be written against each item.)*

### Q9. Ch: Residential Status — Residential Status of a HUF u/s 6(2) (Marks: 4) [Application]
**Question:** Explain the tests for determining the residential status of a **Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)** and when such an HUF is treated as "Not Ordinarily Resident". A HUF's affairs during P.Y. 2024-25 were **partly controlled from India and partly from Nepal**, and its **karta**, who managed the family, has been a non-resident in 9 of the 10 preceding years. State the family's residential status.

**Answer:**
The residential status of an HUF is determined in two stages — first Resident/Non-resident under section 6(2), and then, if resident, the further test in section 6(6)(b).

**Resident or Non-resident — Section 6(2):** An HUF is **resident** in India in any previous year unless the **control and management** of its affairs is situated **wholly outside India** during that year. Consequently, if the control and management is situated **wholly or even partly in India**, the HUF is a resident. It is non-resident only where control and management is **entirely** outside India.

Applying this, since the affairs of the family are controlled **partly from India** during P.Y. 2024-25, the control and management is not wholly outside India, and therefore the **HUF is Resident** in India.

**Ordinarily Resident or Not Ordinarily Resident — Section 6(6)(b):** A resident HUF is **Not Ordinarily Resident** if its **karta (manager)** satisfies **either** of the individual conditions in section 6(6): namely, the karta has been a non-resident in **9 out of the 10** preceding previous years, **or** has been present in India for **729 days or less** in the **7** preceding previous years.

Here, the karta has been a non-resident in **9 of the 10** preceding years, so the first condition is satisfied.

**Conclusion:** The HUF is a **Resident** (as it is controlled partly from India) and, since its karta satisfies section 6(6)(b), the family is **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident** for A.Y. 2025-26.

*(Full-marks tip: State clearly that "wholly outside India" is the test for a non-resident HUF, and that the NOR test is applied to the **karta**, not to the family. Confusing the karta's status test with the family's control test is the usual error.)*

### Q10. Ch: Residential Status — 120-day rule for a visiting Indian citizen u/s 6(1)/6(6)(c) (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Mr. Gagan, an **Indian citizen** residing in Dubai, came to India on a visit during P.Y. 2024-25 and stayed for **130 days**. During the four immediately preceding previous years he was in India for an aggregate of **400 days**. His total income for P.Y. 2024-25, **other than income from foreign sources**, is ₹20,00,000. Determine his residential status for A.Y. 2025-26 and explain the reasoning.

**Answer:**
Where an Indian citizen or a person of Indian origin visits India, a special modification of the 60-day condition in section 6(1)(c) applies, and it depends on the level of his Indian income.

**Basic condition — Section 6(1)(a):** Mr. Gagan was in India for 130 days, which is **less than 182 days**, so condition (a) is not satisfied.

**Modified additional condition — Section 6(1)(c) read with Explanation 1(b):** For an Indian citizen/PIO who comes on a visit to India, the 60-day period in section 6(1)(c) is ordinarily substituted by 182 days. **However**, where such a person has total income (other than income from foreign sources) exceeding **₹15,00,000**, the substituted period is **120 days** (not 182 days).

Here, his Indian-source total income of ₹20,00,000 **exceeds ₹15,00,000**, so the applicable threshold is **120 days**. Testing condition (c): he was in India for **130 days (≥ 120 days)** in P.Y. 2024-25 **and** for **400 days (≥ 365 days)** in the four preceding years. Both limbs are satisfied, so **he is a Resident** under section 6(1)(c).

**Ordinarily Resident or Not — Section 6(6)(c):** An Indian citizen/PIO having total income (other than foreign-source income) exceeding ₹15 lakh, who becomes resident on account of staying **120 days or more but less than 182 days**, is treated as **Not Ordinarily Resident**.

Mr. Gagan's stay of 130 days falls in the 120–181 day range, so section 6(6)(c) applies.

**Conclusion:** Mr. Gagan is a **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident** for A.Y. 2025-26.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner wants the ₹15 lakh income test linked to the 120-day substitution, and then the automatic RNOR consequence under 6(6)(c). Applying the ordinary 182-day visiting rule and concluding "non-resident" is the standard mistake.)*

### Q11. Ch: Income under Salaries — Valuation of Rent-Free Accommodation (post-amendment) (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Harish is a non-Government employee of a private company. He is provided **rent-free unfurnished accommodation** owned by the employer, situated in a city whose population per the 2011 census is **30 lakh**. The employer has also provided furniture costing ₹2,00,000. Mr. Harish recovers ₹1,000 per month towards the accommodation. From the following salary particulars for the year ended 31-3-2025, compute the value of the perquisite of rent-free accommodation (as per the valuation rules effective for A.Y. 2025-26).

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Basic salary (₹50,000 p.m.) | 6,00,000 |
| Dearness allowance (forming part of retirement benefits) (₹10,000 p.m.) | 1,20,000 |
| Bonus | 40,000 |
| Commission (fixed) | 30,000 |
| Taxable special allowance | 60,000 |
| Employer's contribution to recognised PF | 72,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — "Salary" for valuation of RFA [Rule 3]**
Salary here means basic pay, dearness allowance (as it forms part of retirement benefits), bonus, commission and all taxable monetary allowances; it **excludes** the employer's PF contribution and perquisites.

| Component | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Basic salary | 6,00,000 |
| DA (forming part) | 1,20,000 |
| Bonus | 40,000 |
| Commission | 30,000 |
| Taxable special allowance | 60,000 |
| **Salary for RFA** | **8,50,000** |

*(Employer's PF contribution of ₹72,000 is not part of salary for this purpose.)*

**WN-2 — Applicable percentage [employer-owned accommodation]**
For accommodation owned by the employer in a city with 2011-census population **exceeding 15 lakh but not exceeding 40 lakh**, the perquisite value is **7.5% of salary**.
7.5% × 8,50,000 = **₹63,750**.

**WN-3 — Value of furniture**
Furniture owned by the employer is valued at **10% p.a. of original cost** = 10% × 2,00,000 = **₹20,000**.

**Main Solution — Value of perquisite of Rent-Free Accommodation**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| 7.5% of salary (accommodation) | 63,750 |
| Add: Value of furniture (10% of ₹2,00,000) | 20,000 |
| Gross value of perquisite | 83,750 |
| Less: Rent recovered from employee (₹1,000 × 12) | (12,000) |
| **Taxable value of perquisite** | **71,750** |

**Answer:** Value of the rent-free accommodation perquisite chargeable to tax = **₹71,750**.

*(Full-marks tip: Use the current 5%/7.5%/10% slabs against the 15 lakh/40 lakh population thresholds, not the old 7.5%/10%/15% rates. Furniture at 10% of cost is added **before** deducting the amount recovered from the employee.)*

### Q12. Ch: Income under Salaries — Motor-Car Perquisite (employee-owned car) (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Ishaan owns a motor car (engine capacity exceeding 1.6 litres) which he uses **partly for official and partly for personal** purposes. During P.Y. 2024-25 his employer **reimbursed** the running and maintenance expenses of ₹1,20,000 and paid a chauffeur's salary of ₹1,08,000 for the car. **No records** are maintained by the employer to determine the extent of official use. Compute the value of the motor-car perquisite for A.Y. 2025-26.

**Solution:**

### Q17. Ch: Income under Salaries — Retrenchment Compensation vs. VRS Compensation (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish, under the head "Salaries", between the exemption available for **retrenchment compensation** under section 10(10B) and that for **compensation received under a Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS)** under section 10(10C).

**Answer:**
Both receipts are, in substance, "profits in lieu of salary" chargeable under section 17(3), but the Act grants separate, differently-structured exemptions for each.

**Retrenchment compensation — Section 10(10B):** Compensation received by a **workman** at the time of retrenchment is exempt to the extent of the **least** of: (a) the amount actually received; (b) an amount calculated in accordance with section 25F(b) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (i.e., 15 days' average pay for every completed year of service or part thereof in excess of six months); and (c) the specified monetary limit of **₹5,00,000**. Where the compensation is received under a scheme approved by the Central Government, the ₹5,00,000 monetary ceiling does not apply.

**Voluntary Retirement compensation — Section 10(10C):** Compensation received on **voluntary retirement** (or voluntary separation) from specified employers, under a scheme framed in accordance with the prescribed **guidelines (Rule 2BA)**, is exempt up to a maximum of **₹5,00,000**. This exemption can be availed by the employee **only once** in his lifetime; and where relief under section 10(10C) has been allowed, no relief under section 89 can be claimed in respect of the same amount.

**Distinction:**

| Basis | Retrenchment compensation — 10(10B) | VRS compensation — 10(10C) |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient | A "workman" on retrenchment | Employee on voluntary retirement/separation |
| Governing computation | Least of received / ID Act, 1947 amount / ₹5,00,000 | Amount per Rule 2BA guidelines, max ₹5,00,000 |
| Number of times | Not so restricted | Once in a lifetime |
| Interaction with sec. 89 | Section 89 relief available on taxable portion | If 10(10C) claimed, section 89 barred on same sum |

**Conclusion:** Retrenchment compensation is exempt under section 10(10B) with reference to the Industrial Disputes Act formula, whereas VRS compensation is exempt under section 10(10C) up to ₹5,00,000 as a once-in-a-lifetime benefit subject to Rule 2BA.

*(Full-marks tip: Both exemptions cap at ₹5,00,000 but on different bases — quote section 25F for 10(10B) and Rule 2BA plus the once-in-a-lifetime / no-double-relief-with-section-89 conditions for 10(10C). Presenting the distinction as a table earns the comparison marks.)*

### Q21. Ch: Income from House Property — Conditions for Deducting Unrealised Rent (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** While computing the annual value, unrealised rent may be excluded from the actual rent received/receivable. State the conditions prescribed under **Rule 4** (read with the Explanation to Section 23(1)) that must be satisfied before unrealised rent can be so excluded.

**Answer:** The amount of rent which the owner cannot realise is excluded from the actual rent (before comparison with expected rent) only if **all** the conditions specified in **Rule 4 of the Income-tax Rules, 1962** (referred to in the Explanation below Section 23(1)) are satisfied. These conditions are:

1. **Bona fide tenancy** — The tenancy is **bona fide**, i.e. genuine and not a device to reduce the annual value.
2. **Vacation/steps to vacate** — The **defaulting tenant has vacated** the property, or **steps have been taken to compel him to vacate** the property.
3. **Not occupying another property of the owner** — The **defaulting tenant is not in occupation of any other property** of the assessee.
4. **Reasonable steps taken** — The assessee has **taken all reasonable steps to institute legal proceedings** for the recovery of the unpaid rent, **or satisfies the Assessing Officer that legal proceedings would be useless.**

**Conclusion:** Only when **all four** conditions of Rule 4 are cumulatively met can the unrealised rent be excluded from the actual rent for computing the Gross Annual Value.

### Q27. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — ICDS I: Accounting Policies (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the fundamental accounting assumptions recognised under **ICDS I (Accounting Policies)** and state its position on (a) the concept of **prudence/marked-to-market losses** and (b) a **change in accounting policy**.

**Answer:** **ICDS I** deals with the significant accounting policies to be adopted while computing income under the heads "Profits and gains of business or profession" and "Income from other sources."

**(1) Fundamental accounting assumptions:** ICDS I recognises three fundamental accounting assumptions — **Going Concern, Consistency and Accrual**. These are assumed to be followed, and **no specific disclosure** is required if they are followed; if any assumption is **not** followed, that fact **must be disclosed**.

**(2) Prudence / marked-to-market losses:** Unlike the accounting concept of prudence, ICDS I provides that **marked-to-market losses or expected losses shall not be recognised** unless the recognition is in accordance with the provisions of any **other ICDS**. Correspondingly (and to maintain symmetry), an **MTM gain or expected profit is also not recognised** unless permitted by another ICDS. Thus ICDS I removes the asymmetric "recognise losses but not gains" treatment of conservatism.

**(3) Selection / change of accounting policy:** Accounting policies are to be selected so as to represent a **true and fair view**; the treatment/presentation of a transaction should be governed by its **substance and not merely its legal form**. An accounting policy shall **not be changed without a reasonable cause.**

**Conclusion:** ICDS I anchors income computation on going concern, consistency and accrual, **denies recognition of MTM/expected losses** except where another ICDS permits, and permits a change of policy only for **reasonable cause**.

*(Full-marks tip: The MTM-loss point and the "reasonable cause" test are the scoring lines. Common deduction: describing AS 1 instead of ICDS I, or omitting that expected gains are equally not recognised.)*

**WN-2 — Which SDV date [Section 43CA(3)/(4)]:** Where the **date of agreement** and the **date of registration** are different, the SDV **on the date of agreement** may be adopted, **provided** at least a part of the consideration was received by **account payee cheque/draft/ECS/prescribed electronic mode on or before the date of agreement**. 110% of consideration = 110% × ₹90,00,000 = **₹99,00,000**.
Relevant SDV = ₹98,00,000, which is **≤ ₹99,00,000**.
Since the SDV does not exceed 110% of the consideration, the tolerance band applies and the **actual consideration is adopted**.

No addition arises, because the qualifying SDV of ₹98,00,000 (date of agreement, part payment by cheque) is within the **110% tolerance band**. ### Q29. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — Section 43CA vs 50C vs 56(2)(x) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between **Section 43CA, Section 50C and Section 56(2)(x)** as they apply to the transfer of immovable property below its stamp duty value. Bring out the head of income, the person taxed, and the common "tolerance band."

**Answer:** All three provisions counter under-statement of consideration in immovable-property transactions by reference to the **stamp duty value (SDV)**, but they operate on different persons and heads:

| Basis | Section 43CA | Section 50C | Section 56(2)(x) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nature of asset in the hands of transferor** | Land/building held as **stock-in-trade** | Land/building held as a **capital asset** | Relevant to the **buyer/recipient** |
| **Head of income affected** | Profits & gains of business or profession | Capital Gains | Income from Other Sources |
| **Person taxed** | **Seller** (dealer) | **Seller** (owner of capital asset) | **Buyer/recipient** of the property |
| **What is deemed** | SDV deemed full value of consideration if consideration < SDV | SDV deemed full value of consideration if consideration < SDV | Excess of SDV over consideration taxed as income of buyer |
| **Tolerance band** | No adjustment if SDV ≤ **110%** of consideration | No adjustment if SDV ≤ **110%** of consideration | No addition if SDV ≤ **110%** of consideration (excess over ₹50,000 also relevant) |
| **Agreement-date SDV option (part payment by prescribed mode)** | Available [43CA(3)/(4)] | Available [proviso to 50C(1)] | Available [proviso to 56(2)(x)] |
| **Reference to DVO** | Not available | Available [Sec 50C(2)] | Available (via Sec 56(2)(x) proviso applying 50C(2)/(3)) |

**Common features:** In each case the SDV on the **date of agreement** may be adopted (instead of the registration-date SDV) if part of the consideration is received by **account payee cheque/draft/ECS/prescribed electronic mode on/before the agreement date**, and each carries the **110% safe-harbour**.

**Conclusion:** Section 43CA taxes the **dealer-seller** (business income), Section 50C taxes the **investor-seller** (capital gains) and Section 56(2)(x) taxes the **buyer** (other sources); the SDV mechanism and the 110% tolerance are common to all three, so a single transaction can potentially attract 43CA/50C on the seller **and** 56(2)(x) on the buyer.

### Q30. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — Section 40A(3) and Rule 6DD Exceptions (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the disallowance under **Section 40A(3)** for cash expenditure and set out the circumstances (**Rule 6DD**) in which the disallowance does **not** apply even though payment is made otherwise than through a banking channel.

**Answer:** **Section 40A(3)** provides that where an assessee incurs an expenditure in respect of which a **payment, or aggregate of payments made to a person in a day, exceeds ₹10,000** and such payment is made **otherwise than by account payee cheque/bank draft/ECS or a prescribed electronic mode**, **100% of such expenditure shall be disallowed**. For payments made for **plying, hiring or leasing goods carriages** (transporters), the threshold is enhanced to **₹35,000**. Section 40A(3A) makes a corresponding disallowance where a liability allowed in an earlier year is later paid in cash exceeding the limit.

**Rule 6DD — cases where no disallowance is attracted (illustrative):**
1. Payment made to the **RBI, any bank, LIC, or Government** where payment is required to be made in legal tender.
2. Payment made in a **village or town not served by any bank** to a person who ordinarily resides or carries on business there.
3. Payment made on a **day on which banks were closed** on account of a **holiday or strike**.
4. Payment to a **cultivator, grower or producer** for the purchase of **agricultural, horticultural, dairy, poultry, fish or forest produce**, or products of animal husbandry.
5. Payment for **products manufactured/processed without the aid of power in a cottage industry**, to the producer.
6. Payment by way of **book adjustment** in the books of both parties against money due.
7. Payment of **terminal benefits** (gratuity, retrenchment compensation, etc.) to an employee/legal heir where the amount does **not exceed ₹50,000**.
8. Payment made by an **agent** who is required to make cash payments on behalf of the principal, or payment through **letters of credit / mail or telegraphic transfer / bank drafts**.

**Conclusion:** Where a case falls within any clause of Rule 6DD, the cash payment — though above the limit — **escapes disallowance** under Section 40A(3).

### Q33. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — Section 43B(h): Payments to Micro & Small Enterprises (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the deduction rule for statutory and other specified payments under **Section 43B**, and specifically the position of **Section 43B(h)** relating to amounts payable to **micro and small enterprises**. State how clause (h) differs from the other clauses.

**Answer:** **Section 43B** provides that certain deductions — such as any **tax, duty, cess or fee**, employer's contribution to **provident/superannuation/gratuity funds**, **bonus or commission** to employees, **interest on loans** from specified financial institutions/banks, and leave encashment — are allowable **only in the previous year in which they are actually paid**, irrespective of the year of accrual. The **first proviso** relaxes this: the deduction is allowed on an accrual basis if the sum is **actually paid on or before the due date of furnishing the return** under Section 139(1).

**Section 43B(h):** Any sum **payable by the assessee to a micro or small enterprise** beyond the **time limit specified in Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006**, is allowed as a deduction **only in the previous year in which the sum is actually paid**. The time limit under Section 15 is the date agreed in writing (**not exceeding 45 days**), or **15 days** where there is no written agreement.

**Key difference from the other clauses:**

| Basis | Clauses (a)–(g) of Sec 43B | Clause (h) of Sec 43B |
|---|---|---|
| Deduction on payment basis | Yes | Yes |
| Benefit of first proviso (paid by return due date → accrual-basis deduction) | **Available** | **Not available** |
| Consequence of delay | Deduction merely deferred; allowed once paid | Deduction allowed **only in the year of actual payment**, even if paid before the return due date |

**Conclusion:** Unlike the other clauses of Section 43B, the **first proviso does not apply to clause (h)**; hence a sum payable to a micro/small enterprise beyond the MSMED time limit is deductible **strictly in the year of actual payment**, disincentivising delayed payments to MSEs.

*(Full-marks tip: The scoring line is that the "paid before return due date" proviso is UNAVAILABLE for 43B(h). Common deduction: applying the first proviso to clause (h), or misstating the 45/15-day MSMED limits.)*

### Q37. Ch: Capital Gains — Sale of ESOP Shares [Sec 49(2AA)] (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Rohan, an employee of a listed company (not an eligible start-up), was allotted 1,000 equity shares on 1st August 2023 under the company's ESOP scheme. On the date of exercise/allotment the FMV of each share was ₹250 and he paid the exercise price of ₹50 per share. He sold all 1,000 shares on the recognised stock exchange (STT paid) on 20th September 2024 at ₹400 per share. Explain and compute the taxable income arising to Mr. Rohan on (a) allotment and (b) sale, for the relevant assessment years, and show that there is no double taxation.

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Perquisite on allotment (AY 2024-25):**
Under **Section 17(2)(vi)**, the difference between the FMV on the date of exercise and the amount recovered from the employee is a **perquisite taxable as Salary** in the year of allotment (PY 2023-24).
Perquisite = (₹250 − ₹50) × 1,000 = **₹2,00,000** (taxed under head Salaries, AY 2024-25).

**WN-2 — Cost of acquisition & period of holding for capital gains:**
Per **Section 49(2AA)**, the cost of acquisition of ESOP shares is the **FMV that was taken into account for the perquisite**, i.e. ₹250 per share. Period of holding is reckoned from the **date of allotment** (1.8.2023) to sale (20.9.2024) = **more than 12 months** → **long-term**; being STT-paid listed shares, gains fall under **Section 112A**.

**Computation of Capital Gains — AY 2025-26**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Full value of consideration (1,000 × ₹400) | 4,00,000 |
| Less: Cost of acquisition (1,000 × ₹250) [Sec 49(2AA)] | (2,50,000) |
| **Long-Term Capital Gain u/s 112A** | **1,50,000** |
| Less: Exemption u/s 112A | (1,25,000) |
| **Taxable LTCG** | **25,000** |
| **Tax @12.5%** | **3,125** |

**Answer:** (a) On allotment — **Salary perquisite ₹2,00,000** (AY 2024-25). (b) On sale — **LTCG ₹1,50,000**, of which ₹25,000 is taxable @12.5% after the ₹1,25,000 exemption u/s 112A → tax **₹3,125** (AY 2025-26). There is **no double taxation** because the FMV of ₹250 (already offered as perquisite) becomes the cost of acquisition, so only the post-allotment appreciation of ₹150/share is charged as capital gain.

*(Full-marks tip: The key sentence examiners look for is "cost = FMV taken for perquisite u/s 49(2AA)" and "holding period from date of allotment". Applying the ₹1,25,000 exemption and 12.5% rate secures the last mark.)*

### Q38. Ch: Capital Gains — Buy-back of Shares [Sec 46A / Sec 2(22)(f)] (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Explain the taxability, in the hands of the shareholder, of amounts received on **buy-back of shares by a domestic company on or after 1st October 2024**, and contrast it with the earlier position. 2. The **cost of acquisition** of the bought-back shares is treated as a **capital loss** — the full value of consideration for the capital-gains computation is taken as **NIL**, generating a capital loss equal to the cost, which may be set off/carried forward under the capital-gains provisions.

| Less: Cost of acquisition (2,000 × ₹100) | (2,00,000) |
| **Long-Term Capital LOSS** (held > 12 months) | **(2,00,000)** |

**Conclusion:** Mr. Suresh must offer **₹3,60,000 as deemed dividend** under IFOS (taxable at slab rates), and he generates a **long-term capital loss of ₹2,00,000** which can be set off only against long-term capital gains and carried forward for 8 assessment years u/s 74.

### Q44. Ch: Income from Other Sources — SDV Safe-Harbour: Sec 50C & Sec 56(2)(x) Together (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Mr. Anil sold a commercial building to Mr. Bharat on 5th February 2025. The agreed consideration was ₹51,00,000, whereas the stamp-duty value (SDV) on the date of the agreement/registration was ₹55,00,000. (a) Discuss the tax consequences for both the seller and the buyer. (b) How would your answer change if the SDV were ₹60,00,000?

**Answer:**
Two mirror provisions operate on the same transaction: **Section 50C** (seller — capital gains) and **Section 56(2)(x)** (buyer — IFOS). Both incorporate a **10% "safe-harbour"**: the SDV is substituted/charged only if it **exceeds 110% of the actual consideration**.

**Case (a) — SDV ₹55,00,000:**
110% of consideration = 110% × ₹51,00,000 = **₹56,10,000**. Since SDV ₹55,00,000 **does not exceed** ₹56,10,000:
- **Seller (Mr. Anil):** Under the proviso to Section 50C, the **actual consideration ₹51,00,000** is taken as the full value of consideration — no substitution.
- **Buyer (Mr. Bharat):** Under Section 56(2)(x), **no amount** is chargeable, as the difference is within the tolerance band.

**Case (b) — SDV ₹60,00,000:**
110% of consideration = ₹56,10,000. Since SDV ₹60,00,000 **exceeds** ₹56,10,000, the safe-harbour is breached and the **full SDV** applies to both:
- **Seller:** FVC deemed to be **₹60,00,000** u/s 50C for computing capital gains.
- **Buyer:** Difference of (₹60,00,000 − ₹51,00,000) = **₹9,00,000** is chargeable to the buyer as IFOS u/s 56(2)(x).

**Conclusion:** In Case (a) neither party is affected (₹51,00,000 stands). In Case (b) the seller is taxed on FVC of ₹60,00,000 and the buyer additionally pays tax on ₹9,00,000 — the full gap, not merely the amount exceeding the threshold.

*(Full-marks tip: Test SDV against 110% of consideration (not against consideration itself). Once breached, tax the FULL difference — a very common error is charging only the excess over the 10% band. Show both the seller and buyer sides.)*

### Q46. Ch: Clubbing & Set-off — Cross-Transfers [Sec 64(1)(iv)] (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** On the same day, Mr. Xavier gifted ₹5,00,000 to Mrs. Yadav (wife of Mr. Yadav) and, as part of the same arrangement, Mr. Yadav gifted ₹5,00,000 to Mrs. Xavier (wife of Mr. Xavier). During PY 2024-25, Mrs. Xavier earned interest of ₹40,000 on her ₹5,00,000, and Mrs. Yadav earned interest of ₹45,000 on hers. Discuss the clubbing implications and state whose income is clubbed with whom.

**Answer:**
This is a case of **cross-transfers**. Where two transfers are **intimately connected** and are made as a **device to avoid clubbing** (A gifts to B's spouse and B simultaneously gifts to A's spouse for roughly the same amount), the transactions are **treated as if each individual had transferred the asset to his own spouse** — the courts (CIT v. Keshavji Morarji) and the Board have held that the doctrine of substance over form applies and **Section 64(1)(iv)** is attracted.

Applying this:
- The gift by Mr. Xavier to Mrs. Yadav, read with the reciprocal gift, is treated as a transfer by **Mr. Yadav to his own wife (Mrs. Yadav)**. Therefore Mrs. Yadav's interest income of **₹45,000 is clubbed in the hands of Mr. Yadav**.
- Correspondingly, the gift to Mrs. Xavier is treated as a transfer by **Mr. Xavier to his own wife**. Therefore Mrs. Xavier's interest income of **₹40,000 is clubbed in the hands of Mr. Xavier**.

**Conclusion:** ₹45,000 is clubbed in **Mr. Yadav's** total income and ₹40,000 in **Mr. Xavier's** total income. The cross-transfer device fails; clubbing u/s 64(1)(iv) applies to each spouse's income as though the assets had been directly gifted to their own wives.

*(Full-marks tip: Identify it as a "cross-transfer" and invoke substance-over-form — do NOT conclude "no clubbing since husbands did not gift to their own wives". Match each wife's income to the correct husband.)*

### Q53. Ch: Deductions under Chapter VI-A — Sec 80-IA (Infrastructure) (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** "Sec 80-IA operates on a self-contained code where the eligible undertaking is treated as the assessee's only source of income." Explain — (i) the meaning and consequence of the "initial assessment year" and the assessee's option u/s 80-IA(2); (ii) the effect of Sec 80-IA(5) on set-off of the eligible unit's losses; and (iii) whether the deduction can exceed the gross total income. Illustrate point (ii) with the following figures of a power-generation undertaking of Vidyut Ltd.

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Notional brought-forward loss / depreciation of the eligible unit already set off against other income in earlier years | 40,00,000 |
| Profit of the eligible unit for A.Y. 2025-26 (before this section) | 60,00,000 |

**Answer:**
**Statutory basis.** Section 80-IA allows a deduction of **100% of the profits and gains** derived by an eligible undertaking (infrastructure facility, power generation/ distribution, etc.) **for 10 consecutive assessment years**.

**(i) Initial assessment year and the option u/s 80-IA(2).** The deduction of 100% for 10 consecutive years may be claimed, **at the option of the assessee**, out of **15 years** (20 years for certain infrastructure facilities) beginning from the year in which the undertaking commences operation. The **"initial assessment year"** is the first year for which the assessee opts to claim the deduction; the 10-year block runs consecutively from that year. This lets a loss-making unit defer its claim to profitable years so that the 10-year window is not wasted.

**(ii) Effect of Sec 80-IA(5) — the "only source" fiction.** For determining the quantum of deduction from and including the initial assessment year, the eligible business is treated **as if it were the only source of income** of the assessee. Consequently, the losses and unabsorbed depreciation of the eligible unit **already set off against other income** in the years **prior to the initial assessment year are notionally brought forward** and set off against the unit's profits of the initial and subsequent years, even though they were actually absorbed earlier (subject to the position clarified by the Supreme Court in *Velayudhaswamy Spinning Mills*, that losses already absorbed need not be notionally carried forward). Applying the statutory fiction to the given figures:

| Statement Showing Deduction u/s 80-IA (eligible unit) | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Profit of eligible unit for A.Y. 2025-26 | 60,00,000 |
| Less: Notional b/f loss/depreciation of the unit set off under Sec 80-IA(5) | 40,00,000 |
| Profit eligible for deduction | 20,00,000 |
| **Deduction u/s 80-IA @ 100%** | **20,00,000** |

**(iii) Cap by gross total income.** Under **Sec 80A(2)**, the aggregate deduction under Chapter VI-A **cannot exceed the gross total income**; hence 80-IA can never create or increase a loss. Further, no deduction under any other section of the heading "C.—Deductions in respect of certain incomes" is allowed if 80-IA is claimed on the same profits (Sec 80-IA(9)).

**Conclusion:** Deduction u/s 80-IA = **₹20,00,000**, restricted by the "only-source" fiction of Sec 80-IA(5) and by the gross-total-income ceiling of Sec 80A(2).

### Q54. Ch: Deductions under Chapter VI-A — Sec 80GGB / 80GGC (Contributions to Political Parties) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between the deductions under Sec 80GGB and Sec 80GGC in respect of contributions to political parties / electoral trusts. State clearly the mode-of-payment restriction and whether these deductions survive under the default tax regime u/s 115BAC.

**Answer:**
**Statutory basis.** Sections **80GGB** and **80GGC** allow a deduction of **100%** of any sum contributed by an assessee to a **political party registered u/s 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, or to an electoral trust**.

| Basis | Sec 80GGB | Sec 80GGC |
|---|---|---|
| Eligible assessee | An **Indian company** | **Any person**, *other than* a company (i.e., individual, HUF, firm, etc.) *and* other than a local authority and an artificial juridical person wholly or partly funded by Government |
| Quantum of deduction | 100% of the sum contributed | 100% of the sum contributed |
| Recipient | Registered political party / electoral trust | Registered political party / electoral trust |

For a company, the new-regime concessional rates (Sec 115BAA/115BAB) similarly deny **Sec 80GGB**.

### Q55. Ch: TDS — Sec 194R (TDS on Benefit or Perquisite of Business/Profession) (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Examine the applicability of TDS u/s 194R in the following independent situations for F.Y. 2024-25 and state the amount of tax, if any, to be deducted:

| Case | Facts |
|---|---|
| (a) | Zenith Pharma Ltd. (turnover ₹80 crore) distributes free samples worth ₹35,000 during the year to Dr. Rao, a resident medical practitioner, in connection with his profession. |
| (b) | A car dealer sponsors a fully-paid foreign trip (value ₹4,00,000) for its top-performing dealer, Mr. Sen (a resident distributor). |
| (c) | A company gives its resident dealer a cash discount of ₹50,000 and a further volume rebate reflected in the sale invoice. |
| (d) | Mr. Kabir, an individual whose business turnover is ₹90 lakh, gives a gift worth ₹30,000 to a resident business associate. |

**Answer:**
**Statutory basis.** Section **194R** requires any person **responsible for providing** to a resident **any benefit or perquisite** (whether convertible into money or not) **arising from the carrying on of a business or exercise of a profession** by that resident, to deduct tax **@ 10%** of the value/aggregate value of such benefit or perquisite, **before providing** it. TDS applies only where the aggregate value of the benefit/perquisite to a resident **exceeds ₹20,000** in the financial year.

**Case (a) — Free samples to a doctor.** As clarified by CBDT Circular No. 12/2022, free medicine samples given to a doctor are a benefit/perquisite arising from his profession. Value ₹35,000 > ₹20,000, and Zenith's turnover exceeds the individual/HUF exemption limit. **TDS = 10% × ₹35,000 = ₹3,500.**

**Case (b) — Sponsored foreign trip to a dealer.** An incentive foreign trip is a benefit arising from the distributor's business (Circular 12/2022). Value ₹4,00,000 > ₹20,000. **TDS = 10% × ₹4,00,000 = ₹40,000.** (Where the benefit is wholly in kind, the provider must ensure the tax has been paid before releasing the benefit.)

**Case (c) — Cash discount / volume rebate.** CBDT has clarified that **sales discounts, cash discounts and rebates** allowed to a customer are **outside Sec 194R**, though other benefits in kind/cash are covered. **No TDS** is required on the ₹50,000 cash discount and invoice rebate.

**Case (d) — Small business, individual provider.** An individual/HUF is **not required to deduct** u/s 194R if the **total sales/gross receipts do not exceed ₹1 crore (business)/₹50 lakh (profession)** in the preceding year. Mr. Kabir's turnover is ₹90 lakh (< ₹1 crore). **No TDS.**

**Conclusion:** TDS is deductible only in **Case (a) — ₹3,500** and **Case (b) — ₹40,000**; Cases (c) and (d) are outside Sec 194R.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite Circular 12/2022 to exclude sales discounts (c) and the ₹1 crore/₹50 lakh threshold for individual providers (d). Stating that in wholly-in-kind cases the provider ensures tax is paid before releasing the benefit earns the depth mark.)*

### Q56. Ch: TDS/TCS — Sec 194Q vs Sec 206C(1H) (Interplay) (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Ravi Traders (buyer, turnover in F.Y. 2023-24 = ₹15 crore) purchases goods from Mohan Industries (seller, turnover in F.Y. 2023-24 = ₹12 crore) during F.Y. 2024-25. Both furnish valid PAN. The transactions are:

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Purchases by Ravi Traders from Mohan Industries during F.Y. 2024-25 | 90,00,000 |
| Of which, amount paid up to date of this transaction | 70,00,000 |

Determine (i) whether Sec 194Q (TDS by buyer) or Sec 206C(1H) (TCS by seller) applies; (ii) the amount and by whom; and (iii) how the answer changes if the buyer's turnover were only ₹8 crore.

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Threshold checks.**
- **Sec 194Q (buyer deducts):** applies where the buyer's total sales/turnover exceeds **₹10 crore** in the preceding FY, and purchases from a resident seller exceed **₹50 lakh** in the FY. Ravi's turnover ₹15 crore > ₹10 crore, and purchases ₹90 lakh > ₹50 lakh → **194Q is triggered.**
- **Sec 206C(1H) (seller collects):** applies where the seller's turnover exceeds ₹10 crore and receipts of sale consideration from a buyer exceed ₹50 lakh. Mohan's turnover ₹12 crore > ₹10 crore → 206C(1H) is otherwise triggered.

**WN-2 — Resolving the overlap.** The **second proviso to Sec 206C(1H)** provides that if TDS is deductible under any other provision (here Sec 194Q) and has been deducted, **TCS u/s 206C(1H) shall NOT apply**. Thus **Sec 194Q prevails** — the buyer deducts and the seller does not collect.

**WN-3 — Tax base and rate (Sec 194Q).** TDS @ **0.1%** on the purchase value **exceeding ₹50 lakh** (PAN available; else 5%). TDS is on the amount credited/paid on purchases beyond ₹50 lakh.
Taxable amount = ₹90,00,000 − ₹50,00,000 = **₹40,00,000.**

**Main computation**

| Statement Showing TDS u/s 194Q — Ravi Traders (buyer) | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Total purchases from Mohan Industries | 90,00,000 |
| Less: Threshold (not chargeable) | 50,00,000 |
| Amount liable to TDS | 40,00,000 |
| **TDS @ 0.1%** | **4,000** |

Formula: TDS = 0.1% × (₹90,00,000 − ₹50,00,000) = 0.1% × ₹40,00,000 = **₹4,000**, deducted by **Ravi Traders (buyer)**. Mohan Industries does **not** collect TCS u/s 206C(1H).

**(iii) If the buyer's turnover were only ₹8 crore.** Sec 194Q would **not** apply (buyer's turnover ≤ ₹10 crore). Then **Sec 206C(1H)** revives — **Mohan Industries (seller)** collects TCS @ 0.1% on receipts exceeding ₹50 lakh. TCS applies on the **amount received**; on receipts of ₹70,00,000, TCS = 0.1% × (₹70,00,000 − ₹50,00,000) = 0.1% × ₹20,00,000 = **₹2,000** (collected as and when consideration is received beyond ₹50 lakh).

**Answer:** (i)/(ii) **Sec 194Q applies — Ravi Traders deducts ₹4,000**; seller does not collect TCS. (iii) If buyer's turnover ≤ ₹10 crore, **seller collects TCS u/s 206C(1H) = ₹2,000** on receipts beyond ₹50 lakh.

### Q58. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Sec 139(8A) (Updated Return) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the concept of an **"updated return" u/s 139(8A)**. State the time limit, the circumstances in which it **cannot** be filed, and the **additional tax** payable u/s 140B.

**Answer:**
**Statutory basis.** Section **139(8A)** permits any person, whether or not he has furnished an original/belated/revised return, to furnish an **updated return** of income for a previous year at any time **within 48 months from the end of the relevant assessment year** (extended from 24 months by the Finance Act, 2025). Only **one** updated return can be filed for an assessment year.

**Bar on filing — circumstances where an updated return is NOT permitted.** An updated return **cannot** be furnished if it —
- is a **return of a loss**; or
- has the effect of **decreasing the total tax liability** determined on the basis of the earlier return; or
- results in a **refund** or increases the refund due; or
- if a **search u/s 132**, requisition u/s 132A, or survey u/s 133A (other than 133A(2A)) has been initiated against the person for that year or any assessment/reassessment/revision is pending or completed for that year (and other cases specified in the section).

In short, an updated return may be filed **only to declare additional income and pay more tax**, not to reduce tax or claim/enhance a refund or loss.

**Additional tax u/s 140B.** Tax must be paid along with the updated return together with **additional income-tax** on the aggregate of tax and interest, at the following escalating rates (post Finance Act, 2025):

| Updated return furnished within | Additional tax (on tax + interest) |
|---|---|
| 12 months from end of relevant A.Y. | **25%** |
| After 12 up to 24 months | **50%** |
| After 24 up to 36 months | **60%** |
| After 36 up to 48 months | **70%** |

**Conclusion:** Section 139(8A) is a voluntary-compliance window allowing a taxpayer to disclose omitted income within 48 months by paying tax, interest and graded additional tax u/s 140B — but never to reduce tax, claim a refund, or report a loss.

*(Full-marks tip: List the four "cannot-file" bars and the four-slab 140B additional-tax table. Stating the 48-month outer limit (Finance Act 2025) rather than the old 24 months secures the currency mark.)*

### Q59. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Belated (139(4)) & Revised (139(5)) Returns (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between a **belated return u/s 139(4)** and a **revised return u/s 139(5)**. Can a belated return be revised? State the time limit for both.

**Answer:**
**Statutory basis.** Section **139(4)** allows a person who has not furnished a return within the time u/s 139(1) to file a **belated return**; Section **139(5)** allows a person who has furnished a return u/s 139(1) or 139(4) to file a **revised return** on discovering any omission or wrong statement therein.

| Basis | Belated return — Sec 139(4) | Revised return — Sec 139(5) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Filing the return **after** the due date u/s 139(1) | **Correcting** an omission/wrong statement in an already-filed return |
| Pre-condition | No return filed within due date | An original (139(1)) or belated (139(4)) return already filed |
| Time limit | **3 months before the end of the relevant A.Y.** or before completion of assessment, whichever is earlier | Same limit — **3 months before end of A.Y.** or before completion of assessment, whichever earlier |
| Carry-forward of losses | Business/capital/speculation losses **cannot** be carried forward (except house-property loss & unabsorbed depreciation) | Depends on the return being revised |
| Fee u/s 234F | Attracts fee u/s 234F | The character follows the return revised |

**Can a belated return be revised?** **Yes.** Since the Finance Act, 2016, a **belated return filed u/s 139(4) can itself be revised u/s 139(5)**, provided the revision is made within the time limit (3 months before the end of the A.Y. or before completion of assessment, whichever is earlier).

**Conclusion:** A belated return is a late-filed return; a revised return is a corrective return of an earlier one. Both must be filed by **31 December of the A.Y.** (or before assessment is completed), and a belated return is now revisable.

### Q60. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Sec 143(1), 144 & 147 (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Briefly explain the scope of (i) processing/summary assessment u/s 143(1); (ii) best-judgment assessment u/s 144; and (iii) income escaping assessment u/s 147, indicating the trigger for each.

**Answer:**
**(i) Summary assessment / processing — Sec 143(1).** Every return is processed by the CPC in a **faceless, computerised** manner. The total income/loss is computed after making **prescribed adjustments** — arithmetical errors, incorrect claims apparent from the return, disallowance of losses/deductions claimed in a belated return or claims inconsistent with the audit report, etc. An **intimation** is issued specifying the sum payable or refund due; it must be sent within **9 months from the end of the financial year** in which the return is furnished. No opinion on merits is formed.

**(ii) Best-judgment assessment — Sec 144.** The Assessing Officer makes an assessment **to the best of his judgement**, after giving the assessee an opportunity of being heard (show-cause notice), where the assessee —
- **fails to file** the return u/s 139(1)/(4)/(5); or
- **fails to comply** with a notice u/s 142(1) or a direction u/s 142(2A) (special audit); or
- **fails to comply** with all the terms of a notice u/s 143(2).

The AO determines income on a reasonable, though estimative, basis; it cannot be arbitrary or vindictive.

**(iii) Income escaping assessment — Sec 147.** If the AO has **information which suggests that income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment**, he may, subject to the procedure in **Sec 148/148A** (issuing notice after conducting inquiry and giving the assessee an opportunity), **assess or reassess** such income and any other income that comes to notice during the proceedings. The reassessment is time-barred as per **Sec 149** (generally 3 years, extended to up to 5 years where escaped income represented in an asset/expenditure is ₹50 lakh or more, per the Finance Act, 2024 framework).

**Conclusion:** Sec 143(1) is a machine-check of the return, Sec 144 is a penal estimation on default, and Sec 147 reopens a concluded matter where income has escaped assessment — each with its own statutory trigger and limitation.

### Q63. Ch: Deductions under Chapter VI-A — Sec 80-IAC (Eligible Start-up) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions and quantum of deduction available to an "eligible start-up" u/s 80-IAC. How does it differ from Sec 80-IA in respect of the choice of years?

**Answer:**
**Statutory basis.** Section **80-IAC** allows an **eligible start-up** a deduction of **100% of the profits and gains** derived from an eligible business, for **any 3 consecutive assessment years** out of the **first 10 years** beginning from the year of incorporation.

**Conditions for an "eligible start-up".**
- It is a **company or an LLP** (not a firm) incorporated between **1 April 2016 and 31 March 2025** (the incorporation window is periodically extended by the Finance Acts);
- Its **total turnover** does not exceed **₹100 crore** in the previous year for which the deduction is claimed;
- It holds a **certificate of eligible business** from the Inter-Ministerial Board of Certification (DPIIT recognition);
- It is engaged in **innovation, development or improvement of products/processes/services**, or a scalable business model with high potential of employment generation or wealth creation;
- It is **not formed by splitting up or reconstruction** of an existing business, nor by transfer of old plant and machinery beyond the permitted 20%.

**Quantum & option.** Deduction = **100% of eligible profits** for **any 3 consecutive** assessment years, at the option of the assessee, within the **first 10 years** from incorporation. This lets a start-up (typically loss-making in early years) pick its **3 profitable years**.

| Basis | Sec 80-IAC (start-up) | Sec 80-IA (infrastructure) |
|---|---|---|
| Block of deduction | **3** consecutive years | **10** consecutive years |
| Chosen out of | first **10** years | **15/20** years |
| Eligible entity | Company/LLP start-up (DPIIT-certified) | Any assessee running eligible undertaking |

**Conclusion:** Sec 80-IAC gives a DPIIT-recognised start-up a 100% profit-linked deduction for any 3 consecutive years within its first 10 years, subject to the ₹100-crore-turnover and incorporation-window conditions.

### Q66. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Faceless / Best-Judgment (Sec 144, 144B) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Write a short note on the **faceless assessment scheme u/s 144B** — its objectives and broad procedure. Is a best-judgment assessment u/s 144 covered by the faceless scheme?

**Answer:**
**Statutory basis & objective.** Section **144B** provides for **faceless assessment** — assessments u/s 143(3) and 144 are to be made in a **faceless manner** through automated allocation, eliminating the physical interface between the assessee and the Assessing Officer. Its objectives are to impart **greater transparency, efficiency and accountability**, remove territorial jurisdiction, and enable **team-based assessment** with **dynamic jurisdiction**.

**Broad procedure.**
- The **National Faceless Assessment Centre (NaFAC)** is the single point of contact and serves all notices electronically;
- Cases are **randomly allocated** through an automated system to an **Assessment Unit**;
- The Assessment Unit may seek information, and request a **Verification Unit** (for enquiry/verification) or a **Technical Unit** (for legal/technical/valuation inputs);
- A **draft assessment order** is prepared; where a variation prejudicial to the assessee is proposed, a **show-cause notice** is issued and the assessee is given an opportunity to respond (with an option of **personal hearing through video-conferencing**);
- High-variation cases may be sent for **review** by a Review Unit before the order is finalised and communicated with demand notice/penalty proceedings.

**Whether Sec 144 is covered.** **Yes.** Best-judgment assessments u/s 144 are within the ambit of the faceless scheme; however, even in a faceless best-judgment assessment, the assessee must be given a **reasonable opportunity of being heard** (show-cause notice), which cannot be dispensed with.

**Conclusion:** Section 144B mandates faceless, team-based, technology-driven assessment (covering both 143(3) and 144) through the NaFAC, safeguarding the assessee's right to be heard via written response and video-conference hearing.

*(Full-marks tip: Naming the units — Assessment / Verification / Technical / Review — and the NaFAC, plus the mandatory show-cause and VC-hearing safeguard, distinguishes a full-mark answer.)*

### Q69. Ch: GST – Supply — Schedule I Deemed Supply & Related Party (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** M/s Zenith Ltd., registered in Maharashtra, has the following transactions during October 2024. Examine, with reasons under Schedule I / Schedule III of the CGST Act, 2017, whether each constitutes a "supply" even though made **without consideration**, and state whether GST is leviable:

| # | Transaction |
|---|-------------|
| (i) | Head office at Mumbai (Maharashtra, GSTIN-A) transfers finished goods to its own branch at Pune (same State, same GSTIN) |
| (ii) | Mumbai head office transfers finished goods to its own branch at Bengaluru (Karnataka, separate GSTIN) |
| (iii) | Zenith Ltd. gifts a laptop worth ₹90,000 to a whole-time director (an employee) as a Diwali gift |
| (iv) | Zenith Ltd. gifts gold coins worth ₹40,000 to the same employee-director |
| (v) | Zenith Ltd. permanently transfers a machine (on which ITC was availed) to its associated concern "Zenith Retail LLP" (a related person) free of cost |

**Answer:**
The charge under section 7(1)(c) read with **Schedule I** deems specified activities to be "supply" **even when made without consideration**. Each item is examined below.

**(i) HO to branch, same GSTIN (Mumbai to Pune):** Both establishments are covered by a **single registration** within Maharashtra, hence they are the **same person**, not "distinct persons" under section 25(4). A transfer between two units under one GSTIN is **not a supply** — there is no supply to a distinct/related person. **No GST.**

**(ii) HO to branch, different States (Mumbai to Bengaluru):** Establishments in two States are **distinct persons** under **section 25(4)**. Supply of goods between distinct persons **in the course or furtherance of business, even without consideration, is a supply** under **Para 2 of Schedule I**. **GST (IGST) is leviable**, value being determined under Rule 28 (open market value, with the second proviso permitting the invoice value where the recipient is eligible for full ITC).

**(iii) Gift of laptop ₹90,000 to employee:** Under **Para 1 of Schedule I**, gifts by an employer to an employee are **NOT treated as supply if the value does not exceed ₹50,000 in a financial year**; beyond ₹50,000 the excess is a supply. Since ₹90,000 > ₹50,000, the transaction **is a supply** and GST is leviable (on the value in excess of ₹50,000 per the CBIC clarification / press release). Services by an employee to the employer in the course of employment are outside GST under Schedule III, but a **gift is not part of the employment contract**.

**(iv) Gold coins ₹40,000 to the same employee in the same FY:** Gifts to an employee are aggregated for the ₹50,000 threshold in a financial year. Adding ₹40,000 to the earlier ₹90,000 makes the aggregate ₹1,30,000, well beyond ₹50,000; the value in excess of ₹50,000 is a **supply** liable to GST.

**(v) Free transfer of machine to related LLP (ITC availed):** Zenith Retail LLP is a **related person**. Supply of goods between **related persons** in the course/furtherance of business, **even without consideration**, is a supply under **Para 2 of Schedule I**. Additionally, permanent transfer of a business asset on which **ITC has been availed** is a supply under **Para 1 of Schedule I**. Hence the free transfer **is a supply** and GST is payable, value being the open market value under Rule 28.

**Conclusion:** (i) Not a supply; (ii) Supply (IGST); (iii) Supply on excess over ₹50,000; (iv) Supply on excess over ₹50,000; (v) Supply.

### Q84. Ch: GST – Time & Value of Supply — Time of Supply of Goods & Change in Rate (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Determine the **time of supply** and the **rate of tax** applicable in each case for M/s Crest Ltd., citing **section 12** and **section 14** of the CGST Act, 2017. Assume the GST rate on the goods changed from **12% to 18% w.e.f. 01-Mar-2025**.

| Case | Date of removal / supply of goods | Date of invoice | Date of payment received |
|------|-----------------------------------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| (i) | 25-Feb-2025 (before rate change) | 03-Mar-2025 (after) | 05-Mar-2025 (after) |
| (ii) | 04-Mar-2025 (after) | 26-Feb-2025 (before) | 27-Feb-2025 (before) |
| (iii) | 04-Mar-2025 (after) | 28-Feb-2025 (before) | 06-Mar-2025 (after) |

Also, for a **forward-charge supply of goods** generally, state the time of supply rule under section 12(2).

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — General ToS of goods [Sec 12(2)].** For goods under forward charge, ToS is the **earlier of** (a) the date of issue of **invoice** (or the last date by which invoice is required under section 31), and (b) the date of **receipt of payment**. (By Notification No. 66/2017, tax on goods is generally payable on the invoice date, advances for goods not being taxed.)

**WN-2 — Change in rate rule [Sec 14].** Where the rate changes, ToS depends on how many of the **three events** — (a) supply of goods, (b) issue of invoice, (c) receipt of payment — occur **before vs after** the change:
- If **supply is before** the change: ToS is the **earlier of invoice date or payment date** if **both** are after → but the governing sub-clause: if goods supplied before change and both invoice & payment after → ToS = earlier of the two (both after → **new rate**); if either invoice or payment is before → ToS = that earlier event (**old rate**).
- If **supply is after** the change: if **both** invoice and payment are **before** → ToS = earlier of the two (**old rate**); if either is after → ToS = the event occurring after (**new rate**).

**WN-3 — Case (i): Supply BEFORE change; invoice & payment BOTH AFTER.**
Per section 14(a)(i), where goods are supplied before the rate change but invoice and payment are both after → **ToS = earlier of invoice date (03-Mar) or payment date (05-Mar) = 03-Mar-2025**, and the **new rate 18%** applies.

**WN-4 — Case (ii): Supply AFTER change; invoice & payment BOTH BEFORE.**
Per section 14(b)(ii), where goods are supplied after the change but invoice and payment are both before → **ToS = earlier of invoice (26-Feb) or payment (27-Feb) = 26-Feb-2025**, and the **old rate 12%** applies.

**WN-5 — Case (iii): Supply AFTER change; invoice BEFORE, payment AFTER.**
Per section 14(b), where supply is after and only one of invoice/payment is before (here invoice before, payment after), ToS is the date of the event that occurs **after** the change — payment date **06-Mar-2025**, and the **new rate 18%** applies.

**Statement Showing Time of Supply & Rate (Change-in-rate, Sec 14)**

| Case | ToS | Rate |
|------|-----|------|
| (i) | 03-Mar-2025 | 18% |
| (ii) | 26-Feb-2025 | 12% |
| (iii) | 06-Mar-2025 | 18% |

**Answer:** (i) ToS 03-Mar-2025 @18%; (ii) ToS 26-Feb-2025 @12%; (iii) ToS 06-Mar-2025 @18%. General rule [Sec 12(2)]: ToS of goods = earlier of invoice date (or last date for invoice) and date of receipt of payment.

*(Full-marks tip: Section 14 is decided by the **two-of-three events** test relative to supply timing. Reward for correctly picking old vs new rate. Common deduction: applying section 12(2) blindly and ignoring section 14 when a rate change is involved.)*

**Solution:**

IGST paid on machinery = ₹20,00,000 × 18% = **₹3,60,000**.
Since the capital goods are used commonly for taxable and exempt supplies, the credit is credited to the electronic credit ledger and is denoted as common credit "A". This "A" is to be spread over the useful life of 60 months (5 years) as per Rule 43(1)(c).

**WN-2 — Common credit attributable to a tax period (Tm):**
Rule 43(1)(d): amount of common credit attributable to a month =
Tm = Tc ÷ 60, where Tc = aggregate of "A" (common credit on capital goods) = ₹3,60,000.
Tm = ₹3,60,000 ÷ 60 = **₹6,000 per month**.

**WN-3 — Credit attributable to exempt supplies for the month (Te):**

- E = aggregate exempt turnover during the tax period = ₹20,00,000
- F = total turnover during the tax period = ₹80,00,000
Te = (₹20,00,000 ÷ ₹80,00,000) × ₹6,000 = 0.25 × ₹6,000 = **₹1,500**.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|

| Useful life (months) | 60 |
| Common credit for the month (Tm = A ÷ 60) | 6,000 |
| Exempt turnover ratio (E ÷ F) | 0.25 |
| Credit attributable to exempt supply (Te) to be added to output tax | **1,500** |

For a routine monthly reversal made in the return of the same month, no separate interest arises; interest applies only where the reversal is delayed beyond the due date.

**Answer:** ITC to be reversed (added to output tax) for March 2025 = **₹1,500**; interest @18% p.a. only on delay.

### Q89. Ch: GST – Input Tax Credit — Conditions for availing ITC u/s 16 (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State any four conditions, other than possession of a tax invoice, that a registered person must satisfy under Section 16 of the CGST Act, 2017 before he becomes entitled to take input tax credit. Also state the time limit under Section 16(4).

**Answer:** Section 16(2) of the CGST Act, 2017 lays down the cumulative conditions, all of which must be satisfied before a registered person becomes entitled to input tax credit in respect of any supply of goods or services.

The conditions (in addition to possession of a tax invoice or debit note) are as follows:

1. **Details communicated and not restricted [Section 16(2)(aa)]:** The details of the invoice or debit note must have been furnished by the supplier in his statement of outward supplies (GSTR-1) and such details must have been communicated to the recipient in GSTR-2B, and the credit must not have been restricted.

2. **Receipt of goods or services [Section 16(2)(b)]:** The registered person must have actually received the goods or services or both. Where goods are delivered to a third person on the direction of the recipient ("bill-to ship-to"), the recipient is deemed to have received the goods.

3. **Tax actually paid to Government [Section 16(2)(c)]:** The tax charged in respect of such supply must have been actually paid to the Government, either in cash or through utilisation of admissible input tax credit.

4. **Furnishing of return [Section 16(2)(d)]:** The registered person must have furnished the return under Section 39 (GSTR-3B).

Further, as per **the second and third provisos to Section 16(2)**, the recipient must pay the supplier the value of supply along with tax within **180 days** from the date of invoice, failing which the credit availed is added to output liability with interest; the credit is restored on payment.

**Time limit — Section 16(4):** A registered person cannot take ITC in respect of any invoice or debit note after the **30th day of November** following the end of the financial year to which such invoice/debit note pertains, or the date of furnishing of the relevant annual return, whichever is **earlier**.

**Conclusion:** ITC is available only when all conditions of Section 16(2) are cumulatively satisfied and the credit is claimed within the time limit of Section 16(4).

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner looks for the exact clause references (aa)/(b)/(c)/(d) and the "30th November following FY OR annual return, whichever earlier" wording — not the older 'September following FY'.)*

### Q91. Ch: GST – Registration — Compulsory Registration u/s 24 (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Examine, with reference to Section 22 and Section 24 of the CGST Act, 2017, whether registration is required in each of the following independent cases for the financial year 2024-25. Give reasons.

| Case | Facts |
|---|---|

| Case | Registration required? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Mr. A, goods, ₹28 lakh (Rajasthan) | **No** | Rajasthan is a normal-category State with a threshold of ₹40 lakh for exclusive supply of goods; ₹28 lakh < ₹40 lakh, so registration is not required under Section 22. |

| (e) Mr. E, supplies goods through ECO, ₹5 lakh | **Yes** | Section 24(ix) — persons supplying goods through an ECO required to collect TCS must register compulsorily, irrespective of threshold. (Note: for suppliers of services through ECO, an exemption up to ₹20 lakh applies, but here supply is of goods.) |

**Conclusion:** Registration is compulsory in cases (c) and (e); not required in (a), (b) and (d).

*(Full-marks tip: The subtle marks are in case (b) — inter-State supply of services enjoys a ₹20 lakh exemption from the Section 24(i) rigour, a fact students routinely miss and wrongly mark 'Yes'.)*

### Q92. Ch: GST – Registration — Casual Taxable Person & Non-Resident Taxable Person (Marks: 6) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between a "Casual Taxable Person" (CTP) and a "Non-Resident Taxable Person" (NRTP) under the CGST Act, 2017 with respect to definition, advance deposit, validity of registration and eligibility of input tax credit. Also state the time limit for applying for registration.

**Answer:** Both a Casual Taxable Person and a Non-Resident Taxable Person are subject to compulsory registration under Section 24(ii) and Section 24(vii) respectively, and special provisions of Section 27 and Rule 13/15 govern them.

**Definitions:**
- A **Casual Taxable Person** [Section 2(20)] means a person who occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods or services in a State/UT where he has no fixed place of business, but he has a fixed place of business elsewhere in India.
- A **Non-Resident Taxable Person** [Section 2(77)] means any person who occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods or services but who has **no fixed place of business or residence in India**.

**Distinction:**

| Basis | Casual Taxable Person | Non-Resident Taxable Person |
|---|---|---|
| Place of business in India | Has a fixed place of business elsewhere in India | Has no fixed place of business/residence in India |
| PAN | Must have PAN of India | Not required; may register on basis of other prescribed documents (e.g., passport / TIN of foreign country) |
| Advance deposit (Sec 27(2)) | Advance tax equal to estimated tax liability for the period of registration | Same — advance deposit of estimated tax liability |
| Input Tax Credit | Eligible to take ITC on his inward supplies | **Not eligible** for ITC except on goods imported by him (Section 25 / Rule) |
| Return | Files GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B | Files **GSTR-5** |

**Validity of registration [Section 27(1)]:** The certificate of registration issued to a CTP or an NRTP is valid for the period specified in the application or **90 days** from the effective date of registration, whichever is earlier. This may be **extended by a further 90 days** on request, on payment of additional advance tax for the extended period.

**Time limit for applying:** A CTP/NRTP must apply for registration **at least 5 days prior** to the commencement of business (an NRTP applies in FORM GST REG-09).

**Conclusion:** The principal distinction is that an NRTP has no establishment in India, cannot avail ITC (except on imported goods), and files GSTR-5, whereas a CTP is India-based and enjoys normal ITC.

*(Full-marks tip: The distinguishing marks lie in the ITC ineligibility of an NRTP, the different return (GSTR-5), and the 90+90 day validity with advance deposit — state Section 27 explicitly.)*

### Q93. Ch: GST – Registration — Amendment, Cancellation & Revocation (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the circumstances under which the proper officer may cancel the registration of a taxable person on his own motion under Section 29(2) of the CGST Act, 2017. Also state the time limit for applying for revocation of cancellation.

**Answer:** Under Section 29(2) of the CGST Act, 2017, the proper officer may cancel the registration of a person from such date, including any retrospective date, as he may deem fit, where any of the following circumstances exist:

1. A **registered person has contravened** such provisions of the Act or the rules made thereunder as may be prescribed [Section 29(2)(a)].
2. A person paying tax under the **composition scheme (Section 10)** has not furnished the return for a financial year **beyond three months** from the due date of furnishing the said return [Section 29(2)(b)].
3. Any registered person, **other than a composition taxpayer**, has not furnished returns for such continuous **tax period** as may be prescribed [Section 29(2)(c)].
4. A person who has taken **voluntary registration** under Section 25(3) has **not commenced business** within six months from the date of registration [Section 29(2)(d)].
5. Where registration was obtained by means of **fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts** [Section 29(2)(e)].

**Opportunity of being heard:** The proper officer shall not cancel the registration without giving the person a reasonable **opportunity of being heard** (proviso to Section 29(2)).

**Revocation of cancellation [Section 30]:** Where registration is cancelled by the proper officer on his own motion, the registered person may apply for **revocation of cancellation** within **90 days** from the date of service of the cancellation order. This period may be extended by the Additional/Joint Commissioner by a further 30 days and, thereafter, by the Commissioner by another 30 days (i.e., up to 180 days in aggregate on sufficient cause).

**Conclusion:** Cancellation on the officer's motion is permissible only in the five specified situations after affording a hearing, and the affected person has 90 days (extendable) to seek revocation.

*(Full-marks tip: Mention the mandatory opportunity of being heard and the extended revocation window (90 + 30 + 30 days) — the extension mechanism is the fresh point examiners now test.)*

### Q94. Ch: GST – Registration — Aggregate Turnover Computation for Threshold (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** M/s Greenfield Traders, having its principal place of business in Uttar Pradesh (a normal-category State), furnishes the following details of supplies during FY 2024-25 (all figures exclusive of GST). Compute the "aggregate turnover" under Section 2(6) and determine whether the firm is liable to be registered.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Taxable supply of goods (intra-State) | 22,00,000 |
| Exempt supply of goods | 9,00,000 |
| Supplies on which tax is payable by recipient under RCM (outward) | 4,00,000 |
| Export of goods (zero-rated) | 6,00,000 |
| Inward supplies on which firm pays tax under RCM | 3,00,000 |
| Interest earned on fixed deposits (exempt) | 1,50,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Statutory definition (Section 2(6)):**
"Aggregate turnover" = value of all **(a) taxable supplies + (b) exempt supplies + (c) exports + (d) inter-State supplies** of persons having the same PAN, computed on an all-India basis, **excluding** (i) CGST/SGST/IGST/cess, and (ii) the value of **inward supplies on which tax is payable under reverse charge**.

**WN-2 — Inclusions/Exclusions:**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) | Include? |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable intra-State supply of goods | 22,00,000 | Yes |
| Exempt supply of goods | 9,00,000 | Yes (exempt supply included) |
| Outward supplies taxable under RCM (recipient pays) | 4,00,000 | Yes — it is the supplier's outward turnover |
| Export of goods (zero-rated) | 6,00,000 | Yes |
| Interest on FD (exempt supply) | 1,50,000 | Yes — exempt supply forms part of aggregate turnover |
| Inward supply under RCM | 3,00,000 | **No** — expressly excluded |

**WN-3 — Computation:**
Aggregate turnover = 22,00,000 + 9,00,000 + 4,00,000 + 6,00,000 + 1,50,000 = **₹42,50,000**.

**Statement Showing Aggregate Turnover — FY 2024-25**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Taxable supply of goods | 22,00,000 |
| Exempt supply of goods | 9,00,000 |
| Outward supplies under RCM | 4,00,000 |
| Export of goods | 6,00,000 |
| Interest on FD (exempt) | 1,50,000 |
| **Aggregate Turnover** | **42,50,000** |

**Registration:** The threshold for a person engaged **exclusively in supply of goods** in a normal State is ₹40 lakh. However, since the firm also makes an exempt supply of services (interest — a service), and interest income aside, the presence of exempt supply of services could reduce the limit; but interest on deposits is specifically excluded while computing the ₹40 lakh eligibility for the higher goods-threshold. Even so, aggregate turnover of **₹42.50 lakh exceeds ₹40 lakh**.

**Conclusion:** Aggregate turnover = **₹42,50,000**; the firm **is liable to be registered** as it exceeds the ₹40 lakh threshold.

*(Full-marks tip: Outward RCM supplies ARE part of aggregate turnover; only INWARD RCM supplies are excluded. Exempt supplies, including exempt interest, are included — a frequent error is excluding them.)*

### Q95. Ch: GST – Registration — Persons Not Liable to Register u/s 23 (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the categories of persons who are not liable to obtain registration under Section 23 of the CGST Act, 2017. Also state whether a person exclusively making supplies wholly exempt from tax and simultaneously liable under reverse charge is required to register.

**Answer:** Section 23(1) of the CGST Act, 2017 specifies the persons who are **not liable to be registered**, notwithstanding the thresholds in Section 22 or the compulsory categories in Section 24:

1. **Section 23(1)(a):** Any person engaged **exclusively** in the business of supplying goods or services or both that are **wholly exempt** from tax, or **not liable to tax**, under the CGST Act or the IGST Act.

2. **Section 23(1)(b):** An **agriculturist**, to the extent of supply of produce out of cultivation of land.

3. **Section 23(2):** The Government may, on the recommendation of the Council, by notification, specify categories of persons who shall be exempt from obtaining registration. Under this power, notifications exempt, inter alia:
   - Persons making only supplies on which tax is payable by the recipient under **reverse charge** (i.e., persons making only RCM outward supplies);
   - Persons making inter-State supply of **taxable services** up to ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special-category States);
   - Casual taxable persons making inter-State supply of specified **handicraft goods** up to the threshold.

**On the specific question — exempt supplier also liable under RCM:** As per the notification issued under Section 23(2), a person engaged **exclusively** in making supplies wholly exempt from tax is not liable to register, even though he may be liable to pay tax under reverse charge on his inward supplies. Section 23 has been given an overriding effect over Section 24; hence, such a person is **not required to obtain registration** merely on account of the reverse-charge inward liability.

**Conclusion:** Persons under Section 23 are outside the registration net; an exclusively-exempt supplier is not compelled to register even where he bears RCM on inward supplies.

*(Full-marks tip: Post the amendment giving Section 23 overriding effect, the exempt-supplier-with-RCM answer flipped to 'not required' — quoting the override earns full marks; students citing the old Section 24 position lose marks.)*

### Q96. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — E-Invoice & E-Way Bill (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Examine the following independent situations for FY 2024-25 with reference to the provisions relating to e-invoicing (Rule 48(4)) and the e-way bill (Rule 138) under the CGST Rules, 2017.

| Case | Facts |
|---|---|
| (a) | M/s Alpha Ltd. (aggregate turnover ₹8 crore in FY 2022-23) makes a B2B supply of goods. Is e-invoicing mandatory? |
| (b) | M/s Beta Ltd. transports goods worth ₹45,000 (single conveyance) within the State. Is an e-way bill required? |
| (c) | M/s Gamma Ltd. sends goods worth ₹80,000 for job work to another State. |
| (d) | A supply of goods worth ₹2,00,000 is made by a registered person; distance of transport is 40 km within the State. |

**Answer:** E-invoicing under Rule 48(4) and e-way bill under Rule 138 are examined case-wise.

| Case | Requirement | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Alpha Ltd., turnover ₹8 crore, B2B | **E-invoice mandatory** | E-invoicing applies to a registered person whose aggregate turnover in **any** preceding financial year from 2017-18 onwards exceeded **₹5 crore**, for B2B supplies and exports. ₹8 crore > ₹5 crore → mandatory (B2C supplies are outside e-invoicing). |
| (b) Beta Ltd., goods ₹45,000, intra-State | **E-way bill not required** | An e-way bill is required where the consignment value **exceeds ₹50,000**. Here value ₹45,000 ≤ ₹50,000 → not required (subject to State-specific intra-State limits). |
| (c) Gamma Ltd., job work, ₹80,000, inter-State | **E-way bill required** | For **inter-State** movement of goods for **job work**, an e-way bill must be generated **irrespective of the ₹50,000 threshold** (special rule for job work and handicraft goods). |
| (d) Supply ₹2,00,000, 40 km, intra-State | **E-way bill required** | Consignment value ₹2,00,000 > ₹50,000 → e-way bill required. However, Part-B (vehicle details) need not be furnished where distance is **up to 50 km** within the State from consignor to transporter. |

**Conclusion:** E-invoice is mandatory in (a); e-way bill is required in (c) and (d) but not in (b).

*(Full-marks tip: The ₹5 crore e-invoice threshold and the "irrespective of value for inter-State job work" rule are the fresh testing points; also mention the 50-km Part-B relaxation for the last mark.)*

### Q97. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Interest on Delayed Payment u/s 50 (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** M/s Meridian Enterprises, a registered person, had an output tax liability for the month of August 2024 as under. It failed to file GSTR-3B by the due date (20th September 2024) and finally filed it on 15th November 2024. Compute the interest payable under Section 50 of the CGST Act, 2017.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Total output tax liability for August 2024 | 6,00,000 |
| ITC available and credited in electronic credit ledger (as per GSTR-2B) | 4,50,000 |
| Balance in electronic cash ledger already deposited before due date | 50,000 |

Interest rate = 18% p.a. Take the delay period as 56 days (21-Sep-2024 to 15-Nov-2024).

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Net tax liability discharged in cash (proviso to Section 50(1)):**
Interest is chargeable only on that portion of the tax which is paid by **debiting the electronic cash ledger** — i.e., the net cash liability — where the return is filed after the due date (proviso to Section 50(1), retrospective).
- Output tax = ₹6,00,000
- Less: ITC utilised from credit ledger = ₹4,50,000
- Net tax payable = ₹1,50,000
- Less: Amount already lying in cash ledger before due date = ₹50,000
- **Net tax paid in cash after due date = ₹1,00,000**.

**WN-2 — Interest computation:**
Interest = Net cash tax × 18% × (days ÷ 365)
= ₹1,00,000 × 18% × (56 ÷ 365)
= ₹1,00,000 × 0.18 × 0.15342
= **₹2,761.64 ≈ ₹2,762**.

**Statement Showing Interest u/s 50 — August 2024**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Output tax liability | 6,00,000 |
| Less: ITC utilised | (4,50,000) |
| Less: Cash already in ledger before due date | (50,000) |
| Net tax paid in cash after due date | 1,00,000 |
| Days of delay | 56 |
| Interest @18% p.a. (1,00,000 × 18% × 56/365) | **2,762** |

**Note:** As per Section 50(1) proviso, interest is levied only on the net tax liability discharged through the cash ledger, provided the return is furnished after the due date but before commencement of any proceedings under Section 73/74. Had ITC been wrongly availed and utilised, interest @18% under Section 50(3) would apply on such amount separately.

**Answer:** Interest payable under Section 50 = **₹2,762** (rounded under Section 288B).

*(Full-marks tip: Interest is on the NET cash liability (after ITC and after cash already deposited), not on the gross ₹6,00,000 — this proviso is the whole point. Show the 56/365 day-count and round off.)*

### Q98. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — GSTR-3B vs GSTR-1/2B Reconciliation & Order of Set-off (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** M/s Orbit Industries has the following balances and liabilities for the month of October 2024. Determine the manner of utilisation of input tax credit under Section 49, 49A and Rule 88A, and the net GST payable in cash.

IGST credit must be fully utilised **first** — against IGST output, and then against CGST/SGST in any order — before CGST or SGST credit can be used. CGST credit cannot be set off against SGST, and vice versa.

| Less: SGST credit | — | — | (30,000) |
| **Payable in cash** | **Nil** | **Nil** | **60,000** |

### Q99. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Contents & Time Limit of Tax Invoice (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the time limit for issuing a tax invoice under Section 31 read with Rule 47 of the CGST Rules, 2017 in the case of (i) supply of goods involving movement, (ii) supply of goods not involving movement, (iii) supply of services (general), and (iv) supply of services by an insurer/banking company. Also state when a "bill of supply" is issued instead of a tax invoice.

**Answer:** Section 31 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 47 prescribes the time limit for issuance of a tax invoice, which differs for goods and services.

**(A) Supply of goods [Section 31(1)]:**

| Situation | Time limit for issue of tax invoice |
|---|---|
| (i) Supply involving movement of goods | Before or at the time of **removal** of goods for supply to the recipient |
| (ii) Supply not involving movement | Before or at the time of **delivery** of goods or making the goods available to the recipient |

**(B) Supply of services [Section 31(2) read with Rule 47]:**

| Situation | Time limit |
|---|---|
| (iii) General case | Within **30 days** from the date of **supply of service** |
| (iv) Insurer / banking company / financial institution / NBFC | Within **45 days** from the date of supply of service |

**Bill of Supply [Section 31(3)(c)]:** A registered person supplying **exempted goods or services**, or a person paying tax under the **composition scheme (Section 10)**, shall issue a **bill of supply** instead of a tax invoice, since no tax is collected from the recipient. Where the value of supply is less than ₹200 and the recipient is unregistered and does not require an invoice, issuance may be dispensed with subject to conditions.

### Q100. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — GSTR-9 Annual Return Reconciliation (Marks: 4) [Application]
**Question:** M/s Pinnacle Ltd. is preparing its Annual Return (GSTR-9) for FY 2023-24. State (i) who is required to file GSTR-9 and the due date; (ii) whether GSTR-9 can be revised; and (iii) explain, with the given figures, the additional tax payable if the outward supplies declared in GSTR-9 exceed those declared in GSTR-3B during the year.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Total taxable outward supplies as per books / GSTR-9 | 52,00,000 |
| Outward supplies actually declared in GSTR-3B during FY | 48,00,000 |
| Applicable GST rate | 18% |

**Answer:** The Annual Return under Section 44 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 80 consolidates the monthly returns filed during the financial year.

**(i) Who must file & due date:** Every registered person (other than an Input Service Distributor, a person paying TDS under Section 51, a person collecting TCS under Section 52, a casual taxable person and a non-resident taxable person) is required to file the annual return in **FORM GSTR-9**. It is **optional** for a registered person whose aggregate turnover in the financial year is up to **₹2 crore**. The due date is **31st December** following the end of the relevant financial year.

**(ii) Revision:** GSTR-9 **cannot be revised** once filed. It is a consolidated, one-time annual statement; any differences must be paid through **FORM DRC-03**.

**(iii) Additional tax on under-declared outward supplies:**
- Outward supplies as per books/GSTR-9 = ₹52,00,000
- Outward supplies declared in GSTR-3B = ₹48,00,000
- **Under-declared turnover = ₹4,00,000**
- Additional GST payable = ₹4,00,000 × 18% = **₹72,000**.

This differential tax of ₹72,000, being tax short-paid during the year, must be paid through **FORM GST DRC-03** along with **interest @18% p.a. under Section 50** from the respective due dates till the date of payment. Such additional liability disclosed in the annual return cannot be discharged by utilising ITC of the return period unless otherwise available; it is generally paid in cash.

**Conclusion:** GSTR-9 is due by 31st December (optional up to ₹2 crore turnover), cannot be revised, and the shortfall of ₹72,000 is payable via DRC-03 with interest under Section 50.

*(Full-marks tip: State the ₹2 crore optional limit, the 31 December due date, "no revision — pay via DRC-03", and compute the differential tax with Section 50 interest — each is a distinct half-mark.)*
