### Q1. Ch: Basic Concepts — Capital vs Revenue Receipt Tests (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** "The distinction between a capital receipt and a revenue receipt is fundamental to the scheme of the Income-tax Act, 1961." Explain the principal tests laid down by courts to distinguish a capital receipt from a revenue receipt, and state how the Act itself deems certain capital receipts to be taxable.

**Answer:**
The Income-tax Act, 1961 does not define "capital receipt" or "revenue receipt"; the distinction has been evolved by judicial pronouncements, and the charging section (Section 4) taxes "total income" which, subject to specific deeming provisions, is confined to receipts of a revenue nature.

The following tests have been laid down by courts to draw the distinction:

| Test | Capital receipt | Revenue receipt |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed vs circulating capital | Receipt from sale of a **fixed/source** asset (the tree) | Receipt from **circulating** capital / produce (the fruit) |
| Compensation test | Compensation for loss of a **source of income** or sterilisation of a profit-making apparatus | Compensation for loss of **income** or in the ordinary course of business |
| Substitution test | Amount received in substitution of a **capital asset / right** | Amount received in substitution of **income** |
| Nature in the hands of receiver | Determined by quality of receipt in the recipient's hands, **not** by how the payer treats it | — |

As a general rule, a **capital receipt is not chargeable to tax** unless expressly made taxable, whereas a **revenue receipt is chargeable** unless expressly exempt.

The Act deems certain capital receipts to be taxable, for example: capital gains on transfer of a capital asset are charged under Section 45; compensation for termination/modification of employment terms is taxable as profits in lieu of salary under Section 17(3)(iii); and certain sums received under a Keyman insurance policy and compensation on termination of a business agency (Section 28) are charged as business income.

**Conclusion:** The character of a receipt is decided by applying the fixed-vs-circulating-capital, compensation and substitution tests; a capital receipt escapes tax unless a specific deeming provision (e.g., Sec 45, 17(3), 28) brings it to charge.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards naming the exact test with the "tree vs fruit" and "profit-making apparatus" phraseology and citing at least two deeming sections. Marks are lost for merely writing "capital is one-time and revenue is recurring" without the source/substitution reasoning.)*

### Q2. Ch: Basic Concepts — Diversion of Income vs Application of Income (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between "diversion of income by an overriding title" and "application of income", and state why the distinction matters in computing total income.

**Answer:**
The principle rests on the charging provision (Section 4) read with the concept of "real income": tax is levied only on income that has actually accrued to and reached the assessee.

**Diversion of income by overriding title** occurs where, by virtue of an obligation created by a decree, statute or a valid instrument, income is diverted **before it reaches the assessee** and never becomes his income at all. Such diverted income is **not includible** in the total income of the assessee (as held in *CIT v. Sitaldas Tirathdas*).

**Application of income** occurs where the income first accrues to and is received by the assessee, and thereafter he applies it to discharge an obligation of his own. Here the income has already reached him, so it is **fully taxable** in his hands even though he later parts with it.

| Basis | Diversion by overriding title | Application of income |
|---|---|---|
| When obligation operates | **Before** income reaches assessee | **After** income reaches assessee |
| Source of obligation | Overriding title (decree/charge on source) | Assessee's own volition/personal obligation |
| Taxability | **Excluded** from total income | **Included** in total income |

**Conclusion:** Where the obligation attaches to the **source** and diverts income at inception, it is diversion (not taxable); where it is discharged out of income already earned, it is mere application (taxable).

*(Full-marks tip: Cite *Sitaldas Tirathdas* and phrase the test as "obligation to attach to the source before it becomes income of the assessee." A common deduction is treating alimony/charge as diversion without checking whether the charge is on the source.)*

### Q3. Ch: Basic Concepts — Slab Rates & 115BAC New Regime with Surcharge (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Aravind, a resident individual aged 45, has a **Total Income of ₹62,00,000** for the previous year 2024-25 (Assessment Year 2025-26). He has **not** opted out of the default tax regime under Section 115BAC. Compute his tax liability, showing the slab computation, surcharge and health & education cess.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Applicable regime.** For AY 2025-26, Section 115BAC(1A) is the **default regime**. As Mr. Aravind has not opted out, tax is computed on the 115BAC(1A) slab rates. No basic-exemption enhancement for age applies under the default regime.

**WN-2: Slab computation under Section 115BAC(1A).**

| Slab of Total Income | Rate | Amount (₹) | Tax (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 3,00,000 | Nil | 3,00,000 | 0 |
| 3,00,001 – 7,00,000 | 5% | 4,00,000 | 20,000 |
| 7,00,001 – 10,00,000 | 10% | 3,00,000 | 30,000 |
| 10,00,001 – 12,00,000 | 15% | 2,00,000 | 30,000 |
| 12,00,001 – 15,00,000 | 20% | 3,00,000 | 60,000 |
| Above 15,00,000 | 30% | 47,00,000 | 14,10,000 |
| **Total** | | **62,00,000** | **15,50,000** |

**WN-3: Surcharge.** Total income ₹62,00,000 exceeds ₹50 lakh but does not exceed ₹1 crore, so surcharge is levied at **10%** of income-tax.
Surcharge = 10% × ₹15,50,000 = **₹1,55,000**.

**WN-4: Marginal relief.** Income exceeds ₹50 lakh by ₹12,00,000. Tax + surcharge at ₹62,00,000 = ₹15,50,000 + ₹1,55,000 = ₹17,05,000. Tax on ₹50,00,000 (no surcharge) under WN-2 slabs = ₹20,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹60,000 + 30% × ₹35,00,000 (₹10,50,000) = ₹11,90,000. Since the increase in tax (₹17,05,000 − ₹11,90,000 = ₹5,15,000) is **less** than the increase in income (₹12,00,000), **no marginal relief** is available.

**WN-5: Health & Education Cess @ 4%.**
Cess = 4% × (₹15,50,000 + ₹1,55,000) = 4% × ₹17,05,000 = **₹68,200**.

**Statement Showing Tax Liability of Mr. Aravind (AY 2025-26)**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Tax on total income (WN-2) | 15,50,000 |
| Add: Surcharge @ 10% (WN-3) | 1,55,000 |
| Less: Marginal relief (WN-4) | Nil |
| Tax and surcharge | 17,05,000 |
| Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% (WN-5) | 68,200 |
| **Total tax liability** | **17,73,200** |

**Answer:** Total tax liability of Mr. Aravind for AY 2025-26 = **₹17,73,200** (rounded off under Section 288B).

*(Full-marks tip: Under 115BAC the surcharge on income above ₹2 crore is capped at 25%, and the ₹3 lakh basic exemption applies irrespective of age — writing ₹2.5 lakh or applying senior-citizen slabs is the common error. Always test marginal relief when income just crosses ₹50 lakh / ₹1 crore.)*

### Q4. Ch: Basic Concepts — Rebate u/s 87A under Both Regimes (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** For AY 2025-26, compute the rebate under Section 87A and the net tax payable in each of the following independent cases of resident individuals. State the regime assumed.

| Case | Total Income (₹) | Regime |
|---|---|---|
| A | 6,90,000 | Default (115BAC) |
| B | 7,10,000 | Default (115BAC) |
| C | 4,90,000 | Old regime (opted out) |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Rebate thresholds.** Under Section 87A, for the **default regime** the rebate is available where total income does not exceed **₹7,00,000**, and equals income-tax or **₹25,000**, whichever is less, with **marginal relief** where income marginally exceeds ₹7 lakh. Under the **old regime** the rebate limit is total income up to **₹5,00,000**, subject to a cap of **₹12,500**.

**Case A — TI ₹6,90,000 (default):**
Tax = 5% × (7,00,000 − 3,00,000) capped to income = [₹20,000 on 3,00,001–7,00,000 slab, but income is ₹6,90,000] → 5% × (6,90,000 − 3,00,000) = ₹19,500. As TI ≤ ₹7,00,000, rebate = ₹19,500 (lower of ₹19,500 and ₹25,000). **Net tax = Nil.**

**Case B — TI ₹7,10,000 (default):**
Tax = ₹20,000 (up to ₹7,00,000) + 10% × ₹10,000 = ₹21,000. TI exceeds ₹7,00,000, so ordinary rebate is not available; **marginal relief** applies: excess of income over ₹7,00,000 = ₹10,000. Tax payable is restricted to this excess of ₹10,000 (since ₹21,000 > ₹10,000). Rebate under marginal relief = ₹21,000 − ₹10,000 = ₹11,000. Tax after rebate = ₹10,000; add cess 4% = ₹400. **Net tax = ₹10,400.**

**Case C — TI ₹4,90,000 (old regime):**
Tax = 5% × (4,90,000 − 2,50,000) = ₹12,000. TI ≤ ₹5,00,000, rebate = lower of ₹12,000 and ₹12,500 = ₹12,000. **Net tax = Nil.**

**Answer:** Case A – Nil; Case B – **₹10,400**; Case C – Nil.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner specifically tests the marginal-relief limb of Section 87A in the new regime (Case B). Forgetting marginal relief and simply denying the whole rebate for TI just above ₹7 lakh is the standard error.)*

### Q5. Ch: Basic Concepts — Definition of "Person" and "Assessee" (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Enumerate the categories of "person" defined under Section 2(31) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, and briefly explain the expanded meaning of "assessee" under Section 2(7).

**Answer:**
Under **Section 2(31)**, "person" includes the following seven categories:
(i) an individual; (ii) a Hindu Undivided Family (HUF); (iii) a company; (iv) a firm; (v) an association of persons (AOP) or a body of individuals (BOI), whether incorporated or not; (vi) a local authority; and (vii) every artificial juridical person not falling within any of the preceding categories.

The Explanation to Section 2(31) clarifies that an AOP, BOI, local authority or artificial juridical person shall be deemed to be a person **whether or not** it was formed or established with the object of deriving income, profits or gains.

Under **Section 2(7)**, "assessee" means a person **by whom any tax or any other sum of money** (e.g., interest, penalty) is payable under the Act, and includes:
(a) every person in respect of whom any **proceeding** has been taken for assessment of his income/loss or refund due;
(b) every person who is a **deemed assessee** (e.g., legal representative, agent of a non-resident); and
(c) every person **deemed to be an assessee in default** (e.g., a person failing to deduct/pay TDS).

**Conclusion:** Section 2(31) lists seven mutually exclusive categories of person, while Section 2(7) extends "assessee" beyond the taxpayer to include representative assessees and assessees-in-default.

*(Full-marks tip: State all seven categories and both the "deemed assessee" and "assessee in default" limbs. Omitting the Explanation (object of income not necessary) or the artificial-juridical-person category costs marks.)*

### Q6. Ch: Basic Concepts — Previous Year for a Newly Set-up Business (Marks: 4) [Application]
**Question:** Mr. Kabir set up a new consultancy business on **1st December 2024** and also, on the same date, acquired a source of income by way of interest on debentures. Determine the previous year for AY 2025-26 for (a) the new business and (b) the interest income, and explain the rule for a newly set-up source under Section 3.

**Answer:**
The general rule under **Section 3** is that the "previous year" is the financial year immediately preceding the assessment year, i.e., 1st April to 31st March. However, the proviso to Section 3 provides that in the case of a business or profession **newly set up**, or a **source of income newly coming into existence** during the financial year, the previous year begins on the **date of setting up** (or the date the source came into existence) and ends on the **31st March** of that financial year.

**(a) New consultancy business:** Set up on 1st December 2024. Its first previous year is **1st December 2024 to 31st March 2025** (a period of 4 months), assessable in AY 2025-26.

**(b) Interest on debentures (new source):** As the source came into existence on 1st December 2024, its previous year is also **1st December 2024 to 31st March 2025**, assessable in AY 2025-26.

**Conclusion:** For both the newly set-up business and the newly-arising source, the first previous year is the **shorter period from 1st December 2024 to 31st March 2025**; from AY 2026-27 onwards the full financial year will be the previous year.

*(Full-marks tip: Note that the first previous year can be **less than 12 months** for a new source but never more; citing the proviso to Section 3 and giving both start (date of setting up) and end (31 March) earns full marks.)*

### Q7. Ch: Residential Status — Individual Returning to India (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Rehan, an Indian citizen, had been employed abroad for several years. He **returned to India permanently on 5th October 2024** and stayed in India thereafter throughout the previous year 2024-25. His stay in India in the earlier years was:

| Previous Year | Days in India |
|---|---|
| 2023-24 | 30 |
| 2022-23 | 42 |
| 2021-22 | 65 |
| 2020-21 | 90 |
| 2019-20 to 2016-17 (each year) | 60 |

His total Indian income for PY 2024-25 (from house property in India) is ₹4,20,000; he has no other Indian-sourced income. Determine his residential status for AY 2025-26 with full reasoning.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Days of stay in the previous year 2024-25.** Returned on 5 October 2024 and stayed till 31 March 2025.
October (5–31) = 27 days; November = 30; December = 31; January = 31; February = 28; March = 31.
Total = 27 + 30 + 31 + 31 + 28 + 31 = **178 days**.

**WN-2: Basic conditions under Section 6(1).**
(a) In India for **182 days or more** in PY 2024-25 → 178 days → **Not satisfied.**
(b) In India for **60 days or more** in PY and **365 days or more** in the 4 preceding previous years → Stay in PY = 178 (≥ 60 satisfied); 4 preceding years (2023-24 to 2020-21) = 30 + 42 + 65 + 90 = **227 days** (< 365) → **Not satisfied.**

**Exception to condition (b):** For an Indian citizen who leaves India as a member of a crew or for employment, and for a citizen/PIO who comes on a visit, the "60 days" is substituted by "182 days". However, this substitution does not help Mr. Rehan meet condition (b); in any event neither basic condition is satisfied.

**WN-3: Deemed residence — Section 6(1A).** This applies to an Indian citizen having total income (other than from foreign sources) exceeding ₹15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any other country. Mr. Rehan's Indian income is only ₹4,20,000 (≤ ₹15 lakh), so Section 6(1A) does **not** apply.

**WN-4: Conclusion on residence.** Since neither basic condition of Section 6(1) is met and Section 6(1A) does not apply, Mr. Rehan is a **Non-Resident (NR)** for AY 2025-26.

**Statement of Residential Status — Mr. Rehan (AY 2025-26)**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Sec 6(1)(a): ≥182 days in PY | No (178) |
| Sec 6(1)(b): ≥60 days in PY & ≥365 in 4 prior years | No (227 days in 4 years) |
| Sec 6(1A): deemed resident | No (income ≤ ₹15 lakh) |
| **Residential status** | **Non-Resident** |

**Answer:** Mr. Rehan is a **Non-Resident** for AY 2025-26. Consequently, only his income accruing/arising or received in India (house-property income of ₹4,20,000) is taxable in India.

*(Full-marks tip: Count the days of the year of return carefully (exclude/include correctly) and always test Section 6(1A) once an NRI's Indian income is in play. A frequent error is applying the 4-preceding-year totals to the wrong years or forgetting condition (b)'s dual requirement.)*

### Q8. Ch: Residential Status — Seafarer / Crew Member (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Mr. Dinesh, an Indian citizen, is a member of the crew of a foreign-bound Indian ship. During PY 2024-25 his eligible period (as per the Continuous Discharge Certificate) outside India was **195 days**. Explain, with reference to Rule 126, how the period of stay in India is determined for a crew member, and determine his residential status assuming that in the four preceding previous years he was in India for 400 days in total and his Indian income does not exceed ₹15 lakh.

**Answer:**
Under **Explanation 1(a) to Section 6(1)**, an Indian citizen who leaves India as a **member of the crew of an Indian ship** is treated as resident only if he is in India for **182 days or more** in the previous year (the 60-day condition in Section 6(1)(b) is not applied to him).

**Rule 126** prescribes how the period of stay in India for a crew member of an **Indian ship** is computed. For an "eligible voyage" (a voyage originating from a port in India and having its destination outside India, or vice versa), the period **beginning on the date entered into the Continuous Discharge Certificate (CDC) in respect of joining the ship and ending on the date entered for signing off** is treated as the period **outside India**, and hence is **excluded** from the period of stay in India.

**Computation:**
- Eligible period outside India (per CDC) = 195 days → excluded.
- Days in India in PY 2024-25 = 365 − 195 = **170 days**.

**Residential status:**
- Section 6(1)(a): 170 days < 182 → **not satisfied**. As Mr. Dinesh left India as a crew member, only the 182-day condition applies (the 60-day/365-day condition is not available against him).
- Section 6(1A): applies only where Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh; here it does not, so it is inapplicable.

Therefore Mr. Dinesh is a **Non-Resident** for AY 2025-26.

**Conclusion:** By virtue of Rule 126, the CDC voyage period of 195 days is excluded, leaving 170 days in India; failing the 182-day test, Mr. Dinesh is a **Non-Resident**.

*(Full-marks tip: The exact point rewarded is that for a crew member of an Indian ship the CDC period is **excluded** and only the 182-day test (not the 60-day test) applies. Applying the 60-day + 365-day condition to a seafarer is the classic mistake.)*

### Q9. Ch: Residential Status — Scope of Total Income / Incidence of Tax Table (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** For the previous year 2024-25, Mr. Sameer has the following income. State, with reasons in a table, whether each item is taxable in India in the hands of a **Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)**, a **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**, and a **Non-Resident (NR)**.

| Item | Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Income from a profession set up in India, received in Germany | 6,00,000 |
| 2 | Rent from a house in London, received in London | 3,00,000 |
| 3 | Interest on savings account with a bank in Mumbai | 40,000 |
| 4 | Profit from a business in Dubai controlled from India | 5,00,000 |
| 5 | Past untaxed foreign income of PY 2019-20 remitted to India in PY 2024-25 | 2,00,000 |

**Answer:**
The scope of total income is governed by **Section 5**. Income received/deemed received in India, or accruing/arising or deemed to accrue/arise in India, is taxable for **all** residential statuses. Foreign-sourced income is taxable fully for a ROR; for a RNOR it is taxable only if it is **derived from a business controlled in / profession set up in India**; and it is **not** taxable for a NR.

**Statement of Taxability — Incidence of Tax (AY 2025-26)**

| Item | Nature | ROR | RNOR | NR | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Profession set up in India, income received abroad | Taxable | Taxable | Taxable | Income deemed to accrue in India (profession in India) — taxable for all |
| 2 | Foreign house rent received abroad | Taxable | Not taxable | Not taxable | Foreign income; not from Indian business/profession |
| 3 | Interest from Mumbai bank | Taxable | Taxable | Taxable | Accrues and received in India |
| 4 | Dubai business controlled from India | Taxable | Taxable | Not taxable | Foreign income but business **controlled from India** → taxable for RNOR too |
| 5 | Remittance of past (2019-20) foreign income | Not taxable | Not taxable | Not taxable | Only a remittance, not receipt of current-year income; already income of an earlier year |

**Conclusion:** Item 5 (mere remittance) is not taxable for anyone; foreign rent (Item 2) is taxable only for a ROR; the Dubai business income (Item 4), being controlled from India, is taxable for both ROR and RNOR but not for a NR.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner tests (i) that a remittance of past income is never taxed again, and (ii) the RNOR carve-out for income from a business controlled in / profession set up in India. Treating the remittance as fresh receipt or denying the RNOR exception on Item 4 loses marks.)*

### Q10. Ch: Residential Status — Residential Status of an HUF and Firm (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain how the residential status of a **Hindu Undivided Family** is determined under Section 6(2), including when its karta is treated as "not ordinarily resident", and briefly state the rule for a **firm/association of persons** under Section 6(2) and (4).

**Answer:**
**HUF — Section 6(2):** A HUF is said to be **resident** in India in any previous year **unless the control and management of its affairs is situated wholly outside India** during that year. Thus, if control and management is wholly outside India, the HUF is a **non-resident**; if it is wholly or **even partly** in India, the HUF is resident.

**Ordinarily resident vs not ordinarily resident — Section 6(6)(b):** Once a HUF is resident, whether it is **ordinarily resident** or **not ordinarily resident** depends on the personal position of its **karta (manager)**. The HUF is **not ordinarily resident** if the karta does not satisfy the two additional conditions, namely: the karta was a **non-resident in India in 9 out of the 10** previous years preceding that year, **or** was in India for **729 days or less** during the 7 preceding previous years.

**Firm / AOP / other persons — Section 6(2) & 6(4):** A firm, AOP or every other person (except an individual and HUF) is **resident** in India in any previous year unless the control and management of its affairs is situated **wholly outside India** during that year. There is no "ordinarily/not ordinarily resident" classification for these persons — they are either resident or non-resident.

**Conclusion:** A HUF, firm or AOP is non-resident only if control and management is **wholly** outside India; the ROR/RNOR sub-classification applies to a HUF through the karta's status but not to a firm or AOP.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite Section 6(2) with the phrase "wholly outside India" and, for the RNOR test of the HUF, apply the **karta's** 9-out-of-10 / 729-days conditions — not the family's. Confusing partial control with wholly-outside control reverses the answer.)*

| Particulars | Amount |

| Dearness allowance forming part of retirement benefits (per month) | ₹10,000 |
| House Rent Allowance (per month) | ₹22,000 |

**Solution:**

Monthly salary = ₹50,000 + ₹10,000 = ₹60,000. Annual salary = ₹60,000 × 12 = **₹7,20,000**.

| (i) Actual HRA received | ₹22,000 × 12 | 2,64,000 |
| (ii) Rent paid − 10% of salary | (₹25,000 × 12) − 10% × ₹7,20,000 = 3,00,000 − 72,000 | 2,28,000 |

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|

| Less: Exempt under Section 10(13A) (least of the three) | 2,28,000 |

*(Full-marks tip: Chennai is a metropolitan city, so the 50% limit applies; DA is included only if it forms part of retirement benefits. ### Q12. Ch: Income under Salaries — Perquisite of Concessional/Interest-free Loan (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Naveen, an employee (not a specified/director employee for this purpose), was given the following loans by his employer during PY 2024-25. Compute the value of the perquisite in respect of the loans under Rule 3(7)(i). Assume the SBI rate as on 1st April 2024 was **10% p.a. for a housing loan** and **12% p.a. for a personal loan**; the employer charged **5% p.a.** on both.

| Loan | Purpose | Amount (₹) | Date of loan | Employer's rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | House construction | 6,00,000 | 1 April 2024 (outstanding throughout) | 5% |
| 2 | Personal (medical treatment of a disease specified in Rule 3A) | 1,20,000 | 1 April 2024 | 5% |
| 3 | Personal (purchase of car) | 40,000 (max outstanding in year) | 1 April 2024 | 5% |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Rule 3(7)(i) — principle.** The value of the perquisite of an interest-free/concessional loan is the interest computed at the **SBI rate as on the first day of the previous year** on the **maximum outstanding monthly balance** (i.e., the aggregate outstanding as on the last day of each month), **less** interest actually charged by the employer.
**Exemptions:** No perquisite arises if (a) the aggregate of original loans does not exceed ₹20,000, or (b) the loan is for medical treatment in respect of diseases specified in **Rule 3A** (to the extent not reimbursed by insurance).

**WN-2: Loan 1 – House construction.** SBI rate 10%; employer 5%; net differential = 5%.
Perquisite = 5% × ₹6,00,000 = **₹30,000**.

**WN-3: Loan 2 – Medical treatment (Rule 3A disease).** Exempt from perquisite valuation. Perquisite = **Nil**.

**WN-4: Loan 3 – Car (personal).** Loan amount ₹40,000 exceeds the ₹20,000 threshold (aggregate of non-exempt petty loans), so it is chargeable. SBI rate for a personal loan = 12%; employer 5%; differential = 7%.
Perquisite = 7% × ₹40,000 = **₹2,800**.

### Q21. Ch: Income from House Property — Deemed Owner & Composite Rent (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the concept of "deemed owner" under Section 27 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Also state briefly how composite rent (rent for building along with charges for amenities) is taxed.

**Answer:**
Under Section 22, income from house property is chargeable in the hands of the **owner** of the property. Section 27 extends the charge to certain persons who, though not the legal owners, are treated as **deemed owners** and are taxed under this head.

The deemed owners under Section 27 are:

| Clause | Deemed owner situation |
|---|---|
| Sec 27(i) | An individual who **transfers** house property to his/her **spouse** (otherwise than for adequate consideration or in connection with an agreement to live apart) or to a **minor child** (not being a married daughter). |
| Sec 27(ii) | The **holder of an impartible estate** is deemed to be the owner of all properties comprised in the estate. |
| Sec 27(iii) | A **member of a co-operative society, company or AOP** to whom a building is allotted under a house-building scheme is the deemed owner of that building. |
| Sec 27(iiia) | A person who acquires property under a **power of attorney** transaction as referred to in Sec 53A of the Transfer of Property Act. |
| Sec 27(iiib) | A person who acquires a right in a building under a **lease for a term of not less than 12 years** (excluding month-to-month or up-to-one-year leases). |

**Composite rent:** Where the owner receives rent both for the building and for **amenities/services** (lift, security, etc.):
- If the two are **separable**, the rent for the building is taxed under "Income from House Property" and the charges for amenities under "Profits and Gains of Business or Profession" or "Income from Other Sources".
- If the two are **inseparable**, the entire composite rent is taxable under **"Profits and Gains of Business or Profession"** or "Income from Other Sources", as the case may be, and not under house property.

**Conclusion:** Section 27 fastens the charge on deemed owners despite the absence of legal title, and composite rent is bifurcated or fully assessed under the other heads depending on its separability.

**Conclusion:** Section 23(5) grants a two-year window of Nil annual value for unsold stock-in-trade flats, after which they are deemed let out; and the ₹2,00,000 self-occupied interest deduction, available in the old regime, is denied under the default regime of Section 115BAC.

Cost Inflation Index: F.Y. 2001-02 = 100; F.Y. 2024-25 = 363.

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Actual cost of acquisition (June 1990) | 2,00,000 |
| FMV on 1-4-2001 | 8,00,000 |
| Stamp duty value on 1-4-2001 | 6,50,000 |
| Sale consideration (10-5-2024) | 75,00,000 |
| Brokerage on sale | 1,50,000 |

Where a capital asset became the property of the assessee before 1st April 2001, the assessee has the option, under section 55(2)(b), to adopt the **higher of (a) actual cost of acquisition, or (b) FMV as on 1-4-2001** as the cost of acquisition. - FMV on 1-4-2001 = ₹8,00,000, but restricted to stamp duty value ₹6,50,000. Hence FMV to be adopted = **₹6,50,000**.

Since the plot was held for more than 24 months, it is a long-term capital asset, and being transferred before 23-7-2024, indexation is available.

Indexed Cost of Acquisition = ₹6,50,000 × (363 ÷ 100) = **₹23,59,500**.

**Computation of Capital Gains — Mr. Aravind (A.Y. 2025-26):**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Full value of consideration | 75,00,000 |
| Less: Expenditure on transfer (brokerage) | (1,50,000) |
| Net sale consideration | 73,50,000 |
| Less: Indexed cost of acquisition (WN-2) | (23,59,500) |
| **Long-Term Capital Gain** | **49,90,500** |

Tax on LTCG @ 20% u/s 112 (transfer before 23-7-2024) = ₹49,90,500 × 20% = **₹9,98,100** (plus cess).

**Answer:** Long-Term Capital Gain = **₹49,90,500**; tax @ 20% = **₹9,98,100** (plus health & education cess @ 4%).

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards correctly capping FMV at the stamp duty value under the Finance Act, 2020 proviso — most students wrongly take ₹8,00,000. State the "higher of cost/FMV, FMV capped at SDV" rule explicitly, and index only the cost, not the brokerage.)*

### Q39. Ch: Capital Gains — Transactions not regarded as transfer u/s 47 (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State, with the relevant clause of section 47, whether the following are "transfers" giving rise to capital gains: (i) distribution of assets on total partition of a Hindu Undivided Family; (ii) transfer of a capital asset under a gift or will; (iii) conversion of debentures of a company into shares of that company; (iv) transfer of a capital asset by a holding company to its 100% Indian subsidiary company.

**Answer:**
Section 45 charges capital gains only where there is a "transfer" of a capital asset. Section 47 lists transactions which are **not regarded as transfer**, so no capital gains arise. The stated transactions are dealt with as follows:

| Transaction | Clause of Sec 47 | Whether a "transfer"? |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution of assets on **total partition** of a HUF | Sec 47(i) | Not a transfer |
| Transfer of a capital asset under a **gift or will** or irrevocable trust | Sec 47(iii) | Not a transfer |
| **Conversion of debentures** into shares of the same company | Sec 47(x) | Not a transfer |
| Transfer by a holding company to its **wholly-owned Indian subsidiary** | Sec 47(iv) | Not a transfer |

(i) Under section 47(i), distribution of capital assets on the **total partition** of a HUF is not regarded as a transfer; hence no capital gain arises in the hands of the HUF.
(ii) Under section 47(iii), transfer of a capital asset under a gift, will or an irrevocable trust is not a transfer (except gift of ESOP shares by an employer). The donor is not chargeable; the cost to the previous owner becomes the donee's cost u/s 49(1).
(iii) Under section 47(x), the conversion of bonds or debentures of a company into shares of that company is not a transfer; the cost and holding period of the debentures are carried into the shares u/s 49(2A).
(iv) Under section 47(iv), transfer of a capital asset by a holding company to its wholly-owned Indian subsidiary (or vice versa u/s 47(v)) is not a transfer, subject to the conditions of section 47A.

**Conclusion:** None of the four transactions is a "transfer"; consequently, no capital gain is chargeable at the time of these transactions.

*(Full-marks tip: Quote the exact clause number for each item and mention the linked cost-carry-over sections (49(1)/49(2A)). Merely writing "not a transfer" without the clause number does not earn full marks.)*

### Q40. Ch: Capital Gains — Capital gain on depreciable assets (block of assets) u/s 50 (Marks: 5) [Problem]

- Gross interest = 10% × ₹6,00,000 = **₹60,000**; TDS = ₹6,000

**WN-3 — 8% Central Government securities:**
Interest on Central Government securities is paid without TDS; the full coupon is taxable.
- Gross interest = 8% × ₹5,00,000 = **₹40,000**; TDS = Nil

### Q45. Ch: Income from Other Sources — Deductions u/s 57 and disallowances u/s 58 (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the deductions allowable u/s 57 and the amounts expressly not deductible u/s 58 while computing "Income from Other Sources".

**Answer:**
Income chargeable under the head "Income from Other Sources" is computed after allowing the deductions specified in section 57 and subject to the disallowances in section 58.

**Deductions allowable — Section 57:**
(i) In the case of dividends and interest on securities, any reasonable sum paid by way of **commission or remuneration to a banker** or other person for realising such income [Sec 57(i)].
(ii) In the case of income from **letting of plant, machinery, furniture or building** (not chargeable under PGBP), the amounts of current repairs, insurance and **depreciation** [Sec 57(ii)/(iii)].
(iii) In the case of **family pension**, a standard deduction of **1/3rd of such pension or ₹15,000**, whichever is less [Sec 57(iia)].
(iv) Any **other expenditure (not capital)** laid out wholly and exclusively for the purpose of earning such income [Sec 57(iii)].
(v) In respect of interest on compensation/enhanced compensation, a deduction of **50%** of such interest [Sec 57(iv)].

**Amounts not deductible — Section 58:**
(i) Any **personal expenses** of the assessee.
(ii) Any **interest or salary payable outside India** on which tax has not been deducted at source.
(iii) Any expenditure of the nature described in **section 40A** (e.g., excessive payments, cash payments beyond limits).
(iv) In respect of **winnings** from lotteries, crossword puzzles, races, card games, gambling or online games — **no expenditure or allowance** is deductible.

**Conclusion:** Deductions u/s 57 are permitted only if incurred to earn the income concerned, whereas section 58 expressly bars personal expenses, TDS-defaulting foreign payments, and any expense against casual winnings.

*(Full-marks tip: Pair each deduction with its exact sub-clause and mention the ₹15,000/one-third family pension deduction and the 50% deduction on interest on enhanced compensation — these fetch marks. Always add the bar on expenses against winnings u/s 58(4).)*

### Q48. Ch: Clubbing & Set-off — Transfer to a trust & revocable transfers (Sec 64(1)(viii), 61-63) (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Examine the clubbing implications in the following independent situations for A.Y. 2025-26:
(i) Mr. Kunal transfers ₹5,00,000 to a trust with a direction that the income be accumulated for the benefit of his son's wife. During the year the trust earned income of ₹45,000 on this amount.

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Situation (i): Transfer to a person/AOP for benefit of son's wife [Sec 64(1)(viii)]:**
Where an individual transfers assets, otherwise than for adequate consideration, to any person or association of persons for the **immediate or deferred benefit of his son's wife**, the income from such assets, to the extent it is for her benefit, is included in the transferor's income. The direction to **accumulate** the income for the son's wife's deferred benefit is squarely covered.

A transfer is "revocable" if it contains any provision for re-transfer of the asset/income to the transferor, or gives him a right to re-assume power over the asset/income. Since Mr. Kunal retains the right to re-take the property, it is a **revocable transfer**, and under section 61 all income arising from the property is taxable in the hands of the **transferor (Mr. Kunal)**, notwithstanding the transfer.

- Therefore, the rental income of **₹2,40,000 is taxable in Mr. Kunal's hands** (as income from house property, after the standard deduction u/s 24).

|---|---|---|
| (i) Trust for son's wife | Sec 64(1)(viii) | ₹45,000 |
| (ii) Revocable transfer | Sec 61 r/w 63 | Rental income ₹2,40,000 (gross) |

*(Full-marks tip: For (i) cite 64(1)(viii) and note "immediate or deferred benefit"; for (ii) explain why the 6-year/lifetime exception u/s 62 is NOT met (revocable within 5 years). Simply saying "clubbed" without the section fails to earn full marks.)*

### Q51. Ch: Clubbing & Set-off — Carry forward and set-off of losses (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the provisions regarding the number of years for which the following losses can be carried forward and the incomes against which they can be set off in subsequent years: (i) house property loss; (ii) non-speculative business loss; (iii) speculation loss; (iv) loss under "Capital Gains"; and (v) loss from owning and maintaining race horses.

**Answer:**
The set-off in subsequent years of losses carried forward is governed by sections 71B, 72, 73, 74 and 74A. The position is summarised below:

| Loss | Section | Carry-forward period | Set off in later years only against |
|---|---|---|---|
| House property loss | 71B | 8 assessment years | Income from house property |
| Non-speculative business loss | 72 | 8 assessment years | Profits of any business or profession |
| Speculation business loss | 73 | 4 assessment years | Profits of a speculation business only |
| Long-term capital loss | 74 | 8 assessment years | Long-term capital gains only |
| Short-term capital loss | 74 | 8 assessment years | Any capital gain (STCG or LTCG) |
| Loss from owning & maintaining race horses | 74A | 4 assessment years | Income from owning & maintaining race horses only |

(i) **House property loss** [Sec 71B]: After set-off in the current year (limited to ₹2,00,000 against other heads u/s 71(3A)), the balance is carried forward for **8 assessment years** and set off only against income from house property.
(ii) **Non-speculative business loss** [Sec 72]: Carried forward for **8 assessment years** and set off against profits of **any** business or profession (continuity of business is not required); return must be filed within the due date u/s 139(1).
(iii) **Speculation loss** [Sec 73]: Carried forward for **4 assessment years** and set off **only against speculation profits**.
(iv) **Capital loss** [Sec 74]: Carried forward for **8 assessment years** — a long-term capital loss is set off only against LTCG, whereas a short-term capital loss can be set off against any capital gain.
(v) **Loss from owning & maintaining race horses** [Sec 74A]: Carried forward for **4 assessment years** and set off only against income from the same activity.

**Conclusion:** Losses under sections 71B, 72 and 74 are carried forward for 8 years, whereas speculation loss (Sec 73) and race-horse loss (Sec 74A) are carried forward for only 4 years and against their own respective incomes.

*(Full-marks tip: A neatly-drawn table with the exact section, period and set-off head is the fastest route to full marks. Distinguish LTCL (only vs LTCG) from STCL (vs any capital gain), and remember the timely-return condition u/s 139(1)/(3) for carry-forward of business and capital losses.)*

### Q57. Ch: Advance Tax / TDS / TCS — Section 194-IA TDS on transfer of immovable property (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Mr. Sameer (a salaried individual, not carrying on any business) purchased a residential flat from Mr. Bose (resident) during P.Y. 2024-25. Examine whether Mr. Sameer is required to deduct tax at source under section 194-IA, and if so, compute the amount of TDS and state the compliance requirements.

**Answer:**
Section 194-IA requires **any person** (including a salaried individual — there is no exclusion of non-business buyers, unlike section 194-I) responsible for paying to a **resident transferor** any sum by way of consideration for transfer of any **immovable property (other than agricultural land)** to deduct tax at source.

Here, though consideration is ₹48,00,000 (below ₹50 lakh), the **stamp duty value ₹55,00,000 exceeds ₹50 lakh**, so the section is **attracted**, and TDS is computed on ₹55,00,000.

**Rate:** 1% (no surcharge/cess on TDS for resident payee).

**Computation of TDS:** 1% × ₹55,00,000 = **₹55,000**.

**Timing on instalments:** Where consideration is paid in instalments, TDS is deducted on **each instalment** proportionately such that the total on the higher of consideration/SDV is collected. On each instalment, deduction is at 1% of the proportionate amount referable to ₹55,00,000.

**Compliance:**

| Requirement | Provision |
|---|---|
| No TAN required; deduct using PAN | Section 194-IA |
| Deposit TDS + file challan-cum-statement in **Form 26QB** | Rule 30/31A — within **30 days** from end of the month of deduction |
| Issue TDS certificate in **Form 16B** to seller | Within 15 days of due date of Form 26QB |
| If seller's PAN not furnished | TDS @ **20%** (section 206AA) |

**Conclusion:** Mr. Sameer must deduct TDS of **₹55,000** (1% of the higher figure ₹55,00,000), deposit it in Form 26QB, and issue Form 16B — no TAN is needed.

*(Full-marks tip: The scoring point is that the threshold and the base are triggered by the HIGHER of consideration and stamp duty value — a candidate who says "no TDS as consideration is below ₹50 lakh" loses most marks. Also mention no TAN requirement and Form 26QB.)*

### Q62. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Return by a firm and audit cases; due dates (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the **due dates** for furnishing the return of income under section 139(1) for A.Y. 2025-26 for the following, and explain the obligation of a **partnership firm** and its **partners** to file returns: (i) a firm whose accounts are required to be audited; (ii) a working partner of such a firm; (iii) a firm not liable to audit; (iv) a firm that has entered into an international transaction requiring a report u/s 92E.

**Answer:**
Section 139(1) obliges every **firm** (and a company) to furnish a return of its income **whether or not it has income/profit** — i.e., a firm must file a return **compulsorily**, irrespective of whether its total income exceeds the basic exemption limit (the basic exemption limit concept applies to individuals/HUFs, not to a firm).

**Due dates under Explanation 2 to section 139(1) for A.Y. 2025-26:**

| Assessee | Due date |
|---|---|
| (i) Firm whose accounts are required to be **audited** (e.g., under section 44AB) | **31st October 2025** |
| (ii) **Working partner** of a firm whose accounts are required to be audited (and where the partner's remuneration/interest is to be reflected) | **31st October 2025** |
| (iii) Firm **not** liable to audit | **31st July 2025** |
| (iv) Firm required to furnish a **report u/s 92E** (international/specified domestic transaction) | **30th November 2025** |

**Explanatory points:**
1. **Compulsory filing (section 139(1)):** A firm must file its return even if it incurs a loss; further, a firm claiming carry-forward of business loss/loss under capital gains must file the return **within the due date** (section 139(3) read with section 80).
2. **Partner's due date (aligned with firm's audit):** Where a firm is subject to audit, a **partner** of that firm gets the extended due date of 31st October 2025, so that the partner can incorporate the correctly audited share of profit, interest and remuneration.
3. **Tax audit (section 44AB):** A firm carrying on business with turnover exceeding the prescribed threshold (₹1 crore, extended to ₹10 crore where cash receipts/payments ≤ 5%), or a profession with gross receipts exceeding ₹50 lakh, must get its accounts audited; such firms have the 31st October due date.
4. **Signing of return (section 140):** The return of a firm is verified by the **managing partner**, or where there is no managing partner or he is unable, by **any partner** (not being a minor).

**Conclusion:** A firm must file its return compulsorily; the due date is **31.10.2025** if its accounts are audited (and for its partners), **31.07.2025** if not audited, and **30.11.2025** if a section 92E report is required.

*(Full-marks tip: State the "compulsory filing regardless of income" rule for firms, the partner's alignment to the firm's audit due date, and all three due dates (31 Jul / 31 Oct / 30 Nov). Mentioning section 140 (verification by managing partner) fetches the last mark.)*

### Q63. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Belated, revised & updated returns; defective return (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish, with statutory time limits for A.Y. 2025-26, between a **belated return (section 139(4))**, a **revised return (section 139(5))** and an **updated return (section 139(8A))**. Also state briefly what constitutes a **defective return** under section 139(9) and the consequence of not rectifying it.

**Answer:**
The three species of returns and their governing provisions are compared below (A.Y. 2025-26):

| Basis | Belated return — s.139(4) | Revised return — s.139(5) | Updated return — s.139(8A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| When filed | Return not filed within s.139(1) due date | To correct any omission/wrong statement in a return already filed u/s 139(1)/(4) | To furnish/update return, generally to disclose **additional income** on payment of additional tax |
| Time limit | Up to **31.12.2025** (3 months before end of A.Y.) or completion of assessment, whichever earlier | Same as belated — up to **31.12.2025** or completion of assessment, whichever earlier | Within **24 months** from the end of the relevant A.Y. (i.e., up to **31.03.2028** for A.Y. 2025-26) |
| Can it reduce income/create/increase loss or refund? | — | Can correct in either direction | **No** — cannot be filed to reduce income, claim/enhance loss, or claim/increase refund |
| Additional tax | — | — | 25% of aggregate tax+interest if filed within 12 months; **50%** if filed after 12 months but within 24 months |
| Loss carry-forward | Business/capital loss **cannot** be carried forward (s.80) | Preserves original position | Not applicable (only additional income) |

**Defective return — section 139(9):**
A return is treated as **defective** if it is not accompanied by the prescribed particulars/annexures or the statutory conditions (e.g., self-assessment tax not paid, statements of accounts not furnished where required, etc.) are not satisfied. The **Assessing Officer** intimates the defect and gives the assessee an opportunity to rectify it within **15 days** (extendable). 

**Consequence of non-rectification:** If the defect is not rectified within the allowed time, the return is treated as an **invalid return** — as if the assessee had **never furnished** a return, attracting consequences of non-filing (interest under 234A, loss of carry-forward, penalty/prosecution risk, best-judgment assessment).

**Conclusion:** A belated/revised return may be filed up to **31.12.2025**; an updated return up to **31.03.2028** (with additional tax and only for additional income); an unrectified defective return is treated as invalid.

*(Full-marks tip: A comparison table with the exact time limits (31.12.2025 vs 24 months) is what the examiner wants. The distinguishing feature of s.139(8A) — cannot be used to reduce income or claim a refund — and the "invalid return" consequence of an unrectified s.139(9) defect are the high-value points.)*

*(Full-marks tip: Two things earn the marks — (i) rounding total income to the nearest ₹10 under 288A BEFORE computing tax, and the net tax under 288B AFTER, and (ii) treating TCS on the car purchase as an eligible credit alongside TDS. A negative balance is shown as a "refund", not "payable".)*

### Q69. Ch: GST – Supply — Inter-State vs Intra-State & Place of Supply Basics (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Determine, with reasons, whether each of the following independent transactions is an **inter-State** supply or an **intra-State** supply, and state the tax(es) leviable (CGST + SGST or IGST). Give the statutory basis (Sections 7 and 8 of the IGST Act, 2017) in each case.

| Case | Facts |
|------|-------|
| (i) | Mr. A of Jaipur (Rajasthan) sells goods to Mr. B of Udaipur (Rajasthan); goods delivered within Rajasthan. |
| (ii) | Ms. C, a supplier registered in Delhi, provides interior-design service in respect of an immovable property (a hotel) located in Shimla (Himachal Pradesh). |
| (iii) | Mr. D of Chennai (Tamil Nadu) supplies goods to a unit located in a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) situated in Sriperumbudur (Tamil Nadu). |
| (iv) | Mr. E of Mumbai carries goods for supply to Mr. F, and the supply is made to a tourist from Nepal at the Mumbai airport (goods to be taken out of India). |

**Answer:**
The **nature of a supply** (inter-State or intra-State) is decided by **comparing the location of the supplier with the place of supply**, as laid down in **Sections 7 and 8 of the IGST Act, 2017**.

Under **Section 8(1)/8(2)**, where the location of the supplier and the place of supply are in the **same State/Union Territory**, the supply is **intra-State** and attracts **CGST + SGST**. Under **Section 7(1)/7(3)**, where they are in **two different States/Union Territories**, the supply is **inter-State** and attracts **IGST**. By virtue of the **provisos to Section 8(1) and 8(2)**, a supply to or by an **SEZ developer/unit** is **always treated as inter-State**, irrespective of location.

| Case | Location of supplier | Place of supply | Nature | Tax leviable | Basis |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|--------|--------------|-------|
| (i) | Rajasthan | Rajasthan (Sec 10(1)(a) – movement of goods, place of supply is where movement terminates for delivery) | Intra-State | CGST + SGST | Sec 8(1) |
| (ii) | Delhi | Himachal Pradesh (Sec 12(3)(a) – service in relation to immovable property → location of property) | Inter-State | IGST | Sec 7(3) |
| (iii) | Tamil Nadu | Tamil Nadu (SEZ unit) | **Inter-State (deemed)** | IGST | Proviso to Sec 8(1) |
| (iv) | Maharashtra | Place outside India / export | Inter-State (export is inter-State supply) | IGST (zero-rated) | Sec 7(5)(a) |

**Conclusion:** (i) Intra-State – CGST + SGST; (ii) Inter-State – IGST; (iii) Deemed inter-State – IGST; (iv) Inter-State (export, zero-rated) – IGST.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards clearly stating that "location of supplier vs place of supply" is the test, and correctly invoking the SEZ **proviso** for case (iii) even though both parties are in the same State — a very common trap. Do not forget that export/import are inter-State by force of Sec 7(5).)*

### Q70. Ch: GST – Supply — Composite Supply: Works Contract (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the meaning of **"works contract"** and **"composite supply"** under the CGST Act, 2017. A contractor constructs a commercial building for a client under a single contract that includes supply of cement, steel, labour and design. How is such a works contract taxed? State the rate treatment and whether it is a supply of goods or services.

**Answer:**
As per **Section 2(119) of the CGST Act, 2017**, a **"works contract"** means a contract for **building, construction, fabrication, completion, erection, installation, fitting out, improvement, modification, repair, maintenance, renovation, alteration or commissioning of any immovable property** wherein **transfer of property in goods (whether as goods or in some other form) is involved** in the execution of such contract.

As per **Section 2(30)**, a **"composite supply"** means a supply comprising **two or more taxable supplies of goods or services, or any combination thereof, which are naturally bundled and supplied in conjunction with each other in the ordinary course of business**, one of which is a **principal supply**.

A works contract is inherently a composite supply of goods (cement, steel) and services (labour, design). To end the pre-GST controversy of splitting such contracts, the Act provides a clear rule:

Under **Para 6(a) of Schedule II to the CGST Act, 2017**, a **works contract as defined in Section 2(119) is treated as a supply of SERVICES**. Consequently, the entire consideration — including the value of goods incorporated — is taxed as a **single supply of service**; no artificial bifurcation into "goods portion" and "service portion" is permitted.

| Aspect | Treatment |
|--------|-----------|
| Classification | Supply of **service** (Schedule II, Para 6(a)) |
| Valuation | Single value = total contract consideration (Sec 15) |
| Splitting of goods/service | **Not permitted** — taxed as one service |
| Rate | The applicable rate notified for works-contract service (generally 18%, with concessional rates for specified government/affordable-housing works) |

**Conclusion:** The works contract for the commercial building is a **composite supply deemed to be a supply of service** under Para 6(a) of Schedule II, taxed as a whole at the works-contract service rate; the goods embedded in it are **not** taxed separately.

*(Full-marks tip: State BOTH the Sec 2(119) definition AND the Schedule II Para 6(a) deeming fiction. The common deduction is answering "composite supply taxed at principal supply's rate" without citing that the statute already deems the WHOLE thing a service, making the principal-supply analysis unnecessary here.)*

### Q71. Ch: GST – Supply — Bundled Supply: Hotel with Food (Composite vs Mixed) (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** For each situation, decide whether it is a **composite supply** or a **mixed supply**, identify the **principal supply** (if composite), and state which rate governs the whole supply.

| Situation | Facts |
|-----------|-------|
| (i) | A hotel supplies accommodation for a night at ₹9,000, which **includes complimentary breakfast** in the room tariff. Accommodation @ 18%; restaurant/food @ 5%. |
| (ii) | A hotel offers a "Festive Combo" for a single price of ₹5,000 comprising a **dinner buffet, a bottle of imported wine and a spa voucher**, each of which can be sold separately. Food @ 5%, wine @ 28%, spa @ 18%. |
| (iii) | A tour operator sells a package of **rail tickets + hotel stay + sightseeing** for a single lump-sum price. |

**Answer:**
The tests flow from **Sections 2(30) and 2(74) of the CGST Act, 2017**, read with the rate rule in **Section 8**.

A **composite supply** [Sec 2(30)] consists of **naturally bundled** supplies with a **principal supply**; per **Section 8(a)**, it is taxed **at the rate of the principal supply**. A **mixed supply** [Sec 2(74)] consists of **two or more individual supplies for a single price** that are **NOT naturally bundled**; per **Section 8(b)**, it is taxed at the **rate of that particular supply which attracts the highest rate of tax**.

| Situation | Type | Principal supply | Governing rate | Reasoning |
|-----------|------|------------------|----------------|-----------|
| (i) Room + complimentary breakfast | **Composite** [Sec 2(30)] | Hotel accommodation | **18%** (rate of accommodation) [Sec 8(a)] | Breakfast is naturally bundled with, and ancillary to, staying in the hotel. |
| (ii) Dinner + wine + spa combo | **Mixed** [Sec 2(74)] | Not applicable | **28%** (highest — wine) [Sec 8(b)] | Items are independently supplied, sold for a single price, not naturally bundled. |
| (iii) Rail + hotel + sightseeing package | **Composite** (tour package) | The tour service | Rate of the tour-operator service | Naturally bundled as a single tour package. |

**Conclusion:** (i) Composite — taxed at 18%; (ii) Mixed — taxed at the highest rate, i.e., 28%; (iii) Composite — taxed at the tour-operator service rate.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner looks for the "naturally bundled in the ordinary course of business" phrase to justify composite, and "single price + not naturally bundled → highest rate" for mixed. A frequent error is treating the festive combo as composite; because each item is independently marketable and there is no dominant principal supply, it is a mixed supply taxed at the highest (28%) rate.)*

### Q72. Ch: GST – Supply — Schedule I: Supply Without Consideration (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** State, with reference to **Schedule I of the CGST Act, 2017**, whether GST is attracted on the following activities made **without consideration**:

| Case | Facts |
|------|-------|
| (i) | A company permanently transfers business assets (on which ITC had been availed) to its director free of cost on his retirement. |
| (ii) | Head office in Karnataka provides accounting support (own employees) to its branch in Kerala; both are separately registered. |
| (iii) | An employer gifts a gold coin worth ₹80,000 to an employee on completion of 25 years. |
| (iv) | A principal supplies goods to his agent who undertakes to supply such goods on behalf of the principal. |

**Answer:**
Ordinarily, "supply" under **Section 7(1)(a)** requires **consideration**. However, **Section 7(1)(c) read with Schedule I** deems **four categories of activities to be supply even without consideration**.

| Case | Taxable? | Basis |
|------|----------|-------|
| (i) Permanent transfer of business assets with ITC availed | **Yes – supply** | Schedule I, Para 1 – permanent transfer/disposal of business assets where ITC has been availed is a supply even without consideration. |
| (ii) HO to branch (distinct persons) support service | **Yes – supply** | Schedule I, Para 2 – supply between **distinct/related persons** in the course of business is a supply even without consideration (separately-registered units are distinct persons under Sec 25(4)). |
| (iii) Gift to employee of ₹80,000 | **Yes – taxable to the extent above ₹50,000** | Proviso to Schedule I, Para 2 – gifts by an employer to an employee **not exceeding ₹50,000** in a financial year are not a supply; here value exceeds ₹50,000, so the **whole ₹80,000** is a supply. |
| (iv) Principal to agent | **Yes – supply** | Schedule I, Para 3 – supply of goods by a principal to his agent (who supplies on the principal's behalf) is a supply even without consideration. |

**Conclusion:** All four activities are supplies; in case (iii), because the gift crosses the ₹50,000 annual threshold, GST applies to the entire ₹80,000.

*(Full-marks tip: For gifts, the examiner rewards the nuance that once ₹50,000 is crossed the ENTIRE value is taxed (the ₹50,000 is a threshold, not a standard deduction). For HO–branch, cite Sec 25(4)/25(5) "distinct persons" — this is the point most students miss.)*

**Question:** For each service below, state whether tax is payable under **forward charge** or **reverse charge (Section 9(3) of the CGST Act / Section 5(3) of the IGST Act read with Notification No. 13/2017-CT(R))**, and identify the **person liable to pay GST**.

| (i) | ABC Security Services Ltd. (a body corporate) provides security (man-power supply) service to XYZ Ltd., a registered company. |

### Q74. Ch: GST – Charge & RCM — Composition Levy Interplay with RCM (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Mr. Kishore, a registered trader in Nagpur (Maharashtra), has opted for the **composition scheme under Section 10** with an aggregate turnover of ₹90 lakh (all intra-State). During the quarter he:
- Made taxable sales of goods of ₹22,00,000;
- Received **legal consultancy** from an advocate for ₹40,000 (a service liable to RCM);
- Received **goods-transport-agency (GTA)** freight service of ₹15,000 (liable to RCM).

Compute the GST payable by Mr. Kishore for the quarter. (Composition rate for a trader = 0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST; RCM services attract 18%; GTA RCM @ 5%.)

**Solution:**

Tax = ₹22,00,000 × 1% = **₹22,000** (CGST ₹11,000 + SGST ₹11,000).

**WN-2 – RCM liability (Sec 10(5)/proviso to Sec 10):** A composition dealer is **not exempt from RCM**; on inward supplies liable to reverse charge he must pay tax at the **normal rate** (not the composition rate), and **no ITC** of such tax is available.
- Legal service (advocate) @ 18% → ₹40,000 × 18% = ₹7,200
- GTA freight @ 5% → ₹15,000 × 5% = ₹750
- Total RCM tax = **₹7,950** (CGST ₹3,975 + SGST ₹3,975)

**Statement of GST Payable for the Quarter**

| Particulars | Value (₹) | Rate | GST (₹) | Mechanism |
|-------------|-----------|------|---------|-----------|
| Outward supplies of goods | 22,00,000 | 1% | 22,000 | Composition (Sec 10) |
| Legal consultancy (inward) | 40,000 | 18% | 7,200 | RCM (normal rate) |
| GTA freight (inward) | 15,000 | 5% | 750 | RCM (normal rate) |
| **Total GST payable** | | | **29,950** | Wholly in cash |

**Answer:** Total GST payable = **₹29,950** (composition ₹22,000 + RCM ₹7,950), payable entirely in **cash**; ITC of the RCM tax is **not** available to a composition dealer.

*(Full-marks tip: The heart of the question is that RCM tax is charged at the NORMAL rate (18%/5%), NOT the 1% composition rate, and the composition dealer gets NO ITC of it. Students who apply 1% to the RCM services or claim ITC lose the marks. Also state that the whole amount is paid in cash — a composition dealer maintains no electronic credit ledger.)*

|------|-------|
| (i) | Mr. P supplies goods intra-State in Maharashtra; aggregate turnover ₹28 lakh (goods only). |

### Q80. Ch: GST – Time & Value of Supply — Time of Supply under Reverse Charge (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Determine the **time of supply** in each case, citing **Section 12(3) / Section 13(3) of the CGST Act, 2017** (reverse-charge situations).

| Case | Nature | Date of receipt of goods/completion | Date of payment (entry in books / debit in bank) | Date of invoice by supplier |
|------|--------|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| (i) | Goods under RCM | 10th Sept | 25th Sept | 5th Sept |
| (ii) | Goods under RCM | 20th Oct | 15th Nov | 2nd Oct |
| (iii) | Services under RCM (import of service from associated enterprise) | Service completed 12th Aug | Payment 5th Nov; entry in books of account 20th Sept | 1st Aug |
| (iv) | Services under RCM (domestic) | — | Payment 10th Dec | 30th Sept (payment not made within 60 days of invoice) |

**Answer:**
For supplies taxed under reverse charge, the time of supply is fixed by **Section 12(3)** (goods) and **Section 13(3)** (services).

**Section 12(3) – goods under RCM:** Time of supply is the **earliest** of —
(a) date of **receipt of goods**; (b) date of **payment** (as entered in books or debited in bank, whichever earlier); (c) the date **immediately following 30 days** from the date of the supplier's invoice.

**Section 13(3) – services under RCM:** Time of supply is the **earlier** of —
(a) date of **payment**; (b) the date **immediately following 60 days** from the date of the supplier's invoice.
For **import of service from an associated enterprise**, time of supply is the **earlier** of the **date of entry in the books of account of the recipient** or the **date of payment**.

| Case | Applicable clause | Time of supply |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| (i) Goods | Earliest of 10 Sept (receipt), 25 Sept (payment), 6 Oct (30 days + 1 from 5 Sept invoice) | **10th September** |
| (ii) Goods | Earliest of 20 Oct (receipt), 15 Nov (payment), 2 Nov (30 days + 1 from 2 Oct invoice) | **20th October** |
| (iii) Import of service from associated enterprise | Earlier of 20 Sept (entry in books) or 5 Nov (payment) | **20th September** |
| (iv) Domestic service | Earlier of 10 Dec (payment) or 61st day from 30 Sept invoice = 30 Nov | **30th November** |

**Conclusion:** Time of supply — (i) 10 Sept, (ii) 20 Oct, (iii) 20 Sept, (iv) 30 Nov.

*(Full-marks tip: Two traps: (a) for goods it is 30 days + 1, for services it is 60 days + 1 — mixing them costs the mark; (b) for import of service from an ASSOCIATED ENTERPRISE, the special rule (entry in books OR payment) overrides the 60-day rule — case (iii) tests exactly this.)*

### Q81. Ch: GST – Time & Value of Supply — Time of Supply: Continuous Supply & Vouchers (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Determine the **time of supply** and give the statutory basis:

| Case | Facts |
|------|-------|
| (i) | **Continuous supply of goods** where successive statements of accounts / payments are involved; the contract provides for issue of a monthly statement on the last day of each month. |
| (ii) | **Continuous supply of services** (an AMC): due date of payment is ascertainable from the contract as the 7th of each month; May payment received on 3rd May, invoice raised 6th May. |
| (iii) | A supplier issues a **gift voucher** on 1st April for a specific identified TV set; the voucher is redeemed on 20th June. |
| (iv) | A supplier issues a **general-purpose gift card** (usable for any merchandise) on 1st April; redeemed on 20th June. |

**Answer:**
The rules are drawn from **Sections 12 and 13 (continuous supply)** and **Section 12(4)/13(4) (vouchers)** of the CGST Act, 2017.

**Continuous supply of goods [Sec 31(4)/12(2)]:** Where successive statements/payments are involved, the invoice is issued on or before each statement/payment date, and the normal time-of-supply rule (earlier of invoice date or payment) applies with reference to those dates.

**Continuous supply of services [Sec 13 r/w Sec 31(5)]:** Where the **due date of payment is ascertainable** from the contract, the invoice must be issued **on or before that due date**; time of supply is the earlier of invoice/payment.

**Vouchers [Sec 12(4)/13(4)]:** If the supply is **identifiable at the time of issue** of the voucher, time of supply is the **date of issue**; if **not identifiable**, it is the **date of redemption**.

| Case | Time of supply | Basis |
|------|----------------|-------|
| (i) Continuous supply of goods (monthly statement) | Date of issue of **each monthly statement** (or payment, if earlier) | Sec 31(4) r/w Sec 12(2) |
| (ii) AMC – due date 7th, payment 3rd May, invoice 6th May | **3rd May** (earlier of payment 3 May and invoice 6 May) | Sec 13(2) r/w Sec 31(5) |
| (iii) Voucher for identified TV | **1st April** (date of issue — supply identifiable) | Sec 12(4)(a) |
| (iv) General-purpose gift card | **20th June** (date of redemption — supply not identifiable) | Sec 12(4)(b) |

**Conclusion:** (i) each statement date; (ii) 3rd May; (iii) 1st April; (iv) 20th June.

*(Full-marks tip: The voucher rule is the classic examiner favourite — "identifiable at issue → date of issue; not identifiable → date of redemption." For continuous supply of services, the key is the "due date of payment ascertainable from the contract" trigger governing the invoicing deadline.)*

### Q83. Ch: GST – Time & Value of Supply — Valuation: Related Persons & Rule 27 (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Answer with reasons:
(a) State the four situations covered by **Section 15(1)** for transaction value to be accepted, and when valuation must instead be done under the **Valuation Rules (Rules 27–31)**.
(b) M/s Sun Ltd. supplies goods to its **wholly-owned subsidiary** (a related person) at ₹1,80,000. The **open market value** of identical goods is ₹2,20,000. The subsidiary is eligible for **full input tax credit** and will further supply the goods. Determine the value of supply, citing the relevant rule.
(c) Where consideration is **not wholly in money** (part exchange), state the rule and hierarchy of valuation.

**Answer:**
**(a)** Under **Section 15(1)**, the transaction value (the **price actually paid or payable**) is accepted **provided (i) the supplier and recipient are not related, and (ii) the price is the sole consideration** for the supply. Where **either condition fails** — i.e., the parties are **related persons**, or price is not the sole consideration — the value must be determined under the **CGST Valuation Rules (Rules 27 to 31)** as mandated by **Section 15(4)**.

**(b) Supply to related person — Rule 28:** Where the supply is between **related/distinct persons** (other than through an agent), the value is —
(i) the **open market value (OMV)**; failing which
(ii) the value of like kind and quality; failing which
(iii) value under Rule 30/31.

**However, the second proviso to Rule 28** provides that **where the recipient is eligible for FULL input tax credit, the value declared in the invoice shall be deemed to be the open market value.** Here the subsidiary is eligible for full ITC.

Therefore, the value of supply = **₹1,80,000** (invoice value is deemed to be OMV), **not** ₹2,20,000.

**(c) Consideration not wholly in money — Rule 27:** The value is the **open market value** of the supply; if OMV is not available, the **sum of the money consideration plus the money equivalent of the non-money consideration** (if such amount is known at the time of supply); failing that, the value of like kind and quality; failing that, Rule 30/31.

**Conclusion:** (b) Value = **₹1,80,000** by virtue of the full-ITC proviso to Rule 28; (c) valuation follows the Rule 27 hierarchy beginning with open market value.

*(Full-marks tip: The proviso to Rule 28 (full-ITC recipient → invoice value = OMV) is the single most-tested valuation nuance. A student who blindly takes OMV ₹2,20,000 for a related-party supply, ignoring the full-ITC proviso, loses the marks. State the Rule 27/28/30/31 hierarchy explicitly.)*

### Q84. Ch: GST – Time & Value of Supply — Change in Rate of Tax & Value under Rule 32 (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:**
(a) Determine the **time of supply** under **Section 14 (change in rate of tax)** where the **rate changed from 12% to 18% w.e.f. 1st October**:

| Case | Supply of service completed | Invoice issued | Payment received |
|------|-----------------------------|----------------|------------------|
| (i) | Before 1st Oct | After 1st Oct | After 1st Oct |
| (ii) | After 1st Oct | Before 1st Oct | After 1st Oct |
| (iii) | Before 1st Oct | Before 1st Oct | After 1st Oct |

(b) A **money-changer** converts US$ 10,000 into Indian rupees. RBI reference rate for US$ is ₹83. Compute the **value of taxable supply** under **Rule 32(2)(a)** of the CGST Rules.

**Answer:**
**(a) Section 14 – change in rate of tax:** When two of the three events (**supply, invoice, payment**) occur on the **same side** of the rate change, the time of supply follows those two; the rate applicable is fixed accordingly. The rule: identify which **two of the three** events lie on the same side of 1st October.

| Case | Two events on same side | Time of supply | Rate applicable |
|------|-------------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| (i) Supply before; invoice & payment after | Invoice & payment (after) | Earlier of invoice/payment = **after 1 Oct** | **18% (new rate)** |
| (ii) Supply after; invoice before; payment after | Supply & payment (after) | Earlier of the two after-events = **after 1 Oct** | **18% (new rate)** |
| (iii) Supply before; invoice before; payment after | Supply & invoice (before) | Earlier of invoice/supply = **before 1 Oct** | **12% (old rate)** |

**(b) Rule 32(2)(a) – value of supply of foreign-currency exchange:** Where the RBI reference rate is available, value = **difference between the buying/selling rate and the RBI reference rate × total units of currency**.

Value = (RBI reference rate) × 1% of the gross amount? No — under Rule 32(2)(a), where the currency is exchanged **against Indian rupees and the RBI reference rate is available**, the value of supply = **1% of the gross amount of Indian rupees provided/received**, computed as the difference from RBI rate; specifically:

Value = |Transaction rate − RBI reference rate| × total units.
Where the RBI reference rate is available and used as the basis, value = **(RBI reference rate) applied to units** to get INR = ₹83 × 10,000 = ₹8,30,000; the value of supply = **1% of ₹8,30,000 = ₹8,300**.

**Answer:** (a) (i) 18%, (ii) 18%, (iii) 12%; (b) value of taxable supply of the money-changing service = **₹8,300**, GST charged on ₹8,300.

*(Full-marks tip: For Sec 14, always find the "two of three on the same side" — the rate follows those two events. For money-changing under Rule 32(2)(a), the taxable value is 1% of the gross INR amount when the RBI reference rate is available; students often wrongly charge GST on the whole ₹8,30,000 exchanged instead of on the ₹8,300 margin value.)*

### Q85. Ch: GST – Input Tax Credit — Conditions for Availing ITC (Sec 16) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** "Mere possession of a tax invoice is not enough to take input tax credit." In the light of section 16 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with the relevant rules, enumerate the conditions that a registered person must cumulatively satisfy before he becomes entitled to avail input tax credit on inward supplies of goods.

**Answer:**
The entitlement to input tax credit (ITC) flows from section 16(1) of the CGST Act, 2017, which allows a registered person to take credit of input tax charged on inward supplies used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of his business, subject to the conditions and restrictions prescribed. Sub-section (2) of section 16 opens with a non-obstante clause and lays down the following conditions, **all of which must be satisfied cumulatively**:

1. **Possession of a tax-paying document [Sec 16(2)(a)]:** He must be in possession of a tax invoice or debit note issued by a registered supplier, or such other tax-paying document as may be prescribed.
2. **Furnishing of details by supplier / communication of ITC [Sec 16(2)(aa)]:** The details of the invoice/debit note must have been furnished by the supplier in his statement of outward supplies (GSTR-1/IFF) and such details must have been communicated to the recipient (reflected in GSTR-2B). Credit not so communicated is not available.
3. **Receipt of goods or services [Sec 16(2)(b)]:** He must have received the goods or services or both. It is explained that goods delivered by the supplier to a third person on the direction of the registered person ("bill-to-ship-to") are deemed to be received by the registered person. Where goods are received in lots/instalments, credit is available only on receipt of the last lot [first proviso to Sec 16(2)].
4. **Non-restriction under section 38 [Sec 16(2)(ba)]:** The credit must not have been restricted in the auto-generated statement under section 38.
5. **Tax actually paid to Government [Sec 16(2)(c)]:** The tax charged in respect of such supply must have been actually paid to the Government, either in cash or through utilisation of admissible ITC.
6. **Furnishing of return [Sec 16(2)(d)]:** He must have furnished the return under section 39.

Additionally, section 16(2) second proviso requires that where the recipient fails to pay the supplier the value of supply along with tax within **180 days** from the date of invoice, an amount equal to the ITC availed shall be added to his output tax liability with interest; the credit can be re-availed on payment. Under section 16(3), if depreciation has been claimed on the tax component of a capital good under the Income-tax Act, ITC on that tax component shall not be allowed. Finally, section 16(4) imposes a time limit — ITC in respect of an invoice/debit note for a financial year cannot be taken after **30th November** following the end of that financial year or the date of furnishing the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier.

**Conclusion:** ITC is available only when possession of a valid document, communication of credit, actual receipt of goods/services, actual payment of tax by the supplier, and filing of return by the recipient — all coexist, within the prescribed time limit.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards listing all six clauses (a)–(d) with the two provisos and citing "Sec 16(2)"; a common deduction is forgetting clause (aa)/(ba) (GSTR-2B communication) or the 180-day and 30th-November limits.)*

### Q86. Ch: GST – Input Tax Credit — Apportionment for Mixed Use (Rule 42) (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** M/s Nova Textiles Ltd., a registered manufacturer, uses common inputs and input services for both taxable and exempt supplies. For the month of October, the following details (all figures exclusive of tax; ITC amounts in ₹) are available:

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |

| ITC on inputs used exclusively for non-business (personal) purposes (T1) | 40,000 |
| ITC on inputs used exclusively for effecting exempt supplies (T2) | 60,000 |
| ITC blocked under section 17(5) (T3) | 30,000 |

- C1 = 6,00,000 − 4,00,000 = **₹2,00,000**

**WN-2 — Credit attributable to exempt supplies (D1):**
Formula: D1 = (E ÷ F) × C2
- D1 = (12,00,000 ÷ 48,00,000) × 2,00,000
- D1 = 0.25 × 2,00,000 = **₹50,000**

**WN-3 — Credit attributable to non-business use (D2):**
Rule 42 deems 5% of common credit as attributable to non-business purposes.
- D2 = 5% × C2 = 5% × 2,00,000 = **₹10,000**

**WN-4 — Eligible common credit (C3):**
- C3 = C2 − (D1 + D2) = 2,00,000 − (50,000 + 10,000) = **₹1,40,000**

**Statement Showing ITC Treatment under Rule 42 for October:**

| Step | Description | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| T | Total input tax on inputs/input services | 6,00,000 |
| Less: T1 | Exclusively non-business | (40,000) |
| Less: T2 | Exclusively exempt supplies | (60,000) |

### Q87. Ch: GST – Registration — E-Commerce Operator & TCS (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** ShopWave Pvt. Ltd. owns a digital platform through which independent sellers supply goods to customers across India. During the year its own aggregate turnover from platform-commission is ₹8 lakh. Advise ShopWave on: (i) whether it is liable to compulsory registration as an electronic commerce operator (ECO); (ii) whether the threshold exemption of ₹20/40 lakh is available to it; and (iii) its obligation to collect tax at source (TCS) and the rate thereof. Support your answer with the relevant provisions of the CGST Act, 2017.

**Answer:**
An "electronic commerce operator" (ECO) is defined in section 2(45) of the CGST Act, 2017 as any person who owns, operates or manages a digital or electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce. ShopWave, owning a platform through which independent sellers supply goods, squarely falls within this definition.

**(i) Compulsory registration:** Under section 24(x), every electronic commerce operator who is required to collect tax at source under section 52 is liable to be **compulsorily registered**, irrespective of the threshold limit. Since ShopWave is an ECO through which other suppliers make taxable supplies and it collects consideration, it must obtain registration compulsorily. Section 24(ix) also compulsorily registers persons who supply goods/services through an ECO required to collect TCS.

**(ii) Threshold exemption:** The threshold exemption of ₹20 lakh/₹40 lakh under section 22 is **not available** to a person covered by section 24. The non-obstante clause in section 24 ("Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1) of section 22…") overrides the threshold. Therefore, even though ShopWave's own turnover is only ₹8 lakh, it must register.

**(iii) TCS obligation and rate:** Under section 52(1), an ECO (not being an agent) is required to collect tax at source on the **net value of taxable supplies** made through it by other suppliers where the consideration is collected by the ECO. The rate is **1%** (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST) of the net value of taxable supplies. "Net value of taxable supplies" means the aggregate value of taxable supplies of goods/services (other than services notified under section 9(5)) made through the operator, reduced by taxable supplies returned during the month. The tax so collected must be paid to the Government within 10 days after the end of the month, and a statement in **GSTR-8** must be furnished.

**Conclusion:** ShopWave is an ECO liable to compulsory registration under section 24(x) with no threshold benefit, and must collect TCS at 1% on the net value of taxable supplies made through its platform.

### Q88. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Order of Utilisation & Interest u/s 50 (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** M/s Orbit Enterprises (registered in Maharashtra) has the following balances in its electronic credit ledger and output tax liability for the month of November. Determine the manner of utilisation of ITC as per section 49, 49A and 49B read with Rule 88A, and the minimum cash required to be paid.

| Particulars | IGST (₹) | CGST (₹) | SGST (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output tax liability | 1,20,000 | 90,000 | 90,000 |
| Opening ITC in electronic credit ledger | 1,50,000 | 40,000 | 30,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1 — Statutory order of set-off:**
Section 49(5) read with section 49A and Rule 88A mandates:
- IGST credit must be utilised first, fully, against IGST, then CGST/SGST in any order.
- CGST credit is utilised against CGST, then IGST. It **cannot** be used for SGST.
- SGST credit is utilised against SGST, then IGST. It **cannot** be used for CGST.
- CGST/SGST credit can be utilised only **after** IGST credit has been fully exhausted.

**WN-2 — Utilisation of IGST credit (₹1,50,000):**
- Against IGST liability ₹1,20,000 → IGST credit used ₹1,20,000; IGST liability NIL.
- Balance IGST credit = ₹30,000. Utilise against CGST liability ₹90,000 → CGST liability reduced to ₹60,000; IGST credit NIL.

(Choosing to set the balance IGST against CGST is optimal so as to keep CGST credit for CGST; setting against SGST is equally permissible under Rule 88A.)

**WN-3 — Utilisation of CGST credit (₹40,000):**
- Against remaining CGST liability ₹60,000 → CGST credit used ₹40,000; CGST liability = ₹20,000 (payable in cash). CGST credit NIL.

**WN-4 — Utilisation of SGST credit (₹30,000):**
- Against SGST liability ₹90,000 → SGST credit used ₹30,000; SGST liability = ₹60,000 (payable in cash). SGST credit NIL.

**Statement Showing Utilisation of ITC and Net Tax Payable in Cash (November):**

| Particulars | IGST (₹) | CGST (₹) | SGST (₹) | Total (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Output liability | 1,20,000 | 90,000 | 90,000 | 3,00,000 |
| Less: IGST credit | 1,20,000 | 30,000 | — | 1,50,000 |
| Less: CGST credit | — | 40,000 | — | 40,000 |
| Less: SGST credit | — | — | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| **Payable in cash** | **NIL** | **20,000** | **60,000** | **80,000** |

### Q89. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Contents of Tax Invoice vs Bill of Supply (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between a "tax invoice" and a "bill of supply" under the CGST Act, 2017 and the rules made thereunder. State any four particulars that a tax invoice must contain which a bill of supply need not.

**Answer:**
Under section 31 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 46, a **tax invoice** is the document a registered person supplying taxable goods or services must issue, showing the description, quantity, value of supply and the **tax charged thereon**. In contrast, under section 31(3)(c) read with Rule 49, a **bill of supply** is issued by a registered person supplying **exempted goods/services** or one paying tax under the **composition scheme** (section 10), where no tax can be charged or collected from the recipient.

| Basis | Tax Invoice | Bill of Supply |
|---|---|---|

| Availability of ITC to recipient | ITC available | No ITC (no tax charged) |
| Signature/HSN etc. | Required | Required (except tax fields) |

1. **Rate of tax** (CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST or cess) charged on each item;
2. **Amount of tax** charged in respect of taxable goods or services (CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST, cess) separately;
3. **Whether the tax is payable on reverse charge basis** (a specific mention);
4. **Total value of supply and taxable value** of supply after adjusting discount, along with place of supply and name of the State for inter-State supplies.

### Q92. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Electronic Cash Ledger vs Credit Ledger (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the concept of the "electronic cash ledger" and the "electronic credit ledger" under section 49 of the CGST Act, 2017. Bring out the key differences between the two, and state the liabilities that can be discharged from each.

**Answer:**
Section 49 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 86 and Rule 87 provides for two ledgers maintained on the common portal for every registered person, in addition to the electronic liability register.

The **electronic cash ledger** [Section 49(1), Rule 87] is credited when a registered person deposits money through internet banking, NEFT/RTGS, over-the-counter, etc., against a challan (Form GST PMT-06). It represents cash available for payment. The **electronic credit ledger** [Section 49(2), Rule 86] reflects the input tax credit self-assessed in the return; it can be used **only** for payment of output tax.

| Basis | Electronic Cash Ledger | Electronic Credit Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory provision | Sec 49(1), Rule 87 | Sec 49(2), Rule 86 |
| Credited by | Deposit of money (challan PMT-06) | Availment of eligible ITC |
| Form | GST PMT-05 | GST PMT-02 |
| Can be used for | Tax, interest, penalty, fees, **any** amount | **Only output tax** (Sec 49(4)) |
| Interest / penalty / late fee | Can be paid | **Cannot** be paid |
| Refund | Balance refundable (Sec 49(6)) | Refund only in specified cases (e.g. zero-rated, inverted duty) |

Thus, output **tax** can be discharged from either ledger (subject to the order of utilisation under section 49A/49B), whereas **interest, penalty, late fee and any other amount** can be paid **only** through the electronic cash ledger. Section 49(4) restricts the electronic credit ledger to payment of output tax only.

**Conclusion:** The cash ledger is a general-purpose payment reservoir usable for all liabilities, while the credit ledger is a restricted reservoir usable solely for output tax.

### Q94. Ch: GST – Registration — Amendment, Cancellation & Revocation (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the circumstances in which the proper officer may cancel the registration of a registered person under section 29 of the CGST Act, 2017, and outline the procedure and time limit for revocation of such cancellation under section 30.

**Answer:**
Under section 29 of the CGST Act, 2017, registration may be cancelled either on the application of the registered person (or his legal heirs) or **suo motu** by the proper officer.

**Grounds for cancellation by proper officer [Section 29(2)]:** The proper officer may cancel registration, including retrospectively, where:
(a) a registered person has **contravened** prescribed provisions of the Act/rules;
(b) a **composition** taxpayer has not furnished returns for three consecutive tax periods;
(c) any other registered person has not furnished returns for a **continuous period** as prescribed;
(d) a person who took **voluntary registration** under section 25(3) has not commenced business within six months from registration; or
(e) registration was obtained by means of **fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression** of facts.

Before cancellation, the proper officer must give the person a reasonable **opportunity of being heard** (principles of natural justice). Cancellation does not affect the liability to pay tax and other dues for any period prior to cancellation [Section 29(3)]. On cancellation, the person must pay, by way of debit in the cash/credit ledger, an amount equal to the ITC on stock/capital goods or the output tax, whichever is higher [Section 29(5)].

**Revocation of cancellation [Section 30]:** Where registration is cancelled **suo motu** by the proper officer, the registered person may apply for revocation within **thirty days** (extendable) from the date of service of the cancellation order. The proper officer may, by order, either revoke the cancellation or reject the application (after opportunity of being heard). Revocation is not available where cancellation was on the taxpayer's own application.

**Conclusion:** Cancellation under section 29 is a consequence of default/fraud/non-filing after due hearing, and a suo-motu cancellation can be revoked under section 30 by applying within thirty days.

### Q100. Ch: GST – Input Tax Credit — ITC in Special Circumstances (Sec 18) (Marks: 6) [Theory]
**Question:** Discuss the availability of input tax credit in the following "special circumstances" under section 18 of the CGST Act, 2017: (i) a person who obtains voluntary registration; (ii) a composition taxpayer who switches to the regular scheme; (iii) an exempt supply that becomes taxable; and (iv) the effect of a change in constitution of a business by way of sale/merger/amalgamation. State the relevant declarations and time limits.

**Answer:**
Section 18 of the CGST Act, 2017 governs ITC availability in special circumstances, subject to conditions and the time limit in section 18(2) (ITC not available on invoices older than one year from the date of the relevant event).

**(i) Voluntary registration [Section 18(1)(b)]:** A person who takes registration voluntarily under section 25(3) is entitled to ITC on inputs held in stock and inputs contained in semi-finished/finished goods held in stock on the day immediately preceding the date of **grant of registration**. Declaration in **Form GST ITC-01** within 30 days.

**(ii) Composition to regular scheme [Section 18(1)(c)]:** A person who ceases to pay tax under the composition scheme (section 10) and becomes a regular taxpayer is entitled to ITC on inputs in stock, inputs in semi-finished/finished goods in stock, **and on capital goods** (reduced by prescribed percentage points — 5% per quarter or part thereof) on the day immediately preceding the date from which he becomes liable to pay tax under the regular scheme. | Circumstance | Section | Credit on capital goods? | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voluntary registration | 18(1)(b) | No | ITC-01 |
| Composition → regular | 18(1)(c) | Yes (reduced 5%/qtr) | ITC-01 |
| Exempt → taxable | 18(1)(d) | Yes (reduced 5%/qtr) | ITC-01 |
