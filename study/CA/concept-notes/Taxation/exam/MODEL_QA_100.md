### Q1. Previous Year, Assessment Year & the "first previous year" rule (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the terms "Previous Year" and "Assessment Year". State the general rule that income of a previous year is taxed in the following assessment year, and explain how the "previous year" is determined for a **business or profession newly set up** during the financial year.
**Model Answer:**
**Assessment Year — Sec 2(9):** The assessment year is the period of **12 months commencing on 1st April every year** in which the income of the immediately preceding previous year is assessed to tax. Example: for previous year 1.4.2023–31.3.2024, the assessment year is 2024-25.

**Previous Year — Sec 3:** The previous year is the **financial year immediately preceding the assessment year**, i.e. the year in which income is **earned**. Under the present law there is a **uniform previous year (financial year, 1 April to 31 March) for all assessees**, irrespective of the accounting year they follow.

**General rule of charge:** Income earned in a previous year is charged to tax in the **immediately following assessment year** (Sec 4 read with the annual Finance Act).

**Newly set-up business/profession or new source of income:** Where a business or profession is **newly set up**, or a source of income **newly comes into existence**, during the financial year, the previous year is the period **beginning from the date of setting up (or the date the source comes into existence) and ending on 31st March** of that financial year (proviso to Sec 3). Thus the **first previous year can be shorter than 12 months**, but it always ends on 31st March.

**Exceptions (income of a PY taxed in the same year):** In certain cases income is taxed in the **same** previous year to protect revenue — e.g. shipping business of non-residents (Sec 172), persons leaving India (Sec 174), AOP/BOI formed for a particular event/purpose (Sec 174A), persons likely to transfer property to avoid tax (Sec 175), and discontinued business (Sec 176).
**> Full-marks tip:** Name Sec 2(9), Sec 3 and the proviso; the mark for the "newly set up" part is earned only if you state the period runs **from date of set-up to the 31st March** and can be less than 12 months.

### Q2. "Person" and "Assessee" (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Define "Person" under Sec 2(31) and "Assessee" under Sec 2(7) of the Income-tax Act. List all the categories of "person".
**Model Answer:**
**Person — Sec 2(31):** "Person" is an inclusive definition covering **seven categories**:
1. an **Individual**;
2. a **Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)**;
3. a **Company**;
4. a **Firm** (including LLP);
5. an **Association of Persons (AOP) or Body of Individuals (BOI)**, whether incorporated or not;
6. a **Local authority**; and
7. **every artificial juridical person** not falling in any of the above (e.g. a deity, a university).

**Explanation:** An association/BOI etc. is a "person" **whether or not it was formed with the object of deriving income, profits or gains** (Explanation to Sec 2(31)). Tax is a charge on the "person".

**Assessee — Sec 2(7):** "Assessee" means a **person by whom any tax or any other sum (interest, penalty) is payable** under the Act, and includes:
- every person in respect of whom **any proceeding** has been taken for assessment of his income/loss/refund (or the income/loss/refund of another person for whom he is assessable);
- a **deemed assessee** — a person deemed to be an assessee under any provision (e.g. legal representative, agent of a non-resident); and
- an **assessee-in-default** — a person deemed to be in default for failing to fulfil a statutory obligation (e.g. failure to deduct/pay TDS).
**> Full-marks tip:** List all **seven** categories of person for full marks; for "assessee" you must mention the three limbs — ordinary assessee, deemed assessee, and assessee-in-default.

### Q3. Residential status of an individual (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Mr. Arjun, an Indian citizen, left India for the first time on **15th September 2023** for employment in Dubai and returned to India on **20th March 2024**. Determine his residential status for the **Assessment Year 2024-25**, showing the number of days of stay in India. (Assume he is not a "deemed resident".)
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 6(1):** An individual is **resident** in India in a previous year if he satisfies **any one** of the basic conditions:
(a) he is in India for **182 days or more** in the previous year; **or**
(b) he is in India for **60 days or more** in the previous year **and 365 days or more** in the 4 preceding previous years.

**Exception for citizens leaving India for employment:** For an **Indian citizen who leaves India in the previous year for the purpose of employment** outside India, condition (b) does **not** apply — only condition (a) of **182 days** is tested (Explanation 1(a) to Sec 6(1)).

**Working Note — days of stay in India in PY 2023-24:**
| Period in India | Days |
|---|---|
| 1 April 2023 to 15 September 2023 | Apr 30 + May 31 + Jun 30 + Jul 31 + Aug 31 + Sep 15 = **168** |
| 20 March 2024 to 31 March 2024 | 12 |
| **Total** | **180 days** |

**Application:** Mr. Arjun left India **for employment**, so only the 182-day condition applies. His stay is **180 days**, which is **less than 182 days**. Basic condition (a) is **not** satisfied.

**Conclusion:** Mr. Arjun is a **Non-Resident** for AY 2024-25.
**> Full-marks tip:** State the 182-day-only rule for a citizen leaving for employment (dropping the 60-day test is the whole trick); show the day count as a working note and count the departure/arrival months exactly.

### Q4. Scope of total income — incidence of tax by residential status (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** For the previous year 2023-24, the following incomes are earned by Mr. Bose. Compute the **total income** if he is (i) Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR), (ii) Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR), and (iii) Non-Resident (NR).
1. Income from a business in Mumbai — ₹4,00,000.
2. Income from a business in London, controlled from India — ₹3,00,000 (received in London).
3. Income from a business in Canada, controlled from Canada — ₹2,00,000 (₹80,000 received in India, balance in Canada).
4. Interest on Germany bonds — ₹1,00,000 (entirely received in Germany, no business connection in India).
5. Past untaxed foreign income brought into India during 2023-24 — ₹1,50,000.
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 5:** Incidence of tax depends on residential status. **Income received/deemed received in India** and **income accruing/arising or deemed to accrue/arise in India** are taxable for **all** assessees. **Foreign income** (accruing and received outside India) is taxable for an ROR on global basis; for an RNOR it is taxable **only if derived from a business controlled from / profession set up in India**; for an NR it is **not** taxable. Remittance of **past** income is **not** income of the current year.

**Computation of Total Income (₹):**
| Particulars | ROR | RNOR | NR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Business income Mumbai (Indian income) | 4,00,000 | 4,00,000 | 4,00,000 |
| 2. London business, **controlled from India** | 3,00,000 | 3,00,000 | — |
| 3. Canada business (controlled from Canada) — ₹80,000 received in India | 2,00,000 | 80,000 | 80,000 |
| 4. Interest on Germany bonds (foreign income, no Indian nexus) | 1,00,000 | — | — |
| 5. Past untaxed income remitted to India | — | — | — |
| **Total Income** | **10,00,000** | **7,80,000** | **4,80,000** |

**Notes:**
- Item 2 is foreign income but is taxable for an **RNOR** because the business is **controlled from India** (proviso to Sec 5(1)).
- Item 3: for RNOR/NR, only the part **received in India (₹80,000)** is taxable; the business is controlled from Canada so the balance is not taxable to them; the whole ₹2,00,000 is taxable to the ROR as global income.
- Item 5: mere **remittance of past income is not taxable** — it is not income of PY 2023-24.
**> Full-marks tip:** The examiner tests whether you know an RNOR is taxed on foreign income **only if the business is controlled from India**; separately treat the "received in India" portion for RNOR/NR, and explicitly say remittance of past income is not income.

### Q5. ROR / RNOR / NR — additional conditions (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** After an individual is found to be a "resident" under Sec 6(1), how is it decided whether he is **Resident and Ordinarily Resident** or **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident**? State the additional conditions.
**Model Answer:**
**Two-stage test:** Residential status of an individual is decided in two stages — first the **basic conditions (Sec 6(1))** decide *resident vs non-resident*; then, if resident, the **additional conditions (Sec 6(6))** decide *ordinarily resident vs not ordinarily resident*.

**Additional conditions — Sec 6(6):** A resident individual is **Resident and Ordinarily Resident** only if he satisfies **both** of the following:
(i) he has been **resident in India in at least 2 out of the 10** previous years immediately preceding the relevant previous year; **and**
(ii) he has been in India for **730 days or more during the 7** previous years immediately preceding the relevant previous year.

**RNOR:** A resident who **fails to satisfy either or both** of the above additional conditions is **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident**.

**Also treated as RNOR (special cases — verify current AY in ICAI material):**
- an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin whose **total income (other than foreign-source income) exceeds the specified limit** and who is in India for **120 days or more but less than 182 days** (Explanation 1(b) / Sec 6(6)(c)); and
- a **deemed resident** u/s 6(1A) (Indian citizen not liable to tax in any other country whose India-source income exceeds the specified limit) is always **RNOR** (Sec 6(6)(d)).

**Conclusion:** ROR = resident + both additional conditions satisfied; RNOR = resident but at least one additional condition failed (or covered by the special deeming provisions).
**> Full-marks tip:** State the exact numbers — **2 out of 10 years** and **730 days out of 7 years** — and note that failing *either* makes him RNOR.

### Q6. Residential status of an HUF (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain how the residential status of a **Hindu Undivided Family** is determined, and when the **karta**'s stay in India becomes relevant.
**Model Answer:**
**Resident vs Non-resident — Sec 6(2):** An HUF is **resident** in India in any previous year **unless the control and management of its affairs is situated wholly outside India** during that year. In other words, if control and management is situated **wholly or partly in India**, the HUF is resident; only if it is **wholly outside India** is the HUF non-resident.

**Ordinarily resident vs not ordinarily resident — Sec 6(6)(b):** A resident HUF is **Not Ordinarily Resident** if its **karta (manager)** does **not** satisfy the two additional conditions applicable to individuals, i.e. the karta must have been:
(i) resident in India in **at least 2 out of 10** preceding previous years; **and**
(ii) in India for **730 days or more in the 7** preceding previous years.
If the karta satisfies **both**, the HUF is **ROR**; if he fails either, the HUF is **RNOR**.

**Explanation:** "Control and management" refers to the **head and brain — the place where decisions are taken** — not merely the place where the business is carried on. Thus the status of the family turns on where its affairs are controlled; the karta's own past stay is used **only to grade a resident HUF** into ROR or RNOR.
**> Full-marks tip:** Keep the two tests separate — control-and-management decides resident/non-resident (Sec 6(2)); the **karta's** additional conditions decide ROR/RNOR (Sec 6(6)(b)).

### Q7. House Rent Allowance exemption (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Mr. Karan is employed in Delhi. For the year ended 31.3.2024 he received **basic salary ₹40,000 p.m.**, **dearness allowance ₹10,000 p.m. (fully forming part of retirement benefits)**, and **HRA ₹18,000 p.m.** He pays **rent of ₹20,000 p.m.** for accommodation in Delhi. Compute the **HRA exemption** and the **taxable HRA** for AY 2024-25 (old regime).
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 10(13A) read with Rule 2A:** HRA is exempt to the **least** of:
(a) actual HRA received;
(b) rent paid **minus 10% of salary**; and
(c) **50% of salary** (metro city: Delhi/Mumbai/Kolkata/Chennai) or 40% (non-metro).
"Salary" for this purpose = **basic salary + DA forming part of retirement benefits + commission as a fixed % of turnover**.

**Working Note 1 — Salary (per annum):**
Basic = ₹40,000 × 12 = ₹4,80,000
DA (part of retirement benefits) = ₹10,000 × 12 = ₹1,20,000
**Salary = ₹6,00,000**

**Working Note 2 — the three limits:**
| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| (a) Actual HRA = 18,000 × 12 | 2,16,000 |
| (b) Rent paid − 10% of salary = (20,000 × 12) − 60,000 = 2,40,000 − 60,000 | 1,80,000 |
| (c) 50% of salary (Delhi = metro) = 50% × 6,00,000 | 3,00,000 |

**Exemption = least of the three = ₹1,80,000.**

**Taxable HRA = 2,16,000 − 1,80,000 = ₹36,000.**
**> Full-marks tip:** Always show all **three** limits and pick the **least**; include DA in "salary" only because it forms part of retirement benefits, and use **50%** because Delhi is a metro. (HRA exemption is not available under the new regime — verify current AY in ICAI material.)

### Q8. Gratuity exemption — non-government employee (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Mr. Menon retired on **31.12.2023** after **26 years and 8 months** of service and received **gratuity of ₹16,00,000**. He is **covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972**. His salary at retirement was **basic ₹50,000 p.m.** and **DA ₹20,000 p.m.** Compute the taxable gratuity for AY 2024-25.
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 10(10)(ii):** For an employee **covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972**, gratuity is exempt to the **least** of:
(a) actual gratuity received;
(b) **₹20,00,000** (overall statutory ceiling — verify current AY in ICAI material); and
(c) **15/26 × last drawn salary × number of completed years of service (or part exceeding 6 months)**.
Here "salary" = **basic + DA** (whether or not it forms part of retirement benefits), being the **last drawn** salary.

**Working Note 1 — completed years:** 26 years 8 months → the **8 months** (more than 6 months) is rounded up → **27 years**.

**Working Note 2 — last drawn salary:** Basic 50,000 + DA 20,000 = **₹70,000 p.m.**

**Working Note 3 — the three limits:**
| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| (a) Actual gratuity | 16,00,000 |
| (b) Statutory ceiling | 20,00,000 |
| (c) 15/26 × 70,000 × 27 = 15 × 70,000 × 27 / 26 | 10,90,385 |

**Exemption = least = ₹10,90,385.**

**Taxable gratuity = 16,00,000 − 10,90,385 = ₹5,09,615.**
**> Full-marks tip:** For a Gratuity-Act-covered employee use **15/26** and round any part-year **over 6 months up to a full year**; include **full DA** in salary and round the final answer to the nearest rupee.

### Q9. Leave salary encashment on retirement (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Mr. Patel, a **non-government employee**, retired on 31.3.2024 after **30 years** of service and received **leave encashment of ₹4,50,000** for **10 months** of accumulated leave (leave entitlement being 30 days per year of service). His **average salary** for the last 10 months was **₹42,000 p.m.** (basic + DA forming part of retirement benefits). He had already **availed 20 months** of leave during service. Compute taxable leave salary. (Statutory ceiling ₹25,00,000 — verify current AY.)
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 10(10AA)(ii):** For a **non-government employee**, leave salary on retirement is exempt to the **least** of:
(a) actual leave salary received;
(b) **₹25,00,000** (overall ceiling — verify current AY in ICAI material);
(c) **10 months × average salary** of the last 10 months; and
(d) **cash equivalent of leave** to the credit at retirement, based on max **30 days' leave per completed year of service**, at average salary.

**Working Note 1 — leave to credit (in days):**
Leave earned = 30 days × 30 years = 900 days = 30 months
Less: leave availed = 20 months
Leave to credit = **10 months** (at 30 days/year basis this equals 10 months).

**Working Note 2 — average salary = ₹42,000 p.m.**

**Working Note 3 — the four limits:**
| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| (a) Actual leave salary | 4,50,000 |
| (b) Statutory ceiling | 25,00,000 |
| (c) 10 months × 42,000 | 4,20,000 |
| (d) Cash equivalent = 10 months × 42,000 | 4,20,000 |

**Exemption = least = ₹4,20,000.**

**Taxable leave salary = 4,50,000 − 4,20,000 = ₹30,000.**
**> Full-marks tip:** Compute leave-to-credit at **30 days per completed year** (not the employer's higher entitlement), show all **four** limits, and remember the ₹25,00,000 ceiling is a **lifetime aggregate** across employers.

### Q10. Commuted pension exemption (marks: 4) [Practical]
**Question:** Mr. Rao, a **non-government employee**, retired and received **commuted pension of ₹6,00,000** by commuting **60%** of his pension. He **also received gratuity**. Compute the exempt and taxable commuted pension for AY 2024-25.
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 10(10A):**
- **Government employees:** commuted pension is **wholly exempt**.
- **Non-government employees:** exemption depends on whether gratuity is received:
  - If gratuity **is** received → **1/3 of the full value of commuted pension** is exempt;
  - If gratuity is **not** received → **1/2 of the full value** is exempt.

**Working Note — full value of pension commutable:**
₹6,00,000 represents **60%** of full commuted value.
Full value of pension (100%) = 6,00,000 / 60% = **₹10,00,000**.

**Exemption (gratuity received → 1/3 of full value):** 1/3 × 10,00,000 = **₹3,33,333**.

**Taxable commuted pension = 6,00,000 − 3,33,333 = ₹2,66,667.**

**Note:** **Uncommuted (monthly) pension** is **fully taxable** as salary for all employees.
**> Full-marks tip:** Gross up to the **100% value** before applying the fraction; use **1/3** because gratuity was received (1/2 only if no gratuity), and state that uncommuted pension is fully taxable.

### Q11. Perquisite — rent-free unfurnished accommodation (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** Mr. Sen, a **private-sector employee** posted in a city with population above 40 lakh (as per 2011 census), is provided a **rent-free unfurnished house owned by the employer**. His salary details for PY 2023-24: **basic ₹60,000 p.m.**, **DA ₹15,000 p.m. (forming part of retirement benefits)**, **bonus ₹80,000**, and **HRA-in-lieu is nil**. Compute the taxable value of the accommodation perquisite for AY 2024-25. (Use the population-slab rates in the ICAI material — verify current AY.)
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 17(2)(i)/(ii) read with Rule 3(1):** For accommodation **owned by the employer** provided to a private-sector employee, the perquisite value is a **percentage of "salary"** based on population of the city (as per the ICAI/ Rule 3 slabs — **verify current AY in ICAI material**):
- population **> 40 lakh** → **15% of salary**;
- population **> 15 lakh but ≤ 40 lakh** → **10% of salary**;
- population **≤ 15 lakh** → **7.5% of salary**.
"Salary" here = **basic + DA (if part of retirement benefits) + bonus + commission + all taxable allowances + any monetary payment**, but **excludes** perquisites and employer's PF contribution.

**Working Note — salary for Rule 3:**
| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Basic (60,000 × 12) | 7,20,000 |
| DA (part of retirement benefits) (15,000 × 12) | 1,80,000 |
| Bonus | 80,000 |
| **Salary** | **9,80,000** |

**Perquisite value = 15% × 9,80,000 = ₹1,47,000** (population > 40 lakh).

**Taxable value of rent-free accommodation = ₹1,47,000.**
**> Full-marks tip:** Pick the **correct population slab %**, and build the Rule 3 "salary" carefully — include DA only if it enters retirement benefits, include bonus/commission, but **exclude perquisites and employer PF**.

### Q12. Perquisite — motor car provided by employer (marks: 4) [Practical]
**Question:** Explain and value the **motor car perquisite** where the employer provides a car (cubic capacity ≤ 1.6 litres) with a driver, meets all running and maintenance expenses, and the car is used **partly for official and partly for personal** purposes by the employee. State the taxable value per month. (Use current Rule 3 amounts — verify current AY.)
**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Sec 17(2) read with Rule 3(2):** Where a car is **owned/hired by the employer**, expenses met by the employer, and used **partly for official and partly for personal** purposes, the perquisite is a **fixed monthly amount** (no need to maintain detailed logs), being (verify current AY in ICAI material):
- car up to **1.6 litres** engine capacity → **₹1,800 per month**;
- car above **1.6 litres** → **₹2,400 per month**;
- **plus ₹900 per month if a chauffeur/driver** is also provided.

**Application:** Car ≤ 1.6 litres + driver:
| | ₹ per month |
|---|---|
| Car (≤ 1.6 litre) | 1,800 |
| Driver | 900 |
| **Perquisite per month** | **2,700** |
Annual taxable value = **₹2,700 × 12 = ₹32,400**.

**Notes:**
- If used **wholly for official** purposes (with proper records), the value is **nil**.
- If used **wholly for personal** purposes, the value is **actual expenditure + 10% of cost of car (wear & tear) + driver's salary**, less any amount recovered.
- If the **employee owns the car** but the **employer meets expenses** for partly-personal use, only the **personal portion** is a perquisite (fixed amounts of ₹1,800/₹2,400 are deducted as the deemed official portion).
**> Full-marks tip:** Use the **fixed slab amounts** for part-official/part-personal use and remember the extra **₹900 for a driver**; distinguish clearly from the "wholly personal" (actual-cost) valuation.

### Q13. Computation of income under the head Salaries (marks: 8) [Practical]
**Question:** Compute the **income under the head Salaries** of Mr. Vikram for AY 2024-25 (old regime) from the following:
- Basic salary: ₹50,000 p.m.
- Dearness allowance: ₹20,000 p.m. (40% forms part of retirement benefits).
- Bonus: ₹1,00,000.
- Entertainment allowance: ₹2,000 p.m.
- Transport allowance (he is not a specified disabled person): ₹3,000 p.m.
- HRA: ₹12,000 p.m.; rent paid ₹15,000 p.m. in a non-metro city.
- Employer's contribution to Recognised Provident Fund: ₹96,000; interest credited to RPF @ 12% p.a.: ₹60,000.
- Professional tax paid by employee: ₹2,500.
**Model Answer:**
**Working Note 1 — Salary for HRA (basic + DA in retirement benefits):**
Basic = 50,000 × 12 = 6,00,000
DA in retirement benefits = 40% × (20,000 × 12) = 40% × 2,40,000 = 96,000
Salary for HRA = **6,96,000**

(a) Actual HRA = 12,000 × 12 = 1,44,000
(b) Rent − 10% salary = 1,80,000 − 69,600 = 1,10,400
(c) 40% of salary = 2,78,400
Least = **1,10,400 exempt**; taxable HRA = 1,44,000 − 1,10,400 = **33,600**

**Working Note 3 — RPF:**
- Employer contribution taxable to the extent it exceeds **12% of salary**. Salary for RPF = basic + DA (retirement) + commission = 6,00,000 + 96,000 = 6,96,000. 12% = 83,520. Employer contribution 96,000 − 83,520 = **12,480 taxable**.

| Entertainment allowance (fully taxable) | | 24,000 |
| Transport allowance (fully taxable) | | 36,000 |
| HRA — taxable portion (WN 2) | | 33,600 |
| Employer's RPF contribution — taxable excess (WN 3) | | 12,480 |
| Interest on RPF — taxable excess (WN 3) | | 12,500 |
| **Gross Salary** | | **10,58,580** |
| **Less: Deductions u/s 16** | | |
| Standard deduction u/s 16(ia) — verify current AY | 50,000 | |
| Entertainment allowance u/s 16(ii) — non-government, **nil** | Nil | |
| Professional tax u/s 16(iii) | 2,500 | (52,500) |

**> Full-marks tip:** Two big traps — RPF has **two** taxable pieces (employer excess over 12% and interest over 9.5%) and each needs its own working note; and entertainment allowance is **taxable in full** with **no 16(ii) deduction** for a non-government employee. Show the standard deduction and professional tax under Sec 16.

### Q14. Allowances — fully exempt, partly exempt, fully taxable (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Classify allowances into (a) fully taxable, (b) partly exempt, and (c) fully exempt, giving **two examples** of each with the relevant provision.
**Model Answer:**
**(a) Fully taxable allowances (taxed in full as salary):** e.g. **Dearness allowance, City compensatory allowance, Overtime allowance, Servant allowance, Warden/Proctor allowance**. These have no specific exemption.

**(b) Partly exempt allowances (exempt up to a limit; balance taxable):**
- **House Rent Allowance — Sec 10(13A)/Rule 2A** — exempt to the least of the three prescribed limits.
- **Prescribed special allowances u/s 10(14):**
  - *Sec 10(14)(i)* — allowances to meet expenses **wholly, necessarily and exclusively incurred in the performance of duties** (e.g. travelling, daily, conveyance, uniform allowance) — exempt to the **extent actually spent**;

### Q15. Provident funds — tax treatment compared (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Compare the tax treatment of **Statutory Provident Fund (SPF), Recognised Provident Fund (RPF), Unrecognised Provident Fund (URPF) and Public Provident Fund (PPF)** at the stages of (i) employer's contribution, (ii) interest credited, and (iii) lump-sum received on retirement.
**Model Answer:**
**Provision:** The taxability of provident funds turns on the type of fund. Employer contributions and interest to an RPF are taxable only beyond prescribed limits; SPF and PPF enjoy full exemption; URPF is deferred and taxed on withdrawal.

| Stage | SPF | RPF | URPF | PPF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Employer's contribution** | Fully **exempt** | Exempt up to **12% of salary**; excess taxable | **Not taxed** yearly (no employer, or not taxed on accrual) | N/A (only individual can subscribe); own contribution qualifies u/s 80C |
| **Interest credited** | Fully **exempt** | Exempt up to **9.5% p.a.**; excess taxable | **Not taxed** yearly | Fully **exempt** |
| **Lump sum on retirement** | Fully **exempt** | Exempt if conditions met (e.g. **5 years' continuous service**); else taxed | **Employer's share + interest thereon → taxed as salary; interest on own contribution → income from other sources; own contribution → not taxed** | Fully **exempt** |

**Explanation:** For RPF, employer contribution beyond 12% of "salary" (basic + DA in retirement benefits + commission on turnover) and interest beyond 9.5% are taxable **each year** as salary. The RPF lump sum is exempt u/s 10(12) if the employee has rendered **continuous service of 5 years** (or termination beyond his control). URPF converts into an RPF on recognition, and the accumulated employer's share is then taxed on withdrawal.
**> Full-marks tip:** Nail the **two RPF thresholds (12% and 9.5%)** and the **5-year** condition for exemption of the RPF lump sum; the URPF withdrawal split (employer's share = salary, interest on own = other sources) is a frequent mark-earner.

### Q18. Basis of charge of salary — due vs receipt & Sec 89 relief (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the **basis of charge** of income under the head Salaries under Sec 15, and explain the purpose of **relief under Section 89**.
**Model Answer:**
**Basis of charge — Sec 15:** Salary is chargeable to tax on **due or receipt basis, whichever is earlier**. Specifically, the following are taxable:
(a) any salary **due** from an employer (or former employer) in the previous year, **whether paid or not**;
(b) any salary **paid or allowed** in the previous year, **though not due** (e.g. advance salary); and
(c) any **arrears of salary** paid/allowed in the previous year, **if not charged to tax in any earlier year**.
**Explanation (no double taxation):** Salary once taxed on **due** basis is not taxed again when received; salary taxed on **receipt** (advance) is not taxed again when it later becomes due (Explanation 1 to Sec 15).

**Relief u/s 89:** Because salary is taxed on due/receipt basis, receipt of **arrears or advance salary** (or family pension arrears, etc.) in a single year can push the employee into a **higher slab** than if it had been taxed in the years to which it relates. **Section 89** grants **relief** by re-computing tax **as if the arrears/advance had been taxed in the respective years**, and allowing the difference as a deduction from the tax of the year of receipt (mechanism prescribed in **Rule 21A**; Form **10E** must be filed).
**> Full-marks tip:** State "due **or** receipt, whichever is earlier" and the three limbs of Sec 15; for Sec 89 relief mention **Rule 21A** and the requirement to file **Form 10E**.

### Q19. Rounding off & basic rate structure (marks: 3) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the provisions for **rounding off of total income (Sec 288A)** and **rounding off of tax (Sec 288B)**, and state the manner of charging income-tax under Sec 4.
**Model Answer:**
**Charge of tax — Sec 4:** Income-tax is charged for an **assessment year** at the **rates prescribed by the annual Finance Act** on the **total income** of the previous year of every person. Tax is deducted/collected at source or paid in advance where the Act so requires, but the ultimate charge is on the total income of the assessee.

**Rounding off of total income — Sec 288A:** The total income computed is **rounded off to the nearest multiple of ₹10**. For this, any part of ₹10 that is ₹5 or more is **rounded up** to the next ₹10, and less than ₹5 is **ignored** (the last figure, i.e. paise and rupees below 10, is adjusted accordingly).

**Rounding off of tax — Sec 288B:** The amount of tax (including TDS/TCS), interest, penalty, fine or any other sum payable/refundable is **rounded off to the nearest multiple of ₹10** in the same manner (₹5 or more rounded up, less than ₹5 ignored).

**Conclusion:** First compute and **round the total income to nearest ₹10 (Sec 288A)**; apply the Finance Act rates; then **round the tax to nearest ₹10 (Sec 288B)**.
**> Full-marks tip:** Quote **Sec 288A (income)** and **Sec 288B (tax)** distinctly and state the "nearest multiple of ₹10, ₹5-and-above rounds up" rule.

### Q1. [Capital Gains — Basic] (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain how a capital asset is classified as **short-term** or **long-term**, and state the period of holding required in each of the following cases: (i) listed equity shares, (ii) unlisted shares, (iii) immovable property (land/building), (iv) a self-generated capital asset.

**Model Answer:**

**Provision — Sec 2(42A) [STCA] and Sec 2(29A) r/w 2(29AA) [LTCA]:** A **Short-Term Capital Asset** is a capital asset held for **not more than the prescribed period** immediately preceding the date of transfer; anything held **beyond** that period is a **Long-Term Capital Asset**. The prescribed period differs by asset class.

**Prescribed holding periods (verify current AY in ICAI material):**
- **(i) Listed equity shares / units of equity-oriented fund / listed securities:** LTCA if held **> 12 months**.
- **(ii) Unlisted shares & immovable property:** LTCA if held **> 24 months**.
- **(iii) Immovable property (land or building or both):** LTCA if held **> 24 months**.
- **(iv) Any other capital asset** (e.g., unlisted debentures, gold/jewellery): LTCA if held **> 36 months**.

**Explanation — period of holding for special cases:**
- For a **self-generated asset** (e.g., goodwill, self-created trademark), the period of holding is counted from the date the asset came into existence; where cost is deemed **nil** under Sec 55, capital gain is still computed.
- Under **Sec 49(1)** (assets acquired by gift/will/inheritance), the **previous owner's holding period is included**.

**Conclusion:** Classification is purely a **holding-period test** applied to the correct asset category; it decides the rate of tax, availability of indexation and the exemptions (Sec 54 series) that can be claimed.

**> Full-marks tip:** Give the exact months for each asset class and quote Sec 2(42A)/2(29A) — examiners award a mark per correctly matched period.

### Q8. [IFOS — Sec 56(2)(x)] (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the taxability of **gifts** received by an individual under **Section 56(2)(x)**. Cover money, immovable property and movable property, and list the main exclusions.

**2. Immovable property:**

**Conclusion:** Sec 56(2)(x) taxes windfall receipts of money/property beyond the Rs 50,000 threshold except the specified exempt occasions and relationships.

### Q10. [Clubbing — Overview] (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the clubbing provisions relating to (i) **transfer of income without transfer of asset**, (ii) **revocable transfer of assets**, and (iii) **income of spouse** arising from a concern in which the individual has substantial interest.

**Model Answer:**

**Concept:** Clubbing (Sec 60–64) is an **anti-avoidance** mechanism that includes another person's income in the assessee's total income where income is diverted without a genuine transfer of the income-earning asset.

**(i) Transfer of income without transfer of asset — Sec 60:** If a person transfers the **income** from an asset **without transferring the asset itself**, that income is **taxed in the hands of the transferor**, whether the transfer is revocable or not.

**(ii) Revocable transfer of assets — Sec 61:** Where assets are transferred under a **revocable transfer** (transferor can re-assume power/benefit over the income or asset), the income is **clubbed with the transferor**. *(Exception — Sec 62: a transfer not revocable during the lifetime of the beneficiary/transferee is outside clubbing until the power to revoke arises.)*

**(iii) Income of spouse — Sec 64(1)(ii):** Any **remuneration** (salary, commission, fees) received by the **spouse** from a **concern in which the individual has a substantial interest** is clubbed in the hands of the individual —
- *Exception:* not clubbed if the spouse possesses **technical or professional qualifications** and the income is **solely attributable** to that knowledge/experience.
- *Substantial interest:* holding ≥ **20%** of voting power/shares or ≥ 20% profit share (verify current AY).
- Where both spouses receive such remuneration, it is clubbed in the hands of the spouse with **higher total income** (before such clubbing).

**Conclusion:** Sections 60–64 ensure income cannot escape tax by being routed through family members or hollow transfers.

**> Full-marks tip:** Name the exact section for each limb and state the **20% substantial-interest** threshold plus the **technical-qualification exception** — these are the differentiators.

### Q12. [Set-off & Carry-forward — Rules] (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the rules for **inter-head set-off (Sec 71)** and briefly the **carry-forward** period for (i) business loss, (ii) speculation loss, (iii) capital losses, and (iv) house-property loss.

**Model Answer:**

**Provision — Sec 70 (intra-head) & Sec 71 (inter-head):** Losses are first set off **within the same head** (Sec 70), and the remaining loss is then set off **against income under other heads** in the same year (Sec 71), subject to restrictions.

**Key inter-head restrictions (Sec 71) (verify current AY):**
- **Loss from house property** can be set off against other heads only up to **Rs 2,00,000** in a year (Sec 71(3A)); balance is carried forward.
- **Business loss cannot be set off against Salary** income (Sec 71(2A)).
- **Losses under "Capital Gains"** cannot be set off against income of any other head (only within the capital-gains head).
- Losses cannot be set off against **casual income** (lottery/winnings).

**Carry-forward periods:**
| Loss | Section | Carry-forward period | Set off against |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) Non-speculation business loss | 72 | **8 AYs** | Any business income |
| (ii) Speculation loss | 73 | **4 AYs** | Speculation income only |
| (iii) Short-term capital loss | 74 | **8 AYs** | STCG or LTCG |
| (iii) Long-term capital loss | 74 | **8 AYs** | **LTCG only** |
| (iv) House-property loss | 71B | **8 AYs** | House-property income only |

**Note:** Unabsorbed **depreciation** (Sec 32(2)) can be carried forward **indefinitely**. Carry-forward of business/capital losses generally requires filing the **return by the due date u/s 139(1)** (house-property loss is an exception).

**Conclusion:** Set-off follows intra-head → inter-head order with head-specific caps, and unabsorbed losses carry forward for the periods above.

**> Full-marks tip:** The high-value marks are the **Rs 2 lakh HP cap**, "**business loss not vs salary**", "**LTCL only vs LTCG**", and the **timely-return** condition for carry-forward.

### Q7. Income from Other Sources — Taxation of gifts u/s 56(2)(x) (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the taxability of gifts of money and property under Section 56(2)(x). When are such receipts not taxable?

**Model Answer:**
**Provision (Sec 56(2)(x)):** Where any person receives, without or for inadequate consideration, the following, it is taxable as Income from Other Sources:

1. **Money:** If the **aggregate** sum of money received without consideration during the year exceeds **₹50,000** → the **whole** amount is taxable.
2. **Immovable property (without consideration):** If stamp duty value (SDV) > ₹50,000 → **entire SDV** is taxable. **(For inadequate consideration):** if (SDV − consideration) exceeds the higher of ₹50,000 or 10% of consideration → the **difference** is taxable *(verify 10% tolerance band for current AY)*.
3. **Movable property** (specified — jewellery, shares, drawings, bullion, etc.): without consideration, if aggregate **FMV > ₹50,000** → whole FMV taxable; for inadequate consideration, if (FMV − consideration) > ₹50,000 → the difference is taxable.

**Exempt receipts (proviso):** Gifts received —
(a) from a **relative** (as defined — spouse, siblings, lineal ascendants/descendants, spouse's siblings/parents, etc.);
(b) on the occasion of the **marriage** of the individual;
(c) under a **will or inheritance**;
(d) in contemplation of **death** of the payer;
(e) from a **local authority / registered trust / institution** u/s 10(23C) / Sec 12AA/12AB, etc.

**Conclusion:** The ₹50,000 threshold is tested on the **aggregate**, and the exemptions are occasion-/relationship-specific.

**> Full-marks tip:** Distinguish the **"whole amount"** rule (money & without-consideration cases) from the **"difference"** rule (inadequate consideration), and list at least four exempt situations. Note marriage — *not* birthday/anniversary — is the exempt occasion.

### Q9. Clubbing — Overview of provisions (Sec 60–64) (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Briefly explain the circumstances in which income of one person is clubbed in the hands of another under Sections 60 to 64.

**Model Answer:**
**Provision:** Clubbing provisions prevent tax avoidance by diverting income to family members.

1. **Sec 60 — Transfer of income without transfer of asset:** Income arising from an asset is clubbed with the transferor if only the *income* is transferred but the *asset* is retained.
2. **Sec 61 — Revocable transfer of assets:** Income from assets transferred under a revocable transfer is taxed in the transferor's hands.
3. **Sec 64(1)(ii) — Spouse's remuneration:** Salary/commission etc. received by a spouse from a concern in which the individual has a **substantial interest** is clubbed, *unless* the spouse possesses technical/professional qualifications and the income is attributable to that.
4. **Sec 64(1)(iv) — Transfer to spouse:** Income from assets transferred to the spouse without adequate consideration is clubbed.
5. **Sec 64(1)(vi) — Transfer to son's wife:** Similarly clubbed.
6. **Sec 64(1A) — Minor child:** Income of a minor child is clubbed with the parent whose total income is higher, with an exemption of **₹1,500 per child** u/s 10(32) — except income from the child's **manual work/skill/talent** or a **disabled** (Sec 80U) minor.
7. **Sec 64(2) — Conversion of individual property into HUF property** without adequate consideration: income is clubbed with the transferor.

**> Full-marks tip:** Attach the **section number** to each situation and remember the two key exceptions (spouse's *qualification*; minor's *own skill*). Listing sections without the exceptions loses easy marks.

### Q20. Tax computation — Rebate 87A, surcharge & marginal relief (marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Write short notes on: (i) Rebate under Section 87A; (ii) Surcharge on income-tax for individuals; and (iii) Marginal relief.

**Model Answer:**
**(i) Rebate u/s 87A:** Available to a **resident individual**. *(Verify current AY figures.)* Under the **old regime**, if total income ≤ **₹5,00,000**, rebate = lower of tax payable or **₹12,500**. Under the **new regime (115BAC)**, if total income ≤ **₹7,00,000**, rebate = lower of tax payable or **₹25,000**. It reduces tax to nil at/below the threshold; not available on tax on LTCG u/s 112A (as specified).

**(ii) Surcharge:** An additional levy on **income-tax** (before cess) where total income exceeds specified thresholds. Illustrative rates for individuals *(verify AY)*: income > ₹50 lakh–₹1 cr → **10%**; > ₹1 cr–₹2 cr → **15%**; > ₹2 cr–₹5 cr → **25%**; > ₹5 cr → **37%** (old regime). Under the **new regime the maximum surcharge is capped at 25%**. Surcharge on income taxed u/s 111A/112/112A and dividends is capped at 15%.

**(iii) Marginal relief:** A relief ensuring that the **increase in tax (including surcharge)** due to crossing a threshold does **not exceed the increase in income** above that threshold. E.g., where income marginally exceeds ₹50 lakh, the total tax + surcharge cannot exceed [tax on ₹50 lakh + (income − ₹50 lakh)]. It is available at each surcharge threshold (and, in the new regime, near the rebate limit via the 87A marginal benefit).

**Conclusion:** 87A gives full relief at the lower end; surcharge adds to tax at the higher end; marginal relief cushions the jump at each threshold.

**> Full-marks tip:** State the **two different 87A limits** (₹5 lakh/₹12,500 old vs ₹7 lakh/₹25,000 new), and define marginal relief as capping the *extra tax* to the *extra income* — a precise definition earns the mark, a vague one does not.

### Q1. Eligibility & Conditions for Availing ITC (marks: 4) [Theory]

### Q3. Apportionment of Common ITC — Rule 42 (marks: 5) [Practical]
**Question:** For a tax period, M/s Nimbus Enterprises (registered) has the following ITC on inputs/input services. Compute the ITC to be credited to the electronic credit ledger and the amount to be reversed, using **Rule 42** of the CGST Rules.

- Total ITC on inputs & input services (T) = ₹10,00,000
- ITC on inputs used **exclusively for non-business** (T1) = ₹50,000
- ITC on inputs used **exclusively for exempt supplies** (T2) = ₹80,000
- ITC **blocked** under Sec 17(5) (T3) = ₹70,000
- ITC on inputs used **exclusively for taxable (incl. zero-rated) supplies** (T4) = ₹5,00,000
- Aggregate value of **exempt supplies** during the period (E) = ₹20,00,000
- **Total turnover** during the period (F) = ₹80,00,000

**Step 1 — Credit attributable to business/taxable (C1):**
C1 = T − (T1 + T2 + T3) = 10,00,000 − (50,000 + 80,000 + 70,000) = **₹8,00,000**

C2 = C1 − T4 = 8,00,000 − 5,00,000 = **₹3,00,000**

**Step 4 — Credit attributable to non-business use (D2):**
D2 = 5% of C2 = 5% × 3,00,000 = **₹15,000**

**Step 5 — Eligible common credit (C3):**
C3 = C2 − (D1 + D2) = 3,00,000 − (75,000 + 15,000) = **₹2,10,000**

**Result:**
- **ITC credited to electronic credit ledger = T4 + C3 = 5,00,000 + 2,10,000 = ₹7,10,000**
- **ITC to be reversed (added to output liability) = D1 + D2 = ₹90,000**

**> Full-marks tip:** Reverse **D1 and D2 only**; T1, T2, T3 are never credited in the first place (do not double-count them as "reversal"). Label each symbol (C1, C2, D1, D2, C3).

### Q4. ITC on Capital Goods — Rule 43 (marks: 4) [Practical]
**Question:** M/s Orion Ltd. purchased a machine (common capital good used partly for taxable and partly for exempt supplies) on which GST of ₹6,00,000 was paid. For the relevant tax period, aggregate value of exempt supplies (E) = ₹15,00,000 and total turnover (F) = ₹60,00,000. ### Q5. Reversal of ITC on Non-payment within 180 days (marks: 4) [Practical]
**Question:** M/s Delta Traders availed ITC of ₹36,000 (CGST ₹18,000 + SGST ₹18,000) on an invoice dated 10.04.20XX for goods worth ₹2,00,000 + GST. As on 07.10.20XX (180 days), the recipient had paid only ₹1,20,000 (part payment) to the supplier. Explain the consequence and compute the ITC to be reversed. State the position when the balance is paid on 15.12.20XX.

**Model Answer:**
**Provision — 2nd & 3rd proviso to Sec 16(2) read with Rule 37:** Where a recipient fails to pay the supplier the **value + tax within 180 days** from date of invoice, an amount equal to ITC availed **proportionate to the unpaid amount** is added to his output tax liability, with interest under Sec 50, for the period from the date of availment till payment.

- Total invoice value (incl. tax) = 2,00,000 + 36,000 = ₹2,36,000
- Amount paid within 180 days = ₹1,20,000 → **Unpaid = ₹1,16,000**
- ITC to reverse = 36,000 × (Unpaid ÷ Total value) = 36,000 × (1,16,000 ÷ 2,36,000)
- = 36,000 × 0.49153 = **₹17,695 (rounded)** → CGST ₹8,847 + SGST ₹8,848

**Add to output tax liability of the tax period in which 180 days expire, with interest.**

**On later payment (15.12.20XX):** Once the recipient pays the balance to the supplier, he is **entitled to re-avail** the reversed ITC. Re-availment on account of Rule 37 is **not subject to the Sec 16(4) time limit** (specifically excluded).

**Key conditions/restrictions:**
1. **Cannot collect tax** from customers and **cannot avail ITC**; must issue a **Bill of Supply**, not a tax invoice.
2. **Cannot make inter-State outward supplies** and cannot supply through e-commerce operators required to collect TCS; cannot supply non-taxable goods.

**> Full-marks tip:** State the ₹1.5 crore eligibility limit, apply the trader rate to goods turnover, and list the "no ITC / no tax collection / no inter-State supply" restrictions.

### Q19. Cancellation of Registration — Section 29 (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the circumstances in which the proper officer may cancel the registration of a taxable person under Section 29(2) of the CGST Act, and mention the consequence relating to stock on cancellation.

**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Section 29(2), CGST Act:** The proper officer may **cancel** the registration of a person (from any date, including a retrospective date) where —

1. A registered person has **contravened prescribed provisions** of the Act/Rules;
2. A **composition taxpayer** has not furnished returns for **three consecutive tax periods**;
3. Any **other registered person** has not furnished returns for a **continuous prescribed period** (as notified);
4. A person who took **voluntary registration** has **not commenced business** within six months from the date of registration;
5. Registration was obtained by means of **fraud, wilful misstatement, or suppression of facts**.

**Opportunity of being heard:** Cancellation cannot be ordered without giving the person a **show-cause notice and a reasonable opportunity of being heard** (proviso to Sec 29(2)).

**Consequence — Sec 29(5):** On cancellation, the person must **pay an amount equal to the ITC on inputs held in stock, inputs in semi-finished/finished goods, and capital goods** on the day immediately preceding the date of cancellation, **or the output tax payable on such goods, whichever is higher** — debited through the electronic cash/credit ledger.

**> Full-marks tip:** Cite Sec 29(2) grounds with the "3 consecutive periods (composition)" detail and, crucially, the Sec 29(5) stock reversal (higher of ITC vs output tax) — the consequence carries a mark.

### Q20. Tax Invoice — Mandatory Particulars (marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State any eight particulars that a tax invoice must contain under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017.

**Model Answer:**
**Provision — Rule 46, CGST Rules, 2017:** A tax invoice issued under Section 31 must contain, inter alia, the following particulars:

1. **Name, address and GSTIN** of the supplier.
2. A **consecutive serial number** (unique for a financial year), not exceeding 16 characters.
3. **Date of issue** of the invoice.
4. Name, address and **GSTIN/UIN of the recipient** (if registered).
5. Where recipient is **unregistered and value ≥ ₹50,000** — name, address of recipient and address of delivery with State name and code.
6. **HSN code** of goods / SAC of services (as prescribed).
7. **Description of goods or services**, quantity (with unit) and total value.
8. **Taxable value** of supply (after adjusting discount).
9. **Rate and amount of tax** (CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST, cess separately).
10. **Place of supply** with State name, in case of inter-State supply.
11. Whether tax is payable on **reverse charge** basis.
12. **Signature / digital signature** of the supplier or authorised representative.

**Conclusion:** Any eight of the above validly stated satisfy the requirement of Rule 46.

**> Full-marks tip:** Include GSTIN of supplier, consecutive serial number, HSN/SAC, taxable value, and rate/amount of tax split head-wise — these are the "must-have" particulars examiners look for.
