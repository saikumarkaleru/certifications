### Q1. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Definition of "Person" u/s 2(31) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** "Every assessee is a person, but every person need not be an assessee." Discuss. Also enumerate the seven categories of "person" as defined under section 2(31) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, giving one example of each.

**Answer:**
The term **"person"** is defined in an inclusive manner under **section 2(31)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Income-tax is charged in respect of the total income of a "person"; hence the definition is the foundation of the charge of tax under **section 4**. The section provides that "person" **includes** the following **seven categories**:

| No. | Category [Sec 2(31)] | Example |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | An **Individual** | Mr. A, a salaried employee |
| (ii) | A **Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)** | The joint family of Mr. X and his sons |
| (iii) | A **Company** | Reliance Industries Ltd. |
| (iv) | A **Firm** | A partnership firm M/s AB & Co. (incl. LLP) |
| (v) | An **Association of Persons (AOP)** or **Body of Individuals (BOI)**, whether incorporated or not | A joint venture of two companies |
| (vi) | A **Local authority** | A Municipal Corporation / Panchayat |
| (vii) | Every **artificial juridical person** not falling in the above | A university, a deity, an idol |

The word **"includes"** makes the definition **extensive and not exhaustive**, so the list is illustrative of the widest coverage.

Now, an **"assessee"** is defined under **section 2(7)** as a *person by whom any tax or any other sum of money (interest, penalty, etc.) is payable* under the Act, and it also covers a person against whom proceedings have been initiated, a deemed assessee, and an assessee in default. Thus, a person becomes an **assessee only when a liability or proceeding arises** in respect of him.

Therefore, while **every assessee must necessarily be a "person"** (since tax is levied on a person), **every person need not be an assessee** — for instance, an individual whose total income is below the basic exemption limit and against whom no proceeding is pending is a "person" but not an "assessee".

**Conclusion:** All seven categories under section 2(31) are "persons", but a person acquires the status of an "assessee" under section 2(7) only when a tax/other liability or a proceeding under the Act attaches to him.

*(Full-marks tip: Examiner rewards quoting Sec 2(31) with all seven heads AND linking to Sec 2(7); the common deduction is missing that the definition is inclusive or omitting AOP/BOI.)*

### Q2. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Previous Year & Assessment Year u/s 3 & 2(9) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the terms "Previous Year" and "Assessment Year" as per the Income-tax Act, 1961. State the general rule regarding the year in which income is taxable and mention one exception to it.

**Answer:**
**Assessment Year [Section 2(9)]:** Assessment year means the period of **twelve months commencing on the 1st day of April every year** and ending on 31st March of the next year. It is the year in which the income of the previous year is assessed to tax. For example, the assessment year 2025-26 begins on 01.04.2025 and ends on 31.03.2026.

**Previous Year [Section 3]:** Previous year means the **financial year immediately preceding the assessment year**. It is the year in which the income is **earned**. In the case of a **newly set-up business/profession or a newly created source of income**, the previous year begins from the date of setting up of the business/profession (or the date the source comes into existence) and ends on the immediately following 31st March. Hence the first previous year may be a period of **less than twelve months**.

**General Rule:** As per section 4, income earned in a previous year is charged to tax in the **immediately following assessment year**. Thus, income of P.Y. 2024-25 is normally taxed in A.Y. 2025-26.

**Exception — income taxed in the same year in which it is earned:** In certain cases, to protect revenue, income is assessed in the **previous year itself**. One such case is **section 174 — assessment of persons leaving India** with no intention of returning; other cases include shipping business of non-residents (Sec 172), AOP/BOI likely to be dissolved (Sec 174A), persons transferring property to avoid tax (Sec 175), and discontinued business (Sec 176).

**Conclusion:** Previous year is the year of earning [Sec 3] and assessment year is the year of taxation [Sec 2(9)]; ordinarily income of a previous year is taxed in the next assessment year, subject to the exceptions in Sections 172–176.

*(Full-marks tip: State both definitions with section numbers and give at least one exception with its section — omitting the "newly set-up business" rule or the exception loses marks.)*

### Q3. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Capital vs Revenue Receipt (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between a "Capital Receipt" and a "Revenue Receipt". Why is the distinction important under the Income-tax Act, 1961? Give two examples of each.

**Answer:**
The Income-tax Act, 1961 does not define the terms "capital receipt" and "revenue receipt"; the distinction has been evolved through judicial pronouncements. The distinction is **crucial** because, as a **general rule, all revenue receipts are taxable** unless specifically exempt, whereas **capital receipts are not taxable** unless they are specifically brought to charge (for example, capital gains under section 45). The nature of a receipt is determined at the moment of its receipt and does not change afterwards.

The distinction may be drawn as follows:

| Basis | Capital Receipt | Revenue Receipt |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature of source** | Arises from a **fixed asset / source of income itself** | Arises in the ordinary course from a **circulating asset / the earning process** |
| **Analogy** | Sale of the "tree" | Sale of the "fruit" of the tree |
| **Recurrence** | Generally **non-recurring** | Generally **recurring** |
| **Taxability** | **Not taxable** unless specifically charged (e.g., Sec 45 capital gains) | **Taxable** unless specifically exempt |
| **Examples** | (a) Sale proceeds of a machine; (b) Compensation for loss of a source of income / on termination of an agency | (a) Sale of stock-in-trade; (b) Interest, rent, salary, professional fees |

A useful test is: an amount received in **substitution of a source of income** is capital, whereas an amount received in **substitution of income** is revenue. Similarly, compensation for **sterilisation of a profit-earning asset** is capital, but compensation for **loss of profits** is revenue.

**Conclusion:** Since revenue receipts are chargeable while capital receipts are outside the charge (except where the Act specifically taxes them, e.g., capital gains u/s 45), correct classification of a receipt as capital or revenue directly determines its taxability.

*(Full-marks tip: The "tree vs fruit" test plus the rule that capital receipts are taxable only if specifically charged (Sec 45) earns the marks; giving only examples without the reason for the distinction loses half.)*

### Q4. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Gross Total Income vs Total Income (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the difference between "Gross Total Income" and "Total Income" under the Income-tax Act, 1961. Also list the five heads of income under section 14.

**Answer:**
Under the scheme of the Act, income is first computed under five heads and then aggregated to arrive at Gross Total Income, from which deductions are allowed to reach Total Income on which tax is levied.

**Five Heads of Income [Section 14]:** The Act classifies all income, for the purposes of charge and computation, under the following **five heads**:
1. **Salaries** (Sections 15–17);
2. **Income from House Property** (Sections 22–27);
3. **Profits and Gains of Business or Profession** (Sections 28–44);
4. **Capital Gains** (Sections 45–55A); and
5. **Income from Other Sources** (Sections 56–59).

**Gross Total Income (GTI) [Section 80B(5)]:** GTI means the aggregate of income computed under the above five heads **after giving effect to the provisions for clubbing of income (Sec 60–64) and set-off/carry-forward of losses (Sec 70–80)**, but **before allowing deductions under Chapter VI-A** (Sections 80C to 80U).

**Total Income (TI) [Section 2(45)]:** Total income means the total amount of income computed **in the manner laid down in the Act**, i.e., the amount arrived at after **deducting from the GTI the permissible deductions under Chapter VI-A** (Sections 80C to 80U). Tax is charged on the **total income**, and it is rounded off to the nearest ₹10 under section 288A.

In short: **GTI − Deductions under Chapter VI-A (80C to 80U) = Total Income.**

**Conclusion:** GTI is the aggregate of the five heads' income before Chapter VI-A deductions [Sec 80B(5)], whereas Total Income is GTI as reduced by those deductions [Sec 2(45)] and is the base for computing tax.

*(Full-marks tip: The single equation GTI − Chapter VI-A = TI, with sections 80B(5) and 2(45), is what the examiner looks for; forgetting that clubbing and set-off are done before GTI loses a mark.)*

### Q5. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Agricultural Income u/s 2(1A) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Define "Agricultural Income" as per section 2(1A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. State its tax treatment and, with reasons, whether the following are agricultural income: (i) rent received for agricultural land, (ii) dividend from a company engaged in agriculture, (iii) income from the sale of self-grown grass.

**Answer:**
**Definition [Section 2(1A)]:** Agricultural income means —
(a) any **rent or revenue derived from land** which is situated in India and is used for agricultural purposes;
(b) any **income derived from such land by agricultural operations**, including processing of the produce to render it fit for the market and sale of such produce; and
(c) any **income from a farm building** required for agricultural operations, subject to prescribed conditions.

For income to be agricultural, there must be a **basic operation on the land** involving expenditure of human skill and labour (tilling, sowing, planting), as held in **CIT v. Raja Benoy Kumar Sahas Roy**.

**Tax Treatment:** Agricultural income is **exempt from tax under section 10(1)**. However, where the assessee (individual/HUF/AOP, etc.) has **non-agricultural income exceeding the basic exemption limit and net agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000**, the agricultural income is **aggregated for the limited purpose of determining the rate of tax** on non-agricultural income — this is the scheme of **partial integration**.

**Analysis of the given items:**

| Item | Agricultural Income? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Rent for agricultural land | **Yes** | Rent/revenue derived from land used for agriculture — clause (a) of Sec 2(1A) |
| (ii) Dividend from a company doing agriculture | **No** | Dividend is not "derived from land"; the source is shares. It is taxable as Income from Other Sources |
| (iii) Sale of self-grown / spontaneously grown grass | **No** | No basic agricultural operation (no cultivation); hence not agricultural income (Benoy Kumar) — it is taxable |

**Conclusion:** Agricultural income as defined in section 2(1A) is exempt u/s 10(1) but may be partially integrated to fix the rate of tax; of the items given, only the rent for agricultural land qualifies.

*(Full-marks tip: Quote all three limbs of Sec 2(1A) + Sec 10(1) exemption + Benoy Kumar "basic operation" test; the dividend/grass traps are the graded items and must be reasoned, not just answered.)*

### Q6. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Partial Integration of Agricultural Income (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Rakesh, aged 45 years, a resident individual, furnishes the following particulars for the previous year 2024-25. Compute his tax liability for the assessment year 2025-26 under the **old (normal) tax regime**.

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Non-agricultural income (Total Income) | 9,00,000 |
| Net agricultural income | 1,00,000 |

**Solution:**
Since Mr. Rakesh has **net agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000** (₹1,00,000) and **non-agricultural income exceeding the basic exemption limit** of ₹2,50,000, the scheme of **partial integration** applies. Agricultural income, though exempt u/s 10(1), is aggregated only to determine the **rate** of tax.

**WN-1: Tax on (Non-agricultural income + Net agricultural income) — Step 1**
Aggregate = ₹9,00,000 + ₹1,00,000 = **₹10,00,000**

| Slab (old regime) | Rate | Tax (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,50,000 | Nil | 0 |
| 2,50,001 – 5,00,000 | 5% | 12,500 |
| 5,00,001 – 10,00,000 | 20% | 1,00,000 |
| **Tax on ₹10,00,000** | | **1,12,500** |

**WN-2: Tax on (Basic exemption limit + Net agricultural income) — Step 2**
Aggregate = ₹2,50,000 + ₹1,00,000 = **₹3,50,000**

| Slab | Rate | Tax (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,50,000 | Nil | 0 |
| 2,50,001 – 3,50,000 | 5% | 5,000 |
| **Tax on ₹3,50,000** | | **5,000** |

**WN-3: Tax before cess (Step 1 − Step 2)**
= ₹1,12,500 − ₹5,000 = **₹1,07,500**

Rebate u/s 87A is **not available** as total income (₹9,00,000) exceeds ₹5,00,000.

**Statement Showing Computation of Tax Liability of Mr. Rakesh (A.Y. 2025-26)**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Tax on aggregate of non-agri + agri income (₹10,00,000) — WN-1 | 1,12,500 |
| Less: Tax on aggregate of basic exemption + agri income (₹3,50,000) — WN-2 | (5,000) |
| Tax before cess | 1,07,500 |
| Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% | 4,300 |
| **Total Tax Liability (rounded off u/s 288B)** | **1,11,800** |

**Answer:** Tax liability of Mr. Rakesh for A.Y. 2025-26 under the old regime is **₹1,11,800**.

*(Full-marks tip: The two-step partial-integration format and adding agricultural income to the basic exemption limit in Step 2 is the crux; students lose marks by taxing agricultural income directly or skipping the Step-2 deduction.)*

### Q7. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Rebate u/s 87A (Marks: 4) [Problem]
**Question:** Compute the tax liability of Ms. Anita, a resident individual aged 30 years, for A.Y. 2025-26 under the **old regime** in each of the following independent cases:

| Case | Total Income (₹) |
|---|---|
| (a) | 4,50,000 |
| (b) | 5,00,000 |

**Solution:**
Under section 87A, a **resident individual** whose total income does **not exceed ₹5,00,000** is entitled to a rebate equal to **100% of income-tax or ₹12,500, whichever is less** (old regime limits for A.Y. 2025-26).

**WN-1: Case (a) — Total Income ₹4,50,000**
Tax on ₹4,50,000 = 5% × (4,50,000 − 2,50,000) = 5% × 2,00,000 = **₹10,000**
Rebate u/s 87A = lower of ₹10,000 or ₹12,500 = **₹10,000**
Tax after rebate = ₹10,000 − ₹10,000 = **Nil**; Cess = Nil.

**WN-2: Case (b) — Total Income ₹5,00,000**
Tax on ₹5,00,000 = 5% × (5,00,000 − 2,50,000) = 5% × 2,50,000 = **₹12,500**
Rebate u/s 87A = lower of ₹12,500 or ₹12,500 = **₹12,500** (income does not *exceed* ₹5,00,000, so rebate is available)
Tax after rebate = ₹12,500 − ₹12,500 = **Nil**; Cess = Nil.

**Statement Showing Tax Liability (A.Y. 2025-26)**

| Particulars | Case (a) ₹ | Case (b) ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Tax on total income | 10,000 | 12,500 |
| Less: Rebate u/s 87A | (10,000) | (12,500) |
| Tax after rebate | Nil | Nil |
| Add: HEC @ 4% | Nil | Nil |
| **Total Tax Liability** | **Nil** | **Nil** |

**Answer:** The tax liability of Ms. Anita is **Nil** in both cases, as the rebate u/s 87A fully absorbs the tax up to a total income of ₹5,00,000 under the old regime.

*(Full-marks tip: The rebate is available up to and including ₹5,00,000 (not *exceeding*) — note that even ₹1 more removes the rebate entirely; state the "whichever is less of tax or ₹12,500" rule explicitly.)*

### Q8. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Marginal Relief / Surcharge concept (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Mr. Suresh, a resident individual aged 40, has a total income of ₹52,00,000 for A.Y. 2025-26 (old regime). Compute his tax liability and show the working of **marginal relief on surcharge**.

**Solution:**
Surcharge @ **10%** is leviable where total income exceeds **₹50,00,000** but does not exceed ₹1 crore. **Marginal relief** ensures the additional tax (including surcharge) on income above ₹50 lakh does not exceed the income above ₹50 lakh.

**WN-1: Income-tax on ₹52,00,000 (old regime slabs)**
= 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 30% × (52,00,000 − 10,00,000)
= 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 30% × 42,00,000
= 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 12,60,000 = **₹13,72,500**

**WN-2: Surcharge @ 10%** = 10% × ₹13,72,500 = **₹1,37,250**
Tax + surcharge = 13,72,500 + 1,37,250 = **₹15,09,750**

**WN-3: Marginal Relief**
Tax on ₹50,00,000 (no surcharge) = 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 30% × (50,00,000 − 10,00,000) = 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 12,00,000 = **₹13,12,500**
Income above ₹50 lakh = 52,00,000 − 50,00,000 = ₹2,00,000
Maximum permissible (tax on ₹50L + excess income) = 13,12,500 + 2,00,000 = **₹15,12,500**
Since tax+surcharge (₹15,09,750) < ₹15,12,500, **no marginal relief is required** (relief = Nil).

**Statement Showing Computation of Tax Liability of Mr. Suresh (A.Y. 2025-26)**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Income-tax (WN-1) | 13,72,500 |
| Add: Surcharge @ 10% (WN-2) | 1,37,250 |
| Less: Marginal relief (WN-3) | Nil |
| Tax + surcharge | 15,09,750 |
| Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% | 60,390 |
| **Total Tax Liability (rounded off u/s 288B)** | **15,70,140** |

**Answer:** Tax liability of Mr. Suresh for A.Y. 2025-26 is **₹15,70,140** (no marginal relief being required).

*(Full-marks tip: Always test marginal relief by comparing "tax+surcharge" with "tax at ₹50L + excess income"; cess is added after surcharge and marginal relief. Adding cess before surcharge is a common error.)*

### Q9. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Income u/s 2(24) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** "Income" under section 2(24) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 is an inclusive definition. Explain and state any four important characteristics of income.

**Answer:**
**Definition [Section 2(24)]:** The Act does not give an exhaustive definition of "income"; section 2(24) gives an **inclusive definition** which enumerates specific items treated as income — such as profits and gains, dividend, voluntary contributions received by a trust, the value of perquisites, any capital gains chargeable u/s 45, winnings from lotteries/crossword puzzles/races/card games, any sum received under a Keyman insurance policy, and gifts taxable u/s 56(2)(x), among others. Because the definition uses the word **"includes"**, its scope is **very wide** and covers receipts of an income nature even if not expressly listed.

**Four important characteristics of income:**
1. **Regular or definite source:** The concept of income is linked to a source, though income may be received regularly or as a one-time receipt.
2. **Cash or kind:** Income may be received in cash or in kind; where in kind, it is valued as per the rules (e.g., perquisites).
3. **Legal or illegal:** Income is taxable irrespective of whether it is earned legally or illegally; the Act does not distinguish tainted income from clean income.
4. **Receipt vs accrual:** Income may be taxed on **receipt or accrual** basis depending on the method of accounting and the head of income; the same income cannot be taxed twice.

Other well-settled propositions include: income must come from **outside** (a person cannot earn income from himself, e.g., mutuality), a receipt on **revenue account** is generally income, and **temporary or permanent** and **lump-sum or instalments** — the form of receipt does not alter its character as income.

**Conclusion:** Section 2(24) being an inclusive definition, "income" is understood in its widest sense and covers every item specified therein as well as any receipt possessing the essential character of income.

*(Full-marks tip: Emphasise "inclusive" definition + at least four characteristics (illegal income taxable, cash/kind, mutuality, receipt/accrual); merely listing 2(24) items without characteristics loses marks.)*

### Q10. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Diversion vs Application of Income (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between "Diversion of Income by an overriding title" and "Application of Income". Why is the distinction relevant for computing total income?

**Answer:**
The distinction between diversion and application of income is important because **income which is diverted before it reaches the assessee is not taxable** in his hands, whereas **income which reaches the assessee and is thereafter applied is fully taxable**, the application being merely the manner of spending his own income.

**Diversion of income by overriding title** occurs where, **by virtue of an obligation, income is diverted before it accrues or arises to the assessee**, so that it never becomes his income at all. The source itself is charged with the obligation (e.g., a decree of court, statutory charge, or partition), and the assessee receives only the balance. Such diverted income is **excluded** from his total income (*CIT v. Sitaldas Tirathdas*).

**Application of income** occurs where the income **first accrues/arises to the assessee** and he **thereafter applies (spends) it** to discharge an obligation. Here the obligation is met **after** the income has reached him; it is only a mode of applying his own income and is therefore **not deductible** from total income.

| Basis | Diversion of Income | Application of Income |
|---|---|---|
| Point of obligation | Obligation attaches to the **source** before income accrues | Obligation discharged **after** income accrues |
| Whose income | Never the assessee's income | Assessee's own income |
| Overriding title | A **third party has an overriding title** to the income | No overriding title; assessee is the owner |
| Taxability | **Not taxable** in assessee's hands | **Fully taxable**; not deductible |
| Example | Maintenance charged on property under a court decree | Assessee paying maintenance out of his salary voluntarily |

The **test** laid down in *CIT v. Sitaldas Tirathdas (1961)* is: *"Where by an obligation income is diverted before it reaches the assessee, it is deductible; but where the income is required to be applied to discharge an obligation after it has reached the assessee, it is merely an application and is taxable."*

**Conclusion:** Diverted income (by overriding title) is excluded from total income, whereas applied income is included; the point at which the obligation bites — before or after accrual — decides the treatment.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite Sitaldas Tirathdas and the "before vs after it reaches the assessee" test; a comparative table plus one example each secures full marks.)*

### Q11. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Assessee, Deemed Assessee & Assessee-in-default u/s 2(7) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Define "Assessee" under section 2(7) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 and explain the terms "Deemed Assessee" and "Assessee-in-default".

**Answer:**
**Assessee [Section 2(7)]:** "Assessee" means **a person by whom any tax or any other sum of money** (such as interest, penalty or fine) **is payable** under the Act. The definition further **includes**:
(a) **every person in respect of whom any proceeding** under the Act has been taken for the assessment of his income (or of the income of any other person in respect of which he is assessable), or of the loss sustained by him or such other person, or of the amount of refund due to him or such other person;
(b) every person who is **deemed to be an assessee** under any provision of the Act; and
(c) every person who is **deemed to be an assessee-in-default** under any provision of the Act.

**Deemed Assessee:** A deemed assessee is a person who is **treated as an assessee in respect of the income of some other person** for whom he is legally responsible. Examples include the **legal representative** of a deceased person (Sec 159), the **agent of a non-resident** (Sec 160/163), the **guardian/manager** of a minor or a lunatic, and the **executor** of the estate of a deceased person. Such a person, though the income is not primarily his own, is responsible for its assessment and payment of tax.

**Assessee-in-default:** A person is said to be an **assessee-in-default** when he **fails to fulfil a statutory obligation** cast upon him. For example, an employer who deducts tax at source (TDS) from an employee's salary but **fails to deposit it** with the Government is treated as an assessee-in-default under section 201; similarly, an assessee who fails to pay **advance tax / self-assessment tax** is deemed to be in default.

**Conclusion:** Section 2(7) defines "assessee" widely to cover not only the person liable to pay tax but also those against whom proceedings are taken, deemed assessees, and assessees-in-default.

*(Full-marks tip: Reproduce all three limbs of Sec 2(7) and give concrete examples (legal representative for deemed; employer failing to deposit TDS for default). Missing the "proceeding taken" limb costs a mark.)*

### Q12. Ch: Basic Concepts of Income-tax — Capital vs Revenue Expenditure (application) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State, with reasons, whether each of the following is a **capital** or **revenue** expenditure/receipt for income-tax purposes:
(i) ₹2,00,000 spent on replacing the petrol engine of a car with a diesel engine;
(ii) ₹50,000 paid as compensation to a retrenched employee;
(iii) ₹1,00,000 received as compensation for cancellation of a contract of agency, being one of many agencies;
(iv) ₹75,000 spent on advertisement to launch a new product;
(v) Premium received on issue of shares.

**Answer:**
The distinction between capital and revenue turns on whether the outlay/receipt is on the **fixed framework (capital)** or arises in the **ordinary course of earning (revenue)**. Applying the settled tests:

| No. | Item | Nature | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) | Replacing petrol engine with diesel engine | **Capital expenditure** | It brings an **enduring advantage/improvement** to a fixed asset and is not a mere restoration to the original state — an **enduring benefit** test (*Atherton*) |
| (ii) | Compensation to a retrenched employee | **Revenue expenditure** | Incurred for the **smooth carrying on of business / commercial expediency**; laid out wholly for business |
| (iii) | Compensation for cancellation of **one of many** agency contracts | **Revenue receipt** | Where the agency is one of many and cancellation is in the **ordinary course**, it does not affect the profit-making apparatus — hence revenue (*Kettlewell Bullen & Co.*) |
| (iv) | Advertisement to launch a new product | **Revenue expenditure** | It is part of the **ongoing selling cost**; it does not create a capital asset even though benefit may extend to future years |
| (v) | Premium on issue of shares | **Capital receipt** | It relates to the **capital structure** of the company; not received in the course of trading |

The **guiding principle** is the "**enduring benefit**" test in *Atherton v. British Insulated & Helsby Cables Ltd.* — expenditure made once and for all to bring into existence an asset of enduring benefit is capital; and for receipts, compensation touching the **profit-making structure** is capital while compensation for **loss of ordinary trading profits/one of many contracts** is revenue.

**Conclusion:** Items (i) and (v) are capital in nature; items (ii), (iii) and (iv) are revenue in nature.

### Q16. Ch: Residential Status & Scope of Total Income — Basic conditions of residence for an individual [Sec 6(1)] (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the "basic conditions" laid down under section 6(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 for determining whether an individual is resident in India for a previous year. Also state the exceptions where only the first basic condition applies.

**Answer:**
The residential status of an individual for a previous year is determined under **section 6(1)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961 with reference to his physical presence (stay) in India during the relevant previous year and the preceding years. An individual is said to be **resident** in India in any previous year if he satisfies **at least one** of the following two **basic conditions**:

**(i) Basic Condition 1:** He is in India for a period of **182 days or more** during the relevant previous year; **OR**

**(ii) Basic Condition 2:** He is in India for a period of **60 days or more** during the relevant previous year **and** for **365 days or more** during the **four years immediately preceding** the relevant previous year.

If the individual satisfies **either** of the above, he is a **resident**; if he satisfies **neither**, he is a **non-resident** for that previous year.

**Exceptions (only Basic Condition 1 of 182 days applies):** In the following cases, the period of "60 days" in the second basic condition is substituted by "182 days", i.e. the individual becomes resident only if he stays 182 days or more:

| Situation | Applicable condition |
|---|---|
| An Indian citizen who leaves India during the previous year for the purpose of **employment outside India**, or as a **member of the crew of an Indian ship** | Only 182 days |
| An Indian citizen or a **person of Indian origin (PIO)** who, being outside India, **comes on a visit to India** during the previous year | Only 182 days (extended to **120 days** if his total income, other than foreign-source income, exceeds ₹15 lakh) |

**Conclusion:** An individual is resident in India if he satisfies at least one basic condition under section 6(1), subject to the modified 182/120-day rule in the exceptional cases stated above.

### Q18. Ch: Residential Status & Scope of Total Income — Scope of total income [Sec 5] (Marks: 5) [Theory]

### Q20. Ch: Heads of Income — Overview of the five heads [Sec 14] (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Section 14 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 classifies all income under five heads. Enumerate and briefly explain the five heads of income, indicating the charging section of each.

**Answer:**
As per **section 14** of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for the purposes of charge of income-tax and computation of total income, **all income is classified under the following five heads of income**. Income is computed head-wise, and the aggregate (after set-off) forms the Gross Total Income.

| Head of income | Charging section | Nature of income covered |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Salaries** | Sec 15–17 | Income from an **employer-employee relationship** — salary, wages, pension, allowances, perquisites and profits in lieu of salary. |
| **B. Income from House Property** | Sec 22–27 | Annual value of **buildings or lands appurtenant** thereto of which the assessee is the **owner**, not used for his own business/profession. |
| **C. Profits and Gains of Business or Profession** | Sec 28–44 | Income from any **business, trade, commerce, manufacture or profession** carried on by the assessee. |
| **D. Capital Gains** | Sec 45–55 | Gains arising from the **transfer of a capital asset** during the previous year. |
| **E. Income from Other Sources** | Sec 56–59 | **Residuary head** — income not chargeable under the other four heads, e.g. interest, dividend, casual income, gifts. |

### Q22. Ch: GST Basics — Need for GST / deficiencies of the earlier indirect-tax structure (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** "GST was introduced to remove the deficiencies of the earlier indirect-tax regime." Explain the **need for GST** in India by describing the major shortcomings of the pre-GST indirect-tax structure that GST sought to overcome.

**Answer:**
**Goods and Services Tax (GST)** is a **destination-based, comprehensive indirect tax** levied on the **supply of goods and services**, introduced in India with effect from **1st July 2017**. It subsumed a large number of Central and State indirect taxes. The **need for GST** arose from the following **deficiencies of the earlier indirect-tax regime**:

**1. Cascading effect of taxes (tax on tax):** Under the old system, credit of Central taxes (e.g. excise, service tax) could not be set off against State taxes (e.g. VAT) and vice-versa. This break in the input-tax-credit chain caused **"tax on tax"**, increasing the cost of goods and services.

**2. Multiplicity of taxes:** A large number of Central levies (Central Excise, Service Tax, CST, additional customs duties) and State levies (VAT, Entry Tax, Luxury Tax, Entertainment Tax, Octroi) existed simultaneously, causing **complexity and high compliance cost**.

**3. Non-integration of goods and services:** Goods and services were taxed **separately** (excise/VAT on goods, service tax on services), and their bundling in modern transactions created **classification disputes and double taxation**.

**4. Different taxable events:** Manufacture (excise), sale (VAT/CST) and provision of service (service tax) were **different taxable events**, causing overlap and litigation. GST replaced them with a **single taxable event — "supply"**.

**5. Barriers to a common national market:** State-level taxes and check-posts (Entry Tax, Octroi) fragmented the country, obstructing the free flow of goods.

GST addressed these by providing a **seamless flow of input tax credit** across the supply chain and across Centre and State, one taxable event ("supply"), and a **uniform "One Nation, One Tax, One Market"** structure.

**Conclusion:** GST was needed to eliminate the cascading effect, reduce the multiplicity of taxes, integrate goods and services under a single levy, and create a unified national market.

*(Full-marks tip: Anchor every point to a specific old defect (cascading, multiplicity, split goods/services) and end with "seamless ITC / one nation one tax" — vague statements without the cascading concept lose marks.)*

### Q23. Ch: GST Basics — Dual GST model; CGST, SGST, IGST and UTGST (Marks: 6) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the **dual GST model** adopted in India. In this context, distinguish between **CGST, SGST, UTGST and IGST**, stating the statute under which each is levied and the authority that collects it.

**Answer:**
India has adopted a **dual GST model**, meaning that GST is levied **concurrently** by both the **Centre and the States** on a **common base** (the same supply). This model was chosen because India is a **federal country** where both the Union and the States have the constitutional power to levy tax on the supply of goods and services (Article 246A). Accordingly, on every **intra-State supply**, tax is levied simultaneously by the Centre (CGST) and the State (SGST/UTGST); on every **inter-State supply**, an integrated tax (IGST) is levied by the Centre.

**Components of the dual GST structure:**

| Component | Full form | Levied under | Levied by / accrues to | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CGST** | Central GST | **CGST Act, 2017** | Central Government | **Intra-State** supply (within same State/UT) |
| **SGST** | State GST | Respective **SGST Act, 2017** | State Government | **Intra-State** supply (within a State) |
| **UTGST** | Union Territory GST | **UTGST Act, 2017** | Union Territory (without legislature) | **Intra-State** supply within such a **UT** |
| **IGST** | Integrated GST | **IGST Act, 2017** | Central Govt (later apportioned to States) | **Inter-State** supply and **imports** |

**Explanation:**
- On an **intra-State supply** (supplier and place of supply in the same State), **CGST + SGST** are charged; in a Union Territory without legislature, **CGST + UTGST** apply.
- On an **inter-State supply** (supplier and place of supply in different States), or on **import** of goods/services, **IGST** is levied, which is broadly **equal to CGST + SGST** combined.
- IGST is collected by the Centre and later **apportioned** between the Centre and the destination State, since GST is a **destination-based** tax.

**Conclusion:** The dual GST model levies CGST and SGST/UTGST together on intra-State supplies and IGST on inter-State supplies, ensuring both the Centre and the States share the tax on the same supply.

*(Full-marks tip: Correctly linking CGST+SGST to intra-State and IGST to inter-State/imports, plus naming each Act, secures full marks; confusing UTGST with SGST is the frequent error.)*

### Q25. Ch: GST Basics — Taxable event "Supply" [Sec 7 of CGST Act] (Marks: 6) [Theory]
**Question:** "Supply is the taxable event under GST." Explain the meaning and scope of the term **"supply"** as defined under **section 7 of the CGST Act, 2017**.

**Answer:**
Under the GST law, the **taxable event is "supply"** — GST is levied on the **supply** of goods or services or both. The scope of "supply" is defined in an **inclusive** manner under **section 7 of the CGST Act, 2017**. The expression "supply" **includes** the following:

**1. Section 7(1)(a) — Supply for consideration in the course/furtherance of business:** All forms of supply of goods or services such as **sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease or disposal** made or agreed to be made **for a consideration** by a person **in the course or furtherance of business**.

**2. Section 7(1)(b) — Import of services:** Import of services **for a consideration**, whether or not in the course or furtherance of business. (Business condition is not required here.)

**3. Section 7(1)(c) — Schedule I activities without consideration:** Activities specified in **Schedule I**, made or agreed to be made **without a consideration** (e.g. permanent transfer of business assets on which ITC was availed, supply between related/distinct persons in the course of business, etc.).

**4. Section 7(1A) & Schedule II:** Where an activity constitutes a supply, **Schedule II** is used to classify whether it is a supply of **goods** or of **services** (e.g. works contract and restaurant service are treated as supply of services).

**5. Section 7(2) — Negative list (Schedule III):** Certain activities are treated as **neither supply of goods nor services**, e.g. **services by an employee to employer** in the course of employment, functions performed by MPs/MLAs, sale of land, and (subject to conditions) sale of a completed building.

**Table — Essential ingredients of a taxable supply [Sec 7(1)(a)]:**

| Ingredient | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Goods or services | There must be a supply of goods or services or both |
| Consideration | Generally for a consideration (except Schedule I) |
| Business | In the course or furtherance of business (except import of service) |
| Taxable person | By a supplier who is/liable to be registered |

**Conclusion:** "Supply" under section 7 is the single taxable event under GST, covering supplies for consideration in the course of business, import of services, and specified supplies without consideration, while excluding Schedule III activities.

*(Full-marks tip: Cover all limbs — 7(1)(a), 7(1)(b), Schedule I, Schedule II classification, and Schedule III exclusions; forgetting "consideration + furtherance of business" or Schedule III costs marks.)*

### Q26. Ch: GST Basics — Composition levy [Sec 10 of CGST Act] (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Write a short note on the **Composition Levy** under **section 10 of the CGST Act, 2017**. State the eligibility threshold, the applicable rates, and any two conditions/restrictions attached to the scheme.

**Answer:**
The **composition levy** under **section 10 of the CGST Act, 2017** is a **simplified, optional scheme** for **small taxpayers**, under which a registered person may pay tax at a **fixed low rate on turnover** instead of the normal GST, with **minimal compliance** (quarterly payment and annual return). It is designed to reduce the compliance burden on small businesses.

**Eligibility (turnover threshold):** A registered person whose **aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year did not exceed ₹1.5 crore** (₹75 lakh for specified special-category States) may opt for the scheme. | Category of registered person | Rate (CGST + SGST) |
|---|---|
| **Manufacturers** (other than notified goods) | **1%** (0.5% + 0.5%) of turnover in the State |
| **Traders** (other suppliers of goods) | **1%** (0.5% + 0.5%) of taxable turnover |
| **Restaurant / caterer service** (Sch II para 6(b)) | **5%** (2.5% + 2.5%) of turnover |
| **Other service providers** [Sec 10(2A)] | **6%** (3% + 3%) of turnover |

### Q28. Ch: Residential Status & Scope of Total Income — Residence of HUF, firm and company [Sec 6(2), 6(3)] (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain how the **residential status of a Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)**, a **firm/AOP**, and a **company** is determined under sections 6(2) and 6(3) of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

**Answer:**
The residential status of persons other than individuals is determined with reference to the **place of control and management** of their affairs (and, for companies, the place of effective management), under **sections 6(2) and 6(3)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

**1. Hindu Undivided Family [Sec 6(2)]:** An HUF is **resident** in India if the **control and management** of its affairs is situated **wholly or partly in India** during the previous year. It is **non-resident** only if the control and management is situated **wholly outside India**. Further, a resident HUF is **Resident and Ordinarily Resident** if its **karta** satisfies **both** additional conditions of section 6(6) (resident in 2 out of 10 preceding years and 730 days in 7 preceding years); otherwise it is **RNOR**.

**2. Firm / Association of Persons [Sec 6(2)]:** A firm or AOP is **resident** in India if the control and management of its affairs is situated **wholly or partly in India**; it is **non-resident** only if control and management is **wholly outside India**. (There is no ROR/RNOR distinction for a firm/AOP — it is either resident or non-resident.)

**3. Company [Sec 6(3)]:** A company is **resident** in India in any previous year if —
- (i) it is an **Indian company**; **or**
- (ii) its **place of effective management (POEM)** — the place where **key management and commercial decisions** necessary for the conduct of the business as a whole are, in substance, made — is **in India** during that year.

| Person | Resident if… | Non-resident if… |
|---|---|---|
| HUF / Firm / AOP | Control & management **wholly or partly in India** | Control & management **wholly outside India** |
| Company | Indian company **or** POEM **in India** | Foreign company with POEM **outside India** |

**Conclusion:** An HUF, firm or AOP is resident if any part of its control and management is in India, while a company is resident if it is an Indian company or its place of effective management is in India.

*(Full-marks tip: For companies write "POEM", not the old "control and management wholly in India" test; and note that only HUF (via karta) has the ROR/RNOR sub-classification, not firms.)*

### Q29. Ch: Residential Status & Scope of Total Income — Additional conditions for ROR / RNOR [Sec 6(6)] (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** When is a resident individual regarded as **"Not Ordinarily Resident"** in India? Explain the additional conditions under **section 6(6)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Also mention the special "deemed resident" rule under section 6(1A).

**Answer:**
Once an individual is found to be **resident** in India (by satisfying a basic condition under section 6(1)), a further test under **section 6(6)** decides whether he is **Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)** or **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**.

**Additional conditions [Sec 6(6)] — a resident individual is ROR only if he satisfies BOTH:**
1. He has been **resident in India in at least 2 out of the 10** previous years immediately preceding the relevant previous year; **and**
2. He has been in India for a period of **730 days or more** during the **7** previous years immediately preceding the relevant previous year.

**When RNOR:** If the individual is a resident but **fails to satisfy even one** of the above two additional conditions, he is treated as **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**.

**Other RNOR cases as per section 6(6):**
- An individual who is a **citizen of India / person of Indian origin** having total income (other than foreign source) **exceeding ₹15 lakh**, whose stay in India is **120 days or more but less than 182 days**, is treated as **RNOR**.
- A **deemed resident** under section 6(1A) is always **RNOR**.

**Deemed resident [Sec 6(1A)]:** An **Indian citizen** whose **total income (other than foreign-source income) exceeds ₹15 lakh** during the previous year shall be **deemed to be resident** in India if he is **not liable to tax in any other country** by reason of domicile/residence — i.e. a "**stateless**" person is deemed resident, and is classified as **RNOR**.

**Conclusion:** A resident individual is "Not Ordinarily Resident" if he fails either additional condition of section 6(6), and a deemed resident under section 6(1A) is always RNOR.

### Q31. Ch: Basic Concepts — Charge of Income-tax, Previous Year & Assessment Year (Marks: 4) [Theory]

**Answer:**
Under section 3 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, "Previous Year" (PY) means the financial year immediately preceding the assessment year, i.e., the year in which the income is actually earned. | Sec 174 | Income of persons leaving India permanently or for a long period |
| Sec 174A | Income of an AOP/BOI/artificial juridical person formed for a particular event or purpose likely to be dissolved |
| Sec 175 | Income of persons likely to transfer property to avoid tax |
| Sec 176 | Income of a discontinued business or profession |

### Q33. Ch: Basic Concepts — Concept of Income & Diversion vs Application (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between "diversion of income by overriding title" and "application of income" with the help of the tax treatment. Give one example of each.

**Answer:**
The distinction between diversion of income and application of income is a judicially evolved principle used to determine whether an amount is taxable in the hands of the assessee. The test, laid down by the Supreme Court in *CIT v. Sitaldas Tirathdas (1961)*, is whether the income is diverted before it reaches the assessee (by a title superior to that of the assessee) or whether it reaches the assessee first and is then applied to discharge an obligation.

**Diversion of income by overriding title** occurs where, by virtue of an overriding legal obligation (a decree of court, statute, or a charge created on the source), the income is diverted away from the assessee before it accrues or arises to him. Such diverted income never becomes the income of the assessee and is therefore **not taxable** in his hands.

**Application of income** occurs where the income first accrues or arises to the assessee, and thereafter he applies a part of it to discharge an obligation, whether self-imposed or otherwise. Here the income has already become that of the assessee, and its subsequent application is merely the mode of spending; hence the entire income **remains taxable** in his hands.

| Basis | Diversion of income | Application of income |
|---|---|---|
| When obligation attaches | Before income reaches assessee | After income reaches assessee |
| Nature of title | Overriding title exists | No overriding title |
| Taxability | Not taxable | Taxable |
| Example | Amount payable to a widow/coparceners under a decree charged on the estate | Payment of alimony out of income received |

**Conclusion:** Where income is diverted at source by an overriding title it is not taxable; where income is merely applied after being earned, it remains fully taxable in the hands of the assessee.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite *Sitaldas Tirathdas* and give the "before/after it reaches the assessee" test; a bare definition without the test loses marks.)*

### Q44. Ch: Income from House Property — Basis of Charge & Deemed Owner (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the concept of "Annual Value" as the basis of charge under section 22. Also state any four situations in which a person, though not the legal owner, is treated as the "deemed owner" of house property under section 27.

**Answer:**
Under section 22, the annual value of property consisting of any buildings or lands appurtenant thereto, of which the assessee is the owner (other than such portions as are occupied for the purpose of his own business or profession, the profits of which are chargeable to tax), is chargeable to income-tax under the head "Income from house property". **Deemed owners under section 27** — In the following situations, a person is treated as the owner even though he is not the legal/registered owner:

|---|---|

| Sec 27(iii) | A **member of a co-operative society/company/AOP** to whom a building is allotted under a house-building scheme |
| Sec 27(iiia)/(iiib) | A person in possession of property under a **part-performance of contract** (Sec 53A of Transfer of Property Act) / holder of property under a **long-term lease** (12 years or more) |

### Q51. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — Disallowances u/s 40, 40A & 43B (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the disallowance provisions relating to (i) payments to relatives u/s 40A(2), (ii) cash payments u/s 40A(3), and (iii) certain deductions on actual payment basis u/s 43B, under the Income-tax Act, 1961.

**Answer:**
The Income-tax Act restricts certain otherwise-allowable business expenditure to prevent evasion and to enforce timely payment of statutory dues.

**(i) Excessive payments to related parties — Sec 40A(2):** Where an assessee incurs expenditure in respect of which payment is made to a specified relative or related person (e.g., director, partner, relative, a person having substantial interest), and the Assessing Officer is of the opinion that such expenditure is **excessive or unreasonable** having regard to the fair market value of goods/services or the legitimate needs of the business, the excessive portion is **disallowed**. The onus of forming the opinion lies with the AO.

**(ii) Cash payments exceeding the limit — Sec 40A(3):** Where an assessee incurs an expenditure and makes payment or aggregate of payments to a single person **in a day exceeding ₹10,000** (₹35,000 for payments to a transporter/goods carriage operator) **otherwise than by account-payee cheque/draft or prescribed electronic mode**, the whole of such expenditure is **disallowed** (100%). Rule 6DD lists exceptions (e.g., payments to banks, government, on a bank holiday, in villages without banking facility).

**(iii) Deductions allowable only on actual payment — Sec 43B:** Certain expenses are deductible **only in the year of actual payment**, irrespective of the method of accounting, viz.—tax, duty, cess (GST, customs); employer's contribution to PF/gratuity/superannuation fund; bonus/commission to employees; interest on loans from banks/financial institutions/NBFCs; leave encashment; and (from AY 2024-25) sums payable to **micro & small enterprises** beyond the MSMED-Act time limit. A **proviso** allows deduction on accrual basis (except MSME dues) if the amount is actually paid on or before the **due date of filing the return u/s 139(1)**.

| Provision | Nature | Extent of disallowance |
|---|---|---|
| 40A(2) | Excessive payment to relatives | Excess portion |
| 40A(3) | Cash payment > ₹10,000/day | 100% of expenditure |
| 43B | Statutory dues/interest/MSME dues | Until actually paid |

**Conclusion:** Sections 40A(2), 40A(3) and 43B act as anti-avoidance and compliance-enforcing checks, disallowing expenditure that is excessive, made in cash beyond limits, or where statutory dues remain unpaid within the prescribed time.

*(Full-marks tip: State the exact monetary limit (₹10,000 / ₹35,000 transporter) and that 43B's accrual relief is available only if paid before the 139(1) due date — except MSME dues which get no such relief.)*

### Q54. Ch: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession — General deduction u/s 37(1) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions to be satisfied for an expenditure to be allowable as a general deduction u/s 37(1). Also state four items expressly disallowed under the Explanations to Sec 37(1).

**Answer:**
Section 37(1) is a **residuary provision** that allows any expenditure (not being capital or personal expenditure and not covered under Secs 30 to 36) laid out **wholly and exclusively for the purposes of business or profession** in computing business income.

**Conditions for allowability u/s 37(1):**
1. The expenditure must **not** be of the nature described in Sections 30 to 36 (those are specifically dealt with there).
2. It must **not be capital expenditure** — it should be revenue in nature.
3. It must **not be personal expenditure** of the assessee.
4. It must be **wholly and exclusively for the purpose of the business or profession** (not necessarily "necessarily" — commercial expediency suffices).
5. It must have been **incurred during the previous year** and must not be an expense incurred for any purpose which is an **offence or prohibited by law**.

**Items expressly disallowed (Explanations to Sec 37(1)):**
- Any expenditure incurred for a **purpose which is an offence or prohibited by law** (Explanation 1).
- **CSR expenditure** referred to in Sec 135 of the Companies Act, 2013 is deemed not incurred for business (Explanation 2).
- Expenditure to provide any **benefit or perquisite** to a person, where its acceptance violates any law/rule (e.g., freebies to medical practitioners) (Explanation 3).
- Expenditure by way of **compounding of an offence**, or **settlement to avoid proceedings** for contravention of law (Explanation 3 clauses).

**Conclusion:** An expense passes the test of Sec 37(1) only if it is revenue in nature, non-personal, laid out wholly and exclusively for business, and not hit by the Explanations relating to illegal/CSR expenditure.

*(Full-marks tip: Emphasise "wholly and exclusively" (not "necessarily") and cite Explanation 2 on CSR as a specific disallowance the examiner looks for.)*

Sections 54, 54F and 54EC grant exemption from long-term capital gains where the gain/consideration is reinvested in specified assets, subject to conditions.

| Basis | Sec 54 | Sec 54F | Sec 54EC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eligible assessee | Individual / HUF | Individual / HUF | Any assessee |
| Asset transferred | Long-term **residential house** | Any long-term asset **other than a residential house** | Long-term **land or building (or both)** |

| Amount to be invested | The **capital gain** | The **net sale consideration** | The **capital gain** |

### Q62. Ch: Income from Other Sources — Deductions allowable u/s 57 (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Enumerate the deductions allowable u/s 57 while computing "Income from Other Sources", and state any three expenses expressly disallowed u/s 58.

**Answer:**
Section 57 permits the following deductions from income taxable under the head "Income from Other Sources":

1. **Sec 57(i):** In the case of dividend or interest on securities, any **reasonable commission or remuneration** paid to a banker or other person for realising it.
2. **Sec 57(ia):** In respect of employees' contributions to PF/ESI etc. included as income, the amount **credited to the employee's account before the due date** under the relevant Act.

5. **Sec 57(iii):** Any other expenditure (not being capital expenditure) laid out **wholly and exclusively for the purpose of earning** such income.
6. **Sec 57(iv):** In respect of **interest on compensation/enhanced compensation**, a deduction of **50%** of such interest (no other deduction allowed).

**Expenses expressly disallowed u/s 58:**
- Any **personal expenses** of the assessee.
- Interest/salary payable outside India without TDS [Sec 58(1)(a)], and any sum hit by Sec 40A.
- **No deduction whatsoever** is allowed against **winnings from lotteries, crossword puzzles, races, card games, gambling or betting** [Sec 58(4)].

**Conclusion:** Deductions u/s 57 broadly follow the "wholly and exclusively for earning the income" principle, but Sec 58 bars personal expenses and completely disallows any expenditure against casual/gambling incomes.

### Q64. Ch: Clubbing & Set-off — Clubbing provisions (Sec 60–64) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the provisions relating to (i) transfer of income without transfer of asset (Sec 60), (ii) revocable transfer of assets (Sec 61), and (iii) clubbing of income arising to a son's wife (Sec 64) under the Income-tax Act, 1961.

**Answer:**
The clubbing (or "aggregation of income") provisions in Sections 60 to 64 counter attempts to reduce tax liability by diverting income to others while retaining control over the source.

**(i) Transfer of income without transfer of asset — Sec 60:** Where a person transfers the **income** from an asset **without transferring the asset itself** (whether the transfer is revocable or not, and whenever effected), such income is **included in the total income of the transferor**. For example, if a father directs that the rent of his house be paid to his son but retains ownership, the rent is taxed in the father's hands.

**(ii) Revocable transfer of assets — Sec 61:** Where there is a **revocable transfer of an asset**, any income arising from such asset is chargeable in the hands of the **transferor**. A transfer is regarded as revocable if it contains a provision for **re-transfer** of the asset/income, directly or indirectly, to the transferor, or gives the transferor a **right to re-assume power** over the asset/income. However, per **Sec 62**, this does not apply to a transfer by way of trust not revocable during the lifetime of the beneficiary, or to any transfer not revocable during the lifetime of the transferee — provided the transferor derives no direct/indirect benefit and does not re-assume power.

**(iii) Income of son's wife — Sec 64(1)(vi) & (viii):** Income arising, directly or indirectly, to the **son's wife** from assets transferred to her by the individual (or to any person/AOP for her benefit) **without adequate consideration** is clubbed in the hands of the transferor-individual.

| Section | Situation | Clubbed in hands of |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | Income transferred, asset retained | Transferor |
| 61 | Revocable transfer of asset | Transferor |
| 64(1)(vi) | Asset transferred to son's wife w/o consideration | Transferor-individual |

**Conclusion:** Sections 60–64 ensure that income continues to be taxed in the hands of the person who effectively retains ownership or control of the income-earning asset, thereby preventing diversion of income to reduce tax.

*(Full-marks tip: Distinguish Sec 60 (income transferred, asset kept) from Sec 61 (asset transferred but revocably), and quote Sec 62 as the exception where the transfer is irrevocable during the transferee's lifetime.)*

### Q66. Ch: Clubbing & Set-off — Carry-forward provisions (Sec 71B, 72, 73, 74) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the provisions regarding carry-forward and set-off of (i) house property loss, (ii) business loss (non-speculative), (iii) speculation loss, and (iv) capital loss, specifying the number of years for which each may be carried forward and the income against which it may be set off.

**Answer:**
When a loss cannot be fully set off against income of the same year (intra-head u/s 70 and inter-head u/s 71), the unabsorbed loss may be carried forward to subsequent years subject to head-specific rules.

**(i) House property loss — Sec 71B:** Loss under "Income from House Property" (after setting off up to ₹2,00,000 against other heads in the current year) can be **carried forward for 8 assessment years** and set off **only against income from house property** in those years.

**(ii) Business loss (non-speculative) — Sec 72:** Carried forward for **8 assessment years** and set off **only against profits and gains of business or profession** (any business, not necessarily the same one). Continuity of business is not required. It can be carried forward only if the return is filed within the due date u/s 139(1).

**(iii) Speculation loss — Sec 73:** Carried forward for **4 assessment years** and set off **only against income from a speculation business**.

**(iv) Capital loss — Sec 74:** Carried forward for **8 assessment years**. A **short-term capital loss** can be set off against **both STCG and LTCG**; a **long-term capital loss** can be set off **only against LTCG**.

| Loss | Section | C/f period | Set off against |
|---|---|---|---|
| House property | 71B | 8 years | House property income |
| Business (non-speculative) | 72 | 8 years | Business income (any business) |
| Speculation | 73 | 4 years | Speculation income |
| Short-term capital loss | 74 | 8 years | STCG & LTCG |
| Long-term capital loss | 74 | 8 years | LTCG only |

**Conclusion:** Except for house property loss (Sec 71B) and unabsorbed depreciation (which can be carried forward indefinitely), losses may generally be carried forward for a limited number of years and set off only within their respective heads, and business/capital losses require timely filing of the return u/s 139(1) to be carried forward.

### Q76. Ch: Advance Tax, TDS & TCS — Liability to pay advance tax (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** "Every person is liable to pay advance tax." Examine the correctness of this statement with reference to Sections 207 and 208, and state who is exempted from paying advance tax.

**Answer:**
The statement is **not fully correct**. The liability to pay advance tax is governed by Sections 207 and 208 of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

Under **Section 208**, advance tax is payable during a financial year in every case where the amount of tax payable by the assessee for the year is **₹10,000 or more**. Where the estimated tax liability is below ₹10,000, no advance tax is payable and the entire tax is paid by way of self-assessment.

Under **Section 207**, however, a **resident individual** who satisfies both the following conditions is **not liable** to pay advance tax:
(i) he is aged **60 years or more** at any time during the previous year (a resident senior citizen); and
(ii) he does **not have any income chargeable under the head "Profits and gains of business or profession."**

Such a senior citizen may discharge his entire tax by self-assessment without incurring interest under Sections 234B/234C.

The consequences of default are interest under **Section 234B** (for not paying at least 90% of assessed tax as advance tax) and **Section 234C** (for deferment/short payment of the individual instalments), while advance tax itself is payable in four instalments under Section 211 (15%, 45%, 75% and 100% by 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March).

**Conclusion:** Advance tax is payable only where the tax liability is ₹10,000 or more, and a resident senior citizen without business income is wholly exempt from paying it; hence the statement is incorrect.

### Q79. Ch: Return Filing & Assessment — Types of assessment (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain briefly the different types of assessment under the Income-tax Act, 1961.

**Answer:**
"Assessment" is the process of determining the total income and the tax payable by an assessee. The Act provides for the following types of assessment.

**1. Self-assessment (Section 140A):** Before furnishing the return, the assessee himself computes his total income and pays the balance tax (after TDS/TCS/advance tax) together with interest u/s 234A/B/C and fee u/s 234F. The tax so paid is called self-assessment tax.

**2. Summary assessment (Section 143(1)):** After the return is filed, it is processed and the total income/loss is computed after making prescribed adjustments (arithmetical errors, incorrect claims apparent from the return, disallowance of losses/deductions, etc.). An intimation is issued specifying any tax/interest payable or refund due. It must be sent within **nine months** from the end of the financial year in which the return is furnished.

**3. Scrutiny assessment (Section 143(3)):** Where the Assessing Officer considers it necessary to verify the correctness of the return, a notice u/s 143(2) is served, and after hearing the assessee and examining evidence, the AO passes an order determining the total income and tax payable.

**4. Best judgment assessment (Section 144):** Where the assessee fails to file the return, or to comply with notices u/s 142(1)/143(2), or with a special audit direction, the AO assesses the total income to the best of his judgment after giving an opportunity of being heard.

**5. Income escaping assessment (Section 147):** Where any income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment, the AO may, after issuing notice u/s 148, assess or reassess such income.

Assessments under Sections 143(3), 144 and 147 are now generally conducted in a **faceless manner** under Section 144B.

**Conclusion:** The scheme progresses from self-assessment by the taxpayer, to summary processing, and where required to scrutiny, best-judgment or reassessment by the Assessing Officer.

*(Full-marks tip: the examiner rewards citing the correct section against each type and the trigger for Section 144 (non-compliance); common deduction is confusing 143(1) summary processing with 143(3) scrutiny.)*

### Q84. Ch: GST – Concept & Framework — One Nation One Tax, Dual GST & Taxes Subsumed (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** "GST is a destination-based, dual tax on supply of goods and services." In the light of this statement, explain the concept of Dual GST adopted in India, name the components of GST levied on an intra-State and an inter-State supply, and list any four Central and four State levies that stand subsumed in GST.

**Answer:**
GST is a comprehensive, multi-stage, **destination-based tax levied on every supply of goods or services or both**, except the supply of alcoholic liquor for human consumption, under Article 246A of the Constitution read with the CGST Act, 2017, the respective SGST Acts and the IGST Act, 2017.

**Dual GST model.** India has adopted a **Dual GST**, i.e. tax is levied concurrently by the Centre and the States on a common base. This flows from the federal structure of the Constitution where both the Union and the States have the power to levy and collect tax. Accordingly, on the **same taxable supply**, both the Centre and the State/Union Territory levy GST simultaneously.

**Components of GST.**

| Nature of supply | Levy | Charging authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Intra-State** supply (within a State/UT) | **CGST + SGST/UTGST** | Centre + State/UT levy simultaneously on the same value |
| **Inter-State** supply (between two States/UTs), imports | **IGST** (= CGST + SGST rates combined) | Levied and collected by the Centre under Sec 5 of IGST Act; later apportioned with destination State |

Being **destination-based** (or consumption-based), the tax finally accrues to the State where the goods/services are **consumed**, not where they are produced.

**Taxes subsumed.**

| Central levies subsumed | State levies subsumed |
|---|---|
| Central Excise Duty | State VAT / Sales Tax |
| Additional Excise Duties | Central Sales Tax |
| Service Tax | Luxury Tax |
| Additional Customs Duty (CVD) & SAD | Entry Tax / Octroi, Entertainment Tax, Purchase Tax |

**Conclusion:** GST unifies multiple Central and State levies into a single, destination-based Dual GST — CGST + SGST/UTGST on intra-State supplies and IGST on inter-State supplies — achieving the "One Nation, One Tax" objective.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the clear intra vs inter-State split (CGST+SGST vs IGST) and correct lists of subsumed taxes; a common deduction is forgetting IGST is levied by the Centre and apportioned to the destination State.)*

### Q85. Ch: GST – Supply — Scope of Supply, Sec 7 and Schedules I, II & III (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the scope of "supply" under Section 7 of the CGST Act, 2017. In your answer, state the essential ingredients of a supply, and briefly explain the role of Schedule I, Schedule II and Schedule III with one example of each.

**Answer:**
"Supply" is the **taxable event** under GST. Section 7(1) of the CGST Act, 2017 provides an **inclusive** definition; the expression "supply" includes—

(a) **all forms of supply of goods or services or both** such as sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease or disposal **made or agreed to be made for a consideration** by a person **in the course or furtherance of business** [Sec 7(1)(a)];
(b) **import of services** for a consideration whether or not in the course or furtherance of business [Sec 7(1)(b)];
(c) the activities specified in **Schedule I**, made or agreed to be made **without a consideration** [Sec 7(1)(c)]; and
(d) [read with 7(1A)] activities to be treated as supply of goods or supply of services as referred to in **Schedule II**.

**Essential ingredients of a supply** are therefore: (i) supply of goods or services or both; (ii) for a **consideration**; (iii) in the **course or furtherance of business**. Where these are all present, the transaction is a supply (import of services and Schedule I are the exceptions to the consideration/business conditions).

**Role of the three Schedules.**

| Schedule | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Schedule I** | Activities treated as supply **even if made without consideration** | Permanent transfer/disposal of business assets on which ITC has been availed; supply between **related/distinct persons** in the course of business; principal–agent supplies; import of services from a related person |
| **Schedule II** | Classifies specified activities as supply of **goods** or supply of **services** (removes ambiguity) | Transfer of title in goods = supply of **goods**; renting of immovable property, works contract, temporary transfer of IPR = supply of **services** |
| **Schedule III** | "Negative list" — activities/transactions that are **neither supply of goods nor of services** | Services by an employee to employer in the course of employment; services by a court/tribunal; sale of **land** and **completed buildings**; actionable claims other than specified actionable claims |

**Conclusion:** A transaction is taxable only if it qualifies as "supply" under Sec 7 — generally requiring consideration and a business nexus — read together with Schedule I (supplies without consideration), Schedule II (goods-vs-services classification) and Schedule III (exclusions).

*(Full-marks tip: State all three ingredients and cite Sec 7(1)(a)–(c) and 7(1A); the frequent deduction is confusing Schedule II (classification) with Schedule III (exclusion).)*

### Q88. Ch: GST – Charge & RCM — Forward Charge vs Reverse Charge, Sec 9 (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the concepts of "forward charge" and "reverse charge" under Section 9 of the CGST Act, 2017. State the two situations in which tax is payable under reverse charge, and bring out clearly how reverse charge differs from forward charge.

**Answer:**
**Charging section.** Section 9(1) of the CGST Act, 2017 is the charging provision: CGST is levied on all intra-State supplies of goods or services at the notified rate on the value determined under Section 15, and is **paid by the taxable person (supplier)** — this is the **forward charge** mechanism, the normal rule.

**Reverse charge.** Section 2(98) defines "reverse charge" as the liability to pay tax by the **recipient** of supply of goods or services instead of the supplier. In this mechanism the **entire liability** to pay tax and to comply with the law shifts from the supplier to the recipient. Reverse charge is attracted in **two situations**:

1. **Section 9(3) — notified supplies:** The Government notifies specific categories of goods/services on which tax is payable by the recipient, e.g. Goods Transport Agency (GTA) services, legal services by an advocate, sponsorship services, services by a director to the company, import of goods/services, supply of raw cotton, cashew nuts, etc.
2. **Section 9(4) — supplies by unregistered to registered persons:** Where a **registered person** receives specified categories of goods/services from an **unregistered supplier**, the registered recipient pays tax under reverse charge (currently applicable to notified classes such as promoters in real estate).

**Distinction.**

| Basis | Forward Charge [Sec 9(1)] | Reverse Charge [Sec 9(3)/(4)] |
|---|---|---|
| Person liable to pay tax | **Supplier** | **Recipient** |
| Coverage | Default, all supplies | Only notified supplies / specified unregistered purchases |
| Payment mode | May be paid from ITC (electronic credit ledger) | Must be paid in **cash** (electronic cash ledger); ITC cannot be used to discharge RCM liability |
| Composition scheme supplier | Available | A composition dealer **also pays RCM at normal rates** |
| Compulsory registration | Threshold-based | Recipient liable under RCM must register **compulsorily** [Sec 24] |

**Conclusion:** Under forward charge the supplier collects and pays GST; under reverse charge the statutory liability shifts to the recipient in the two situations covered by Section 9(3) and 9(4), and the recipient must discharge such tax in cash.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner looks for the two distinct sub-sections 9(3) and 9(4) and the rule that RCM must be paid in cash and triggers compulsory registration under Sec 24.)*

### Q91. Ch: GST – Exemptions — Exemption vs Nil-Rated vs Non-Taxable (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between "exempt supply", "nil-rated supply", "non-taxable supply" and "zero-rated supply" under the GST law. State clearly the ITC consequence of each.

**Answer:**
Under Section 2(47), "**exempt supply**" is an umbrella term meaning any supply that attracts a **nil rate** of tax, or is **wholly exempt** under Section 11 (or Sec 6 of IGST), or is a **non-taxable supply**. These must be carefully distinguished from a **zero-rated supply** under Section 16 of the IGST Act, 2017, which is fundamentally different in its ITC treatment.

| Type | Meaning | Rate | ITC available? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nil-rated supply** | Supply appearing in the tariff at a **0% (nil) rate** (e.g. grains, salt) | 0% (in tariff) | **No** ITC |
| **Exempt supply** (by notification) | Supply wholly exempt under Sec 11, CGST / Sec 6, IGST | Exempt | **No** ITC (Sec 17(2) — proportionate reversal) |
| **Non-taxable supply** [Sec 2(78)] | Supply **not leviable** to GST at all (e.g. alcoholic liquor for human consumption, petrol) | Outside GST | **No** ITC |
| **Zero-rated supply** [Sec 16, IGST] | **Exports** and supplies to a **SEZ** unit/developer | 0% but **fully creditable** | **Yes** — ITC allowed; supplier may claim **refund** of unutilised ITC or export under LUT/on payment of IGST |

**Key contrast:** In exempt/nil-rated/non-taxable supplies, **ITC is blocked** and inputs bear the tax cost. In a **zero-rated** supply the outward supply bears no tax **and** the input tax is **refunded**, so the entire supply chain is relieved of GST.

**Conclusion:** Exempt, nil-rated and non-taxable supplies all deny ITC; only a **zero-rated** supply (exports/SEZ) enjoys both a nil output tax and full ITC/refund.

**Question:** Determine the **time of supply** in each of the following independent cases under Sections 12 and 13 of the CGST Act, 2017:

| Case | Particulars | Date of removal / provision | Date of invoice | Date of payment (entry in books / credit in bank, earlier) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) | Supply of goods (forward charge) | 5 Oct | 10 Oct | 15 Oct |
| (ii) | Supply of goods (forward charge) | 12 Oct | 20 Oct | 8 Oct |
| (iii) | Supply of services, invoice issued within 30 days | Service completed 2 Nov | 10 Nov | 25 Nov |
| (iv) | Goods taxable under **reverse charge** | Received 3 Dec | Supplier's invoice 1 Dec | Payment 20 Dec |
| (v) | Services under **reverse charge** (import from associated enterprise) | — | 4 Jan | Date of payment 10 Feb; date of entry in books 5 Feb |

**Solution:**
**WN-1 — Rules.**
- **Goods, forward charge [Sec 12(2)]:** ToS = **earlier** of (a) date of invoice (or last date on which invoice ought to be issued u/s 31), or (b) date of receipt of payment. *(By Notification, for goods the "receipt of payment" limb is generally not applied — tax is due on the invoice date; the standard exam rule taken is earlier of invoice/removal date.)*
- **Services, forward charge [Sec 13(2)]:** If invoice issued **within 30 days**, ToS = earlier of date of **invoice** or date of **payment**; if not, earlier of date of **provision of service** or payment.
- **Goods, RCM [Sec 12(3)]:** ToS = **earliest** of (a) date of **receipt of goods**, (b) date of **payment**, or (c) date **immediately following 30 days** from date of invoice.
- **Services, RCM [Sec 13(3)]:** ToS = **earlier** of (a) date of **payment**, or (b) date **immediately following 60 days** from invoice; for **associated enterprises** (import), earlier of date of **entry in books** of recipient or date of payment.

**WN-2 — Determination.**

| Case | Time of supply | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | **10 Oct** | Goods FC: invoice date 10 Oct (earlier of invoice/removal-based due date) |
| (ii) | **12 Oct** | Invoice due by removal date (12 Oct) precedes actual invoice (20 Oct); ToS = 12 Oct |
| (iii) | **10 Nov** | Services, invoice within 30 days → earlier of invoice (10 Nov) or payment (25 Nov) = 10 Nov |
| (iv) | **3 Dec** | Goods RCM: earliest of receipt (3 Dec), payment (20 Dec), 31st day from invoice (31 Dec) = **3 Dec** |
| (v) | **5 Feb** | Services RCM, associated enterprise: earlier of entry in books (5 Feb) or payment (10 Feb) = 5 Feb |

**Answer:** ToS is (i) 10 Oct, (ii) 12 Oct, (iii) 10 Nov, (iv) 3 Dec, (v) 5 Feb.

### Q94. Ch: GST – Input Tax Credit — Eligibility, Conditions & Blocked Credits, Sec 16 & 17(5) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions that a registered person must satisfy to be **eligible to take input tax credit** under Section 16 of the CGST Act, 2017. Also list any five supplies on which ITC is **blocked** under Section 17(5).

**Answer:**
**Entitlement.** Under **Section 16(1)**, every registered person is entitled to take credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services **used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business**, credited to the electronic credit ledger.

**Four conditions under Section 16(2).** ITC is available only if **all** the following are satisfied:
(a) the registered person is in possession of a **tax invoice / debit note** issued by a registered supplier;
(aa) the details of the invoice have been **furnished by the supplier** in his outward return and **communicated to the recipient** (GSTR-2B matching);
(b) he has **received** the goods or services (including "bill-to-ship-to" deliveries);
(c) the **tax charged has actually been paid** to the Government by the supplier; and
(d) he has **furnished the return** under Section 39.

Two further conditions: where goods are received in lots, credit is available only on receipt of the **last lot** [1st proviso]; and the recipient must **pay the supplier the value + tax within 180 days**, failing which the credit is added back with interest [2nd proviso to Sec 16(2)]. Also, ITC cannot be taken after the **30th November** following the end of the financial year or filing of annual return, whichever is earlier [Sec 16(4)].

**Blocked credits — Section 17(5).** ITC is **not available** on, inter alia:

| # | Blocked credit under Sec 17(5) |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Motor vehicles** for transport of persons (≤ 13 seats), except where used for further supply, passenger transport or driving training |
| 2 | **Food & beverages, outdoor catering, health services, club/health & fitness membership, beauty treatment** (unless used to make an outward taxable supply of the same category) |
| 3 | **Works contract services** for construction of immovable property (except plant & machinery) |
| 4 | Goods/services used for **construction of immovable property on own account** |
| 5 | **Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off or given as gifts/free samples**; membership; travel benefits to employees on leave |

**Conclusion:** ITC under Sec 16 requires a valid invoice, receipt of goods/services, actual payment of tax by the supplier, filing of return, and payment to the supplier within 180 days; even then it is denied for the specific categories blocked by Sec 17(5).

*(Full-marks tip: List all four conditions of Sec 16(2) plus the 180-day and 30-November limits; a clean, correctly-cited Sec 17(5) list (motor vehicles, food, works contract, own-construction, free samples) secures the balance.)*

### Q96. Ch: GST – Registration — Persons Liable & Compulsory Registration, Sec 22 & 24 (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the provisions relating to persons liable for registration under Section 22 and persons required to obtain **compulsory registration** irrespective of turnover under Section 24 of the CGST Act, 2017. State the threshold limits applicable.

**Answer:**
**Persons liable under Section 22.** Every supplier is liable to be registered in the State/UT from where he makes a taxable supply if his **aggregate turnover** in a financial year exceeds the prescribed threshold. The thresholds are:

| Category of State | Goods (exclusive supply) | Services / Mixed |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal category States** | ₹40 lakh | ₹20 lakh |
| **Special category / specified States** (e.g. Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura) | ₹20 lakh | ₹10 lakh |

"**Aggregate turnover**" [Sec 2(6)] means the all-India, PAN-based total of taxable supplies + exempt supplies + exports + inter-State supplies of persons having the same PAN, but **excludes** CGST/SGST/IGST/cess and the value of inward supplies taxed under reverse charge.

**Compulsory registration — Section 24.** Notwithstanding Section 22, the following persons must register **irrespective of turnover** (threshold is not available):
(i) persons making any **inter-State taxable supply** (of goods; a threshold relief exists for inter-State services and certain goods);
(ii) **casual taxable persons** making taxable supply;
(iii) persons liable to pay tax under **reverse charge**;
(iv) persons liable to pay tax under Section 9(5) (**e-commerce operator** liability);
(v) **non-resident taxable persons**;
(vi) persons required to **deduct TDS** (Sec 51) / **collect TCS** (Sec 52);
(vii) **input service distributors**;
(viii) persons who supply goods/services as **agents** on behalf of other taxable persons;
(ix) persons supplying through an **e-commerce operator** required to collect TCS; and
(x) every **e-commerce operator**, and persons supplying online information and database access or retrieval (OIDAR) services from outside India to an unregistered person in India.

**Conclusion:** Registration is turnover-based under Sec 22 (₹40/20 lakh for goods/services, halved for special category States), but the eleven categories in Sec 24 — including casual/non-resident persons, RCM payers, e-commerce operators and inter-State suppliers — must register **compulsorily regardless of turnover**.

*(Full-marks tip: Quote both threshold sets correctly and list Sec 24 categories; the frequent error is applying the ₹40/20-lakh limit to a person already covered by compulsory registration under Sec 24.)*

### Q98. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Tax Invoice: Contents & Time Limits, Sec 31 (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State the provisions of Section 31 of the CGST Act, 2017 regarding the **time limit for issuing a tax invoice** for goods and for services. Also state when a **bill of supply** is issued instead of a tax invoice, and what is a **receipt voucher**.

**Answer:**
Under **Section 31** of the CGST Act, 2017, a **tax invoice** is the primary document evidencing a taxable supply and is the basis on which the recipient claims ITC.

**Time limit for goods [Sec 31(1)].** A registered person supplying **taxable goods** shall issue a tax invoice **before or at the time of**—
(a) **removal** of goods for supply to the recipient, where the supply involves movement of goods; or
(b) **delivery** of goods or making them available to the recipient, in any other case.

**Time limit for services [Sec 31(2) read with Rule 47].** A registered person supplying **taxable services** shall issue the invoice **before or after** the provision of service but **within 30 days** from the date of supply of service (**45 days** in the case of banks, insurers and other notified financial institutions).

**Bill of supply [Sec 31(3)(c)].** A **bill of supply** (not a tax invoice) is issued by (i) a registered person supplying **exempted** goods/services, or (ii) a person paying tax under the **composition scheme** [Sec 10]. Since no tax is charged/collected in these cases, no tax invoice is issued and the recipient gets **no ITC**.

**Receipt voucher [Sec 31(3)(d)].** On receipt of an **advance payment** with respect to any supply, the registered person shall issue a **receipt voucher** evidencing receipt of such payment. If the supply does not materialise and no invoice is issued, a **refund voucher** is issued to the payer.

**Conclusion:** A tax invoice must be issued at/before removal or delivery for goods and within 30 days (45 for financial institutions) for services; exempt/composition supplies use a bill of supply, and advances are covered by a receipt voucher.

*(Full-marks tip: State both the goods (removal/delivery) and services (30/45 days) time limits, and correctly link bill of supply to exempt/composition supplies; forgetting the 45-day rule for banks/insurers is a routine deduction.)*

### Q99. Ch: GST – Tax Invoice, Payment & Returns — Payment of Tax, Ledgers & GST Returns (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the three electronic ledgers maintained on the GST portal and the **order of utilisation** of input tax credit under Sections 49, 49A and 49B of the CGST Act, 2017. Also name the periodic returns GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-9 and state their purpose.

**Answer:**
**Electronic ledgers [Sec 49].** Every registered person maintains three ledgers on the common portal:

| Ledger | Contents / Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Electronic Cash Ledger** [Sec 49(1)] | Amounts deposited in cash towards tax, interest, penalty, fee; used to pay any liability |
| **Electronic Credit Ledger** [Sec 49(2)] | Self-assessed **input tax credit**; can be used **only to pay output tax** (not interest/penalty/RCM) |
| **Electronic Liability Register** [Sec 49(7)] | Records the total tax and other liabilities of the registered person |

**Order of utilisation of ITC [Sec 49A, 49B read with Rule 88A].** The credit of **IGST** must be **fully utilised first** — towards IGST, then CGST, then SGST/UTGST in any order — before CGST or SGST credit can be used. Thereafter, **CGST credit** is used for CGST then IGST; **SGST/UTGST credit** for SGST/UTGST then IGST. Importantly, **CGST credit cannot be set off against SGST**, and vice versa. RCM liability must always be paid in **cash**.

**Periodic returns.**

| Return | Filed by / Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **GSTR-1** | Every regular registered person, monthly/quarterly (QRMP) | Statement of **outward supplies** (invoice-wise details) |
| **GSTR-3B** | Every regular person, monthly/quarterly | **Summary return** of outward supplies, ITC claimed and tax **paid** |
| **GSTR-9** | Regular taxpayers (above prescribed turnover), annually | **Annual return** consolidating the year's supplies, ITC and tax |

**Conclusion:** Tax is discharged through the electronic cash and credit ledgers following the mandatory IGST-first set-off order of Sec 49A/49B, and compliance is reported through GSTR-1 (outward supplies), GSTR-3B (summary/payment) and GSTR-9 (annual return).

*(Full-marks tip: The credit ledger pays only output tax (never RCM/interest), and IGST credit must be exhausted first — stating the cross-utilisation bar between CGST and SGST earns the key mark.)*
