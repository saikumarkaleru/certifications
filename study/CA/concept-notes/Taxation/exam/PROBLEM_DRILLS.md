**New regime u/s 115BAC(1A) — resident individual, slabs *(verify current AY)*:**
| Slab | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |

- **Rebate u/s 87A (new regime):** total income ≤ **₹12,00,000** → rebate = tax, **max ₹60,000** *(verify current AY)*, resident individual only; **marginal relief** available.
- **Old regime slabs** (below 60 yrs): Nil ≤ ₹2,50,000; 5% ₹2.5–5L; 20% ₹5–10L; 30% > ₹10L. **87A**: total income ≤ **₹5,00,000** → rebate max **₹12,500**.

### D1. [Ch.1 Basic Concepts — AY/PY & new-regime tax] (level: Easy)
**Problem:** Mr. Arjun, a resident individual aged 40, earned income during the year 1 April 2025 – 31 March 2026. His **total income is ₹7,00,000**. He does **not** opt out of the default regime. (a) State his Previous Year and Assessment Year. (b) Compute his tax liability.

**Solution:**
(a) **Previous Year (PY) = 2025-26** (year in which income is earned); **Assessment Year (AY) = 2026-27** (year in which it is taxed) — s.2(9)/s.3.

(b) Default = **new regime u/s 115BAC(1A)**. *(verify current AY)*

| Slab | Amount | Rate | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | 4,00,000 | Nil | 0 |
| ₹4,00,001–₹7,00,000 | 3,00,000 | 5% | 15,000 |
| **Tax before rebate** | | | **15,000** |

- **Rebate u/s 87A:** total income ₹7,00,000 ≤ ₹12,00,000 → rebate = ₹15,000 (< ₹60,000 cap) → **tax after rebate = Nil.**
- HEC 4% on Nil = Nil.
- **Tax liability = ₹NIL.**

**> Marks tip:** State PY and AY separately (½+½) and *show* the 87A rebate line explicitly — writing "tax = Nil" without the ₹15,000 → rebate step loses the working mark.

### D2. [Ch.2 Residential Status — basic condition] (level: Easy)
**Problem:** Mr. Bhaskar, an Indian citizen, was physically present in India for **185 days** during PY 2025-26. In the 4 years preceding, he was in India for 900 days in total. Determine his residential status for AY 2026-27.

**Solution:** An individual is **resident** u/s 6(1) if he satisfies **either** basic condition:
- **BC-1:** in India ≥ **182 days** in the PY, **or**
- **BC-2:** in India ≥ **60 days** in the PY **and** ≥ **365 days** in the 4 preceding PYs.

Here presence = **185 days ≥ 182** → **BC-1 satisfied.**
**Mr. Bhaskar is a RESIDENT for AY 2026-27** (satisfying even one basic condition is enough; no need to test BC-2).

**> Marks tip:** Quote the "182 **or** 60+365" structure and name the section (6(1)). Concluding "resident" without stating *which* condition is satisfied costs the reasoning mark.

### D4. [Ch.4 House Property — self-occupied, interest u/s 24(b)] (level: Easy)
**Problem:** Mr. Deepak owns one residential house which he uses for his own residence throughout PY 2025-26. He had taken a housing loan (for **purchase**, disbursed in 2016) on which **interest of ₹1,80,000** accrued this year. He **opts for the old regime**. Compute income from house property.

**Solution:** *(old regime — self-occupied property, s.23(2))*

**Computation — Income from House Property (Self-Occupied)**
| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Gross Annual Value (self-occupied) — s.23(2) | Nil |
| Less: Municipal taxes | Nil |
| Net Annual Value | Nil |
| Less: Standard deduction 30% u/s 24(a) (30% of Nil) | Nil |

### D6. [Ch.2 Residential Status — ROR vs RNOR (additional conditions)] (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Mr. Farhan was in India for **190 days** during PY 2025-26 (so he is a resident). Regarding the preceding years: he was a **resident in only 1 of the 10 preceding previous years**, and his total stay in India during the **7 preceding previous years was 400 days**. Determine whether he is Resident & Ordinarily Resident (ROR) or Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).

**Solution:** A resident individual is **ROR** only if he satisfies **BOTH** additional conditions u/s 6(6):
- **AC-1:** resident in **≥ 2 of the 10** preceding PYs, **and**
- **AC-2:** in India **≥ 730 days in the 7** preceding PYs.

| Condition | Requirement | Actual | Met? |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC-1 | Resident in ≥2 of 10 yrs | 1 of 10 | **No** |
| AC-2 | ≥730 days in 7 yrs | 400 days | **No** |

He fails **both** additional conditions (failing even one is enough).
**Mr. Farhan is RESIDENT but NOT ORDINARILY RESIDENT (RNOR) for AY 2026-27.**

**> Marks tip:** ROR needs **both** additional conditions satisfied; failing **either one** → RNOR. Present AC-1 and AC-2 as a two-row table and name s.6(6) — examiners award marks for the framework, not just the label.

### D7. [Ch.3 Salaries — HRA exemption u/s 10(13A)] (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Mr. Gaurav works in **Delhi**. During PY 2025-26 he received basic salary **₹40,000 p.m.**, dearness allowance (forming part of retirement benefits) **₹10,000 p.m.**, and **HRA ₹20,000 p.m.** He paid rent of **₹22,000 p.m.** Compute the taxable HRA (old regime).

**Solution:** *(old regime — s.10(13A) r/w Rule 2A; HRA exemption is **not** available in the new regime)*

**WN 1 — "Salary" for HRA** = Basic + DA (in terms) + turnover commission = (₹40,000 + ₹10,000) × 12 = **₹6,00,000.**

**WN 2 — Least of the three (Delhi = metro → 50%):**
| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| (a) Actual HRA received (₹20,000×12) | 2,40,000 |
| (b) Rent paid − 10% of salary = ₹2,64,000 − ₹60,000 | 2,04,000 |
| (c) 50% of salary (metro) | 3,00,000 |
| **Least = exempt** | **2,04,000** |

**Taxable HRA = ₹2,40,000 − ₹2,04,000 = ₹36,000.**

**> Marks tip:** All three limbs must be shown; the exemption is the **least**, not limb (b). Use **50%** only for the 4 metros (Delhi/Mumbai/Kolkata/Chennai) — 40% elsewhere. Rent paid in limb (b) is **rent − 10% of salary**, not rent alone.

### D8. [Ch.3 Salaries — Gratuity exemption u/s 10(10)] (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Mr. Harish retired on 31 March 2026 after **25 years 7 months** of service. He is **covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972.** His last drawn salary (basic + DA) was **₹40,000 p.m.** and he received **gratuity of ₹7,00,000.** Compute the taxable gratuity.

**Solution:** *(s.10(10)(ii) — employee covered by the Gratuity Act)*

**WN — Completed years:** 25 yrs 7 months → part > 6 months rounds **up** → **26 years.**

**Least of the following is exempt:**
| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| (a) Actual gratuity received | 7,00,000 |
| (b) 15/26 × last drawn salary × completed years = 15/26 × 40,000 × 26 | 6,00,000 |
| (c) Statutory limit *(verify current AY)* | 20,00,000 |
| **Least = exempt** | **6,00,000** |

**Taxable gratuity = ₹7,00,000 − ₹6,00,000 = ₹1,00,000.**

**> Marks tip:** Under the **Gratuity Act** use **15/26** and "salary = last drawn basic+DA"; round part-years > 6 months **up**. (For employees **not** covered by the Act: **½ month's *average* salary of last 10 months × completed years**, ignoring fractions — don't confuse the two formulas.)

### D9. [Ch.4 House Property — let-out with vacancy, s.23(1)(c)] (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Mr. Ishan owns a house let out at **₹24,000 p.m.** Details: Municipal Value ₹2,40,000; Fair Rent ₹2,60,000; Standard Rent ₹2,50,000. The house remained **vacant for 2 months**; it was actually let for 10 months. Municipal taxes of **₹24,000** were paid by the owner. Interest on housing loan **₹1,50,000.** Compute income from house property (old regime).

**Solution:** *(old regime)*

**WN 1 — Expected Rent (ER):** higher of MV ₹2,40,000 and FR ₹2,60,000 = ₹2,60,000, **restricted to Standard Rent ₹2,50,000 → ER = ₹2,50,000.**

**WN 2 — Actual rent received/receivable** (let 10 months) = ₹24,000 × 10 = **₹2,40,000.**

**WN 3 — GAV:** But for the vacancy, annual rent (₹2,88,000) would have exceeded ER. The actual rent (₹2,40,000) is **lower than ER solely because of the 2-month vacancy** → **s.23(1)(c)** applies → **GAV = actual rent received = ₹2,40,000.**

**Computation — Income from House Property**
| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Gross Annual Value (WN 3) | 2,40,000 |
| Less: Municipal taxes paid | (24,000) |
| Net Annual Value | 2,16,000 |
| Less: Standard deduction 30% u/s 24(a) | (64,800) |
| Less: Interest on borrowed capital u/s 24(b) | (1,50,000) |
| **Income from House Property** | **1,200** |

**> Marks tip:** Nail the GAV logic: ER first (capped at standard rent), then invoke **s.23(1)(c)** to take actual rent as GAV *because the shortfall is caused by vacancy*. The 30% deduction is on **NAV**, and municipal taxes are deductible only if **actually paid by the owner**.

### D10. [Ch.3 Salaries — Rent-Free Accommodation perquisite] (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Mr. Jatin is employed with a private company in a city whose **population is 30 lakh** (2011 census). He is provided **rent-free unfurnished accommodation owned by the employer** throughout PY 2025-26. His salary: basic ₹60,000 p.m., DA (in terms) ₹10,000 p.m., and a bonus of ₹50,000. Compute the value of the accommodation perquisite (old regime).

**Solution:** *(Rule 3(1) — accommodation owned by employer; revised population slabs applicable from AY 2024-25) (verify current AY)*

**WN — "Salary" for perquisite valuation** = basic + DA (in terms) + bonus + commission + all taxable allowances
= (₹60,000 + ₹10,000) × 12 + ₹50,000 = ₹8,40,000 + ₹50,000 = **₹8,90,000.**

**Perquisite value (employer-owned):**
| City population (2011 census) *(verify current AY)* | Rate |
|---|---|
| > 40 lakh | 10% of salary |
| **15 lakh – 40 lakh** | **7.5% of salary** |
| < 15 lakh | 5% of salary |

Population 30 lakh → **7.5% slab** → 7.5% × ₹8,90,000 = **₹66,750.**

**Perquisite value = ₹66,750** (taxable in both regimes; accommodation perquisite is not exempt under the new regime).

**> Marks tip:** Use the **revised slabs (10% / 7.5% / 5%)** — the old 15%/10%/7.5% slabs are outdated. State clearly that "salary" for this perquisite **includes bonus** but **excludes** employer PF contribution and DA not forming part of retirement benefits.

### D11. [Ch.2 Scope of Total Income u/s 5] (level: Medium)
**Problem:** For PY 2025-26, Mr. Karan has the following incomes. Compute his total income if he is (i) ROR, (ii) RNOR, (iii) Non-Resident.
- (a) Salary received in India for services rendered in India — ₹5,00,000
- (b) Profit from a business in USA **controlled from India** — ₹2,00,000
- (c) Profit from a business in USA **controlled from USA** — ₹3,00,000
- (d) Income accrued and received outside India (no nexus to India) — ₹1,50,000
- (e) Past foreign income (already earned in an earlier year) remitted to India this year — ₹1,00,000

**Solution:** *(s.5 — scope of total income)*

| Income | ROR | RNOR | NR |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Received & accrues in India | 5,00,000 | 5,00,000 | 5,00,000 |
| (b) Foreign business **controlled from India** | 2,00,000 | 2,00,000 | — |
| (c) Foreign business **controlled from abroad** | 3,00,000 | — | — |
| (d) Foreign income, no India nexus | 1,50,000 | — | — |
| (e) Remittance of **past** income | — | — | — |
| **Total income** | **11,50,000** | **7,00,000** | **5,00,000** |

**Reasoning:** Indian income (received/accruing in India) is taxable for **everyone** → (a). Foreign income is taxable for **ROR fully**; for **RNOR only if it is from a business controlled from / profession set up in India** → (b) taxable, (c)/(d) not; for **NR, foreign income is wholly outside scope.** A **remittance of past income (e)** is not "income" of the current year → taxable for none.

**> Marks tip:** Build the answer as a status × income **matrix**. The two examiner-favourite traps: the RNOR carve-out (**only** business-controlled-from-India / profession-set-up-in-India foreign income is taxed) and item (e) — a **remittance is not income**, so it is never taxed on receipt.

### D12. [Ch.3 Salaries — comprehensive, both regimes] (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Mr. Lakshya is employed in **Mumbai**. For PY 2025-26: Basic ₹80,000 p.m.; DA (forming part of retirement benefits) ₹20,000 p.m.; HRA ₹30,000 p.m. (rent paid ₹35,000 p.m.); a taxable "special allowance" ₹2,000 p.m.; employer's contribution to Recognised PF = 15% of basic; professional tax paid by him ₹2,500. Compute his income under "Salaries" under **(i) the old regime** and **(ii) the new (default) regime.**

- Least of: (a) HRA ₹3,60,000; (b) Rent ₹4,20,000 − 10%×₹12,00,000 = ₹3,00,000; (c) 50%×₹12,00,000 = ₹6,00,000 → **exempt ₹3,00,000.**

**WN 2 — Employer RPF contribution:** 15% of basic = ₹1,44,000. Exempt up to **12%** = ₹1,15,200. **Taxable excess = 3% of basic = ₹28,800** (both regimes).

**Computation — Income from Salaries**
| Particulars | Old regime ₹ | New regime ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Basic (₹80,000×12) | 9,60,000 | 9,60,000 |

### D13. [Ch.4 House Property — SOP + let-out, pre-construction interest, loss set-off] (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Mr. Manav owns two houses (old regime):
- **House 1 (self-occupied all year):** housing loan (for construction, disbursed FY 2015-16, construction completed FY 2017-18); interest for PY 2025-26 = **₹2,20,000.**
- **House 2 (let out all year at ₹30,000 p.m.):** MV ₹3,00,000; FR ₹3,20,000; SR ₹3,10,000; municipal taxes ₹30,000 (paid by owner); current-year interest ₹2,00,000; **pre-construction period interest** totalled ₹5,00,000 (this is the 2nd of the 5 annual instalments year).

Compute income from house property and state the treatment of any loss.

**Solution:** *(old regime)*

**House 1 — Self-Occupied:**
| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| GAV / NAV (s.23(2)) | Nil |
| Less: Interest u/s 24(b), capped at ₹2,00,000 *(verify current AY)* (actual ₹2,20,000) | (2,00,000) |
| **Loss — House 1** | **(2,00,000)** |

**House 2 — Let out:**
- **ER** = higher(MV ₹3,00,000, FR ₹3,20,000) = ₹3,20,000, capped at SR ₹3,10,000 → **₹3,10,000.**
- **Actual rent** = ₹30,000×12 = ₹3,60,000 → **GAV = higher of ER & actual = ₹3,60,000.**
- **Pre-construction interest:** ₹5,00,000 ÷ 5 = **₹1,00,000** allowed this year (2nd instalment).

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Gross Annual Value | 3,60,000 |
| Less: Municipal taxes paid | (30,000) |
| Net Annual Value | 3,30,000 |
| Less: 30% standard deduction u/s 24(a) | (99,000) |
| Less: Interest u/s 24(b): current ₹2,00,000 + pre-construction ₹1,00,000 | (3,00,000) |
| **Loss — House 2** | **(69,000)** |

So **₹2,00,000** is set off against other income; the balance **₹69,000 is carried forward u/s 71B** (up to 8 AYs) to be set off only against future house-property income.

**> Marks tip:** Three separately-marked points: (1) SOP interest **capped at ₹2,00,000**; (2) pre-construction interest = **1/5th** allowed from the year of completion; (3) the **₹2,00,000 s.71(3A) cap** with **₹69,000 carried forward u/s 71B** — stating the carry-forward is what separates a full-marks answer.

### D14. [Ch.2 + Ch.3 — residential status + scope of income] (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Mr. Nikhil, an **Indian citizen**, **left India for the first time for employment abroad on 15 September 2025** (he was in India from 1 April 2025 up to that date). His incomes for PY 2025-26: (a) salary for services rendered in India (Apr–Sep), received in India — ₹4,00,000; (b) salary earned abroad and received abroad — ₹6,00,000; (c) interest credited by an Indian bank on his savings account — ₹20,000; (d) dividend from a foreign company, received abroad — ₹50,000. Determine his residential status and total income (new regime).

**Solution:**

**Step 1 — Residential status (s.6(1)):**
- Days in India = 1 Apr → 15 Sep = 30+31+30+31+31+15 = **168 days.**
- He is an **Indian citizen leaving India for employment** → **Explanation 1(a) to s.6(1)** applies: **BC-2 (the 60-day test) is extended to 182 days**; only BC-1 remains testable.
- 168 days **< 182** → **basic condition NOT satisfied** → **Mr. Nikhil is a NON-RESIDENT for AY 2026-27.**

**Step 2 — Total income of a Non-Resident (s.5(2)):** only income **received/accruing in India** is taxable.
| Income | Taxable? | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Salary — services in India, received in India | Yes (Indian income) | 4,00,000 |
| (b) Salary earned & received abroad | No (foreign income of NR) | — |
| (c) Interest from Indian bank | Yes (accrues in India) | 20,000 |
| (d) Foreign dividend received abroad | No | — |
| **Total income (NR)** | | **4,20,000** |

**> Marks tip:** The decisive step is invoking **Explanation 1(a) to s.6(1)** — for a citizen leaving India for employment the 60-day limb becomes 182, so 168 days makes him **non-resident**. Then apply s.5(2): a NR is taxed only on Indian-source income. Salary for services rendered in India is **deemed to accrue in India** (s.9(1)(ii)) regardless of where paid.

### D15. [Ch.1–4 integrated — total income & tax, both regimes, with advice] (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Mr. Om (aged 45, resident) for PY 2025-26: **Salary** ₹1,00,000 p.m. (basic only, no allowances). **House property** — self-occupied, housing-loan interest ₹2,00,000. **Other sources** — savings-bank interest ₹15,000 and fixed-deposit interest ₹45,000. **Investments/payments:** LIC + PPF ₹1,50,000 (80C), mediclaim premium ₹25,000 (80D). | **Total income** | **8,25,000** |

**(ii) NEW REGIME (default)**
| Head | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Salary (₹12,00,000 − SD ₹75,000) | 11,25,000 |
| House property — SOP interest **not allowed** | Nil |
| Other sources | 60,000 |
| **Gross Total Income** | **11,85,000** |
| Less: Chapter VI-A (80C/80D/80TTA **not available**) | Nil |

| | Old | New |
|---|---|---|

### D7. Supply — composite vs mixed supply (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Determine the GST rate and tax for each: (a) A retailer sells a **single-price gift hamper for ₹2,000** containing canned foods (12%), aerated drinks (28%) and chocolates (18%), each available separately. (b) A manufacturer supplies goods for ₹1,00,000 (goods taxable @18%) and, as part of the same contract, arranges **transport ₹5,000** (would be 5% if standalone) and **insurance ₹2,000** for delivery; the goods are the principal supply. Compute tax in each case.

**Solution:**
*(a) Mixed supply (Sec 2(74)):* items are supplied together for a single price but are not naturally bundled → **taxed at the highest rate** among them = **28%**.
Tax = ₹2,000 × 28% = **₹560**.

*(b) Composite supply (Sec 2(30), Sec 8):* goods + transport + insurance are naturally bundled; **principal supply = goods (18%)**. Value includes incidental transport and insurance (Sec 15).
Value = 1,00,000 + 5,000 + 2,000 = ₹1,07,000; tax = 1,07,000 × 18% = **₹19,260**.

**> Marks tip:** Name the section (2(74) mixed → highest rate; 2(30)/8 composite → principal-supply rate) and apply the correct rate to the **whole** value. The classic error is taxing transport at 5% separately in a composite supply.

### D9. Return Filing — belated return, 234A interest, 234F fee (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Mr. Deepak, an individual not liable to tax audit, has total income ₹8,00,000 for AY 2026-27. His self-assessment tax payable (after TDS) is ₹15,000. The due date under Sec 139(1) is **31 July 2026**; he actually files on **20 December 2026**. Compute interest under Sec 234A and the fee under Sec 234F, and state the section under which he files.

**Solution:**
*Return filed after due date but on or before 31 Dec 2026 → **belated return under Sec 139(4)** (valid).* 

*Section 234A (1% p.m. on unpaid tax, from day after due date to date of filing):*
- Period: Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec 2026 = **5 months** (part month = full month).

### D13. Charge — Composition scheme eligibility and tax (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Mr. Gopal, a **trader** of goods in Maharashtra, had aggregate turnover of ₹80,00,000 in PY 2024-25 and validly opted into the **composition scheme** for FY 2025-26. | Inter ₹10,00,000 @18% | IGST 1,80,000 |
| Exempt ₹5,00,000 | Nil |

*Working Note 2 — RCM (paid in cash, Sec 49(4)):* GTA ₹1,00,000 @5% = CGST ₹2,500 + SGST ₹2,500 = **₹5,000 cash**. This becomes ITC.

*Working Note 3 — ITC pool:*
| Source | CGST | SGST |
|---|---|---|
| Purchases ₹15,00,000 @18% | 1,35,000 | 1,35,000 |
| RCM (GTA) re-credit | 2,500 | 2,500 |
| **Total ITC** | **1,37,500** | **1,37,500** |

*Working Note 4 — Utilisation (Sec 49A/49B, Rule 88A; CGST cannot pay SGST and vice-versa):*
| Liability | CGST | SGST | IGST |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output liability | 1,80,000 | 1,80,000 | 1,80,000 |
| Less: CGST ITC | (1,37,500) | — | — |
| Less: SGST ITC | — | (1,37,500) | — |
| **Cash on output** | **42,500** | **42,500** | **1,80,000** |

*Net cash payable:*
- Output: CGST 42,500 + SGST 42,500 + IGST 1,80,000 = ₹2,65,000
- RCM: CGST 2,500 + SGST 2,500 = ₹5,000
- **Total cash = ₹2,70,000.**

*Cross-check: total output tax 5,40,000 − total ITC 2,75,000 = 2,65,000 (output cash) + RCM 5,000 = ₹2,70,000 ✓.*

**> Marks tip:** Two things earn the big marks: (1) RCM tax on GTA is paid in **cash** and only then re-enters the ITC pool — never net it directly against output; (2) CGST ITC cannot discharge SGST liability (and vice-versa), which is why ₹42,500 of each survives as cash even though total ITC looks "enough". Always end with the reconciliation line.

### D1. GST — Exemptions (level: Easy)
**Problem:** Mr. Arjun provides the following during a month. Classify each as taxable or exempt and compute the **value of taxable supplies** and the **GST payable @ 18% (verify current)**:
(a) Transportation of agricultural produce by road (goods transport) — ₹40,000;
(b) Private coaching for a competitive examination — ₹2,00,000;
(c) Renting of a residential dwelling to an **unregistered** individual for use as his residence — ₹1,50,000;
(d) Interest earned on a fixed deposit with a bank — ₹25,000;
(e) Legal consultancy to a business entity (taxable service) — ₹3,00,000.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Classification:*
| Supply | Amount (₹) | Treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Transport of agricultural produce by road | 40,000 | **Exempt** | Specifically exempt (transport of agricultural produce) |
| (b) Coaching for competitive exam | 2,00,000 | **Taxable** | Only education by a recognised institution up to higher secondary / recognised qualification is exempt; private coaching is **not** |
| (c) Renting of residential dwelling for residence (to unregistered person) | 1,50,000 | **Exempt** | Renting of residential dwelling for use as residence is exempt (taxable only where let to a **registered** person) |
| (d) Interest on deposit | 25,000 | **Exempt** | Services by way of extending deposits/loans where consideration is interest are exempt |
| (e) Legal consultancy | 3,00,000 | **Taxable** | (Note: often payable by recipient under **RCM**; here computed as taxable value) |

*Working Note 2 — Totals:*
- Value of **taxable** supplies = 2,00,000 + 3,00,000 = **₹5,00,000**
- Value of **exempt** supplies = 40,000 + 1,50,000 + 25,000 = ₹2,15,000
- **GST payable @ 18% = 5,00,000 × 18% = ₹90,000** (CGST ₹45,000 + SGST ₹45,000 if intra-State)

**> Marks tip:** The trap items are coaching (taxable, not "education") and interest (exempt). State the *reason* for each classification — examiners award marks for the reasoning, not just the ₹90,000.

### D2. GST — Value of Supply (level: Easy)
**Problem:** Zenith Ltd sells a machine intra-State. List price ₹5,00,000; a **trade discount of 10%** is shown on the face of the invoice. In addition it charges: packing ₹15,000, installation ₹20,000, pre-delivery inspection ₹5,000. Compute the **transaction value** and GST **@ 18% (verify current)**.

**Solution:**

*Working Note — Value u/s 15:*
| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| List price | 5,00,000 |
| Less: Trade discount @10% (recorded on invoice → excluded, Sec 15(3)(a)) | (50,000) |
| Net price | 4,50,000 |
| Add: Packing charges (incidental expense, Sec 15(2)(c)) | 15,000 |
| Add: Installation charges (Sec 15(2)(c)) | 20,000 |
| Add: Inspection charges (Sec 15(2)(c)) | 5,000 |
| **Transaction value** | **4,90,000** |

- **GST @ 18% = 4,90,000 × 18% = ₹88,200** → CGST ₹44,100 + SGST ₹44,100.

**> Marks tip:** Discount shown on the invoice is *excluded*; all incidental expenses (packing/installation/inspection) charged before delivery are *included* under Sec 15(2)(c). Cite the sub-section beside each line.

### D3. GST — Time of Supply of Goods (level: Easy)
**Problem:** Goods (forward charge) are removed for delivery on **5 September**. The supplier actually issues the tax invoice **late, on 8 September**. Payment is received on **20 September**. Determine the **time of supply**.

**Solution:**

*Reasoning (Sec 12(2)):* Time of supply of goods = **earlier of** (i) date of invoice, or (ii) the **last date on which the invoice was required to be issued** u/s 31.
- For supply **involving movement**, invoice must be issued **at or before removal** → last date to issue = date of removal = **5 September**.
- Invoice was actually issued 8 September (later), so limb (ii) governs.
- Advance/payment is **not** relevant for goods (Notification 66/2017-CT: registered persons need not pay tax on advances for goods).

**Time of supply = 5 September.**

**> Marks tip:** The whole mark is in spotting that a *late* invoice throws you onto the "last date to issue" limb. Do **not** pick the payment date for goods.

### D4. GST — Registration Threshold (level: Easy)
**Problem:** Mr. Bose operates in a normal (non-special-category) State and supplies **only goods**, entirely **intra-State**. During the year: taxable supplies ₹25,00,000; exempt supplies ₹8,00,000; exports ₹5,00,000; value of inward supplies on which he pays tax under RCM ₹3,00,000. The threshold for goods is **₹40,00,000 (verify current)**. Is he liable to register?

**Solution:**

*Working Note — Aggregate turnover (Sec 2(6)):* All-India PAN-based total of taxable + exempt + exports + inter-State supplies, **excluding** central/State taxes and **excluding** inward supplies taxed under RCM.
| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Taxable supplies | 25,00,000 |
| Exempt supplies | 8,00,000 |
| Exports | 5,00,000 |
| Inward RCM supplies | Excluded |
| **Aggregate turnover** | **38,00,000** |

- ₹38,00,000 < ₹40,00,000 threshold **(verify current)**, and he makes **no** inter-State outward supply (which would trigger compulsory registration u/s 24). **He is NOT liable to register.**

**> Marks tip:** Inward RCM value is *excluded* from aggregate turnover — including it (→ ₹41 lakh) is the classic wrong answer. Also state the escape clause: if he had made an inter-State outward supply, Sec 24 would force registration regardless of turnover.

### D5. GST — Composite vs Mixed Supply (level: Easy)
**Problem:** A retailer makes two supplies for a **single price** each:
(i) A gift hamper containing sweets (5%), chocolates (18%), aerated drinks (28%) and dry fruits (12%) for ₹2,000;
(ii) Goods for ₹10,000 on which he arranges transit insurance and freight of ₹1,000, naturally bundled and supplied together, principal supply being the goods taxable @18%.
Determine the GST on each. **(rates verify current)**

**Solution:**

*Supply (i) — Mixed supply (Sec 2(74) r/w Sec 8(b)):* Items not naturally bundled, single price → taxed at the **highest rate** among them = **28%**.
- GST = 2,000 × 28% = **₹560**.

*Supply (ii) — Composite supply (Sec 2(30) r/w Sec 8(a)):* Naturally bundled, one is principal → taxed at the **rate of the principal supply** (goods @18%) on the **whole value** (10,000 + 1,000 = ₹11,000).
- GST = 11,000 × 18% = **₹1,980**.

**> Marks tip:** State the rule (Sec 8: mixed → highest rate; composite → principal-supply rate) *before* the number. Misclassifying the hamper as composite (and charging a lower rate) loses the core mark.

### D6. GST — Input Tax Credit: Blocked Credits (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Ganga Manufacturers Ltd (regular taxpayer) paid GST on the following inward supplies in a month. Compute **eligible ITC**, ignoring blocked credits u/s 17(5):
(a) Raw materials — ₹1,80,000;
(b) Motor car (seating 5) for the personal/business travel of a director — ₹1,00,000;
(c) Health insurance of employees (not obligatory under any law) — ₹30,000;
(d) Goods lost/stolen in the warehouse — ₹12,000;
(e) Works contract service for the **foundation of plant & machinery** — ₹50,000;
(f) Goods distributed as free samples — ₹8,000.

**Solution:**

*Working Note — Eligibility (Sec 16 & 17(5)):*
| Item | ₹ | ITC |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Raw materials | 1,80,000 | **Eligible** |
| (b) Motor car (≤13 seats, not for further supply/passenger transport/training) | 1,00,000 | Blocked — 17(5)(a) |
| (c) Employee health insurance (not obligatory by law) | 30,000 | Blocked — 17(5)(b) |
| (d) Goods lost/stolen | 12,000 | Blocked — 17(5)(h) |
| (e) Works contract for **foundation of P&M** (P&M is the exception) | 50,000 | **Eligible** — 17(5)(c) proviso |
| (f) Free samples ("goods disposed of by way of gift/free samples") | 8,000 | Blocked — 17(5)(h) |

- **Eligible ITC = 1,80,000 + 50,000 = ₹2,30,000.**

**> Marks tip:** The subtle one is (e): works-contract ITC is blocked for immovable property **except plant & machinery** — foundation/structural support to P&M qualifies. Quote the 17(5)(c) proviso.

### D7. GST — Value of Supply: Subsidy & Discount (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Meghna Ltd supplies goods, base price ₹1,00,000. It receives a price-linked subsidy of ₹10,000 from a **private third party** and ₹5,000 from the **State Government**. Packing ₹4,000 is charged. As per a pre-agreed scheme (known at the time of supply and linked to specific invoices), a post-supply discount of ₹3,000 is given and the recipient reverses the corresponding ITC. Separately, the buyer pays ₹2,000 as interest for late payment. Compute the value of supply and GST @18% **(verify current)**.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Value at the time of supply (Sec 15):*
| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Base price | 1,00,000 |
| Add: Subsidy from **non-Government** party, linked to price (Sec 15(2)(e) — only Govt subsidies excluded) | 10,000 |
| Add: Packing (Sec 15(2)(c)) | 4,000 |
| Less: State Government subsidy | Excluded |
| Less: Post-supply discount — pre-agreed, invoice-linked, ITC reversed (Sec 15(3)(b)) | (3,000) |
| **Transaction value** | **1,11,000** |

- GST @18% = 1,11,000 × 18% = **₹19,980**.

*Working Note 2 — Interest for delayed payment (Sec 15(2)(d)):* Includible in value, but taxable **when received**. ₹2,000 (treated as inclusive/additional consideration): GST @18% = **₹360** in the period of receipt.

**> Marks tip:** Two discriminators: **Government** subsidy is excluded but a **private** price-linked subsidy is included; and the post-supply discount is deductible **only** because it satisfies both conditions of Sec 15(3)(b) (pre-agreed + ITC reversed). State both conditions.

### D8. GST — Time of Supply under Reverse Charge (level: Medium)
**Problem:** A registered recipient is liable under **RCM** on an inward supply of **goods**. Supplier's invoice dated **1 October**; goods received **15 October**; payment made **5 November**; entry in recipient's books **16 October**. Determine the time of supply. Also state the rule for RCM on **services**.

**Solution:**

*Reasoning — Goods under RCM (Sec 12(3)):* **Earliest of**
- (a) date of receipt of goods = **15 Oct**;
- (b) date of payment = 5 Nov;
- (c) date immediately following **30 days** from invoice = ~31 Oct.

Earliest = **15 October** → **Time of supply = 15 October.**

*Services under RCM (Sec 13(3)) — for reference:* earliest of (a) date of payment, or (b) date immediately following **60 days** from invoice; if neither determinable, date of entry in the recipient's books.

**> Marks tip:** For goods RCM the "30-day" limb and "receipt of goods" limb both exist — quote all three limbs and pick the earliest. Don't confuse the 30-day (goods) vs 60-day (services) windows.

### D9. GST — Payment of Tax: Order of ITC Set-off (level: Medium)
**Problem:** For a tax period, output tax is IGST ₹30,000, CGST ₹25,000, SGST ₹25,000. ITC available: IGST ₹40,000, CGST ₹15,000, SGST ₹12,000. Determine the tax payable **in cash**, showing the utilisation order (Sec 49, 49A, 49B r/w Rule 88A).

**Solution:**

*Rule:* IGST credit must be **fully utilised first** (against IGST output, then CGST/SGST output in any order); only then may CGST credit and SGST credit be used against their own heads. CGST credit cannot pay SGST, and vice-versa.

*Working Note — Utilisation:*
| Step | Credit used | Against | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IGST 40,000 | IGST output 30,000 | 30,000 → IGST output nil |
| 2 | IGST balance 10,000 | CGST output | 10,000 → CGST output 25,000 − 10,000 = 15,000 |
| 3 | CGST 15,000 | CGST output 15,000 | CGST output nil |
| 4 | SGST 12,000 | SGST output 25,000 | SGST output 25,000 − 12,000 = 13,000 |

*Cash payable:* IGST **Nil**, CGST **Nil**, SGST **₹13,000**.

*Check:* Total output 80,000 − total ITC 67,000 = ₹13,000. ✓

**> Marks tip:** The examiner is testing the **mandatory IGST-credit-first** rule. Applying leftover IGST credit to CGST/SGST (Step 2) *before* touching CGST/SGST credit is what earns the marks; the cash figure follows automatically.

### D10. GST — ITC Apportionment: Rule 42 (level: Medium)
**Problem:** For a month a registered person has total input tax ₹5,00,000, of which: inputs exclusively for **non-business/personal** use ₹20,000 (T1); exclusively for **exempt** supplies ₹50,000 (T2); **blocked** u/s 17(5) ₹30,000 (T3); exclusively for **taxable (incl. zero-rated)** supplies ₹3,00,000 (T4). Exempt turnover ₹10,00,000; total turnover ₹40,00,000. Compute the eligible ITC and the amount to be **reversed**.

**Solution:**

*Working Note — Rule 42:*
| Step | Formula | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| C1 (credited to ECL) | T − (T1+T2+T3) = 5,00,000 − 1,00,000 | 4,00,000 |
| C2 (common credit) | C1 − T4 = 4,00,000 − 3,00,000 | 1,00,000 |
| D1 (attributable to exempt) | (E/F) × C2 = (10/40) × 1,00,000 | 25,000 |
| D2 (non-business, 5% of C2) | 5% × 1,00,000 | 5,000 |
| C3 (eligible common credit) | C2 − D1 − D2 = 1,00,000 − 25,000 − 5,000 | 70,000 |

- **Total eligible ITC = T4 + C3 = 3,00,000 + 70,000 = ₹3,70,000.**
- **Reversal (added to output liability) = D1 + D2 = 25,000 + 5,000 = ₹30,000.**

**> Marks tip:** Keep the C1/C2/C3 and D1/D2 labels exactly as in Rule 42 — the steps *are* the marks. D2 is a flat 5% of C2 (a proxy for personal use); don't forget it.

### D11. GST — Composition Scheme (level: Medium)
**Problem:** Mr. Krishna, a **trader of goods** in one State, had aggregate turnover of ₹90,00,000 last year and opts for the composition levy (limit ₹1.5 cr — **verify current**). This year his turnover of goods is ₹1,20,00,000 and he also earns ₹2,00,000 as **interest** on loans given. Compute his composition tax. Rate for traders **1% (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST) — verify current**.

**Solution:**

*Eligibility:* Prior-year turnover ₹90 lakh ≤ ₹1.5 cr → eligible. Interest income (exempt service) is **ignored** for the eligibility limit and for the "10% of turnover / ₹5 lakh" service cap (proviso to Sec 10).

*Working Note — Tax (Sec 10):* Trader pays **1% of turnover of taxable supplies of goods/services in the State**; exempt interest is not taxed.
- Composition tax = 1% × 1,20,00,000 = **₹1,20,000** → CGST ₹60,000 + SGST ₹60,000.

*Conditions:* Cannot collect tax from customers, cannot claim ITC, must issue a **bill of supply** (not a tax invoice), no inter-State outward supplies.

**> Marks tip:** The trap is the ₹2,00,000 interest — it is *disregarded* for both the eligibility limit and the tax base. Note the "no tax collection / no ITC / bill of supply" conditions to bag the full marks.

### D12. GST — Comprehensive Net Tax Payable with RCM (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Shakti Manufacturers Ltd (Maharashtra, regular) has the following for a month. All rates **18% (verify current)** unless stated; all invoices valid.
**Outward:** intra-State taxable supply ₹20,00,000; inter-State taxable supply ₹10,00,000.
**Inward:** intra-State raw material ₹12,00,000; inter-State raw material ₹6,00,000; a **motor car** (inter-State) ₹5,00,000 (blocked); intra-State **GTA freight** ₹1,00,000 taxable **@5% under RCM (verify current)**.
Compute the GST payable **in cash** under each head, showing set-off.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Output tax (forward charge):*
| Supply | Value | Tax |
|---|---|---|
| Intra-State @18% | 20,00,000 | CGST 1,80,000 + SGST 1,80,000 |
| Inter-State @18% | 10,00,000 | IGST 1,80,000 |

*Working Note 2 — RCM liability (GTA, intra-State @5%):* CGST ₹2,500 + SGST ₹2,500 — **payable in cash** (RCM cannot be discharged from ITC). This RCM tax then becomes **eligible ITC** in the same month.

*Working Note 3 — Eligible ITC:*
| Source | IGST | CGST | SGST |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intra RM (12,00,000 @18%) | — | 1,08,000 | 1,08,000 |
| Inter RM (6,00,000 @18%) | 1,08,000 | — | — |
| Motor car (blocked 17(5)(a)) | Nil | — | — |
| RCM GTA (paid in cash) | — | 2,500 | 2,500 |
| **Total ITC** | **1,08,000** | **1,10,500** | **1,10,500** |

*Working Note 4 — Set-off (IGST credit first):*
| Head | Output | Less ITC | Cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| IGST | 1,80,000 | 1,08,000 | **72,000** |
| CGST | 1,80,000 | 1,10,500 | **69,500** |
| SGST | 1,80,000 | 1,10,500 | **69,500** |

*Total cash payable:*
- Forward-charge cash = 72,000 + 69,500 + 69,500 = ₹2,11,000
- RCM cash (WN2) = 2,500 + 2,500 = ₹5,000
- **Grand total cash = ₹2,16,000.**

*Check:* Total liability (5,40,000 forward + 5,000 RCM) = 5,45,000; total ITC used = 3,29,000; cash = 5,45,000 − 3,29,000 = ₹2,16,000. ✓

**> Marks tip:** Two make-or-break points: (1) RCM tax is paid in **cash** and simultaneously becomes ITC — don't net it off; (2) motor-car ITC is **blocked**, so it never enters the credit pool. Both errors change the final cash figure.

### D13. GST — Value of Supply: Related Persons, Pure Agent & Exchange (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Determine the value of supply and GST @18% **(verify current)** in each case:
(a) **X Ltd** supplies goods to its subsidiary **Y Ltd** (related person). Invoice value ₹80,000; open market value ₹1,00,000. **Y is eligible for full ITC.**
(b) A **customs broker** pays customs duty of ₹50,000 on the client's behalf (reimbursed at actual, separately shown, pure-agent conditions met) and charges his own agency fee of ₹10,000.
(c) A dealer sells a new phone (list price ₹30,000); the customer gives an old phone valued ₹5,000 in part exchange and pays ₹25,000 cash.

**Solution:**

*(a) Related persons — Rule 28:* Value = OMV; but the **proviso** says where the recipient is eligible for **full ITC**, the value declared in the invoice is deemed to be the OMV. → **Value = ₹80,000**; GST = 80,000 × 18% = **₹14,400**.

*(b) Pure agent — Rule 33:* Reimbursement of customs duty is **excluded** (all pure-agent conditions met). → **Value = ₹10,000** (agency fee only); GST = 10,000 × 18% = **₹1,800**.

*(c) Consideration not wholly in money — Rule 27:* Value = **OMV of the new phone = ₹30,000** (not the ₹25,000 cash). → GST = 30,000 × 18% = **₹5,400**.

**> Marks tip:** Each sub-part hinges on the *right rule*: 28-proviso (full-ITC → invoice value), 33 (pure agent excludes reimbursement), 27 (part-exchange → OMV, not cash). Naming the wrong rule loses the sub-part even if arithmetic is fine.

### D14. GST — ITC on Capital Goods (Rule 43) + Interest on Reversal (level: Hard)
**Problem:**
(A) Bhima Ltd buys a machine used for **both taxable and exempt** supplies; GST on it ₹3,60,000. Exempt turnover in the month ₹15,00,000; total turnover ₹60,00,000. Compute the ITC to be **reversed for the month** under Rule 43 (useful life 60 months).
(B) The company had **availed and utilised** ITC of ₹90,000 in April that was later found **ineligible**, and reversed it after **92 days**. Compute interest u/s 50(3) @ **18% p.a. (verify current)**.

**Solution:**

*(A) Rule 43 — common capital-goods credit:*
| Step | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Tc (credited to ECL, spread over life) | 3,60,000 |
| Tm (monthly credit) = Tc ÷ 60 | 6,000 |
| Te (attributable to exempt) = (E/F) × Tm = (15,00,000/60,00,000) × 6,000 | **1,500** |

- **ITC reversed for the month = ₹1,500** (added to output tax liability). The eligible portion for the month = 6,000 − 1,500 = ₹4,500.

*(B) Interest u/s 50(3) — wrongly availed **and utilised** ITC @18%:*
- Interest = 90,000 × 18% × 92/365 = 16,200 × 92 ÷ 365 = **₹4,083** (rounded).

**> Marks tip:** In Rule 43 the credit is spread over **60 months** and only the exempt-attributable slice (Te) is reversed monthly — don't reverse the whole Tm. Interest u/s 50(3) applies only when ITC is both wrongly availed **and utilised**; show the day-count fraction.

### D15. GST — Returns, Interest & Late Fee (level: Hard)
**Problem:** Yamuna Traders' net tax payable **in cash** for April (GSTR-3B due **20 May**) is CGST ₹60,000 + SGST ₹60,000. The return is actually filed on **5 June** — **16 days late**. There were outward supplies (not a nil return). Compute (a) interest u/s 50(1) and (b) late fee u/s 47. Interest **18% p.a. (verify current)**; late fee **₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST per day (verify current)**.

**Solution:**

*(a) Interest u/s 50(1):* Charged only on the portion of tax paid **in cash** (net liability), for the period of delay (16 days).
| Head | Computation | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| CGST | 60,000 × 18% × 16/365 | 473 |
| SGST | 60,000 × 18% × 16/365 | 473 |
| **Total interest** | | **₹946** |

*(b) Late fee u/s 47:* ₹50/day (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST) × 16 days.
- Late fee = 50 × 16 = **₹800** (CGST ₹400 + SGST ₹400), subject to the prescribed maximum cap **(verify current)**. (A **nil** return would be ₹20/day.)

*Total additional payment = interest ₹946 + late fee ₹800 = ₹1,746.*

**> Marks tip:** Interest u/s 50(1) runs on the **net cash** liability, **not** on the ITC-set-off portion (post-2021 amendment) — computing 18% on gross tax over-states it. Keep interest and late fee (Sec 47) as **separate** line items.
