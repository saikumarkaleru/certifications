### Q1. Ch: AS 2 Inventory — Valuation at Lower of Cost or NRV (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Sunrise Textiles Ltd. manufactures a single product "FabX". As on 31st March, 2026, the following particulars relating to closing inventory are available. You are required to determine the value at which the closing inventory of 2,000 units should be stated in the Balance Sheet, in accordance with AS 2 (Revised).

| Particulars | Amount (₹ per unit) |
|---|---|
| Cost of purchase of raw material | 180 |
| Direct labour | 90 |
| Variable production overhead | 40 |
| Fixed production overhead (see WN) | — |
| Selling price | 360 |
| Estimated selling expenses per unit | 25 |

Additional information: Total fixed production overhead for the year was ₹6,00,000, incurred at a normal capacity of 20,000 units. Actual production during the year was 15,000 units. Freight inward on raw material already included in cost of purchase.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Absorption of Fixed Production Overhead**
As per AS 2, fixed production overheads are absorbed on the basis of **normal capacity**, not actual production, so that the low-production shortfall is charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss and is not carried in inventory.
Fixed overhead per unit = ₹6,00,000 ÷ 20,000 units (normal capacity) = **₹30 per unit**.
Since actual production (15,000 units) is below normal capacity, the rate of ₹30 is retained; the unabsorbed overhead on 5,000 units (₹1,50,000) is expensed.

**WN-2: Cost per unit**

| Element | ₹ per unit |
|---|---|
| Cost of purchase (raw material) | 180 |
| Direct labour | 90 |
| Variable production overhead | 40 |
| Fixed production overhead (WN-1) | 30 |
| **Total cost per unit** | **340** |

**WN-3: Net Realisable Value (NRV) per unit**
NRV = Estimated selling price − Estimated selling expenses = ₹360 − ₹25 = **₹335 per unit**.

**Statement Showing Valuation of Closing Inventory (AS 2)**

| Particulars | Cost (₹) | NRV (₹) | Lower (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per unit | 340 | 335 | 335 |
| Closing inventory = 2,000 units × ₹335 | | | **6,70,000** |

Inventory is valued at lower of cost (₹340) and NRV (₹335); as NRV is lower, ₹335 is adopted.

**Answer:** Closing inventory of 2,000 units of FabX = **₹6,70,000**.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards absorbing fixed overhead on normal capacity — using actual production (₹40/unit) is the classic error. Selling expenses are deducted for NRV but never added to cost; state the AS 2 "lower of cost or NRV" rule explicitly on an item-by-item basis.)*

### Q2. Ch: AS 2 Inventory — Treatment of Various Costs (Excluded Costs) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** State, with reasons, how each of the following items should be dealt with while determining the cost of inventories as per AS 2 (Revised) "Valuation of Inventories": (i) Abnormal amount of wasted materials; (ii) Storage costs necessary in the production process before a further production stage; (iii) Selling and distribution costs; (iv) Trade discounts and duty drawbacks recoverable from taxing authorities; (v) Interest on a borrowing specifically to finance inventory that ordinarily takes a long time to get ready for sale.

**Answer:**
As per **AS 2 (Revised)**, the cost of inventories comprises all costs of purchase, costs of conversion and other costs incurred in bringing the inventories to their present location and condition. Certain costs are specifically **excluded** and recognised as expenses of the period.

| Item | Treatment under AS 2 | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Abnormal wasted materials | **Excluded** from cost; charged to Statement of P&L | Abnormal amounts of wasted material, labour or other production costs are not "normal" conversion costs and hence are expensed in the period incurred. |
| (ii) Storage cost necessary in the production process before a further stage | **Included** in cost of inventory | Storage that is a necessary part of the production process (e.g., maturing of wine) is a cost of bringing inventory to its present condition. |
| (iii) Selling and distribution costs | **Excluded** from cost | These are incurred after inventory reaches saleable condition; they are period costs. |
| (iv) Trade discounts and duty drawbacks recoverable | **Deducted** in determining cost of purchase | Cost of purchase excludes trade discounts, rebates and other similar items and duties subsequently recoverable from taxing authorities. |
| (v) Interest on specific borrowing for inventory that takes a long time to be ready for sale | **Included** to the extent permitted by AS 16 | Ordinarily interest is excluded, but where inventories are qualifying assets (require a substantial period to get ready for sale), borrowing costs may be capitalised per AS 16. |

**Conclusion:** Only costs incurred to bring inventory to its present location and condition are inventoriable; abnormal wastage, selling costs and (ordinarily) interest are expensed, while necessary process storage and recoverable-duty adjustments follow the specific AS 2 rules above.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite AS 2 for exclusions and cross-reference AS 16 for item (v). Deductions of trade discount/duty drawback are commonly stated incompletely — mention "duties subsequently recoverable from taxing authorities.")*

### Q3. Ch: AS 2 Inventory — Retail Method & Reversal of Write-down (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Verma Retail Stores Ltd. values its inventory of a garment line by the retail method. In the previous year, the inventory of "Line-J" was written down from a cost of ₹8,00,000 to NRV of ₹6,50,000 because of a fall in market prices. During the current year, prices recovered. As on 31st March, 2026, the same lot is still on hand; its original cost remains ₹8,00,000 and its NRV is now assessed at ₹8,90,000. Separately, for a fresh purchase of "Line-K", the following retail-method data is available.

| Particulars | Cost (₹) | Retail (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Opening inventory | 1,20,000 | 2,00,000 |
| Purchases during the year | 9,00,000 | 14,00,000 |
| Sales during the year | — | 13,20,000 |

Determine (a) the value at which "Line-J" should be carried, and (b) the cost of closing inventory of "Line-K" under the retail method as per AS 2.

**Solution:**

**Part (a): Reversal of write-down — Line-J**
Per AS 2, when the circumstances that previously caused inventories to be written down below cost no longer exist, the amount of the write-down is **reversed** so that the new carrying amount is the **lower of cost and revised NRV**.
- Cost = ₹8,00,000; Revised NRV = ₹8,90,000; Lower = **₹8,00,000**.
- The reversal is limited to the amount of the original write-down (₹1,50,000); inventory cannot be written up above cost.

**Carrying value of Line-J = ₹8,00,000.**

**Part (b): Retail method — Line-K**

**WN-1: Cost-to-retail ratio**
Goods available for sale: Cost = ₹1,20,000 + ₹9,00,000 = ₹10,20,000; Retail = ₹2,00,000 + ₹14,00,000 = ₹16,00,000.
Cost-to-retail ratio = ₹10,20,000 ÷ ₹16,00,000 = **63.75%**.

**WN-2: Closing inventory at retail**
Closing inventory (retail) = Goods available at retail − Sales = ₹16,00,000 − ₹13,20,000 = **₹2,80,000**.

**Statement of Closing Inventory — Line-K (Retail Method)**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Closing inventory at retail | 2,80,000 |
| Cost-to-retail ratio | 63.75% |
| **Closing inventory at cost** = ₹2,80,000 × 63.75% | **1,78,500** |

**Answer:** (a) Line-J carried at **₹8,00,000** (reversal restricted to cost); (b) Line-K closing inventory at cost = **₹1,78,500**.

*(Full-marks tip: The reversal is capped at original cost — writing Line-J up to ₹8,90,000 is the trap. In the retail method, always compute the ratio on goods available for sale, not on purchases alone.)*

### Q4. Ch: AS 7 Construction Contracts — Percentage of Completion Method (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Bridgeway Infra Ltd. undertook a construction contract with a fixed contract price of ₹120 lakhs. The contract was expected to be completed in 3 years. The following data relates to the contract as on 31st March, 2026 (end of Year 2). Compute, as per AS 7, the profit/loss to be recognised in Year 2, showing separately the amounts recognised up to Year 1 and in Year 2.

| Particulars | Up to end of Year 1 (₹ lakhs) | Up to end of Year 2 (₹ lakhs) |
|---|---|---|
| Contract costs incurred to date | 30 | 78 |
| Estimated further costs to complete | 60 | 26 |
| Progress billings raised | 28 | 70 |
| Amounts received from customer | 24 | 60 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Total estimated contract cost and outcome**

| Particulars | Year 1 (₹ lakhs) | Year 2 (₹ lakhs) |
|---|---|---|
| Costs incurred to date | 30 | 78 |
| Estimated further costs | 60 | 26 |
| **Total estimated cost** | **90** | **104** |
| Contract price | 120 | 120 |
| **Total estimated profit** | **30** | **16** |

**WN-2: Stage (percentage) of completion (cost-to-cost method)**
- Year 1: 30 ÷ 90 = 33.33%
- Year 2: 78 ÷ 104 = **75%**

**WN-3: Profit to be recognised**
Cumulative profit = Total estimated profit × Stage of completion.
- Up to Year 1: ₹30 lakhs × 33.33% = **₹10 lakhs**
- Up to Year 2: ₹16 lakhs × 75% = **₹12 lakhs**
- Profit to be recognised **in Year 2** = ₹12 − ₹10 = **₹2 lakhs**

**Statement Showing Recognition of Contract Revenue, Costs and Profit (AS 7) — Year 2**

| Particulars | Cumulative to Yr 2 (₹ lakhs) | Recognised to Yr 1 (₹ lakhs) | Recognised in Yr 2 (₹ lakhs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract revenue (₹120 × 75% / 33.33%) | 90.00 | 40.00 | 50.00 |
| Contract cost (₹104 × 75% / 33.33%) | 78.00 | 30.00 | 48.00 |
| **Profit** | **12.00** | **10.00** | **2.00** |

**Answer:** Profit recognised in Year 2 = **₹2 lakhs** (cumulative profit ₹12 lakhs less ₹10 lakhs already recognised in Year 1).

*(Full-marks tip: Recognise cumulative profit then deduct prior-period profit — computing Year-2 profit on incremental costs alone is wrong. Contract revenue = price × stage; the examiner wants revenue and cost shown, not just the profit figure.)*

### Q5. Ch: AS 7 Construction Contracts — Foreseeable Loss (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Skyline Constructions Ltd. is executing a contract with a total contract price of ₹300 lakhs. As on 31st March, 2026, the following information is available. Determine the amount of loss, if any, to be recognised in the Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ended 31st March, 2026 as per AS 7, and pass the relevant journal treatment reasoning.

| Particulars | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Contract price | 300 |
| Costs incurred up to 31.03.2026 | 210 |
| Estimated further costs to complete | 130 |
| Progress billings certified up to 31.03.2026 | 190 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Total estimated cost and expected outcome**

| Particulars | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Costs incurred to date | 210 |
| Estimated further costs | 130 |
| **Total estimated cost** | **340** |
| Contract price | 300 |
| **Expected total loss on contract** | **(40)** |

Since total estimated costs (₹340 lakhs) exceed contract price (₹300 lakhs), the contract is a **loss-making (onerous) contract**.

**WN-2: Stage of completion**
Stage = Costs incurred ÷ Total estimated cost = 210 ÷ 340 = **61.76%**.

**WN-3: Recognition of loss under AS 7**
AS 7 requires that when it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue, the **expected loss is recognised as an expense immediately**, irrespective of the stage of completion and the amount of work performed.

| Particulars | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Contract revenue recognised (₹300 × 61.76%) | 185.29 |
| Contract costs recognised (₹340 × 61.76%) | 210.00 |
| Loss on work done to date | (24.71) |
| Add: Provision for foreseeable future loss (balance) | (15.29) |
| **Total loss to be recognised now** | **(40.00)** |

**Answer:** The entire foreseeable loss of **₹40 lakhs** must be recognised as an expense in the year ended 31st March, 2026, even though only 61.76% of the work is complete.

*(Full-marks tip: The whole ₹40 lakhs loss is booked immediately — recognising only the proportionate ₹24.71 lakhs is the standard error. State the AS 7 rule that expected loss is recognised "immediately as an expense" regardless of stage of completion.)*

### Q6. Ch: AS 7 Construction Contracts — Amounts Due To/From Customers & Disclosure (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** From the following particulars of a single construction contract of Meridian Builders Ltd. as on 31st March, 2026, you are required to compute (a) the amount of "Gross amount due from/to customers for contract work" and (b) the amounts to be disclosed as per AS 7.

| Particulars | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Contract costs incurred to date | 88 |
| Recognised profits to date | 22 |
| Recognised losses to date | Nil |
| Progress billings raised to date | 105 |
| Advances received | 15 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Costs incurred plus recognised profits**
As per AS 7, the gross amount due from/to customers = (Costs incurred + Recognised profits) − (Recognised losses + Progress billings).

| Particulars | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Contract costs incurred to date | 88 |
| Add: Recognised profits | 22 |
| Less: Recognised losses | Nil |
| **Sub-total** | **110** |
| Less: Progress billings | (105) |
| **Gross amount due FROM customers** | **5** |

Since the figure is positive (₹110 lakhs > ₹105 lakhs), it is presented as an **asset — "Gross amount due from customers for contract work."**

**WN-2: Disclosures required by AS 7**

| Disclosure item | ₹ lakhs |
|---|---|
| Contract revenue recognised | (Costs 88 + Profit 22) = 110 |
| Aggregate costs incurred + recognised profits | 110 |
| Progress billings | 105 |
| Advances received | 15 |
| Gross amount due from customers (asset) | 5 |

**Answer:** Gross amount **due from customers = ₹5 lakhs**, shown as an asset; advances of ₹15 lakhs are disclosed separately as a liability, not netted against this amount.

*(Full-marks tip: The formula is (costs + recognised profits − recognised losses) − progress billings. Advances are disclosed separately and never deducted in arriving at the gross amount due — mixing them in is the common deduction.)*

### Q7. Ch: AS 7 Construction Contracts — Contract Costs & Combining/Segmenting (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** (a) State the costs that are attributable to contract activity in general and can be allocated to a specific construction contract as per AS 7. (b) When should a group of contracts be treated as a single construction contract under AS 7?

**Answer:**
**(a) Costs allocable to a specific contract (AS 7):** Contract costs comprise (i) costs that relate **directly** to the specific contract; (ii) costs that are **attributable to contract activity in general** and can be allocated to the contract; and (iii) such other costs as are **specifically chargeable to the customer** under the terms of the contract. Costs attributable to contract activity in general and allocable to specific contracts include:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Insurance | Insurance premium relating to construction activity |
| Design and technical assistance | Costs of design and technical assistance not directly related to a specific contract |
| Construction overheads | Costs such as preparation and processing of construction personnel payroll, allocated on a systematic and rational basis based on the normal level of construction activity |

Borrowing costs may also be included where AS 16 conditions are met.

**(b) Combining contracts as a single contract:** A group of contracts, whether with a single customer or several customers, should be treated as a **single construction contract** when: (i) the group of contracts is negotiated as a **single package**; (ii) the contracts are so closely **interrelated** that they are, in effect, part of a single project with an overall profit margin; and (iii) the contracts are performed **concurrently or in a continuous sequence**.

**Conclusion:** Only directly related, allocable-general and customer-chargeable costs form contract cost; a package of interrelated, concurrently performed contracts negotiated together is accounted for as one contract under AS 7.

*(Full-marks tip: List all three cost categories with the "systematic and rational allocation on normal level of activity" phrase for overheads. For combining, all three conditions must be stated together.)*

### Q8. Ch: AS 9 Revenue Recognition — Timing of Sale of Goods (Special Situations) (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** State, with reasons, whether and when revenue should be recognised as per AS 9 "Revenue Recognition" in each of the following independent situations for the year ended 31st March, 2026:
(i) Goods sold on 28th March, 2026 for ₹5,00,000 are subject to installation and inspection; installation is completed on 10th April, 2026 and is a significant part of the contract.
(ii) Goods worth ₹2,00,000 despatched to an agent on a "sale or return" basis on 20th March, 2026; the buyer's acceptance is still awaited on 31st March, 2026.
(iii) A company sells ₹8,00,000 of goods to a customer on 15th March, 2026 and simultaneously agrees to buy them back on 15th June, 2026 at ₹8,40,000 (a financing arrangement).
(iv) Delivery of goods worth ₹3,50,000 is delayed at the buyer's request on 30th March, 2026, but the buyer has taken title and accepted billing.

**Answer:**
Per **AS 9**, revenue from the sale of goods is recognised when (a) the seller has transferred to the buyer the property in the goods (or all significant risks and rewards of ownership) and retains no effective control, and (b) no significant uncertainty exists regarding the amount of consideration and its collectability.

| Situation | Recognition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Sale subject to significant installation & inspection | **Not in FY 2025-26**; recognise on 10th April, 2026 | AS 9 provides that when installation is a significant part of the contract not yet complete, revenue is recognised only on completion of installation and buyer's acceptance. |
| (ii) Sale or return | **Not recognised** on 31.03.2026 | Under sale-or-return, revenue is recognised only when the goods are formally accepted by the buyer or the time to reject has elapsed; acceptance is awaited. |
| (iii) Sale with buy-back (financing arrangement) | **No revenue**; treat as a financing transaction | In substance the risks and rewards are retained by the seller; the ₹40,000 differential is financing cost, not sale revenue. Substance over form applies. |
| (iv) Delivery delayed at buyer's request ("bill and hold") | **Recognise ₹3,50,000 in FY 2025-26** | Buyer has taken title, accepted billing and delivery is delayed only at the buyer's request; risks and rewards have passed, so revenue is recognised. |

**Conclusion:** Revenue is recognised in situation (iv) only; situations (i) and (ii) are deferred until the earning process is complete, and situation (iii) is a financing arrangement with no sale revenue.

*(Full-marks tip: Each item must cite the risks-and-rewards / no-significant-uncertainty tests. Situation (iii) is the discriminator — recognising ₹8,00,000 there ignores substance over form.)*

### Q9. Ch: AS 9 Revenue Recognition — Rendering of Services (Proportionate Completion) (Marks: 5) [Problem]
**Question:** Zenith Advisory Ltd. entered into a service contract for ₹24,00,000 to provide consultancy over an assignment estimated to require 800 professional hours. As on 31st March, 2026, 480 hours have been rendered. The company had received an advance of ₹10,00,000. Additionally, an annual maintenance contract (AMC) of ₹3,60,000 was signed on 1st October, 2025 covering 12 months of routine servicing performed evenly. Compute the revenue to be recognised for the year ended 31st March, 2026 as per AS 9, stating the method applied.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Consultancy — Proportionate completion method**
For rendering of services, AS 9 permits recognition either by the **proportionate completion method** or the **completed service contract method**. Where services are performed by more than one act over a period, the proportionate completion method is appropriate.
Stage of completion = Hours rendered ÷ Total estimated hours = 480 ÷ 800 = **60%**.
Revenue = ₹24,00,000 × 60% = **₹14,40,000**.
(The advance of ₹10,00,000 does not determine revenue; it is only a receipt.)

**WN-2: AMC — Even (time-proportion) performance**
The AMC service is performed evenly over 12 months; revenue is recognised on a straight-line basis.
Period from 1.10.2025 to 31.3.2026 = 6 months.
Revenue = ₹3,60,000 × 6/12 = **₹1,80,000**.

**Statement of Revenue to be Recognised (AS 9) — Year ended 31.03.2026**

| Service | Basis | Revenue (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Consultancy assignment | Proportionate completion (60%) | 14,40,000 |
| Annual maintenance contract | Time-proportion (6/12) | 1,80,000 |
| **Total revenue recognised** | | **16,20,000** |

**Answer:** Total service revenue for the year = **₹16,20,000** (Consultancy ₹14,40,000 + AMC ₹1,80,000).

*(Full-marks tip: Do not equate revenue with the advance received. AMC revenue must be time-apportioned for exactly 6 months (Oct–Mar); recognising the full ₹3,60,000 is the trap.)*

### Q10. Ch: AS 9 Revenue Recognition — Effect of Uncertainty & Interest/Dividend/Royalty (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** State, with reasons, the amount of revenue to be recognised in the books of Falcon Ltd. for the year ended 31st March, 2026 as per AS 9 in each case:
(i) Sales of ₹6,00,000 made during the year; subsequently the customer became bankrupt and ₹1,50,000 is now doubtful. When the sale was made there was no uncertainty about collection.
(ii) Interest of ₹90,000 accrued on a fixed deposit for the year on a time-proportion basis.
(iii) Dividend of ₹40,000 on shares held; the investee company declared the dividend in its AGM on 5th April, 2026.
(iv) Royalty of ₹1,20,000 accrued as per the terms of a licensing agreement for use of the company's patent during the year.

**Answer:**
Per **AS 9**, revenue arising from the use by others of enterprise resources yielding interest, royalties and dividends is recognised as follows: **interest** on a time-proportion basis; **royalties** on an accrual basis per the relevant agreement; and **dividends** when the owner's right to receive payment is established.

| Situation | Recognition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Sale of ₹6,00,000; ₹1,50,000 later doubtful | Recognise full **₹6,00,000** as revenue; provide ₹1,50,000 as **bad-debt expense** | AS 9: where uncertainty about collectability arises **after** revenue was recognised, the uncollectable amount is a separate expense, not a reduction of the revenue already recognised. |
| (ii) Interest ₹90,000 | Recognise **₹90,000** | Interest is recognised on a time-proportion basis taking into account the amount outstanding and the rate applicable. |
| (iii) Dividend declared 5.4.2026 | **Not recognised** in FY 2025-26 | The right to receive payment is established only on declaration (5th April, 2026), which falls in the next year. |
| (iv) Royalty ₹1,20,000 | Recognise **₹1,20,000** | Royalties accrue in accordance with the terms of the relevant agreement and are recognised on that basis. |

**Conclusion:** Revenue recognised in FY 2025-26 = Sales ₹6,00,000 + Interest ₹90,000 + Royalty ₹1,20,000 = **₹8,10,000**; the dividend is recognised only in the next year and the doubtful debt is a separate expense.

*(Full-marks tip: The distinction in (i) — uncertainty arising post-recognition does not reduce revenue but creates an expense — is the graded point. Dividend timing (right established on declaration) must be tied to the AGM date.)*

### Q11. Ch: AS 10 Property, Plant & Equipment — Cost of an Item (Marks: 6) [Problem]
**Question:** Orion Manufacturing Ltd. acquired a new machine. From the following details, compute the cost at which the machine should be capitalised as per AS 10 (Revised) "Property, Plant and Equipment". Give reasons for inclusion or exclusion of each item.

| Freight and insurance in transit | 1,20,000 |

| Cost of trial runs (net of ₹30,000 realised from sale of samples produced) | 90,000 |
| Administration and general overhead | 60,000 |
| Estimated dismantling & site restoration cost (present value) | 1,80,000 |

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Items to be included / excluded**

| Item | Include? | Reason (AS 10) |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price ₹40,00,000 less trade discount ₹2,00,000 | Include net ₹38,00,000 | Cost of purchase is net of trade discounts and rebates. |

| Trial run cost (net) ₹90,000 | Include | Cost of testing whether the asset functions properly, net of sale proceeds of items produced during testing. |
| Administration & general overhead ₹60,000 | Exclude | General overheads are not directly attributable. |
| Dismantling & restoration (PV) ₹1,80,000 | Include | Initial estimate of dismantling/restoration obligation forms part of cost. |

**Statement Showing Cost of Machine to be Capitalised (AS 10)**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | 40,00,000 |
| Less: Trade discount | (2,00,000) |
| Net purchase price | 38,00,000 |
| Add: Freight and insurance | 1,20,000 |
| Add: Installation and site preparation | 2,50,000 |
| Add: Trial run cost (net of sample sales) | 90,000 |
| Add: Dismantling & restoration cost (PV) | 1,80,000 |
| **Cost to be capitalised** | **44,40,000** |

**Answer:** The machine should be capitalised at **₹44,40,000**.

*(Full-marks tip: GST with available ITC is excluded (recoverable tax); trial-run cost is netted against sample sale proceeds; dismantling PV is added. Capitalising the initial operating loss or admin overhead is the standard deduction.)*

### Q12. Ch: AS 10 Property, Plant & Equipment — Revaluation Model (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Cobalt Industries Ltd. owns a building carried under the cost model at 1st April, 2023 at a cost of ₹50,00,000 with an estimated useful life of 25 years (nil residual value), depreciated on the straight-line basis. On 1st April, 2025 the company adopted the revaluation model and the building was revalued to ₹60,00,000. On 1st April, 2026 a further revaluation reduced its fair value to ₹40,00,000. Show the accounting treatment (with amounts and journal reasoning) for the revaluations as per AS 10 (Revised). The company transfers no excess depreciation to retained earnings.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Carrying amount on 1st April, 2025 (date of first revaluation)**
Annual depreciation (cost model) = ₹50,00,000 ÷ 25 = ₹2,00,000.
Depreciation for 2 years (2023-24, 2024-25) = ₹4,00,000.
Carrying amount on 1.4.2025 = ₹50,00,000 − ₹4,00,000 = **₹46,00,000**.

**WN-2: First revaluation (upward) on 1.4.2025**
Revalued amount ₹60,00,000 − Carrying amount ₹46,00,000 = **₹14,00,000 upward revaluation**.
As per AS 10, an increase on revaluation is credited to **Revaluation Surplus** (through Other Comprehensive Income / directly to owners' funds), unless it reverses a previous decrease charged to P&L (here none).
**Revaluation Surplus created = ₹14,00,000.**

### Q15. Ch: AS 10 Property, Plant & Equipment — Exchange of Assets & Government Grant (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Answer the following as per AS 10 (Revised), with reasons:
(a) Pioneer Ltd. acquired a new machine by exchanging an old machine (carrying amount ₹6,00,000, fair value ₹7,50,000) plus paying ₹2,00,000 cash. The transaction has commercial substance and the fair value of the acquired machine is reliably measurable. At what amount should the new machine be recorded?
(b) Same facts, except the exchange **lacks commercial substance**. At what amount is the new machine recorded?
(c) Redwood Ltd. received a government grant of ₹5,00,000 towards a machine costing ₹20,00,000. How is the machine's cost and the grant to be presented, given AS 10 requires the cost of an item of PP&E not to be reduced by a grant except as permitted by AS 12?

**Answer:**
**(a) Exchange with commercial substance:** Per AS 10, when an item of PP&E is acquired in exchange for a non-monetary asset and the transaction has **commercial substance**, the cost is measured at the **fair value of the asset given up** (adjusted for cash paid/received), unless the fair value of the asset acquired is more clearly evident.
Cost of new machine = Fair value of old machine ₹7,50,000 + Cash paid ₹2,00,000 = **₹9,50,000**.
(A gain of ₹1,50,000 — fair value ₹7,50,000 less carrying amount ₹6,00,000 — arises on the asset given up.)

**(b) Exchange lacking commercial substance:** Where the transaction lacks commercial substance (or fair value is not reliably measurable), the cost of the acquired asset is measured at the **carrying amount of the asset given up** (plus cash paid).
Cost of new machine = Carrying amount ₹6,00,000 + Cash paid ₹2,00,000 = **₹8,00,000**. No gain is recognised.

**(c) Government grant related to PP&E:** AS 10 states the cost of an item of PP&E is not reduced by government grants except as permitted by **AS 12 "Accounting for Government Grants"**. Under AS 12 either (i) the grant is **deducted from the gross value of the asset** (machine shown at ₹20,00,000 − ₹5,00,000 = **₹15,00,000**, with depreciation on ₹15,00,000), or (ii) the grant is treated as **deferred income** and recognised in P&L over the asset's useful life (machine at full ₹20,00,000).

**Conclusion:** New machine at **₹9,50,000** (with substance) or **₹8,00,000** (without substance); the grant is presented per AS 12 as either a deduction from the asset (₹15,00,000) or as deferred income.

*(Full-marks tip: Commercial substance decides fair-value vs carrying-amount measurement — reversing them is the error. For the grant, cite the AS 10 cross-reference to AS 12 and give both permitted presentations.)*

### Q16. Ch: AS 9 Revenue Recognition — Cross-topic with AS 7 (Real Estate / Long-term Service) (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between the recognition of revenue for the **sale of goods** and for the **rendering of services** under AS 9, and state how the recognition principle for the rendering of services differs from the percentage-of-completion method under AS 7.

**Answer:**
Under **AS 9**, both categories require that no significant uncertainty exists regarding the amount of consideration and its ultimate collection; the timing of transfer differs.

| Basis | Sale of Goods (AS 9) | Rendering of Services (AS 9) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger for recognition | Transfer of property in goods / significant risks and rewards of ownership to the buyer | Performance of the service |
| Method | Point of sale | **Proportionate completion method** or **completed service contract method** |
| Measurement of performance | Not applicable — recognised at a point | By reference to the acts performed / completion of service |

**Difference from AS 7 percentage-of-completion:** AS 7 mandates the **percentage-of-completion method** for construction contracts, and — importantly — requires an **expected loss to be recognised immediately** as an expense regardless of stage of completion. AS 9's proportionate completion method for services does not have this specific immediate-loss provision; it is applied only where the outcome can be reliably estimated, and AS 9 explicitly **scopes out** construction contracts (which fall under AS 7).

**Conclusion:** Sale of goods is recognised at the point of transfer of risks and rewards; services are recognised as performed (proportionate completion or completed contract), while construction contracts follow AS 7's percentage-of-completion with immediate loss recognition.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner wants the scope boundary — construction contracts are excluded from AS 9 and governed by AS 7 — plus AS 7's immediate-loss rule as the key contrast.)*

### Q18. Ch: AS 4 — Contingencies and Events Occurring After the Balance Sheet Date (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Sunrise Textiles Ltd. finalised its accounts for the year ended 31st March, 2026 on 20th June, 2026 (date of approval by the Board). Give your treatment, with reasons under AS 4, for each of the following independent events:

| S.No. | Event |
|-------|-------|
| (i) | A debtor of ₹18,00,000 outstanding on 31.03.2026 was declared insolvent on 12.05.2026 and only 20% is now recoverable. |
| (ii) | The company's warehouse and stock (WDV ₹40,00,000) were destroyed by a flood on 28.05.2026. |
| (iii) | The Board proposed a dividend of ₹6,00,000 on 20.06.2026 for the year 2025-26. |
| (iv) | A suit filed against the company by a supplier was decided against it on 30.04.2026, fixing the liability at ₹7,50,000 (the case was pending on the balance sheet date). |
| (v) | Due to a fire on 15.06.2026 in a competitor's plant, market prices of the company's finished goods rose sharply, giving an expected extra profit of ₹9,00,000. |

**Answer:**
AS 4 "Contingencies and Events Occurring After the Balance Sheet Date" governs events that occur between the balance sheet date (31.03.2026) and the date on which the financial statements are approved by the Board (20.06.2026). Such events are classified as **adjusting events** (those providing further evidence of conditions that *existed* at the balance sheet date) and **non-adjusting events** (those concerning conditions that *arose after* the balance sheet date). Adjusting events require amounts in the financial statements to be adjusted; non-adjusting events are ordinarily not adjusted but are disclosed if material.

**(i) Insolvency of debtor — Adjusting event.** The debtor was outstanding on the balance sheet date and his insolvency on 12.05.2026 provides additional evidence of a condition (his weak financial position) that *existed* on 31.03.2026. Hence the company must adjust and write down the debtor. A provision for doubtful debts of ₹14,40,000 (80% of ₹18,00,000) must be created and only ₹3,60,000 carried as recoverable.

**(ii) Flood destroying warehouse and stock — Non-adjusting event.** The flood occurred on 28.05.2026, i.e. the condition arose *after* the balance sheet date; the assets existed and were sound on 31.03.2026. It is therefore a non-adjusting event and no adjustment is made to the 2025-26 accounts. However, since the loss of ₹40,00,000 is material and may affect the *going concern* assumption, its nature and financial effect should be **disclosed** in the notes; if going concern is impaired, AS 4 requires a fundamental change in the basis of accounting.

**(iii) Proposed dividend — Adjusting/disclosure.** As per the amended AS 4 (consequent to the Companies (Accounting Standards) Amendment), dividends proposed *after* the balance sheet date are **not** recognised as a liability at the balance sheet date; they are only **disclosed** in the notes to accounts. Hence ₹6,00,000 is not provided but disclosed.

**(iv) Court decision on a pending suit — Adjusting event.** The suit was pending on 31.03.2026, so a *present obligation* existed on that date. The decision on 30.04.2026 confirms the existence and amount of the obligation. The company must adjust and create a **provision of ₹7,50,000**.

**(v) Price rise from competitor's fire — Non-adjusting event, no disclosure of profit.** The event arose after the balance sheet date and merely relates to an *anticipated* future profit which is not certain. As per prudence, anticipated profits are not recognised; no adjustment and no provision is made.

**Conclusion:** Adjust for (i) ₹14,40,000 provision and (iv) ₹7,50,000 provision; disclose (ii) ₹40,00,000 flood loss and (iii) ₹6,00,000 proposed dividend; ignore (v).

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the correct "existed vs arose" test for each item and the amended treatment of proposed dividend (disclose, not provide). Marks are lost for providing for proposed dividend or for adjusting the flood loss.)*

### Q19. Ch: AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** State, with reasons under AS 5, how each of the following should be dealt with by Meridian Ltd. in its financial statements for the year ended 31st March, 2026:

| S.No. | Situation |
|-------|-----------|
| (i) | The company changed its method of inventory valuation from FIFO to weighted average, raising the current year's profit by ₹4,20,000. |
| (ii) | Depreciation of ₹1,10,000 relating to a machine was omitted by mistake in the financial statements of 2024-25 and is detected now. |
| (iii) | The useful life of a plant was revised from 10 years to 7 years based on technical assessment, increasing this year's depreciation by ₹2,00,000. |
| (iv) | A provision for warranty made last year at ₹3,00,000 is now found to be excess by ₹80,000 as actual claims were lower. |

**Answer:**
AS 5 deals with the treatment of ordinary items, extraordinary items, prior period items, changes in accounting estimates and changes in accounting policies, so that the statements are comparable over time and across enterprises.

**(i) Change in method of inventory valuation — Change in accounting policy.** A shift from FIFO to weighted average is a **change in an accounting policy** (a change in the basis of measurement). As per AS 5, such a change is permitted only if required by statute or an accounting standard, or if it results in a more appropriate presentation. The effect of the change, being material, must be **quantified and disclosed** — the increase in profit of ₹4,20,000 and the reasons for the change are shown in the notes. There is no retrospective restatement under AS 5; the effect is recognised in the current period.

**(ii) Omission of depreciation of an earlier year — Prior period item.** Depreciation of ₹1,10,000 that ought to have been charged in 2024-25 was omitted due to an error. This is a **prior period item** — income or expense arising from errors or omissions in the preparation of prior periods' statements. As per AS 5, it must be **separately disclosed** in the current statement of profit and loss (either shown as a separate line or in notes) so that its impact on the current profit is understood; it is charged in 2025-26 as ₹1,10,000.

**(iii) Revision of useful life of plant — Change in accounting estimate.** Revising the useful life from 10 to 7 years is a **change in an accounting estimate**, not a policy. As per AS 5, the effect of a change in estimate is recognised **prospectively** in the period of change (and future periods). The additional depreciation of ₹2,00,000 is charged in the current year; no restatement of past periods is done. If material, it is disclosed.

**(iv) Excess warranty provision written back — Change in accounting estimate.** The reversal of ₹80,000 excess provision results from a revised estimate of the outflow required to settle the warranty obligation. It is a **change in accounting estimate** and is recognised in the current period's profit and loss (as income/reduction of expense). If material, the amount is disclosed.

**Conclusion:** (i) is a policy change — disclose ₹4,20,000; (ii) is a prior period item — separately disclose ₹1,10,000 charge; (iii) and (iv) are changes in estimate — recognise prospectively (₹2,00,000 extra depreciation and ₹80,000 write-back respectively).

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner looks for the precise label — policy vs estimate vs prior period — and the correct treatment (disclose / prospective / separate disclosure). A common deduction is treating the useful-life revision as a prior period error.)*

### Q20. Ch: AS 11 — The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Oceanic Exports Ltd. entered into the following foreign currency transactions during the year ended 31st March, 2026. Prepare the necessary computations showing the exchange differences and the treatment of the forward contract.

| S.No. | Transaction | Amount | Rate on transaction date | Rate on 31.03.2026 | Settlement / other |
|-------|-------------|--------|--------------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| (i) | Import of raw material on 01.02.2026 (creditor) | USD 60,000 | ₹82.50 / USD | ₹83.80 / USD | Unpaid on 31.03.2026 |
| (ii) | Export of goods on 15.02.2026 (debtor) | USD 40,000 | ₹82.90 / USD | ₹83.80 / USD | Unrealised on 31.03.2026 |
| (iii) | Purchase of machinery on 10.01.2026 (creditor, imported) | USD 1,00,000 | ₹82.00 / USD | ₹83.80 / USD | Unpaid on 31.03.2026 |
| (iv) | To hedge the USD 60,000 import creditor, a 3-month forward contract was taken on 01.02.2026 at a forward rate of ₹83.10 / USD. Spot on 01.02.2026 was ₹82.50 / USD. | — | — | — | Contract runs 01.02.2026 to 30.04.2026 |

The company does not follow the option under AS 11 of capitalising exchange differences on the machinery liability. Show the amounts to be charged / credited to the profit and loss account.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Import creditor — monetary item (i)**
Monetary items are restated at the closing rate under AS 11.
Loss = USD 60,000 × (₹83.80 − ₹82.50) = 60,000 × ₹1.30 = **₹78,000 (exchange loss)**

**WN-2: Export debtor — monetary item (ii)**
Gain = USD 40,000 × (₹83.80 − ₹82.90) = 40,000 × ₹0.90 = **₹36,000 (exchange gain)**

**WN-3: Machinery creditor — monetary item (iii)**
The machinery (non-monetary asset) is carried at ₹82.00; the *liability* is monetary and is restated.
Loss = USD 1,00,000 × (₹83.80 − ₹82.00) = 1,00,000 × ₹1.80 = **₹1,80,000 (exchange loss)**
As the company does not exercise the capitalisation option, this loss is charged to profit and loss.

**WN-4: Forward contract (iv) — premium and MTM**
The contract is taken to hedge an existing monetary item (not a firm commitment / highly probable forecast), so AS 11 requires:
- Premium/discount on the forward contract = (Forward rate − Spot rate at inception) × amount, amortised over the life of the contract.
- Premium = (₹83.10 − ₹82.50) × 60,000 = ₹0.60 × 60,000 = ₹36,000, over 3 months.
- Amortised for Feb & Mar 2026 (2 months) = ₹36,000 × 2/3 = **₹24,000 (charged to P&L)**.
- The exchange difference on the underlying creditor (WN-1, ₹78,000 loss) is recognised in P&L in the normal way; being a hedge of a recognised monetary item, the creditor and the forward receivable are both restated at the closing rate so their exchange differences offset — hence only the amortised premium is a net cost. (Under AS 11 for a forward on a monetary item, exchange differences on the contract are recognised in P&L each year and the premium is amortised separately.)

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|-------------|-----------:|
| (i) Loss on import creditor (WN-1) | 78,000 |
| (ii) Gain on export debtor (WN-2) | (36,000) |
| (iii) Loss on machinery creditor (WN-3) | 1,80,000 |
| (iv) Forward contract premium amortised (WN-4) | 24,000 |
| **Net exchange loss charged to P&L** | **2,46,000** |

### Q21. Ch: AS 12 — Accounting for Government Grants (Marks: 8) [Problem]
**Question:** Vindhya Cement Ltd. received the following government grants during the year ended 31st March, 2026. Explain and give the accounting treatment under AS 12 with computations.

| S.No. | Grant |
|-------|-------|
| (i) | A grant of ₹50,00,000 towards the cost of a new plant costing ₹2,50,00,000 (useful life 10 years, SLM). The company follows the "reduction from gross value of asset" method. |
| (ii) | A grant of ₹12,00,000 received as compensation for expenses (pollution-control levy) already incurred in the previous year. |
| (iii) | A non-monetary grant of land recorded by the government at a concessional value of ₹8,00,000 (fair value ₹30,00,000). |
| (iv) | A grant of ₹20,00,000 received in the nature of promoters' contribution for setting up a unit in a backward area; no repayment is expected and no specific costs are attached. |

Also state the treatment if the plant grant in (i) becomes refundable in a later year.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Depreciation after grant on plant (i)**
Under the "reduction from asset" method, the grant is deducted from the gross value of the asset:
Book value of plant = ₹2,50,00,000 − ₹50,00,000 = **₹2,00,00,000**
Annual depreciation (SLM, 10 years) = ₹2,00,00,000 / 10 = **₹20,00,000 per annum**
(Effectively the grant of ₹50,00,000 is recognised in P&L over 10 years through reduced depreciation of ₹5,00,000 p.a.)

**WN-2: Grant as compensation for past expenses (ii)**
A grant receivable as compensation for expenses or losses **already incurred** in a previous period, or given for immediate financial support with no future related costs, is recognised as **income of the period in which it becomes receivable** (an extraordinary/other income item as appropriate). Hence ₹12,00,000 is credited to the P&L of 2025-26.

**WN-3: Non-monetary grant of land (iii)**
Where a non-monetary asset is given at a **concessional rate**, it is accounted at its **acquisition cost** (the concessional value). Hence land is recorded at **₹8,00,000**; the fair value of ₹30,00,000 is not recognised. (Only where an asset is given free of cost is it recorded at a nominal value.)

**WN-4: Grant in the nature of promoters' contribution (iv)**
A grant given as promoters' contribution — i.e. with reference to the total investment in an undertaking or by way of contribution to total capital, with no repayment ordinarily expected and no specific related cost — is treated as **capital reserve**. Hence ₹20,00,000 is credited to **Capital Reserve**, not to P&L.

**Statement Showing Treatment of Grants (2025-26)**

| Grant | Treatment | Amount (₹) |
|-------|-----------|-----------:|
| (i) Plant grant | Deducted from cost of plant; net WDV ₹2,00,00,000, dep. ₹20,00,000 p.a. | 50,00,000 (netted) |
| (ii) Compensation for past expense | Credited to P&L as income | 12,00,000 |
| (iii) Non-monetary land grant | Land recorded at concessional cost | 8,00,000 |

### Q22. Ch: AS 16 — Borrowing Costs (Marks: 8) [Problem]

### Q24. Ch: AS 18 — Related Party Disclosures (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** Examine, with reference to AS 18, whether the following are related parties of Nova Ltd. for the year ended 31st March, 2026, and state the disclosure requirement:

| S.No. | Situation |
|-------|-----------|
| (i) | Mr. Rao holds 30% of the equity share capital and voting power of Nova Ltd. |
| (ii) | Bright Ltd. and Nova Ltd. are both subsidiaries of the same holding company, Apex Ltd. |
| (iii) | Nova Ltd. buys 60% of its raw materials from Delta Ltd., its sole major supplier, purely on commercial terms. |
| (iv) | Mr. Sen is a Key Management Person of Nova Ltd.; his brother's proprietary firm supplied goods worth ₹9,00,000 to Nova Ltd. |
| (v) | State Bank of India is Nova Ltd.'s principal banker providing regular finance. |

**Answer:**
AS 18 "Related Party Disclosures" requires disclosure of related party relationships and transactions where **control** or **significant influence** exists. Parties are considered related if one party has the ability to control the other or exercise significant influence over it in making financial and/or operating decisions. Significant influence is presumed where a party holds, directly or indirectly, **20% or more** of the voting power.

**(i) Mr. Rao (30% holding) — Related party.** Holding 30% of the voting power gives Mr. Rao significant influence over Nova Ltd. Therefore he is a related party, and transactions with him must be disclosed.

**(ii) Bright Ltd. (fellow subsidiary) — Related party.** Enterprises under **common control** — here both are subsidiaries of Apex Ltd. — are related parties (fellow subsidiaries). Transactions between Nova Ltd. and Bright Ltd. must be disclosed.

**(iii) Delta Ltd. (major supplier) — Not a related party.** AS 18 specifically **excludes** a single customer, supplier, franchiser, distributor or general agent with whom significant volume of business is transacted, merely by virtue of the resulting economic dependence. Delta Ltd. deals on normal commercial terms and does not control or significantly influence Nova; hence not a related party.

**(iv) KMP's brother's firm — Related party (relative of KMP).** A **relative** of a Key Management Person is a related party. A brother falls within the meaning of relative. The firm supplying ₹9,00,000 is controlled by a relative of KMP; the transaction must be disclosed.

**(v) State Bank of India (banker) — Not a related party.** AS 18 excludes providers of finance, trade unions, public utilities and government departments in the course of their normal dealings with the enterprise merely by virtue of those dealings. Hence SBI is not a related party.

**Disclosure:** For related parties (i), (ii) and (iv), Nova Ltd. must disclose the name of the related party, the nature of the relationship, the nature and volume of transactions, amounts outstanding, and any provisions for doubtful debts / amounts written off.

**Conclusion:** Related parties = Mr. Rao (i), Bright Ltd. (ii) and the KMP's brother's firm (iv); Delta Ltd. (iii) and SBI (v) are **not** related parties.

### Q28. Ch: AS 24 — Discontinuing Operations (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Define a "discontinuing operation" under AS 24 and state (a) the event that triggers the **initial disclosure** and (b) the principal disclosures required. Also state whether AS 24 lays down any new recognition or measurement principles.

**Answer:**
AS 24 "Discontinuing Operations" establishes principles for reporting information about discontinuing operations, thereby enhancing the ability of users to make projections of an enterprise's cash flows, earnings-generating capacity and financial position by segregating the effects of continuing from discontinuing operations.

**Definition.** A **discontinuing operation** is a relatively large component of an enterprise that (a) the enterprise, pursuant to a single plan, is either disposing of substantially in its entirety (e.g., by sale in a single transaction or by demerger/spin-off), or is terminating through abandonment or piecemeal disposal; and (b) represents a **separate major line of business or geographical area** of operations; and (c) can be **distinguished operationally and for financial reporting purposes**.

**(a) Initial disclosure event.** The disclosures required by AS 24 commence from the earlier of:
(i) the enterprise has entered into a **binding sale agreement** for substantially all of the assets attributable to the discontinuing operation; or
(ii) the enterprise's **Board of Directors (or similar governing body) has both approved a detailed, formal plan** for the discontinuance and made an announcement of the plan.

**(b) Principal disclosures.** Once the initial disclosure event has occurred, the enterprise discloses, among others:
- a **description** of the discontinuing operation and the business/geographical segment(s) in which it is reported under AS 17;
- the **date and nature** of the initial disclosure event;
- the **date or period** in which the discontinuance is expected to be completed;
- the **carrying amounts** of the total assets and total liabilities to be disposed of;
- the amounts of **revenue, expenses, pre-tax profit or loss** from ordinary activities attributable to the discontinuing operation and the related income tax expense;
- the net cash flows attributable to **operating, investing and financing** activities of the discontinuing operation;
- and, on disposal, the **pre-tax gain or loss** recognised and the related income tax expense.

**Recognition and measurement.** AS 24 does **not** establish any new recognition or measurement principles. The recognition and measurement of assets, liabilities, income and expenses of a discontinuing operation continue to be governed by **other applicable Accounting Standards** (e.g., AS 28 for impairment, AS 29 for provisions such as restructuring/termination costs).

**Conclusion:** AS 24 is essentially a **disclosure standard**; it segregates and highlights discontinuing operations but relies on other standards for recognition and measurement.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the three-part definition, the "earlier of binding sale agreement / board-approved announced plan" trigger, and the express point that AS 24 sets no new recognition/measurement rules. A common deduction is confusing it with segment reporting.)*

### Q29. Ch: AS 25 — Interim Financial Reporting (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** With reference to AS 25, examine the following and state the correct treatment in the interim financial report for the quarter ended 30th June, 2025 of Solaris Ltd.:

| S.No. | Situation |
|-------|-----------|
| (i) | The company expects annual bonus of ₹24,00,000 to employees, contractually obligated and reliably estimable. |
| (ii) | A planned major repair of ₹8,00,000 is expected to be incurred in the last quarter. |
| (iii) | The effective annual income-tax rate is estimated at 25%; the quarter's pre-tax income is ₹30,00,000. |
| (iv) | A volume rebate of ₹2,00,000 is expected to be given to a customer at the year-end if annual purchase targets are met, which is probable. |

**Answer:**
AS 25 "Interim Financial Reporting" applies the principle that an interim period is regarded as an **integral part of the annual period** for the purpose of recognising income and expenses (the "integral view"), while measurements are made on a **year-to-date** basis. Revenues and costs are recognised in an interim period on the same basis as in the annual financial statements.

**(i) Annual bonus — Provide proportionately.** A bonus is anticipated and recognised in an interim period only if (a) it is a **present legal or constructive obligation** for which the enterprise has no realistic alternative but to settle, and (b) it can be **reliably estimated**. As it is contractual and estimable, a proportionate amount is accrued. For the first quarter, ₹24,00,000 × 3/12 = **₹6,00,000** is provided.

**(ii) Planned major repair — Do not provide.** A cost that is expected to be incurred later in the year is **not anticipated** in an interim period unless an obligating event has already occurred by the interim date creating a present obligation. A planned repair is a future cost with no present obligation; hence **no provision** of ₹8,00,000 is made in the first quarter.

**(iii) Income tax — Use estimated annual effective rate.** Interim-period income tax is accrued using the **weighted-average annual effective income-tax rate** expected for the full year. Tax for the quarter = ₹30,00,000 × 25% = **₹7,50,000**.

**(iv) Volume rebate — Accrue if probable and estimable.** A volume rebate/discount that is anticipated to be given is accrued in interim periods if it is **probable** that it will be earned and it can be **reliably estimated**. As it is probable, a proportionate rebate of ₹2,00,000 × 3/12 = **₹50,000** is recognised (as a reduction of revenue) for the quarter.

**Conclusion:** Provide bonus ₹6,00,000 (i), tax ₹7,50,000 (iii), and rebate ₹50,000 (iv) on a year-to-date proportionate/effective-rate basis; do **not** provide for the planned major repair (ii).

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the "obligation-exists" test for costs and the estimated-annual-effective-rate for tax. A common deduction is providing for the planned repair or applying a period-specific tax rate instead of the annual effective rate.)*

### Q32. Ch: AS 29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Advise Trident Ltd., with reasons under AS 29, on the correct treatment of the following as at 31st March, 2026:

| S.No. | Situation |
|-------|-----------|
| (i) | The company sells goods with a warranty. Based on past experience, warranty claims are estimated at ₹5,00,000 on the year's sales. |
| (ii) | A legal suit is pending; the company's lawyers assess only a 30% chance of an outflow of ₹10,00,000. |
| (iii) | The company has a non-cancellable contract to buy raw material for ₹8,00,000; the material's expected use will yield output realisable at only ₹6,00,000 (an onerous contract). |
| (iv) | The Board decided on 20.03.2026 to restructure a division, but no announcement was made and no implementation begun before the year-end. |
| (v) | The company is virtually certain to receive ₹4,00,000 as damages awarded by a court (decree passed, appeal period expired). |

**Answer:**
AS 29 provides that a **provision** is recognised only when: (a) an enterprise has a **present obligation** as a result of a past (obligating) event; (b) it is **probable** that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will be required to settle it; and (c) a **reliable estimate** can be made of the amount. If any condition is not met, no provision is made; a **contingent liability** is only disclosed (not recognised), and a **contingent asset** is neither recognised nor disclosed (recognised only when realisation is virtually certain).

**(i) Warranty — Provide.** The sale of goods with a warranty creates a present obligation (a constructive/legal obligation) as a result of a past event (the sale); an outflow is probable and reliably estimable from past experience. A **provision of ₹5,00,000** must be recognised.

**(ii) Legal suit (30% chance) — Contingent liability, disclose only.** A present obligation exists but the outflow is **not probable** (only 30% — a possible, not probable, obligation). Hence **no provision**; it is disclosed as a **contingent liability** of ₹10,00,000.

**(iii) Onerous contract — Provide.** AS 29 requires that if an enterprise has a contract that is **onerous** (the unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations exceed the expected economic benefits), the present obligation under the contract is recognised as a provision. Provision = unavoidable cost ₹8,00,000 − benefit ₹6,00,000 = **₹2,00,000**.

**(iv) Restructuring decision without announcement — No provision.** A provision for restructuring arises only when the enterprise has a **detailed formal plan** and has **raised a valid expectation** in those affected by starting to implement it or announcing its main features. Here the decision was internal, unannounced and not begun; hence **no present obligation** and **no provision** at 31.03.2026.

**(v) Damages virtually certain — Recognise as asset.** Where the realisation of income is **virtually certain** (decree passed, appeal time expired), the item is **no longer a contingent asset** but an asset; ₹4,00,000 is **recognised** as receivable/income.

**Conclusion:** Provide for warranty ₹5,00,000 (i) and onerous contract ₹2,00,000 (iii); disclose the ₹10,00,000 suit as a contingent liability (ii); make no provision for the unannounced restructuring (iv); recognise the ₹4,00,000 virtually-certain damages as an asset (v).

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the three recognition tests, the onerous-contract provision (excess of cost over benefit), the "announced/begun" trigger for restructuring, and the virtually-certain test for contingent assets. A common deduction is providing for the unannounced restructuring or the merely possible legal claim.)*

### Q33. Ch: AS 22 — Accounting for Taxes on Income (Marks: 6) [Application]
**Question:** Answer the following under AS 22, with reasons:
(a) Ganga Ltd. has carry-forward business losses and unabsorbed depreciation under tax law. Under what condition may it recognise the resulting Deferred Tax Asset, and how does this differ from other DTAs?
(b) Trinetra Ltd., in the first year of a **tax holiday** under the Income-tax Act, has timing differences that will reverse partly during and partly after the tax holiday period. How should deferred tax be recognised?

**Answer:**
AS 22 requires deferred tax to be recognised on **timing differences** — differences between taxable income and accounting income that originate in one period and are capable of reversal in one or more subsequent periods.

**(a) DTA on carry-forward losses / unabsorbed depreciation.**
Ordinarily a Deferred Tax Asset is recognised and carried forward only to the extent that there is a **reasonable certainty** that sufficient future taxable income will be available against which the DTA can be realised. However, where a DTA arises from the **carry-forward of losses and unabsorbed depreciation** under tax laws, AS 22 imposes a **stricter test** — such a DTA is recognised only to the extent that there is **virtual certainty** supported by convincing evidence that sufficient future taxable income will be available against which it can be realised. Thus the distinction is:

| Type of DTA | Recognition test |
|-------------|------------------|
| DTA from ordinary deductible timing differences (e.g., disallowed provisions) | **Reasonable certainty** of future taxable income |
| DTA from carry-forward of tax losses / unabsorbed depreciation | **Virtual certainty** supported by convincing evidence |

Ganga Ltd. may recognise the DTA only if the higher "virtual certainty" test is satisfied; otherwise it is not recognised (though reviewed at each balance sheet date).

**(b) Timing differences arising in a tax holiday period.**
As per AS 22, deferred tax in respect of timing differences that **originate and reverse within the tax holiday period** is **not recognised**, to the extent the enterprise's gross total income is subject to the deduction/exemption during that period (because no tax effect will crystallise — they reverse when income is exempt). Deferred tax is recognised only for those timing differences that **reverse after the tax holiday period**. For allocation, the timing differences which reverse first are considered to reverse first. Hence Trinetra Ltd. recognises deferred tax **only on that portion of the timing differences expected to reverse after the tax holiday ends**, and ignores the portion reversing within the holiday.

**Conclusion:** (a) DTA on carried-forward losses/unabsorbed depreciation needs **virtual certainty with convincing evidence**, a stricter test than the "reasonable certainty" for other DTAs; (b) deferred tax is created only for timing differences reversing **after** the tax-holiday period.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the precise "virtual certainty vs reasonable certainty" distinction and the tax-holiday reversal rule with the FIFO reversal assumption. A common deduction is applying "reasonable certainty" to carry-forward-loss DTAs.)*

### Q34. Ch: AS 11 — The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** State, with reasons under AS 11, how each of the following should be dealt with in the financial statements of Kaveri Ltd. for the year ended 31st March, 2026:

| S.No. | Situation |
|-------|-----------|
| (i) | Cash and bank balances of USD 20,000 held on 31.03.2026; rate on receipt ₹82.00, closing rate ₹83.80. |
| (ii) | Property (a building) bought for USD 1,00,000 when the rate was ₹80.00; closing rate ₹83.80. |
| (iii) | Investment in equity shares of a foreign company for USD 50,000 at ₹81.00 (carried at cost); closing rate ₹83.80. |
| (iv) | A forward contract taken purely for **trading/speculation** for USD 30,000; forward rate at inception ₹83.00, forward rate for the remaining maturity at year-end ₹83.90. |

**Answer:**
AS 11 requires **monetary items** (money held and assets/liabilities to be received or paid in fixed or determinable amounts of money) to be reported at the **closing rate**, with exchange differences recognised in the Profit & Loss Account. **Non-monetary items** carried at historical cost are reported using the exchange rate at the **date of the transaction** (not restated at the closing rate).

**(i) Cash and bank balances — Monetary item.** Foreign-currency cash/bank balances are monetary items and are restated at the closing rate. Value = USD 20,000 × ₹83.80 = ₹16,76,000. Exchange gain = 20,000 × (₹83.80 − ₹82.00) = **₹36,000 credited to P&L**.

**(ii) Building — Non-monetary item at historical cost.** A building carried at cost is a non-monetary item; it is reported at the transaction-date rate and **not** restated. It remains at USD 1,00,000 × ₹80.00 = **₹80,00,000**; no exchange difference is recognised.

**(iii) Investment in foreign equity shares (at cost) — Non-monetary item.** Equity investment carried at historical cost is non-monetary; reported at the transaction-date rate. It stays at USD 50,000 × ₹81.00 = **₹40,50,000**; the closing rate is not applied and no exchange difference arises.

**(iv) Speculative forward contract — MTM through P&L.** A forward contract taken for **trading or speculation** (not to hedge an existing asset/liability or a firm commitment) is measured by marking it to market: the **gain or loss is the difference between the forward rate contracted and the forward rate available for the remaining maturity** at the reporting date, recognised in the Profit & Loss Account. The premium/discount is **not** amortised. Gain = USD 30,000 × (₹83.90 − ₹83.00) = 30,000 × ₹0.90 = **₹27,000 credited to P&L**.

**Conclusion:** Restate the cash balance (gain ₹36,000) (i) and mark the speculative forward to market (gain ₹27,000) (iv) through P&L; carry the building (ii) and the foreign equity investment (iii) at their historical-rate amounts without restatement.

*(Full-marks tip: The examiner rewards the monetary vs non-monetary classification and the special MTM treatment (not amortisation) for speculative forwards. A common deduction is restating the building or investment at the closing rate.)*

### Q36. Ch: AS 3 Cash Flow Statements — Classification of Interest, Dividend & Taxes (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain, with reference to **AS 3 "Cash Flow Statements"**, how cash flows arising from (i) interest and dividends, and (ii) taxes on income, should be classified and disclosed. Distinguish the treatment for a financial enterprise from that for other enterprises.

**Answer:** AS 3 requires cash flows from interest and dividends received and paid to each be **disclosed separately** and **classified consistently** from period to period as arising from operating, investing or financing activities (para 30). For a **financial enterprise** (such as a bank or NBFC), interest paid, and interest and dividends received, are cash flows arising from its principal revenue-producing activities and are therefore **classified as operating activities**. For **other (non-financial) enterprises**, the treatment prescribed by AS 3 is set out below.

| Item | Classification for other (non-financial) enterprises | Reasoning (AS 3) |
|---|---|---|
| Interest **paid** (on loans/debentures) | **Financing activity** | It is the cost of obtaining financial resources (para 30) |
| Interest **received** (on investments/loans given) | **Investing activity** | It is a return on resources deployed in investments (para 30) |
| Dividend **received** | **Investing activity** | Return on investments made (para 30) |
| Dividend **paid** | **Financing activity** | Cost of servicing owners' funds (para 31) |

Regarding **taxes on income**, para 34 provides that cash flows arising from taxes on income should be **separately disclosed** and should be **classified as cash flows from operating activities unless they can be specifically identified** with financing or investing activities. Because a single tax payment often relates to transactions across all activities and identification is frequently impracticable, tax paid is, as a rule, shown under operating activities.

**Conclusion:** Interest and dividends are classified by the nature of the enterprise (operating for financial enterprises; investing/financing for others), each disclosed separately, while taxes on income are generally an operating cash flow unless specifically identifiable with a particular investing or financing item.

**Answer:** **(i) Non-cash transactions:** Para 40 of AS 3 provides that **investing and financing transactions that do not require the use of cash or cash equivalents should be excluded from the cash flow statement**, since they do not give rise to any cash flow in the current period. ### Q40. Ch: Redemption of Preference Shares — Conditions & Capital Redemption Reserve (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions and the sources laid down by **Section 55 of the Companies Act, 2013** read with **Rule 9 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014** for the redemption of preference shares. Explain the purpose and creation of the **Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR)**.

**Answer:** Under **Section 55(1)** no company limited by shares can issue **irredeemable** preference shares; under **Section 55(2)** a company may issue redeemable preference shares which must be redeemed within a period **not exceeding twenty years** from the date of issue (with a limited exception up to thirty years for infrastructure projects). The statutory conditions for redemption are:

1. **Only fully paid-up shares can be redeemed** — no preference share which is partly paid up may be redeemed [Section 55(2)(a)].
2. Redemption may be made only **out of the profits of the company which would otherwise be available for dividend, or out of the proceeds of a fresh issue of shares** made for the purpose of redemption [Section 55(2)(a)].
3. The **premium payable on redemption**, if any, must be provided **out of the profits of the company or out of the company's Securities Premium account** [Section 55(2)(b)].
4. Where redemption is out of profits, a sum **equal to the nominal amount of the shares redeemed** must be transferred to the **Capital Redemption Reserve account** [Section 55(2)(c)].

**Capital Redemption Reserve:** The CRR is created to protect creditors by ensuring that the capital base is not depleted merely because redeemable capital has been paid off out of divisible profits — capital is thus maintained. The amount transferred equals the nominal value of shares redeemed **less** the nominal value of any fresh shares issued for the purpose. The CRR is treated as **capital** and can be applied only in **paying up unissued shares to be issued as fully paid bonus shares** (Section 69 also applies the same restriction for buy-back).

**Conclusion:** Preference shares must be fully paid, redeemed within the permitted period out of divisible profits or a fresh issue, with premium met from profits or securities premium, and a CRR equal to the nominal value redeemed (net of any fresh nominal issue) must be created to preserve capital.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite Section 55(2)(a)/(b)/(c) precisely and state that CRR = nominal redeemed − nominal of fresh issue. Common deduction: omitting the "fully paid only" condition or the permissible uses of CRR.)*

### Q46. Ch: Buy-back of Securities — Conditions, Limits & Sources (Section 68/69/70) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the conditions, quantitative limits and permissible sources for the **buy-back of its own shares** by a company under **Sections 68, 69 and 70 of the Companies Act, 2013**.

**Answer:** **Section 68(1)** permits a company to purchase its own shares or other specified securities out of **(a) its free reserves, (b) the securities premium account, or (c) the proceeds of the issue of any shares or other specified securities** — but buy-back of any kind of shares cannot be made out of the proceeds of an earlier issue of the **same kind** of shares.

The conditions and limits under **Section 68(2)** are:

| Requirement | Provision |
|---|---|
| Authorisation | Articles must authorise; a **special resolution** is required. A **Board resolution** alone suffices where buy-back is **≤ 10%** of paid-up **equity** capital and free reserves |
| Overall quantitative ceiling | Buy-back in a year must not exceed **25% of the aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves** |
| Shares-outstanding ceiling (equity) | Buy-back of **equity** shares in any financial year must not exceed **25% of paid-up equity share capital** |
| Debt-equity ratio | Post buy-back, the ratio of aggregate **debt (secured + unsecured) to (paid-up capital + free reserves)** must not exceed **2:1** |
| Fully paid | Only **fully paid-up** shares can be bought back |
| Completion & cooling period | Buy-back must be completed within **1 year** of the resolution; no further buy-back within **1 year (365 days)** of the preceding one |

**Section 69** requires that where shares are bought back **out of free reserves or the securities premium account**, a sum **equal to the nominal value of the shares bought back** must be transferred to the **Capital Redemption Reserve**, which is then treated as capital. **Section 70** prohibits buy-back through any subsidiary, through investment companies, or when the company has defaulted in repayment of deposits/interest, redemption of debentures/preference shares or payment of dividend, etc.

**Conclusion:** A valid buy-back must satisfy the source rule (68(1)), the 25%-resources, 25%-equity-shares and 2:1 debt-equity limits with proper authorisation (68(2)), create a CRR for the nominal value bought back (69), and not fall within the prohibitions of Section 70.

*(Full-marks tip: Present the three numerical limits distinctly and cite Section 69 for the CRR transfer of nominal value. Common deduction: confusing the 25%-of-equity-shares limit with the 25%-of-resources limit — they are separate tests.)*

### Q49. Ch: Buy-back of Securities — Post Buy-back Obligations & Restrictions (Marks: 4) [Application]
**Question:** A company has just completed a buy-back of its equity shares. State the **obligations and restrictions** that apply to the company after the buy-back under the Companies Act, 2013.

**Answer:** The principal post buy-back obligations and restrictions are:

1. **Extinguishment of securities [Section 68(7)]:** The company must **physically destroy** the bought-back shares/securities **within 7 days** of the last date of completion of the buy-back — the shares cannot be re-issued.
2. **Capital Redemption Reserve [Section 69]:** Where the buy-back is out of free reserves or the securities premium account, an amount equal to the **nominal value** of the shares bought back must be transferred to the CRR.
3. **No further issue of same kind of shares [Section 68(8)]:** The company shall not make a **further issue of the same kind of shares** (including by way of rights, but excluding bonus shares or discharge of subsisting obligations such as conversion, sweat equity, ESOP) **within 6 months** of the completion of the buy-back.
4. **Cooling period [Section 68(2) / proviso]:** No further buy-back can be made within a period of **one year (365 days)** reckoned from the date of the completion of the preceding buy-back.
5. **Return of buy-back [Section 68(10)]:** The company must file a **return (Form SH-11) with the Registrar and the SEBI** within **30 days** of completion, and maintain a **register of buy-back (Form SH-10)**.

**Conclusion:** After buy-back the company must extinguish the securities within 7 days, create the CRR, observe the 6-month bar on fresh issue of the same kind of shares and the 1-year cooling period, and file the prescribed return within 30 days.

*(Full-marks tip: Attach the exact time limits — 7 days (extinguish), 6 months (no same-kind issue), 30 days (return), 1 year (cooling period). Common deduction: mixing up the 6-month and 1-year periods.)*

### Q53. Ch: Rights Issue — Value of a Right & Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (Marks: 5) [Problem]

(ii) the value of one right (per existing share and per new share offered);
(iii) whether he is better off selling his rights or subscribing, given he takes up all his entitlement.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP)**
Consider a block of 5 existing + 1 new = 6 shares.

| | Shares | Value (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Existing (cum-rights) | 5 × ₹48 | 240 |
| New (rights) | 1 × ₹30 | 30 |
| **Total** | **6** | **270** |

**WN-2: Value of a right**
Value of right per **new share** = Cum-rights (or TERP) benefit = TERP − Issue price = ₹45 − ₹30 = **₹15**.

Cross-check: 5 existing shares carry rights worth 5 × ₹3 = ₹15 = value of the 1 new share's right. ✓

**WN-3: Shareholder holding 500 shares**

| Option | Computation | ₹ |

### Q57. Ch: Bonus Issue — Conditions & Legal Restrictions (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the conditions and restrictions governing the issue of bonus shares by a company under Section 63 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with the SEBI (ICDR) Regulations and the ICAI guidance. Can bonus shares be issued in lieu of dividend?

**Answer:**
A bonus issue is the capitalisation of a company's accumulated profits or reserves by issuing fully-paid shares to existing shareholders without any fresh inflow of cash, governed by **Section 63 of the Companies Act, 2013**.

Section 63(1) permits a company to issue fully-paid bonus shares out of only three sources: (a) its **free reserves**; (b) the **Securities Premium Account**; and (c) the **Capital Redemption Reserve Account**. Reserves created by the **revaluation of assets** cannot be capitalised for a bonus issue, as they are unrealised.

Section 63(2) lays down that no company shall capitalise its profits or reserves for issuing bonus shares unless: (i) it is **authorised by its Articles of Association**; (ii) it is **recommended by the Board and authorised in general meeting** by the members; (iii) the company **has not defaulted** in payment of interest or principal on deposits or debt securities; (iv) the company **has not defaulted** in payment of statutory dues of employees (PF, gratuity, bonus); and (v) the **partly-paid shares, if any, outstanding are made fully paid-up** before the bonus issue.

Further conditions from the ICAI guidance and SEBI (ICDR): the bonus issue must be **implemented within the time stipulated** (SEBI: within 15 days / on Board approval where members' approval is not required), it **cannot be issued in lieu of dividend**, and once announced it **cannot be withdrawn**.

**Distinction — permissible vs non-permissible sources:**

| Permissible for bonus | Not permissible |
|---|---|
| Free reserves (General Reserve, P&L surplus) | Revaluation Reserve |
| Securities Premium | Capital Reserve arising from asset revaluation |
| Capital Redemption Reserve | Reserve not available for distribution as dividend |

**Conclusion:** Bonus shares may be issued only from free reserves, Securities Premium or CRR after satisfying Section 63(2); they **cannot** be issued in lieu of dividend and revaluation reserves are ineligible.

*(Full-marks tip: quote Section 63(1) sources and 63(2) pre-conditions verbatim, and explicitly state the two prohibitions — no bonus in lieu of dividend and partly-paid shares must first be made fully-paid.)*

### Q59. Ch: ESOP — Difference between ESOS and ESPS; SAR (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between an **Employee Stock Option Scheme (ESOS)** and an **Employee Stock Purchase Scheme (ESPS)**, and explain briefly the accounting treatment of a **cash-settled** stock appreciation right (SAR).

**Answer:**
An **Employee Stock Option Scheme (ESOS)** is an arrangement whereby a company grants employees an **option (a right, not an obligation)** to buy its shares at a pre-determined exercise price after a specified vesting period, subject to conditions. An **Employee Stock Purchase Scheme (ESPS)** is a scheme under which the company **offers shares to employees directly** as part of a public/rights issue or otherwise, generally at a concessional price, with the shares being allotted immediately.

| Basis | ESOS | ESPS |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Option (right) to buy later | Direct offer/allotment of shares now |
| Vesting period | Present — options vest over time | Generally nil / immediate |
| Exercise | Employee exercises after vesting | Employee applies and shares allotted at once |
| Lapse | Options may lapse if conditions unmet | No lapse — shares issued outright |
| Lock-in | On shares after exercise | Usually one-year lock-in on shares allotted |

**Cash-settled SAR:** In an **equity-settled** ESOP the expense is measured at grant-date fair value; but in a **cash-settled SAR** the employee receives cash equal to the appreciation in share price. The company recognises a **liability** (not equity), measured at the **fair value of the SAR at each reporting date** and **remeasured every period-end** until settlement, with changes in fair value taken to the Statement of Profit and Loss.

**Conclusion:** ESOS confers a future right to buy shares, ESPS is an immediate share offer; a cash-settled SAR creates a **remeasured liability**, unlike the fixed grant-date measurement of an equity-settled option.

*(Full-marks tip: the crucial distinction the examiner looks for is equity-settled = fixed grant-date FV vs cash-settled = liability remeasured each period.)*

### Q71. Ch: Investment Accounts — Classification & Carrying Amount under AS 13 (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain, under **AS 13 "Accounting for Investments"**, the classification of investments and the basis of determining their carrying amount in the financial statements. Also state how (i) the cost of an investment, (ii) a right issue, and (iii) a pre-acquisition dividend are dealt with.

**Answer:**
As per **AS 13**, investments are classified into two categories. A **current investment** is one that is by its nature *readily realisable* and is intended to be held for **not more than one year** from the date of acquisition; a **long-term investment** is an investment other than a current investment. This classification governs measurement.

**Carrying amount:**

| Basis | Current Investment | Long-term Investment |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement | Carried at **lower of cost and fair value** (fair value may be determined on a global/category basis) | Carried at **cost** |
| Diminution in value | Any reduction to fair value is charged to the Statement of Profit & Loss | Cost is reduced only for a **decline other than temporary (permanent)**, determined for each investment individually |

**(i) Cost of an investment** — The cost of an investment includes **acquisition charges** such as brokerage, fees and duties. Where an investment is acquired by issue of shares/other securities, the acquisition cost is the fair value of the securities issued; where acquired in exchange for another asset, cost is the fair value of the asset given up.

**(ii) Right issue** — If rights shares are **subscribed to**, the amount paid is **added to the cost** of the investment. If the rights are **not subscribed but sold (renounced)**, the sale proceeds are taken to the **Statement of Profit & Loss**. However, where the investment is acquired on **cum-right** basis and the market value immediately after the rights becomes ex-right falls below the cost, a suitable part of the sale proceeds of rights may be applied to reduce the carrying cost to the market value.

**(iii) Pre-acquisition dividend** — Dividend (or interest) received which relates to a period **prior to the acquisition** of the investment represents a **recovery of cost** and is **deducted from the cost of the investment** (a capital receipt), not credited to income; only the post-acquisition portion is treated as income.

**Conclusion:** Under AS 13, current investments are stated at the lower of cost and fair value and long-term investments at cost (less permanent diminution), with acquisition charges capitalised, rights subscribed added to cost, rights renounced taken to P&L, and pre-acquisition dividend reduced from cost.

*(Full-marks tip: quote the "**not more than one year**" test and the "**lower of cost and fair value**" (current) vs "**cost less permanent diminution**" (long-term) measurement — the examiner rewards the exact AS 13 wording and a clear rights/pre-acquisition-dividend rule.)*

### Q73. Ch: Internal Reconstruction — Concept & Distinction from External Reconstruction (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the meaning and objectives of **internal reconstruction** and distinguish it from **external reconstruction**. What is a "Capital Reduction Account"?

**Answer:**
**Internal reconstruction** means the re-arrangement of the financial structure of a company — typically by **reducing or re-organising its share capital and settling the claims of creditors/debenture-holders** — so as to write off the accumulated past losses and over-valued/fictitious assets and present a true and fair balance sheet, **without liquidating the company or forming a new company**. It is carried out through a scheme sanctioned by the **Tribunal (NCLT) under Section 66 of the Companies Act, 2013** (reduction of share capital).

**Objectives:** to eliminate the debit balance of the Statement of Profit & Loss and other fictitious/intangible assets, to bring the book values of assets in line with their real worth, to reduce or extinguish liabilities by obtaining sacrifices from members and creditors, and thereby to place the company on a sound footing so that it may pay dividends in future.

**Distinction:**

| Basis | Internal Reconstruction | External Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| Formation of new company | No new company is formed; the same company continues | A **new company** is formed to take over the business of the existing (loss-making) company |
| Liquidation | The existing company is **not** liquidated | The existing (transferor) company is **liquidated** |
| Governing provision | Reduction of capital u/s 66, Companies Act, 2013 | Amalgamation/absorption; accounted as per **AS 14** |
| Capital reduction | Losses written off against capital reduced | Losses do not survive; the new company starts afresh |

A **Capital Reduction Account** (also called Reconstruction Account) is a temporary account opened to which all the **sacrifices** made by shareholders, debenture-holders and creditors are **credited** and against which the **accumulated losses, fictitious assets and asset write-downs are debited**; any surplus remaining is transferred to **Capital Reserve**.

**Conclusion:** Internal reconstruction re-organises an existing company's capital under Section 66 without forming a new company, whereas external reconstruction involves liquidating the old company and floating a new one.

*(Full-marks tip: cite **Section 66** and stress "no new company / no liquidation" for internal reconstruction — the examiner rewards the Capital Reduction Account definition (sacrifices credited, losses debited, surplus to Capital Reserve).)*

### Q77. Ch: Amalgamation of Companies — Merger vs Purchase & Treatment of Reserves (AS 14) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** As per **AS 14 "Accounting for Amalgamations"**, state the conditions that must be satisfied for an amalgamation to be treated as an **"amalgamation in the nature of merger"**, and explain how the two accounting methods differ in the treatment of **reserves** and of the difference between purchase consideration and net assets.

**Answer:**
Under **AS 14**, an amalgamation is treated as an **amalgamation in the nature of merger** only if **all** the following five conditions are satisfied:
1. **All the assets and liabilities** of the transferor company become, after amalgamation, the assets and liabilities of the transferee company.
2. Shareholders holding **not less than 90%** of the face value of the equity shares of the transferor company (other than those already held by the transferee) become **equity shareholders of the transferee** company.
3. The **consideration** payable to the above equity shareholders is discharged by the transferee **wholly by the issue of equity shares** (except that cash may be paid for fractional shares).
4. The **business** of the transferor company is intended to be **carried on** by the transferee company.
5. **No adjustment** is intended to be made to the book values of the assets and liabilities of the transferor company, except to ensure **uniformity of accounting policies**.

If **any one** of these conditions is **not** satisfied, the amalgamation is an **amalgamation in the nature of purchase**.

**Method and treatment:**

| Aspect | Merger (Pooling of Interests Method) | Purchase (Purchase Method) |
|---|---|---|
| Recording of assets/liabilities | At **existing book values** | At **agreed values** (may be revised) |
| Reserves of transferor | **All reserves** (including statutory and free reserves and P&L balance) are **carried forward** and shown as they appeared | Only **statutory reserves** are carried (via "Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve"); other reserves (General Reserve, P&L) are **not** carried forward |
| Difference between consideration & share capital / net assets | Difference between purchase consideration and share capital of transferor is **adjusted against reserves** | Excess of consideration over net assets = **Goodwill**; shortfall = **Capital Reserve** |

**Conclusion:** In a merger (pooling), the identity of the reserves is preserved and any difference is adjusted in reserves; in a purchase, only statutory reserves survive and the difference gives rise to Goodwill or Capital Reserve.

*(Full-marks tip: reproduce **all five** merger conditions with the "90%", "wholly by equity shares" and "no adjustment to book values" phrases verbatim — the examiner marks these individually. Distinguishing the reserve treatment clinches full marks.)*

### Q81. Ch: Amalgamation of Companies — Treatment of Reserves & Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve (Marks: 5) [Application]
**Question:** In an amalgamation in the nature of **purchase**, the transferor company had the following reserves on the date of amalgamation: General Reserve ₹1,20,000; Profit & Loss A/c ₹40,000; Investment Allowance Reserve (statutory, to be maintained for 3 more years) ₹30,000; and Securities Premium ₹25,000. Explain how each of these is dealt with in the books of the transferee company, and describe the purpose and subsequent treatment of the **Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve**.

**Answer:**
Under the **purchase method** of AS 14, the transferee company records the assets and liabilities of the transferor at their agreed values and, as a rule, does **not** carry forward the transferor's reserves — because the identity of the transferor's reserves is not preserved in a purchase. The **exception** is a **statutory reserve** which the law requires to be maintained.

| Reserve of transferor | Treatment in transferee's books |
|---|---|
| General Reserve ₹1,20,000 | **Not** carried forward (it is a free reserve; it merges into the purchase-consideration/goodwill computation) |
| Profit & Loss A/c ₹40,000 | **Not** carried forward |
| Investment Allowance Reserve ₹30,000 (statutory) | **Carried forward** to comply with the statute, by crediting the statutory reserve and **debiting Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve** ₹30,000 |
| Securities Premium ₹25,000 | **Not** carried forward as such (it is part of the acquired net worth reflected in the consideration) |

**Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve (AAR):** When a statutory reserve is required to be continued, AS 14 requires the transferee to **preserve** that reserve in its books even though, under the purchase method, it would not otherwise be carried. To achieve this while keeping the balance sheet balanced, the transferee **credits the statutory reserve** and **debits an "Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve"**. The AAR is disclosed as a **separate line item** — presented as a **deduction from reserves** (i.e., it carries a debit balance under "Reserves and Surplus"). When the statutory reserve is **no longer required** to be maintained (here, after 3 years), the **reverse entry** is passed — the statutory reserve is debited and the Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve is credited — thereby eliminating both.

**Conclusion:** Free reserves (General Reserve, P&L, Securities Premium) of the transferor are not carried forward under the purchase method; only the **statutory reserve** is preserved through the Amalgamation Adjustment Reserve, which is reversed once the statutory requirement lapses.

*(Full-marks tip: the examiner rewards the precise mechanics — statutory reserve credited, AAR debited and shown as a **negative reserve**, then reversed when the statute no longer applies. State clearly that free reserves are NOT carried under purchase.)*

### Q88. Ch: Branch Accounts — Dependent vs. Independent Branch (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Distinguish between a **Dependent Branch** and an **Independent Branch**, and state the systems of accounting suitable for each.

**Answer:**
A **branch** is a segment of an enterprise located at a place different from the head office. For accounting purposes, branches are broadly classified as *dependent* and *independent* branches, the distinction turning on whether the branch maintains a complete set of books of its own.

A **dependent branch** does not keep a full set of double-entry books; its transactions are recorded in the head office books, which retains control over the branch's cash, stock and debtors. An **independent branch** keeps a complete self-balancing set of books, prepares its own trial balance, trading and profit & loss account, and communicates with the head office through the *Head Office Current Account*.

| Basis | Dependent Branch | Independent Branch |
|---|---|---|
| Books of account | Maintained by Head Office | Complete set kept by the branch itself |
| Accounting record | Memorandum/limited records at branch | Full double-entry, self-balancing |
| Profit ascertainment | H.O. ascertains branch profit | Branch prepares its own Trading & P&L A/c |
| Reciprocal account | Not required | Branch Current A/c / H.O. Current A/c |
| Suitable systems | Debtors System, Stock & Debtors System, Final Accounts System, Wholesale Branch System | Incorporation of branch trial balance into H.O. books |
| Autonomy | Low — closely controlled by H.O. | High — operates autonomously |

For a **dependent branch**, where transactions are few, the **Debtors System** is used; where stock control is important, the **Stock & Debtors System** is preferred; for larger branches, the **Final Accounts System** may be adopted. For an **independent branch**, the branch's trial balance is **incorporated** into the head office books at the year-end.

**Conclusion:** A dependent branch is accounted for *within* the H.O. books, whereas an independent branch keeps its own books and is *incorporated* into the H.O. accounts at the year-end through reciprocal current accounts.

*(Full-marks tip: A tabulated distinction plus a sentence naming the appropriate system for each type earns full marks; merely defining "branch" without the systems loses the application mark.)*

### Q91. Ch: Departmental Accounts — Basis of Inter-departmental Transfer Pricing (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the **bases on which one department may transfer goods to another department**, and state how unrealised profit on such transfers is dealt with in departmental accounts.

**Answer:**
Where an organisation is divided into departments, goods and services are frequently transferred from one department to another. The transfer is recorded as a **sale by the transferor department and a purchase by the transferee department**, and may be priced on any of the following bases:

1. **At cost price:** Goods are transferred at their actual cost. No profit element is loaded, so **no unrealised profit** arises and no stock reserve is required.
2. **At current market price / selling price:** Goods are transferred at the price at which they could be sold to outsiders. The transferor department earns a profit; if the goods remain unsold in the transferee department's closing stock, the profit element is **unrealised**.
3. **At cost plus an agreed percentage (transfer price):** Goods are transferred at cost plus a fixed mark-up, again giving rise to an unrealised profit element on unsold stock.

Where goods are transferred at a price **above cost**, the profit remains unrealised to the extent such goods are still lying in the closing stock of the transferee department at the year-end. To present a true and fair view, a **Stock Reserve (provision for unrealised profit)** is created by debiting the General Profit & Loss Account and reducing the closing stock, computed as:

> Unrealised profit = Closing inter-departmental stock × (Rate of profit ÷ (100 + Rate of profit))

Any **opening stock reserve** created in the previous year is **written back** (credited to the General Profit & Loss Account) in the current year.

**Conclusion:** Transfers may be made at cost, market price or cost-plus; only transfers above cost create unrealised profit, which is eliminated through a Stock Reserve on the transferee's unsold closing stock.

*(Full-marks tip: Name all three bases and give the exact formula for the reserve; the write-back of the *opening* reserve is the point most students omit.)*

### Q94. Ch: Consolidated Financial Statements — Unrealised Profit on Intra-group Stock (Marks: 5) [Problem]

Compute the **unrealised profit** to be eliminated, and show the consolidation adjustment (i) if the sale is by the **holding company (downstream)**, and (ii) if the sale had instead been by the **subsidiary (upstream)**.

**Solution:**

**WN-1: Profit loading on the sale**
Profit = Selling price 50,000 − Cost 40,000 = ₹10,000 ⇒ profit margin = 10,000 / 50,000 = **20% of sale value**.

**WN-2: Unrealised profit in closing stock**
Goods unsold = 3/5 of goods sold; value in stock = 3/5 × 50,000 = ₹30,000.

- Reduce Consolidated Profit & Loss A/c by ₹6,000
- Reduce Consolidated Inventory by ₹6,000
- **Minority Interest is not affected.**

**(ii) Upstream sale — S Ltd. (subsidiary) is the seller**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Total unrealised profit eliminated from Inventory | 6,000 |
| Borne by Consolidated P&L (Holding's share 90%) | 5,400 |
| Borne by Minority Interest (10%) | 600 |

### Q95. Ch: Consolidated Financial Statements — Control & Minority Interest (Marks: 4) [Theory]
**Question:** With reference to **AS 21 "Consolidated Financial Statements"**, explain the meaning of **"control"** and state when an enterprise is treated as a **subsidiary**. Also explain the concept of **Minority Interest**.

**Answer:**
**AS 21** lays down the principles and procedures for preparing and presenting **consolidated financial statements** for a group of enterprises under the control of a parent. Consolidation is founded on the concept of **control**.

As per AS 21, **control** means:
1. the ownership, directly or indirectly through subsidiary(ies), of **more than one-half of the voting power** of an enterprise; **or**
2. **control of the composition of the Board of Directors** (in the case of a company) or of the corresponding governing body of another enterprise, so as to obtain economic benefits from its activities.

A **subsidiary** is an enterprise that is controlled by another enterprise (the **parent** or **holding** company). Thus, an enterprise becomes a subsidiary the moment the parent acquires more than half the voting power *or* the power to control its board — irrespective of the actual percentage of shares held.

**Minority Interest** is that part of the **net results of operations and of the net assets of a subsidiary** which is attributable to interests **not owned, directly or indirectly through subsidiaries, by the parent**. In other words, where the parent holds less than 100% of a subsidiary, the balance shareholding is held by outsiders (the minority), and their proportionate share of the subsidiary's share capital, reserves and accumulated profits/losses is shown separately in the consolidated balance sheet as **"Minority Interest"**, between "Shareholders' Funds" and "Non-current/Current Liabilities".

**Conclusion:** An enterprise is a subsidiary when the parent holds more than half its voting power or controls its board; the outsiders' proportionate share in such a subsidiary's net assets and results is presented as Minority Interest in the consolidated financial statements.

### Q97. Ch: Accounting for LLP — Features and Distinction from Partnership (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** State the salient **features of a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** under the **Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008**, and distinguish an LLP from a **traditional partnership firm**.

**Answer:**
A **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** is a body corporate formed and incorporated under the **Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008**. It is a hybrid business vehicle that combines the **flexibility of a partnership** with the **limited liability and perpetual existence of a company**. Its salient features are:

1. It is a **body corporate** and a **separate legal entity** distinct from its partners (Section 3), with **perpetual succession** — a change in partners does not affect its existence or continuity.
2. The **liability of each partner is limited** to his agreed contribution; a partner is not personally liable for the wrongful acts of other partners.
3. It must have **at least two partners** and **at least two designated partners**, of whom **at least one must be resident in India**; there is no maximum limit on the number of partners.
4. The mutual rights and duties of partners are governed by the **LLP Agreement**; it can **own property**, sue and be sued in its own name.
5. It must maintain proper **books of account** and file an annual **Statement of Account and Solvency** and an **Annual Return** with the Registrar.

| Basis | Limited Liability Partnership | Traditional Partnership Firm |
|---|---|---|
| Governing law | LLP Act, 2008 | Indian Partnership Act, 1932 |
| Legal status | Separate legal entity / body corporate | No separate legal entity from partners |
| Liability of partners | Limited to agreed contribution | Unlimited, joint and several |
| Perpetual succession | Yes | No — dissolves on death/insolvency of a partner (subject to agreement) |
| Maximum number of partners | No limit | Limited (100 under Companies Rules) |
| Registration | Compulsory with Registrar of LLP | Optional (though desirable) |

**Conclusion:** An LLP is a body corporate under the LLP Act, 2008 offering limited liability and perpetual succession to its partners, unlike a traditional partnership firm where partners have unlimited liability and the firm has no independent legal existence.

*(Full-marks tip: Cite the LLP Act, 2008 and the minimum "two partners / two designated partners, one resident" requirement; a tabulated LLP-vs-firm distinction secures the comparison marks.)*

### Q100. Ch: Company Financial Statements — Current vs. Non-current Classification (Schedule III) (Marks: 5) [Theory]
**Question:** Explain the criteria laid down in **Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013** for classifying an **asset** and a **liability** as **"current"** or **"non-current"**, and state what is meant by an **"operating cycle."**

**Answer:**
Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013 requires every company (other than those following Ind AS) to present its Balance Sheet with assets and liabilities classified into **current** and **non-current**, the classification turning on the concept of the **operating cycle**.

**Operating cycle** means the time between the **acquisition of assets for processing and their realisation into cash or cash equivalents**. Where the normal operating cycle cannot be identified, it is **assumed to be twelve months**.

**An asset is classified as a current asset** when it satisfies **any one** of the following conditions:
1. it is expected to be **realised in, or is intended for sale or consumption in**, the company's normal operating cycle; or
2. it is held **primarily for the purpose of being traded**; or
3. it is expected to be realised **within twelve months** after the reporting date; or
4. it is **cash or cash equivalent**, unless restricted from being exchanged or used to settle a liability for at least twelve months after the reporting date.

**All other assets are classified as non-current.**

**A liability is classified as a current liability** when it satisfies **any one** of the following conditions:
1. it is expected to be **settled in the company's normal operating cycle**; or
2. it is held **primarily for the purpose of being traded**; or
3. it is **due to be settled within twelve months** after the reporting date; or
4. the company **does not have an unconditional right to defer** settlement of the liability for at least twelve months after the reporting date.

**All other liabilities are classified as non-current.**

**Conclusion:** An asset or liability is **current** if it is expected to be realised/settled within the operating cycle or within twelve months (or is held for trading, or is unrestricted cash); everything else is **non-current**. Where the operating cycle is not ascertainable, a period of twelve months is presumed.

*(Full-marks tip: List all four tests for each of a current asset and a current liability, and define "operating cycle" with the twelve-month default. The "unconditional right to defer" test for liabilities and the "cash restricted for 12 months" nuance for assets are the points that distinguish a full-marks answer.)*
