### D1. AS 1 — Disclosure of Accounting Policies (level: Easy)

**Problem:** State, with reasons, how each situation is dealt with under AS 1 for the year ended 31.03.2024:
(a) Sun Ltd changed its inventory valuation method from FIFO to Weighted Average; the effect is an increase in profit of ₹3,20,000.
(b) The company raised its provision for doubtful debts from 2% to 5% of debtors because recovery experience has deteriorated.
(c) The financial statements are prepared on going concern, accrual and consistency bases, and management has NOT made any separate note disclosing these three assumptions.

**Solution:**
- **(a) Change in accounting policy.** FIFO vs Weighted Average is a change in the *measurement basis*, i.e., a change in accounting policy. AS 1 requires that any change in an accounting policy that has a material effect be **disclosed**, and the **amount by which any item is affected (₹3,20,000) be disclosed** to the extent ascertainable. If the change has no material effect in the current year but is reasonably expected to have a material effect in later periods, that fact should also be disclosed.
- **(b) Change in accounting estimate, NOT a policy.** Revising the percentage for doubtful debts is a change in *estimate* driven by fresh information about recoverability. It is not a change in accounting policy; hence it is accounted **prospectively** (governed by AS 5), and no AS 1 policy-change disclosure is triggered.
- **(c) No disclosure required.** Going concern, accrual and consistency are the **three fundamental accounting assumptions** under AS 1. When they **are followed**, no specific disclosure is needed. Disclosure is required **only if any of them is NOT followed**. So the absence of a note here is correct.

**> Marks tip:** The marks are in *classifying* correctly — "policy vs estimate" and "fundamental assumption followed → silence is allowed." Quoting the ₹3,20,000 disclosure requirement in (a) bags the computation mark.

### D2. AS 2 — Cost vs Net Realisable Value (level: Easy)

**Problem:** From the following, compute the value of 1,000 units of finished goods in closing inventory of Moon Ltd:
Purchase price ₹4,00,000; Freight inward ₹20,000; Non-refundable import duty ₹30,000; Trade discount received ₹10,000; Storage cost (not necessary for further production) ₹15,000; Selling & distribution cost ₹25,000. Selling price is ₹460 per unit and estimated selling cost is ₹30 per unit.

**Solution:**

**Working Note 1 — Cost of purchase (AS 2 para 6–7):**
| Item | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | 4,00,000 |
| Add: Freight inward | 20,000 |
| Add: Non-refundable import duty | 30,000 |
| Less: Trade discount | (10,000) |
| **Cost of 1,000 units** | **4,40,000** |

Storage cost (not needed for a further production stage) and selling & distribution cost are **excluded** from inventory cost. Cost per unit = 4,40,000 ÷ 1,000 = **₹440**.

**Working Note 2 — NRV:** NRV per unit = Selling price 460 − Estimated selling cost 30 = **₹430**.

**Valuation:** Inventory is valued at **lower of Cost (₹440) and NRV (₹430)** = ₹430 per unit.

**Closing inventory = 1,000 × ₹430 = ₹4,30,000** (write-down of ₹10,000 charged to P&L).

**> Marks tip:** You lose marks if storage/selling costs sneak into cost. Show the "lower of cost and NRV" comparison explicitly — that line itself carries a mark.

### D3. AS 10 — Cost of an Item of PPE (level: Easy)

**Problem:** Star Ltd acquired a machine. Determine the amount to be capitalised:
Invoice price ₹10,00,000; Trade discount ₹50,000; Non-refundable import duty ₹1,00,000; Freight ₹25,000; Site preparation ₹40,000; Installation & assembly ₹75,000; Professional fees for installation ₹10,000; Cost of test runs ₹20,000 and sale proceeds of samples produced during testing ₹5,000; General administrative overheads ₹30,000; Staff training on the new machine ₹15,000; Initial operating loss before the machine reached planned capacity ₹40,000.

**Solution:**

**Working Note — Directly attributable costs (AS 10 para 16–19):**
| Item | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Invoice price | 10,00,000 |
| Less: Trade discount | (50,000) |
| Add: Non-refundable import duty | 1,00,000 |
| Add: Freight | 25,000 |
| Add: Site preparation | 40,000 |
| Add: Installation & assembly | 75,000 |
| Add: Professional fees (installation) | 10,000 |
| Add: Net testing cost (20,000 − 5,000 samples) | 15,000 |
| **Cost of machine** | **12,15,000** |

**Excluded** (charged to P&L): general administrative overheads ₹30,000, staff training ₹15,000, initial operating loss ₹40,000. Net proceeds of samples produced in testing are **deducted** from testing cost, not credited to income.

**Cost to be capitalised = ₹12,15,000.**

**> Marks tip:** The examiner plants "trap" items (training, admin OH, operating loss). Explicitly write *why each is excluded* — reasons earn marks, not just the final total.

### D4. AS 12 — Government Grant related to a Depreciable Asset (level: Easy)

**Problem:** Green Ltd bought a machine for ₹50,00,000 (useful life 10 years, SLM, nil residual) and received a government grant of ₹10,00,000 towards it. Show the accounting under **both** methods permitted by AS 12 and the annual charge to P&L.

**Solution:**

**Method I — Grant deducted from the cost of the asset**
- Capitalised cost = 50,00,000 − 10,00,000 = **₹40,00,000**
- Annual depreciation = 40,00,000 ÷ 10 = **₹4,00,000** charged to P&L.

**Method II — Grant treated as Deferred Income**
- Machine recorded at ₹50,00,000; annual depreciation = 50,00,000 ÷ 10 = ₹5,00,000.
- Grant credited to P&L over 10 years = 10,00,000 ÷ 10 = ₹1,00,000 per year.
- **Net annual charge = 5,00,000 − 1,00,000 = ₹4,00,000** (same P&L effect as Method I).

**Journal (Method II, Year 1):**
| Particulars | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Bank A/c ..... Dr | 10,00,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Deferred Government Grant A/c | | 10,00,000 |
| Depreciation A/c ..... Dr | 5,00,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Machinery A/c | | 5,00,000 |
| Deferred Government Grant A/c ..... Dr | 1,00,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To P&L A/c | | 1,00,000 |

**Net P&L charge in Year 1 = ₹4,00,000 under either method.**

**> Marks tip:** Show that *both methods hit the P&L identically* (₹4,00,000). Stating that the grant is recognised over the periods that bear the related depreciation (not upfront) is the core AS 12 principle worth a mark.

### D5. AS 9 — Revenue Recognition, Sale on Approval (level: Easy)

**Problem:** For the year ended 31.03.2024, Delta Ltd recorded sales of ₹10,00,000. This includes goods invoiced at ₹2,00,000 (cost ₹1,50,000) sent to customers on a **sale-or-return** basis, for which the approval period had not expired and no confirmation was received by year-end. Compute the revenue to be recognised and the effect on inventory. Also state the recognition timing for interest, royalty and dividend.

**Solution:**

**Working Note — Revenue adjustment (AS 9):** Revenue on goods sent on approval is recognised only when the buyer accepts, or the approval period lapses. Since neither happened, ₹2,00,000 is **not** revenue yet.
- Revenue to be recognised = 10,00,000 − 2,00,000 = **₹8,00,000**.
- Goods lying with customers (unapproved) are the seller's **inventory**, included at **cost ₹1,50,000** (not invoice value). The ₹50,000 unrealised profit is reversed.

**Recognition timing (AS 9 para 13):**
- **Interest** — on a time-proportion basis (effective yield).
- **Royalties** — on accrual basis per the terms of the relevant agreement.
- **Dividends** — when the owner's **right to receive payment is established** (dividend declared).

**Revenue recognised = ₹8,00,000; add ₹1,50,000 to closing inventory.**

**> Marks tip:** The classic error is leaving goods with customers at invoice value — always bring them back at **cost**. The interest/royalty/dividend one-liners are standard "fill-marks."

### D6. AS 7 — Construction Contract, Percentage of Completion (level: Medium)

**Problem:** Bridge Ltd has a contract with agreed price ₹1,00,00,000. As on 31.03.2024: cost incurred to date ₹40,00,000; estimated further cost to complete ₹40,00,000; progress bills raised ₹45,00,000; cash received ₹35,00,000. Show the amounts recognised in the Statement of P&L and the presentation in the Balance Sheet.

**Solution:**

**Working Note 1 — Stage of completion:**
Total estimated cost = 40,00,000 + 40,00,000 = ₹80,00,000.
% completion = Cost to date ÷ Total estimated cost = 40,00,000 ÷ 80,00,000 = **50%**.

**Working Note 2 — Recognition in P&L:**
| Item | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Contract revenue (50% × 1,00,00,000) | 50,00,000 |
| Contract cost recognised | (40,00,000) |
| **Profit recognised** | **10,00,000** |

**Working Note 3 — Balance Sheet (Amount due from/to customers):**
Costs incurred + Recognised profits − Progress billings
= 40,00,000 + 10,00,000 − 45,00,000 = **₹5,00,000 due FROM customers** (shown as an asset — "Gross amount due from customers").
Receivable outstanding = Progress bills 45,00,000 − Cash 35,00,000 = ₹10,00,000 (Trade receivable).

**Recognise Revenue ₹50,00,000, Cost ₹40,00,000, Profit ₹10,00,000; Due from customers ₹5,00,000.**

**> Marks tip:** Keep "progress billing" out of revenue — revenue is % × contract price, not the amount billed. The *due from/to customers* formula (Cost + Profit − Billing) is a guaranteed 2 marks.

### D7. AS 4 — Events After the Balance Sheet Date (level: Medium)

**Problem:** The Balance Sheet of Ray Ltd is dated 31.03.2024 and is approved by the Board on 15.06.2024. State the treatment of each event:
(a) A debtor owing ₹4,00,000 as on 31.03.2024 was declared insolvent on 20.04.2024; only 25% is recoverable.
(b) Inventory worth ₹6,00,000 was destroyed by fire on 05.05.2024.
(c) The Board proposed an equity dividend of ₹5,00,000 on 10.05.2024 for FY 2023-24.
(d) A court case pending on 31.03.2024 was settled on 30.04.2024, confirming a liability of ₹2,00,000.

**Solution:**
- **(a) Adjusting event.** Insolvency after the B/S date provides additional evidence of a condition (doubtful recovery) that existed at 31.03.2024. **Adjust:** write off 75% and provide; carry the debtor at ₹1,00,000. Charge ₹3,00,000 to P&L.
- **(b) Non-adjusting event.** The fire is a *new* condition arising after year-end; it does not relate to conditions existing on 31.03.2024. **No adjustment**; disclose the nature and estimated financial effect (₹6,00,000) in the notes if material.
- **(c) Proposed dividend — non-adjusting (post-amendment).** Under AS 4 (revised), a dividend proposed after the balance sheet date is **not recognised as a liability** at 31.03.2024 (it is not a present obligation then). It is **disclosed** in the notes to accounts.
- **(d) Adjusting event.** Settlement confirms a **present obligation that existed** on the B/S date. **Provide** ₹2,00,000 as a liability.

**> Marks tip:** The high-frequency trap is proposed dividend — after the AS 4 amendment it is **disclosure only, not a provision**. Always justify "adjusting vs non-adjusting" by whether the condition *existed on the balance sheet date*.

### D8. AS 5 — Policy vs Estimate vs Prior Period Item (level: Medium)

**Problem:** Classify and state the treatment under AS 5 for the year 2023-24 of each item for Nova Ltd, and prepare the disclosure line where relevant:
(a) The company changed its method of charging depreciation from SLM to WDV.
(b) The rate of provision for warranty was revised from 1% to 2% of sales based on recent claim data.
(c) Electricity expense of ₹50,000 relating to March 2023 was omitted last year and recorded now.
(d) A one-time loss of ₹8,00,000 from an earthquake that damaged a plant.

**Solution:**
- **(a) Change in accounting estimate.** Under revised AS 10, a change in the method of depreciation is treated as a **change in accounting estimate** — applied **prospectively** over the remaining useful life; the unamortised carrying amount is depreciated on the new basis. (It is *not* a change in policy and there is no retrospective restatement.)
- **(b) Change in accounting estimate.** Warranty provisioning is an estimate; a revision based on new claim experience is applied **prospectively**. Disclose the effect (change in warranty charge) if material.
- **(c) Prior period item.** Omitted electricity expense of a prior period. Include ₹50,000 in the current P&L but **disclose it separately** so the current-period result is understood. Suggested disclosure: *"Prior period item — electricity expense of FY 2022-23 ₹50,000."*
- **(d) Ordinary activity, disclosed separately (not extraordinary).** AS 5 recognises very few items as extraordinary; an earthquake loss is disclosed as an item of such **size/nature/incidence that separate disclosure** is relevant to explain performance — shown as a separate line within profit from ordinary activities, ₹8,00,000.

**> Marks tip:** Depreciation-method change = **estimate (prospective)**, NOT policy — this reversal from the old rule is heavily tested. For prior period items, the marks are for "*include in current P&L but disclose separately*," not for restating last year.

### D9. AS 11 — Foreign Exchange incl. Forward Contract (level: Medium)

**Problem:** On 01.01.2024, Orbit Ltd imported machinery for **USD 1,00,000**, payment due 30.06.2024. Spot rate on 01.01.2024 was ₹74/USD and on 31.03.2024 (year-end) was ₹75/USD. On 01.01.2024 the company also booked a **6-month forward contract** to buy USD 1,00,000 at a forward rate of ₹76/USD to hedge the payable. Show the accounting for the year ended 31.03.2024.

**Solution:**

**Working Note 1 — Machinery (non-monetary item):** Recorded at the transaction-date spot rate and **not restated**.
Cost = 1,00,000 × 74 = **₹74,00,000** (carried at this figure at year-end).

**Working Note 2 — Creditor (monetary item):** Restated at the closing rate.
- Initial: 1,00,000 × 74 = ₹74,00,000
- At 31.03.2024: 1,00,000 × 75 = ₹75,00,000
- **Exchange loss = ₹1,00,000** charged to P&L (does not touch the machinery cost).

**Working Note 3 — Forward contract (AS 11 para 36):** Premium/discount on a forward contract is amortised over its life.
- Forward premium = (Forward rate 76 − Spot at inception 74) × 1,00,000 = ₹2,00,000.
- Life = 6 months; portion for 3 months (Jan–Mar) = 2,00,000 × 3/6 = **₹1,00,000** charged to P&L.
- (The exchange-difference leg of the forward — restatement from 74 to 75 — offsets against the payable's loss; the premium is a separate cost.)

**P&L for FY 2023-24: exchange loss on payable ₹1,00,000 + forward premium amortised ₹1,00,000; Machinery stays at ₹74,00,000.**

**> Marks tip:** Never restate a non-monetary asset (machinery) — that is the number-one AS 11 error. Split the forward into **premium amortisation** (over life) vs **exchange difference** (to P&L) — showing both legs earns full marks.

### D10. AS 13 — Cost of Investment Purchased Cum-Interest (level: Medium)

**Problem:** On 01.08.2023, Comet Ltd purchased **500, 12% Debentures of ₹100 each** at **₹105 cum-interest**. Brokerage was 1% of the cum-interest price. Interest is payable half-yearly on 30th September and 31st March. Compute the cost of the investment and the interest to be accounted.

**Solution:**

**Working Note 1 — Amounts paid:**
- Cum-interest price = 500 × 105 = ₹52,500
- Brokerage @1% of 52,500 = ₹525 (added to cost of investment per AS 13)
- Total cash paid = 52,500 + 525 = ₹53,025

**Working Note 2 — Accrued interest built into price:** Last interest date = 31.03.2023; purchase = 01.08.2023 → **4 months** accrued.
Face value = 500 × 100 = ₹50,000.
Accrued interest = 50,000 × 12% × 4/12 = **₹2,000** (this is income, not part of cost).

**Working Note 3 — Cost of investment:**
| Item | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Cum-interest price | 52,500 |
| Add: Brokerage | 525 |
| Less: Accrued interest | (2,000) |
| **Capital cost of investment** | **51,025** |

**Journal:**
| Particulars | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Investment in Debentures A/c ..... Dr | 51,025 | |
| Interest on Debentures A/c ..... Dr | 2,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Bank A/c | | 53,025 |

**Cost of investment = ₹51,025; accrued interest ₹2,000 taken to Interest income.**

**> Marks tip:** Brokerage is **added** to cost; accrued interest is **removed** from cost and parked in the Interest account. Splitting the ₹53,025 into these two legs is exactly what the marks scheme checks.

### D11. AS 3 — Cash Flow from Operating Activities, Indirect Method (level: Medium)

**Problem:** From the data of Vega Ltd for FY 2023-24, compute cash generated from operating activities (indirect method):
Net profit before tax ₹5,00,000; Depreciation ₹1,20,000; Loss on sale of machinery ₹20,000; Interest expense (on borrowings) ₹40,000; Increase in trade receivables ₹60,000; Decrease in inventory ₹30,000; Increase in trade payables ₹25,000; Income tax paid ₹90,000.

**Solution:**

**Cash Flow from Operating Activities (Indirect Method):**
| Particulars | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Net profit before tax | | 5,00,000 |
| Add: Depreciation | 1,20,000 | |
| Add: Loss on sale of machinery | 20,000 | |
| Add: Interest expense (financing item) | 40,000 | 1,80,000 |
| **Operating profit before working capital changes** | | **6,80,000** |
| Less: Increase in trade receivables | (60,000) | |
| Add: Decrease in inventory | 30,000 | |
| Add: Increase in trade payables | 25,000 | (5,000) |
| **Cash generated from operations** | | **6,75,000** |
| Less: Income tax paid | | (90,000) |
| **Net cash from operating activities** | | **5,85,000** |

**Net cash from operating activities = ₹5,85,000.**

**> Marks tip:** Non-cash and financing/investing items (depreciation, loss on sale, interest) must be **added back** before working-capital adjustments. Interest is added here but belongs to *financing* in the full statement — never leave it inside operating profit.

### D12. AS 7 — Construction Contract with a Foreseeable Loss (level: Hard)

**Problem:** Titan Ltd has a contract with agreed price ₹1,20,00,000. As on 31.03.2024: cost incurred to date ₹60,00,000; estimated further cost to complete ₹80,00,000. Show the amounts to be recognised in the Statement of P&L and the treatment of the expected loss under AS 7.

**Solution:**

**Working Note 1 — Total outcome:**
Total estimated cost = 60,00,000 + 80,00,000 = ₹1,40,00,000.
This **exceeds** the contract price ₹1,20,00,000 → an **expected total loss of ₹20,00,000**.

**AS 7 (para 35) rule:** When it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue, the **expected loss is recognised as an expense immediately**, irrespective of the stage of completion.

**Working Note 2 — Stage of completion:** = 60,00,000 ÷ 1,40,00,000 = **42.857%**.

**Working Note 3 — Recognition in P&L:**
| Item | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Contract revenue (42.857% × 1,20,00,000) | 51,42,857 |
| Contract cost recognised (balancing → to give full loss) | (71,42,857) |
| **Loss recognised (= full expected loss)** | **(20,00,000)** |

**Working Note 4 — Provision for foreseeable loss:**
Cost charged to P&L 71,42,857 − Cost actually incurred to date 60,00,000 = **₹11,42,857** → recognised as a **Provision for expected loss** (liability), since it relates to future work.

**Recognise Revenue ₹51,42,857 and the full expected loss ₹20,00,000 immediately; create a provision of ₹11,42,857 for the loss on remaining work.**

**> Marks tip:** The whole ₹20,00,000 loss must hit *this* year regardless of 42.857% completion — recognising only the "loss to date" is the classic wrong answer. Deriving the provision as (cost charged − cost incurred) shows the examiner you understand *why* a liability arises.

### D13. AS 13 — Investment Account with Bonus, Rights & Sale (level: Hard)

**Problem:** Prepare the Investment Account (equity shares of Y Ltd, face value ₹10) in the books of X Ltd (a long-term investor) for FY 2023-24:
- 01.04.2023: Opening balance 10,000 shares at cost ₹1,50,000.
- 01.06.2023: Purchased 5,000 shares @ ₹20 each; brokerage 1%.
- 01.08.2023: Received bonus shares in the ratio **1:5**.
- 01.10.2023: Rights issue **1:6** at ₹12 per share. X Ltd subscribed to **60%** of its rights and **sold the remaining 40% rights** @ ₹3 per right.
- 01.02.2024: Sold 8,000 shares @ ₹25 each; brokerage 1%.
- Market price on 31.03.2024: ₹18 per share.

**Solution:**

**Working Note 1 — Shareholding & cost build-up:**
| Event | Shares | Cost added (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 10,000 | 1,50,000 |
| 01.06 Purchase 5,000 @20 = 1,00,000 + 1% brokerage 1,000 | 5,000 | 1,01,000 |
| Sub-total | 15,000 | 2,51,000 |
| 01.08 Bonus 1:5 on 15,000 (free) | 3,000 | 0 |
| Sub-total | 18,000 | 2,51,000 |
| 01.10 Rights 1:6 on 18,000 = 3,000; subscribe 60% = 1,800 @12 | 1,800 | 21,600 |
| **Before sale** | **19,800** | **2,72,600** |

**Working Note 2 — Rights renounced (sold):** 40% of 3,000 = 1,200 rights sold @ ₹3 = **₹3,600**. For a long-term investment, sale proceeds of rights renounced are **credited to Profit & Loss** (not deducted from cost).

**Working Note 3 — Sale on 01.02.2024 (average cost basis):**
- Average cost per share = 2,72,600 ÷ 19,800 = ₹13.7677
- Cost of 8,000 shares sold = 8,000 × 13.7677 = **₹1,10,141**
- Sale proceeds = 8,000 × 25 = 2,00,000 − 1% brokerage 2,000 = **₹1,98,000**
- **Profit on sale = 1,98,000 − 1,10,141 = ₹87,859**

**Working Note 4 — Closing valuation:** Closing = 19,800 − 8,000 = **11,800 shares**; cost = 2,72,600 − 1,10,141 = **₹1,62,459**. Market value = 11,800 × 18 = ₹2,12,400 > cost. A long-term investment is carried at **cost** (no permanent diminution). Balance c/d = ₹1,62,459.

**Investment Account (in books of X Ltd)**
| Date | Particulars | Face (₹) | Cost (₹) | Date | Particulars | Face (₹) | Cost (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01.04 | To Balance b/d | 1,00,000 | 1,50,000 | 01.02 | By Bank (sale) | 80,000 | 1,98,000 |
| 01.06 | To Bank | 50,000 | 1,01,000 | 31.03 | By Balance c/d | 1,18,000 | 1,62,459 |
| 01.08 | To Bonus | 30,000 | — | | | | |
| 01.10 | To Bank (rights) | 18,000 | 21,600 | | | | |
| 01.02 | To P&L (profit on sale) | — | 87,859 | | | | |
| | **Total** | **1,98,000** | **3,60,459** | | **Total** | **1,98,000** | **3,60,459** |

Rights sold ₹3,600 → credited to P&L separately (not routed through the Investment A/c cost column).

**Closing investment (11,800 shares) carried at cost = ₹1,62,459; profit on sale ₹87,859 and rights sale ₹3,600 to P&L.**

**> Marks tip:** Bonus shares add units but **zero cost** (they lower the average cost) — sale cost must use that reduced average. Sale of *renounced rights* goes to **P&L**, not against cost. The account must balance (₹3,60,459 = ₹3,60,459); an unbalanced ledger signals an error to the examiner.

### D14. AS 14 — Amalgamation in the Nature of Purchase (level: Hard)

**Problem:** A Ltd takes over the business of B Ltd on 31.03.2024. B Ltd's assets are agreed at ₹15,50,000 and its sundry creditors of ₹3,00,000 are taken over. The purchase consideration is discharged by A Ltd issuing **1,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at ₹12 per share** plus **cash ₹1,00,000**. It is an amalgamation in the nature of **purchase**. Compute the purchase consideration, goodwill/capital reserve, and pass the journal entries in the books of A Ltd.

**Solution:**

**Working Note 1 — Purchase Consideration (payments made):**
| Component | ₹ |
|---|---|
| 1,00,000 equity shares × ₹12 | 12,00,000 |
| Cash | 1,00,000 |
| **Purchase Consideration** | **13,00,000** |

**Working Note 2 — Goodwill / Capital Reserve:**
Net assets taken over = Agreed assets 15,50,000 − Creditors 3,00,000 = ₹12,50,000.
PC 13,00,000 − Net assets 12,50,000 = **Goodwill ₹50,000** (purchase method: excess of PC over net assets = goodwill).

**Journal Entries in the books of A Ltd:**
| Particulars | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| **(1)** Business Purchase A/c ..... Dr | 13,00,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Liquidator of B Ltd A/c | | 13,00,000 |
| *(Being purchase consideration due)* | | |
| **(2)** Sundry Assets A/c ..... Dr | 15,50,000 | |
| Goodwill A/c ..... Dr | 50,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Sundry Creditors A/c | | 3,00,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Business Purchase A/c | | 13,00,000 |
| *(Being assets & liabilities recorded at agreed values)* | | |
| **(3)** Liquidator of B Ltd A/c ..... Dr | 13,00,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Equity Share Capital A/c | | 10,00,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Securities Premium A/c | | 2,00,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Bank A/c | | 1,00,000 |
| *(Being PC discharged: 1,00,000 shares of ₹10 at ₹2 premium + cash)* | | |

**Cross-checks:** Entry (2): Dr 16,00,000 = Cr 16,00,000. Entry (3): shares 10,00,000 + premium 2,00,000 + cash 1,00,000 = 13,00,000. **Goodwill recognised = ₹50,000.**

**> Marks tip:** In the **purchase** method, assets/liabilities go in at **agreed (fair) values** and the excess of PC over net assets is **goodwill** (a shortfall would be Capital Reserve). The ₹2 premium on shares must sit in **Securities Premium**, not Share Capital — mis-splitting this loses the discharge-entry marks.

### D1. AS 16 — Borrowing Costs (level: Easy)

**Problem:** Alpha Ltd borrowed **Rs 50,00,000 @ 10% p.a.** on 1 April 2024 *specifically* to construct a qualifying asset. Construction began on 1 April 2024 and the asset was ready for its intended use on **31 January 2025**. Surplus funds were **not** temporarily invested. Determine the borrowing cost to be capitalised for FY 2024-25 and the amount charged to the Statement of Profit & Loss.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Total interest for the year*
Rs 50,00,000 × 10% = **Rs 5,00,000** (12 months).

*Working Note 2 — Capitalisation period (AS 16, para 14 & 21)*
Capitalisation runs from commencement (1 Apr 2024) until the asset is ready for use (31 Jan 2025) = **10 months**. Interest after that date is a period cost.

| Element | Period | Amount (Rs) | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interest 1 Apr 2024 – 31 Jan 2025 | 10 months | 50,00,000 × 10% × 10/12 = **4,16,667** | Capitalise (add to asset) |
| Interest 1 Feb 2025 – 31 Mar 2025 | 2 months | 50,00,000 × 10% × 2/12 = **83,333** | Charge to P&L |
| **Total** | 12 months | **5,00,000** | — |

**Answer: Borrowing cost capitalised = Rs 4,16,667; charged to Statement of P&L = Rs 83,333.**

**> Marks tip:** The single most-tested point is *stopping* capitalisation the day the asset is ready for use — show the 10-month vs 2-month split explicitly; students who capitalise the full Rs 5,00,000 lose half the marks.

### D2. AS 29 — Provisions & Contingent Liabilities (level: Easy)

**Problem:** Beta Ltd sells goods with a warranty covering repair of manufacturing defects for one year. Past experience shows that of all items sold: **80%** carry no defect, **15%** develop minor defects, and **5%** develop major defects. If minor defects were found in *all* products the repair cost would be **Rs 1,00,000**; if major defects were found in all products it would be **Rs 5,00,000**. Compute the warranty provision to be recognised at year-end and justify recognition.

**Solution:**

*Recognition test (AS 29, para 14):* A provision is recognised because (a) there is a **present obligation** (legal — the warranty) arising from a past event (the sale), (b) an **outflow is probable**, and (c) the amount can be **reliably estimated**.

*Measurement — "expected value" method (AS 29, para 40)* — where a large population of items is involved, the obligation is measured by weighting all possible outcomes by their probabilities:

| Outcome | Probability | Cost if applied to all (Rs) | Expected cost (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| No defect | 0.80 | 0 | 0 |
| Minor defect | 0.15 | 1,00,000 | 15,000 |
| Major defect | 0.05 | 5,00,000 | 25,000 |
| **Provision** | | | **40,000** |

**Answer: Warranty provision = Rs 40,000.**

**> Marks tip:** State the three recognition conditions in one line *before* computing — examiners award marks for the AS 29 test, not just the arithmetic. Use "expected value" (not "most likely outcome") because a large population of items is involved.

### D3. AS 20 — Basic EPS (level: Easy)

**Problem:** Gamma Ltd earned a **net profit after tax of Rs 25,00,000** for FY 2024-25. It has **5,00,000 equity shares of Rs 10** outstanding throughout the year and **10% cumulative preference shares of Rs 20,00,000**. Compute Basic Earnings Per Share.

**Solution:**

*Working Note — Preference dividend (AS 20, para 12):* For **cumulative** preference shares, the dividend for the period is deducted **whether or not declared**.
Preference dividend = 10% × Rs 20,00,000 = **Rs 2,00,000**.

*Basic EPS formula (para 10):*
= (Net profit attributable to equity ÷ Weighted average number of equity shares)
= (25,00,000 − 2,00,000) ÷ 5,00,000
= 23,00,000 ÷ 5,00,000

**Answer: Basic EPS = Rs 4.60 per share.**

**> Marks tip:** The trap is forgetting to subtract preference dividend. For *cumulative* shares you deduct it even if unpaid; for *non-cumulative* only if declared — state which rule you applied.

### D4. AS 26 — Intangible Assets (level: Easy)

**Problem:** Delta Ltd incurred the following on an internal project during FY 2024-25: **research phase Rs 6,00,000** and, after all recognition criteria were met, **development phase Rs 14,00,000**. The developed asset became available for use on **1 October 2024** and has a useful life of **10 years** (SLM, nil residual). Show the accounting treatment and the carrying amount at 31 March 2025.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Research vs development (AS 26, paras 41 & 44):*
- Research expenditure of **Rs 6,00,000** → cannot be capitalised → **charged to P&L**.
- Development expenditure of **Rs 14,00,000** → recognition criteria met → **capitalised** as an intangible asset.

*Working Note 2 — Amortisation (para 63):* Begins when the asset is available for use (1 Oct 2024), i.e. **6 months** in FY 2024-25.
Amortisation = (14,00,000 ÷ 10) × 6/12 = **Rs 70,000**.

*Carrying amount at 31 March 2025* = 14,00,000 − 70,000 = **Rs 13,30,000**.

**Answer: Charge Rs 6,00,000 (research) + Rs 70,000 (amortisation) to P&L; carry the intangible asset at Rs 13,30,000.**

**> Marks tip:** Two easy marks are lost by capitalising research cost and by amortising for the full year — amortisation starts only from the date the asset is *available for use*.

### D5. AS 22 — Deferred Tax (single timing difference) (level: Easy)

**Problem:** For FY 2024-25 Epsilon Ltd charged depreciation of **Rs 1,00,000** in its books (SLM) but claimed **Rs 1,50,000** as depreciation under the Income-tax Act (WDV). The applicable tax rate is **30%**. Compute the deferred tax for the year and state whether it is an asset or a liability.

**Solution:**

*Working Note — timing difference (AS 22, para 13):*
Tax depreciation (1,50,000) exceeds book depreciation (1,00,000) by **Rs 50,000**. Because taxable income is *lower* than book income now (more tax deduction taken early), tax payable is *deferred to later years* → this creates a **Deferred Tax Liability**.

Deferred tax = Timing difference × Tax rate = 50,000 × 30% = **Rs 15,000**.

*Journal entry:*
Statement of P&L (Deferred Tax Expense) A/c ....... Dr 15,000
   To Deferred Tax Liability A/c ................................. 15,000

**Answer: Deferred Tax Liability = Rs 15,000 (debited to the Statement of P&L).**

**> Marks tip:** State the *direction* logic in words ("tax dep > book dep → income lower now → DTL"). Just quoting the number without identifying DTA vs DTL costs a mark.

### D6. AS 16 — Specific + General Borrowings (level: Medium)

**Problem:** On 1 April 2024, Zeta Ltd began constructing a qualifying asset. Expenditure was incurred as: **Rs 20,00,000 on 1 April 2024**, **Rs 24,00,000 on 1 August 2024**, and **Rs 12,00,000 on 1 January 2025**. Borrowings outstanding for the whole year:
- Specific loan for this asset: **Rs 20,00,000 @ 9%** (drawn 1 April 2024).
- General borrowings: **Rs 30,00,000 @ 11%** and **Rs 20,00,000 @ 13%**.

Construction was still in progress at 31 March 2025. Compute the borrowing cost to be capitalised.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Specific borrowing (AS 16, para 10):* Capitalise actual interest on the specific loan (no temporary investment income).
= 20,00,000 × 9% = **Rs 1,80,000**.

*Working Note 2 — Capitalisation rate on general borrowings (para 12):*
= Total general interest ÷ Total general borrowings
= (30,00,000 × 11% + 20,00,000 × 13%) ÷ (30,00,000 + 20,00,000)
= (3,30,000 + 2,60,000) ÷ 50,00,000
= 5,90,000 ÷ 50,00,000 = **11.8%**.

*Working Note 3 — Expenditure funded by general borrowings (weighted for time):*
The Rs 20,00,000 spent on 1 April is fully met by the specific loan. The balance of the outlay is funded from general borrowings:

| Date | Expenditure from general funds (Rs) | Months outstanding | Weighted amount (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Aug 2024 | 24,00,000 | 8/12 | 16,00,000 |
| 1 Jan 2025 | 12,00,000 | 3/12 | 3,00,000 |
| **Total** | | | **19,00,000** |

General borrowing cost = 19,00,000 × 11.8% = **Rs 2,24,200**
(within the actual general interest of Rs 5,90,000 → fully capitalisable).

*Total to capitalise* = 1,80,000 + 2,24,200 = **Rs 4,04,200**.

**Answer: Borrowing cost capitalised = Rs 4,04,200** (Rs 1,80,000 specific + Rs 2,24,200 general); balance of general interest Rs 3,65,800 charged to P&L.

**> Marks tip:** Two things earn the marks: (i) computing the capitalisation rate correctly, and (ii) applying it only to the *time-weighted* expenditure funded by general borrowings (not the whole outlay). Cap the general amount at actual general interest — mention it even though it does not bind here.

### D7. AS 19 — Finance Lease, Lessee (arrears) (level: Medium)

**Problem:** X Ltd took a machine on lease on **1 April 2024**. Fair value **Rs 10,00,000**; lease term **3 years**; annual lease rental **Rs 4,00,000** payable at the **end** of each year; guaranteed residual value nil; interest rate implicit in the lease **12%**. Ownership does not pass at the end. Classify the lease, state the recorded amount, prepare the finance-charge allocation table, and give the Year-1 journal entries.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Classification & recorded amount (AS 19, paras 8 & 11):*
PV of Minimum Lease Payments = 4,00,000 × PVIFA(12%, 3 yrs)
PVIFA(12%,3) = (1 − 1.12⁻³) / 0.12 = (1 − 0.71178) / 0.12 = **2.40183**
PV of MLP = 4,00,000 × 2.40183 = **Rs 9,60,732** (≈ 96% of fair value → substantially all risks & rewards transfer → **finance lease**).
Recorded at **lower of fair value (10,00,000) and PV of MLP (9,60,732) = Rs 9,60,732**.

*Working Note 2 — Finance-charge allocation (para 25, effective interest):*

| Year | Opening liability (Rs) | Interest @12% (Rs) | Payment (Rs) | Principal (Rs) | Closing liability (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 9,60,732 | 1,15,288 | 4,00,000 | 2,84,712 | 6,76,020 |
| 2 | 6,76,020 | 81,122 | 4,00,000 | 3,18,878 | 3,57,142 |
| 3 | 3,57,142 | 42,858 | 4,00,000 | 3,57,142 | 0 |

(Total interest 2,39,268 = total payments 12,00,000 − principal 9,60,732 ✓)

*Working Note 3 — Depreciation (para 23):* No reasonable certainty of ownership → depreciate over the **shorter of lease term (3 yrs) and useful life** = 3 years.
Depreciation = 9,60,732 ÷ 3 = **Rs 3,20,244** p.a.

*Year-1 journal entries:*

|---|---|---|
| Machinery A/c ..... Dr / To Lease Liability A/c *(initial recognition)* | 9,60,732 | 9,60,732 |

| Lease Liability A/c ..... Dr / To Bank A/c *(rental paid)* | 4,00,000 | 4,00,000 |
| Depreciation A/c ..... Dr / To Machinery A/c | 3,20,244 | 3,20,244 |

**Answer: Finance lease recorded at Rs 9,60,732; Year-1 finance charge Rs 1,15,288 and depreciation Rs 3,20,244 charged to P&L.**

**> Marks tip:** Record at the *lower* of FV and PV of MLP, and depreciate over the *shorter* of lease term and useful life (not the full life) — these two "lower/shorter" rules are where marks are won or lost. Always show the finance-charge table closing to zero.

### D8. AS 28 — Impairment of Assets (level: Medium)

**Problem:** At 31 March 2025 Theta Ltd finds impairment indicators for a machine with a **carrying amount of Rs 20,00,000**. Its **net selling price is Rs 14,00,000**. Value in use is based on expected cash flows of **Rs 5,00,000 per year for 4 years**, discounted at **10%**. Compute the impairment loss (if any) and the revised carrying amount.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Value in use (AS 28, para 4.15):*
VIU = 5,00,000 × PVIFA(10%, 4 yrs)
PVIFA(10%,4) = (1 − 1.10⁻⁴) / 0.10 = (1 − 0.68301) / 0.10 = **3.16987**
VIU = 5,00,000 × 3.16987 = **Rs 15,84,935**.

*Working Note 2 — Recoverable amount (para 4.10):* = higher of net selling price and value in use
= higher of (14,00,000; 15,84,935) = **Rs 15,84,935**.

*Working Note 3 — Impairment loss (para 58):* = Carrying amount − Recoverable amount
= 20,00,000 − 15,84,935 = **Rs 4,15,065**.

*Journal entry:*
Impairment Loss A/c ..... Dr 4,15,065
   To Machine A/c ............................. 4,15,065
(Impairment loss transferred to Statement of P&L.)

**Answer: Impairment loss = Rs 4,15,065; revised carrying amount = Rs 15,84,935.**

**> Marks tip:** Recoverable amount is the *higher* of net selling price and value in use — candidates who take the lower (or stop at NSP) invert the rule and lose the question. Show the PVIFA computation as a working note.

### D9. AS 20 — Diluted EPS with convertible debentures (level: Medium)

**Problem:** Kappa Ltd reports **net profit after tax of Rs 50,00,000**. It has **10,00,000 equity shares** outstanding all year (no preference shares) and **50,000 12% convertible debentures of Rs 100 each**, each convertible into **5 equity shares**. Tax rate **30%**. Compute Basic and Diluted EPS.

**Solution:**

*Basic EPS* = 50,00,000 ÷ 10,00,000 = **Rs 5.00**.

*Working Note 1 — Adjusted earnings for diluted EPS (AS 20, para 26):* Add back post-tax interest that would be saved on conversion.
Interest = 12% × (50,000 × 100) = 12% × 50,00,000 = 6,00,000
Post-tax add-back = 6,00,000 × (1 − 0.30) = **Rs 4,20,000**
Adjusted earnings = 50,00,000 + 4,20,000 = **Rs 54,20,000**.

*Working Note 2 — Potential equity shares:* 50,000 × 5 = **2,50,000 shares**
Diluted shares = 10,00,000 + 2,50,000 = **12,50,000**.

*Dilution test (para 31):* incremental EPS = 4,20,000 ÷ 2,50,000 = Rs 1.68 < basic Rs 5.00 → **dilutive**, so include.

*Diluted EPS* = 54,20,000 ÷ 12,50,000 = **Rs 4.336 → Rs 4.34**.

**Answer: Basic EPS = Rs 5.00; Diluted EPS = Rs 4.34.**

**> Marks tip:** Always add interest back *net of tax*, and confirm the security is *dilutive* (incremental EPS below basic) before including it — an anti-dilutive security must be ignored. Both steps carry marks.

### D10. Redemption of Preference Shares (level: Medium)

**Problem:** The Balance Sheet of Lambda Ltd (extract) at 31 March 2025 shows: Equity share capital (Rs 10) **Rs 20,00,000**; 10% Redeemable Preference Share Capital (Rs 100) **Rs 5,00,000**; Securities Premium **Rs 1,00,000**; General Reserve **Rs 6,00,000**; Surplus in P&L **Rs 2,00,000**. The preference shares are due for redemption **at a 10% premium**. To part-finance the redemption the company issues **10,000 equity shares of Rs 10 at par** for cash. Pass the journal entries and show the transfer to Capital Redemption Reserve.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Amount payable:* Face Rs 5,00,000 + 10% premium Rs 50,000 = **Rs 5,50,000**.
Premium on redemption Rs 50,000 is met from **Securities Premium** (Sec 52).

*Working Note 2 — Capital Redemption Reserve (Sec 55):* Only the portion **not** covered by a fresh issue of shares must be transferred to CRR out of free reserves.
CRR = Nominal value of shares redeemed − Nominal value of fresh issue
= 5,00,000 − (10,000 × 10) = 5,00,000 − 1,00,000 = **Rs 4,00,000** (from General Reserve).

*Journal entries:*

| Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|
| Bank A/c ..... Dr / To Equity Share Capital A/c *(fresh issue)* | 1,00,000 | 1,00,000 |

| Securities Premium A/c ..... Dr / To Premium on Redemption of Pref. Shares A/c | 50,000 | 50,000 |
| General Reserve A/c ..... Dr / To Capital Redemption Reserve A/c | 4,00,000 | 4,00,000 |
| Preference Shareholders A/c ..... Dr / To Bank A/c | 5,50,000 | 5,50,000 |

**Answer: Transfer to Capital Redemption Reserve = Rs 4,00,000; premium on redemption Rs 50,000 adjusted against Securities Premium; total paid to preference shareholders Rs 5,50,000.**

**> Marks tip:** The formula CRR = (nominal value redeemed − nominal value of fresh issue) is the crux; do not transfer the whole Rs 5,00,000. Premium on redemption is charged to Securities Premium/free reserves, *never* to CRR.

### D11. AS 22 — Deferred Tax, multiple differences (level: Medium)

**Problem:** For FY 2024-25 Mu Ltd reports **book profit before tax of Rs 10,00,000**. Adjustments: depreciation per books **Rs 2,00,000** vs per Income-tax Act **Rs 3,50,000**; expenses disallowed under Sec 40A (permanent) **Rs 50,000**; provision for doubtful debts (not deductible until actually written off) **Rs 40,000**. Tax rate **30%**. Compute current tax, deferred tax, and total tax expense, and prove the reconciliation.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Taxable income:*

| Particulars | Rs |
|---|---|
| Book profit before tax | 10,00,000 |
| Add: Depreciation per books | 2,00,000 |

### D13. AS 19 — Finance Lease, rentals in advance (level: Hard)

**Problem:** D Ltd took machinery on lease on **1 April 2024**: lease term **4 years**; annual rental **Rs 3,00,000 payable at the beginning of each year (in advance)**; fair value **Rs 10,50,000**; interest rate implicit in the lease **12%**; no residual value; ownership not transferred; useful life of the machine **5 years**. Determine the recorded value, prepare the finance-charge schedule, give the Year-1 journal entries and the Year-1 P&L charge.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — PV of MLP (annuity due):*
PVIFA-due(12%, 4) = 1 + PVIFA(12%, 3) = 1 + 2.40183 = **3.40183**
PV of MLP = 3,00,000 × 3.40183 = **Rs 10,20,549**
Recorded at **lower of fair value (10,50,000) and PV of MLP (10,20,549) = Rs 10,20,549** → **finance lease**.

*Working Note 2 — Finance-charge schedule (payment first, then interest — because rentals are in advance):*

| Year | Opening (Rs) | Payment at start (Rs) | Balance (Rs) | Interest @12% (Rs) | Closing (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10,20,549 | 3,00,000 | 7,20,549 | 86,466 | 8,07,015 |
| 2 | 8,07,015 | 3,00,000 | 5,07,015 | 60,842 | 5,67,857 |
| 3 | 5,67,857 | 3,00,000 | 2,67,857 | 32,143 | 2,99,999* |
| 4 | 2,99,999 | 3,00,000 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

*Closes to zero (Re 1 rounding). Total interest 1,79,451 = payments 12,00,000 − principal 10,20,549 ✓.

*Working Note 3 — Depreciation (para 23):* shorter of lease term (4) and useful life (5) = **4 years**.
Depreciation = 10,20,549 ÷ 4 = **Rs 2,55,137** p.a.

*Year-1 journal entries:*

| Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|
| Machinery A/c ..... Dr / To Lease Liability A/c *(initial recognition, 1 Apr 24)* | 10,20,549 | 10,20,549 |
| Lease Liability A/c ..... Dr / To Bank A/c *(first rental in advance, 1 Apr 24)* | 3,00,000 | 3,00,000 |
| Finance Charges A/c ..... Dr / To Lease Liability A/c *(interest for year)* | 86,466 | 86,466 |
| Depreciation A/c ..... Dr / To Machinery A/c | 2,55,137 | 2,55,137 |

*Year-1 charge to P&L* = Finance charges 86,466 + Depreciation 2,55,137 = **Rs 3,41,603**.

**Answer: Finance lease recorded at Rs 10,20,549; Year-1 finance charge Rs 86,466, depreciation Rs 2,55,137 (total P&L charge Rs 3,41,603).**

**> Marks tip:** With advance rentals the first payment reduces the liability *immediately* (before any interest accrues) — deduct the payment first, then charge interest on the reduced balance. Candidates who compute interest on the full opening balance in Year 1 overstate the finance cost and lose method marks.

### D14. Redemption of Preference Shares — minimum fresh issue (level: Hard)

**Problem:** The Balance Sheet of Z Ltd at 31 March 2025 (extract): 30,000 Equity shares of Rs 10 fully paid **Rs 3,00,000**; 8,000 **10% Redeemable Preference shares of Rs 100** fully paid **Rs 8,00,000**; Securities Premium **Rs 40,000**; General Reserve **Rs 2,00,000**; Surplus in P&L **Rs 3,00,000**; Bank balance **Rs 4,50,000**. The preference shares are to be redeemed **at a 5% premium**. The company wants to retain a **minimum bank balance of Rs 90,000** after redemption and will issue the **minimum number of equity shares of Rs 10 at a premium of Rs 2** to raise the balance. Determine the number of fresh shares to issue and pass the journal entries.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Cash required for redemption:*
= Face 8,00,000 + 5% premium 40,000 = **Rs 8,40,000**.

*Working Note 2 — Cash available from existing bank:*
= Opening bank 4,50,000 − minimum balance to retain 90,000 = **Rs 3,60,000**.

*Working Note 3 — Funds to raise from fresh issue:*
= 8,40,000 − 3,60,000 = **Rs 4,80,000**.
Issue price per share = Rs 10 + Rs 2 premium = Rs 12.
Number of fresh shares = 4,80,000 ÷ 12 = **40,000 shares** (nominal Rs 4,00,000 + premium Rs 80,000).

*Working Note 4 — Transfer to CRR (Sec 55):*
= Nominal value redeemed − Nominal value of fresh issue = 8,00,000 − 4,00,000 = **Rs 4,00,000**
(from free reserves: General Reserve 2,00,000 + Surplus in P&L 2,00,000).

*Working Note 5 — Premium on redemption Rs 40,000* met from Securities Premium (existing 40,000 + fresh-issue premium 80,000 = 1,20,000 available).

*Journal entries:*

| Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|
| Bank A/c ..... Dr / To Equity Share Capital A/c / To Securities Premium A/c | 4,80,000 | 4,00,000 / 80,000 |
| 10% Redeemable Pref. Share Capital A/c ..... Dr / Premium on Redemption A/c ..... Dr / To Preference Shareholders A/c | 8,00,000 / 40,000 | 8,40,000 |
| Securities Premium A/c ..... Dr / To Premium on Redemption of Pref. Shares A/c | 40,000 | 40,000 |
| General Reserve A/c ..... Dr / Surplus in P&L A/c ..... Dr / To Capital Redemption Reserve A/c | 2,00,000 / 2,00,000 | 4,00,000 |
| Preference Shareholders A/c ..... Dr / To Bank A/c | 8,40,000 | 8,40,000 |

*Verification of bank:* Opening 4,50,000 + fresh issue 4,80,000 − redemption 8,40,000 = **Rs 90,000** = minimum required ✓.

**Answer: Issue 40,000 fresh equity shares (raising Rs 4,80,000); transfer Rs 4,00,000 to Capital Redemption Reserve; closing bank Rs 90,000.**

**> Marks tip:** The "minimum fresh issue" logic is: cash needed − cash available (keeping the required minimum balance) = proceeds required; divide by the *issue price* (including premium) to get the number of shares. Then CRR uses only the *nominal* value of that issue. Mixing up issue price and nominal value is the classic error.

### D15. AS 20 — Basic & Diluted EPS with Rights, Bonus and Convertibles (level: Exam-Hard)

**Problem:** For the year ended 31 March 2025, Omega Ltd's **net profit attributable to equity shareholders is Rs 40,00,000**. Equity shares outstanding on 1 April 2024: **10,00,000**. During the year:
- **1 August 2024:** Rights issue of **1 share for every 5 held at Rs 12**; fair value of a share immediately before the rights issue was **Rs 18**.
- **1 December 2024:** Bonus issue of **1 for 4**.

The company also has **10% Convertible Debentures of Rs 20,00,000**, convertible into **2,00,000 equity shares**, outstanding the whole year. Tax rate **30%**. Compute Basic and Diluted EPS.

**Solution:**

*Working Note 1 — Theoretical ex-rights price (TERP) and rights factor (AS 20, para 22 & Appendix):*
TERP = (Fair value of shares before rights + total amount received on exercise) ÷ shares after rights
= (18 × 10,00,000 + 12 × 2,00,000) ÷ 12,00,000
= (1,80,00,000 + 24,00,000) ÷ 12,00,000 = **Rs 17.00**
Rights adjustment factor = Fair value before rights ÷ TERP = 18 ÷ 17 = **1.05882**.

*Working Note 2 — Bonus factor:* 1-for-4 bonus multiplies pre-bonus shares by **5/4 = 1.25**; a bonus issue is treated as if it existed from the *start* of the earliest period presented.

*Working Note 3 — Weighted average number of equity shares:*

| Period | Months | Shares | Rights adj. | Bonus adj. | Weighted shares |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Apr – 31 Jul | 4/12 | 10,00,000 | × 18/17 | × 1.25 | 4,41,176 |
| 1 Aug – 30 Nov | 4/12 | 12,00,000 | — | × 1.25 | 5,00,000 |
| 1 Dec – 31 Mar | 4/12 | 15,00,000 | — | — | 5,00,000 |
| **Weighted average** | | | | | **14,41,176** |

*Basic EPS* = 40,00,000 ÷ 14,41,176 = **Rs 2.78**.

*Working Note 4 — Diluted EPS:*
Post-tax interest add-back on debentures = 10% × 20,00,000 × (1 − 0.30) = 2,00,000 × 0.70 = **Rs 1,40,000**
Adjusted earnings = 40,00,000 + 1,40,000 = **Rs 41,40,000**
Potential shares on conversion = **2,00,000** (assumed outstanding all year)
Diluted weighted average shares = 14,41,176 + 2,00,000 = **16,41,176**
*Dilution check:* incremental EPS = 1,40,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = Rs 0.70 < basic Rs 2.78 → **dilutive**.

*Diluted EPS* = 41,40,000 ÷ 16,41,176 = **Rs 2.52**.

**Answer: Basic EPS = Rs 2.78; Diluted EPS = Rs 2.52.**

**> Marks tip:** The exam-hard trick is sequencing the adjustments: (i) split the rights issue into a *bonus element* (the 18/17 factor applied only to shares before the rights date) and a *fresh-issue element*, then (ii) apply the 1.25 bonus factor to **every** period before the bonus date. Miss either adjustment and the weighted average — and both EPS figures — are wrong. Always confirm the convertible is dilutive before including it.

| Item | ₹ |

The Board decides to make a **fully-paid bonus issue in the ratio 1:2** (one bonus share for every two shares held). Pass the journal entries and state the order in which the reserves are utilised (Sec. 63, Companies Act 2013).

**Solution:**

**Working Note — bonus amount required**

| Source | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Capital Redemption Reserve | 1,00,000 |
| Securities Premium | 2,00,000 |
| General Reserve | 2,00,000 |
| **Total** | **5,00,000** |

(1) Capital Redemption Reserve A/c      Dr.   1,00,000

(2) Bonus to Shareholders A/c           Dr.   5,00,000
        To Equity Share Capital A/c                     5,00,000
    (Being 50,000 fully-paid bonus shares of ₹10 issued 1:2)
```

**Final answer: Bonus issue of 50,000 fully-paid equity shares of ₹10 each; ₹5,00,000 capitalised from CRR ₹1,00,000 + Securities Premium ₹2,00,000 + General Reserve ₹2,00,000.**

### D2. Rights Issue — Value of a Right (level: Easy)

**Problem:** B Ltd. has 2,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, currently quoted at **₹50** per share (cum-rights). It offers a **rights issue of 1 share for every 4 held at ₹30** per share. Compute (a) the theoretical ex-rights (fair) value of one share, (b) the value of a right per rights share, and (c) the value of a right attaching to one existing share.

**Solution:**

**Working Note — value of a "package" of (4 existing + 1 rights) shares**

| | Shares | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Existing shares held | 4 × ₹50 | 200 |
| Add: 1 rights share subscribed | 1 × ₹30 | 30 |
| **Total** | **5** | **230** |

(a) **Ex-rights (fair) value per share** = 230 ÷ 5 = **₹46**

(b) **Value of right per rights share** = Ex-rights value − subscription price = 46 − 30 = **₹16**

(c) **Value of right per existing share held** = (Cum-rights price − Ex-rights price) = 50 − 46 = **₹4** (also = ₹16 ÷ 4 existing shares = ₹4 ✓)

**Final answer: Ex-rights value ₹46; value of right = ₹16 per rights share = ₹4 per existing share held.**

**> Marks tip:** The two "value of right" figures (per rights share vs per existing share) confuse students — always label which one; the cross-check ₹16 ÷ 4 = ₹4 proves your working.

### D4. Redemption of Preference Shares (level: Easy)

**Problem:** C Ltd. has 10,000 8% Redeemable Preference Shares of ₹100 each, fully paid, **redeemable at a premium of 10%**. Available: General Reserve ₹8,00,000, Securities Premium ₹1,50,000, Surplus (P&L) ₹4,00,000, and sufficient cash. To fund the redemption the company issues **4,000 equity shares of ₹100 each at par**. Pass journal entries and compute the Capital Redemption Reserve required.

**Solution:**

**WN 1 — CRR required** (Sec. 55): Nominal value of preference shares redeemed *not* covered by a fresh issue must be transferred from free reserves to CRR.
= 10,000 × ₹100 − 4,000 × ₹100 = 10,00,000 − 4,00,000 = **₹6,00,000**

**WN 2 — Premium on redemption** = 10% × 10,00,000 = ₹1,00,000, met out of **Securities Premium** (₹1,50,000 available).

**Journal Entries**

```
(1) Bank A/c                              Dr.  4,00,000
        To Equity Share Capital A/c                    4,00,000
    (Fresh issue of 4,000 equity shares of ₹100 at par)

(2) 8% Redeemable Pref. Share Capital A/c Dr. 10,00,000
    Premium on Redemption of Pref. Shares A/c Dr. 1,00,000
        To Preference Shareholders A/c                11,00,000
    (Amount due to preference shareholders incl. 10% premium)

(3) Securities Premium A/c                Dr.  1,00,000
        To Premium on Redemption of Pref. Shares A/c    1,00,000
    (Premium on redemption adjusted against securities premium)

(4) General Reserve A/c                   Dr.  6,00,000
        To Capital Redemption Reserve A/c              6,00,000
    (Nominal value of shares redeemed not covered by fresh issue
     transferred to CRR)

(5) Preference Shareholders A/c           Dr. 11,00,000
        To Bank A/c                                   11,00,000
    (Payment to preference shareholders)
```

**Final answer: CRR to be created = ₹6,00,000; premium on redemption ₹1,00,000 met from Securities Premium; total cash paid to preference shareholders ₹11,00,000.**

**> Marks tip:** CRR = nominal redeemed − nominal *freshly issued* (never − proceeds; par vs premium of the fresh issue does not change the nominal). Premium on redemption must come from securities premium/P&L, never from CRR.

### D5. Accounting for LLP — Profit Distribution (level: Easy)

### D6. ESOP — Employee Stock Options (level: Medium)

**Problem:** On 1 April 2023 X Ltd. granted **10,000 stock options** to employees at an exercise price of ₹50 per share (face value ₹10). Vesting period is 3 years; options are exercisable only after vesting. Fair value of each option on grant date is **₹30**. It is estimated (and the estimate does not change) that **10% of the employees will leave** during the vesting period. (a) Compute the annual employee compensation expense; (b) pass the entry for Year 1; (c) pass the entry when all vested options are exercised in Year 4.

**Solution:**

**WN 1 — Total compensation cost**
Options expected to vest = 10,000 × 90% = 9,000
Total cost = 9,000 × ₹30 (fair value) = **₹2,70,000**
Annual charge = 2,70,000 ÷ 3 = **₹90,000 per year**

**(b) Year 1 entry**

```
Employee Compensation Expense A/c   Dr.   90,000
    To ESOP Outstanding A/c                        90,000
(1/3 of expected vesting cost recognised)
```

**(c) Exercise of 9,000 options in Year 4** (per share: cash ₹50 + ESOP reserve ₹30 → capital ₹10 + premium ₹70)

```
Bank A/c (9,000 × 50)                Dr.  4,50,000
ESOP Outstanding A/c (9,000 × 30)    Dr.  2,70,000
    To Equity Share Capital A/c (9,000 × 10)        90,000
    To Securities Premium A/c (bal. fig.)         6,30,000
(Issue of 9,000 shares on exercise of options)
```

Check: Securities Premium = 9,000 × (₹40 excess of exercise price over face + ₹30 fair value) = 9,000 × 70 = 6,30,000; debits 7,20,000 = credits 90,000 + 6,30,000 ✓.

**Final answer: Annual compensation expense ₹90,000; on exercise, ₹90,000 to capital and ₹6,30,000 to securities premium.**

**> Marks tip:** The compensation cost is fixed at grant-date fair value spread over the vesting period; on exercise the *whole* ESOP Outstanding balance is transferred to securities premium — don't route it through the P&L again.

### D7. Underwriting of Shares (level: Medium)

**Problem:** Y Ltd. issued 1,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, fully underwritten by X, Y and Z in the ratio **5:3:2**. There was **no firm underwriting**. Marked applications received (excluding firm): X 40,000 shares, Y 20,000, Z 10,000; unmarked applications 15,000 shares. Determine the net liability of each underwriter and the underwriting commission @ 5% of the issue price.

**Solution:**

**WN 1 — Total applications** = 40,000 + 20,000 + 10,000 + 15,000 = 85,000 → **shortfall of 15,000 shares** to be taken up by underwriters.

**WN 2 — Statement of net liability** (unmarked applications credited in gross-liability ratio 5:3:2)

| | X | Y | Z | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross liability (5:3:2) | 50,000 | 30,000 | 20,000 | 1,00,000 |
| Less: Marked applications | 40,000 | 20,000 | 10,000 | 70,000 |
| Balance | 10,000 | 10,000 | 10,000 | 30,000 |
| Less: Unmarked (5:3:2 of 15,000) | 7,500 | 4,500 | 3,000 | 15,000 |
| **Net liability (shares)** | **2,500** | **5,500** | **7,000** | **15,000** |

Net total 15,000 = shortfall ✓.

**WN 3 — Commission @ 5% on issue price (on gross liability, ₹10 each)**

| | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross liability × ₹10 × 5% | 25,000 | 15,000 | 10,000 |

**Final answer: Net shares to be taken — X 2,500, Y 5,500, Z 7,000 (total 15,000); commission X ₹25,000, Y ₹15,000, Z ₹10,000.**

**> Marks tip:** Unmarked applications go in the *gross-liability* ratio (not equally); commission is on the **gross** liability underwritten, not the net shares actually taken.

### D8. Buy-Back of Securities (level: Medium)

**Problem:** E Ltd.'s balance sheet shows: Equity Share Capital (2,00,000 shares of ₹10 each) ₹20,00,000; General Reserve ₹15,00,000; Securities Premium ₹5,00,000; Surplus (P&L) ₹8,00,000. The company has no borrowings. It buys back **25,000 equity shares at ₹40 per share** out of free reserves. Verify the buy-back limits and pass the journal entries (including CRR transfer).

**Solution:**

**WN 1 — Limit tests (Sec. 68)**
- *Shares test:* 25,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = 12.5% ≤ 25% of paid-up equity ✓
- *Resources test:* Buy-back amount = 25,000 × ₹40 = ₹10,00,000. Max = 25% × (Paid-up capital + Free reserves) = 25% × (20,00,000 + 15,00,000 + 5,00,000 + 8,00,000) = 25% × 48,00,000 = **₹12,00,000** ≥ 10,00,000 ✓
- *Debt-equity test:* No borrowings → satisfied ✓

**WN 2 — Split of buy-back price** (per share ₹40 = ₹10 capital + ₹30 premium)
Nominal value bought back = 25,000 × ₹10 = ₹2,50,000
Premium on buy-back = 25,000 × ₹30 = ₹7,50,000 → met from Securities Premium ₹5,00,000 + General Reserve ₹2,50,000

**WN 3 — CRR** (Sec. 69): nominal value of shares bought back out of free reserves = **₹2,50,000** transferred to CRR.

**Journal Entries**

```
(1) Equity Share Capital A/c        Dr.  2,50,000

### D10. Investment Account — Fixed Income (level: Medium)

**Problem:** On 1 April 2023, G Ltd. held **₹3,00,000 (face value) 12% Government Bonds** at a cost of ₹2,94,000. Interest is payable half-yearly on **30 June and 31 December**. Transactions during 2023-24:
- 1 Sept 2023: purchased ₹1,20,000 face value at **₹96.50 cum-interest**.
- 1 Nov 2023: sold ₹60,000 face value at **₹99 ex-interest**.

Prepare the Investment Account (three-column: Nominal, Interest, Cost) for the year ended 31 March 2024. Closing stock is valued at cost (weighted average). Ignore brokerage.

**Solution:**

**WN 1 — Opening accrued interest (b/d):** Interest last paid 31 Dec 2022; at 1 April 2023, 3 months (Jan–Mar) accrued = 3,00,000 × 12% × 3/12 = **₹9,000**.

**WN 2 — Purchase (1 Sept, cum-interest):**
Cum price = 1,20,000 × 96.50% = ₹1,15,800
Accrued interest included (30 Jun → 1 Sept = 2 months) = 1,20,000 × 12% × 2/12 = ₹2,400
Cost of investment = 1,15,800 − 2,400 = **₹1,13,400**

**WN 3 — Sale (1 Nov, ex-interest):**
Sale proceeds (cost portion) = 60,000 × 99% = ₹59,400
Accrued interest received (30 Jun → 1 Nov = 4 months) = 60,000 × 12% × 4/12 = ₹2,400
Weighted-avg cost before sale = (2,94,000 + 1,13,400) ÷ (3,00,000 + 1,20,000) = 4,07,400 ÷ 4,20,000 = 97% of face
Cost of 60,000 sold = 60,000 × 97% = ₹58,200 → **Profit on sale = 59,400 − 58,200 = ₹1,200**

**WN 4 — Interest received / accrued:**
30 Jun 2023 (on 3,00,000): 3,00,000 × 6% = ₹18,000
31 Dec 2023 (on holding 3,60,000): 3,60,000 × 6% = ₹21,600
Closing accrued c/d (1 Jan → 31 Mar on 3,60,000): 3,60,000 × 12% × 3/12 = ₹10,800

**WN 5 — Closing balance:** Face 3,60,000; Cost 4,07,400 − 58,200 = **₹3,49,200** (= 3,60,000 × 97% ✓)

**Investment Account (12% Govt. Bonds)**

| Dr. | Nominal | Interest | Cost | | Cr. | Nominal | Interest | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| To Bal b/d | 3,00,000 | 9,000 | 2,94,000 | | By Bank (int. 30 Jun) | — | 18,000 | — |
| To Bank (1 Sep purchase) | 1,20,000 | 2,400 | 1,13,400 | | By Bank (int. 31 Dec) | — | 21,600 | — |
| To P&L (profit on sale) | — | — | 1,200 | | By Bank (1 Nov sale) | 60,000 | 2,400 | 59,400 |
| To P&L (interest income) | — | 41,400 | — | | By Bal c/d | 3,60,000 | 10,800 | 3,49,200 |
| **Total** | **4,20,000** | **52,800** | **4,08,600** | | **Total** | **4,20,000** | **52,800** | **4,08,600** |

**Final answer: Closing investment — Face ₹3,60,000 at cost ₹3,49,200; profit on sale ₹1,200; interest income transferred to P&L ₹41,400.**

**> Marks tip:** Cum-interest = accrued interest sits *inside* the price (subtract to get cost); ex-interest = interest received *separately* (goes to the interest column). Bringing in the ₹9,000 opening accrued interest is the step most students miss — without it the interest column won't reconcile.

**Final answer: Net profit u/s 198 = ₹35,90,000; maximum overall remuneration (11%) = ₹3,94,900; to one whole-time director (5%) = ₹1,79,500.**

**> Marks tip:** Add back income tax, remuneration and **capital losses**; exclude **capital profits** (profit on sale of investments). Only depreciation *as per Sec. 123* is allowed — add back the excess book depreciation. Each add-back/deduction with the correct section reference is a marks-earning line.
