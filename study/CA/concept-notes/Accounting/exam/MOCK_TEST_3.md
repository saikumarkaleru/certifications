### Solution 1(a) — Consolidated Balance Sheet (AS 21)

**Basic facts:** H Ltd. holds 30,000 of 40,000 shares in S Ltd. → **Holding = 75%, Minority = 25%.** Date of acquisition: **1 October 2025 (mid-year)**.

**Working Note 1 — Split of S Ltd.'s Profit & Loss balance into capital (pre-acquisition) and revenue (post-acquisition)**

- P&L at 1 April 2025 (opening): ₹40,000; P&L at 31 March 2026 (closing): ₹1,20,000.
- Profit earned during the year = 1,20,000 − 40,000 = ₹80,000, accruing evenly (₹40,000 per half-year).
- P&L at 1 October 2025 (acquisition date) = 40,000 + 40,000 = **₹80,000 (capital)**.
- Post-acquisition (1 Oct 2025 – 31 Mar 2026) profit = **₹40,000 (revenue)**.

**Working Note 2 — Capital profits of S Ltd. at the date of acquisition**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Reserves (unchanged, all pre-acquisition) | 1,00,000 |
| P&L balance at acquisition (WN 1) | 80,000 |
| Upward revaluation of Fixed Assets on acquisition | 40,000 |
| **Total capital profits** | **2,20,000** |

Net worth of S Ltd. at acquisition = Share Capital 4,00,000 + Capital profits 2,20,000 = **₹6,20,000**.

**Working Note 3 — Goodwill / Capital Reserve on consolidation**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Cost of investment | 4,50,000 |
| Less: Parent's share of net worth at acquisition = 75% × 6,20,000 | (4,65,000) |
| **Capital Reserve on consolidation** | **15,000** |

**Working Note 4 — Minority Interest** (25% of net assets of S Ltd. at 31 March 2026, including the revaluation)

= 25% × (Share Capital 4,00,000 + Reserves 1,00,000 + P&L 1,20,000 + Revaluation 40,000)
= 25% × 6,60,000 = **₹1,65,000**

**Working Note 5 — Consolidated Profit & Loss Account**

| Particulars | ₹ |
|---|---|
| H Ltd.'s own balance | 2,00,000 |
| Add: 75% of S Ltd.'s post-acquisition (revenue) profit = 75% × 40,000 | 30,000 |
| **Consolidated P&L balance** | **2,30,000** |

*(S Ltd.'s Reserves are entirely pre-acquisition, so nothing is added to consolidated Reserves; H Ltd.'s Reserves of ₹3,00,000 carry through.)*

**Consolidated Balance Sheet of H Ltd. and its Subsidiary S Ltd. as on 31 March 2026**

### Solution 1(b) — Construction Contracts (AS 7)

**Part (i) — Profitable contract**

| Particulars | ₹ lakh |
|---|---|
| Contract price | 100 |
| Costs incurred to date | 40 |
| Estimated costs to complete | 40 |
| Total estimated cost | 80 |
| Estimated total profit (100 − 80) | 20 |

**Stage of completion** (cost-to-cost method) = Costs to date ÷ Total estimated cost = 40 ÷ 80 = **50%**.

| Item recognised for the year | ₹ lakh |
|---|---|
| Contract revenue = 50% × 100 | 50 |
| Contract cost = 50% × 80 (= costs to date) | 40 |
| **Profit recognised** = 50% × 20 | **10** |

**Amount due from customer** (gross amount due from customers for contract work):
= Costs incurred + Recognised profit − Progress billings
= 40 + 10 − 45 = **₹5 lakh (an asset — amount due FROM the customer).**

**Part (ii) — Loss-making contract (total estimated cost revised to ₹105 lakh)**

Now expected total cost (₹105 lakh) exceeds the contract price (₹100 lakh), giving an **expected loss of ₹5 lakh**. Under AS 7, when it is probable that total contract costs will exceed total contract revenue, the **expected loss is recognised as an expense immediately and in full**, irrespective of the stage of completion. So the entire ₹5 lakh loss is charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss in the current year (not restricted to the 50% stage).

### Solution 2(b) — Segment Reporting (AS 17)

A segment is a **reportable segment** if it satisfies **any one** of the three 10% quantitative thresholds.

**Test 1 — Segment Revenue ≥ 10% of total revenue (external + inter-segment)**

Total revenue = (400+40)+(60+10)+(30+0)+(20+5)+(10+0) = 440+70+30+25+10 = **575**. 10% = **57.5**.

| Segment | Total revenue | ≥ 57.5? |
|---|---|---|
| A | 440 | Yes |
| B | 70 | Yes |
| C | 30 | No |
| D | 25 | No |
| E | 10 | No |

**Test 2 — Segment Result ≥ 10% of the greater of (all profits) or (all losses), in absolute amount**

Total of profit-making segments = 80 + 15 + 5 + 5 = **105**. Total of loss-making segments (absolute) = **25**. Greater = **105**; 10% = **10.5**.

| Segment | \|Result\| | ≥ 10.5? |
|---|---|---|
| A | 80 | Yes |
| B | 15 | Yes |
| C | 25 | Yes |
| D | 5 | No |
| E | 5 | No |

**Test 3 — Segment Assets ≥ 10% of total assets**

Total assets = 300+90+40+25+20 = **475**. 10% = **47.5**.

| Segment | Assets | ≥ 47.5? |
|---|---|---|
| A | 300 | Yes |
| B | 90 | Yes |
| C | 40 | No |

**Conclusion:** A segment qualifies if it meets **any** test. Reportable segments = **A (all tests), B (Tests 1 & 3), C (Test 2)**. Segments D and E meet none and may be shown together under "Other segments".

**75% external-revenue check:** Total external revenue = 400+60+30+20+10 = **520**. External revenue of reportable segments (A+B+C) = 400+60+30 = **490**. 490 ÷ 520 = **94.2% ≥ 75%** — requirement satisfied, so no further segments need to be designated reportable.

### Solution 4(a) — Amalgamation in the nature of merger (AS 14 — pooling of interests)

**Working Note 1 — Purchase Consideration**

A Ltd. has 50,000 equity shares. B Ltd. issues 4 shares for every 5 held:
Shares issued = 50,000 × 4/5 = **40,000 equity shares of ₹10 each (at par)**.
**Purchase Consideration = 40,000 × ₹10 = ₹4,00,000** (discharged wholly in equity shares).

**Working Note 2 — Nature of amalgamation:** All conditions of an amalgamation in the nature of **merger** are stated to be satisfied (all assets and liabilities taken over, business continued, consideration discharged wholly by issue of equity shares, and assets/liabilities recorded at existing book values). Hence the **pooling of interests method** applies — assets, liabilities and reserves are recorded at their existing carrying amounts, and no goodwill/capital reserve arises on recording the assets.

**Working Note 3 — Reserve adjustment (AS 14, para 35):** The difference between the share capital *issued* by B Ltd. (₹4,00,000) and the share capital of A Ltd. (₹5,00,000) is adjusted in reserves. Since the capital issued (₹4,00,000) is **less** than A Ltd.'s share capital (₹5,00,000), the difference of **₹1,00,000 is credited (added) to reserves** (here, General Reserve).

### Solution 6(b) OR — Interim Financial Reporting (AS 25)

**(i) Objective and general principle.** AS 25 prescribes the **minimum content** of an interim financial report and the **recognition and measurement principles** for interim periods. Its objective is to ensure that reliable and relevant information is provided for a period shorter than a full financial year. The governing principle is that an enterprise should apply **the same accounting policies** in its interim financial statements as it applies in its annual financial statements (the "discrete-period with annual consistency" approach). Measurements for interim purposes are made on a **year-to-date** basis, so that the frequency of reporting does not affect the annual results.

**(ii) Specific treatments.**
- **(a) Seasonal / occasional revenue:** Revenue received seasonally, cyclically or occasionally within a financial year should **not be anticipated or deferred** at an interim date if such anticipation or deferral would not be appropriate at the end of the financial year. It is recognised **when it occurs** — for example, seasonal sales are recognised in the interim period in which they arise, not spread evenly across the year.
- **(b) Income tax expense:** Interim-period income tax is accrued using the **estimated average annual effective tax rate** expected for the full financial year, applied to the pre-tax income of the interim period (the "effective tax rate" approach). This prevents distortion from the uneven incidence of income or tax concessions across the year.
