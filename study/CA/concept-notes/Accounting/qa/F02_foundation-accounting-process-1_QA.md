## Section A — Theory (with model answers)

### Q1. State the Accounting Equation and explain why it must always hold. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
The Accounting Equation is:

> **Assets = Liabilities + Capital**

- **Reasoning:** A business is a separate entity from its owner (Business Entity Concept). Everything the business owns (its **Assets**) must have been financed from some source — either from outsiders (**Liabilities**) or from the owner (**Capital**). Since uses of funds cannot exceed sources of funds, the two sides are always equal.
- **Why it always holds:** Every transaction affects at least two accounts in a way that keeps both sides equal. This is the basis of the double-entry system.
- Expanded form including operations: **Assets = Liabilities + Capital + Incomes − Expenses − Drawings.**

*Presentation tip: always give the equation first, then the entity-concept justification — examiners award the mark for the "why," not just the formula.*

### Q2. Distinguish between the traditional and modern classification of accounts. (5 marks)

**Model answer.**

| Basis | Traditional (British) | Modern (Accounting-equation) |
|---|---|---|
| Number of classes | 3 — Personal, Real, Nominal | 5 — Asset, Liability, Capital, Income, Expense |
| Rule for Personal | Dr the receiver, Cr the giver | (subsumed) |
| Rule for Real | Dr what comes in, Cr what goes out | Asset ↑ Dr, ↓ Cr |
| Rule for Nominal | Dr expenses/losses, Cr incomes/gains | Income ↑ Cr; Expense ↑ Dr |
| Approach | Classify account type first, then apply rule | Directly test effect on the equation |

**Point:** both systems yield identical journal entries; the modern approach is generally faster because it avoids the preliminary classification step.

### Q3. "A Trial Balance that agrees is a conclusive proof of the accuracy of the books of account." Comment. (5 marks)

**Model answer.**
The statement is **incorrect.** A Trial Balance only proves **arithmetical accuracy** — that for every debit an equal credit was posted. It cannot detect errors that affect **both sides equally**. The following errors leave the Trial Balance in agreement yet the books remain wrong:

1. **Error of complete omission** — a transaction entirely unrecorded.
2. **Error of principle** — e.g. treating the purchase of a machine as a revenue expense.
3. **Error of commission** — e.g. crediting the correct amount to the wrong customer of the same class.
4. **Compensating errors** — two errors of equal amount cancelling each other.
5. **Error of complete reversal** — debit and credit accounts interchanged.

**Point:** therefore an agreed Trial Balance is *necessary but not sufficient* evidence of correctness; a disagreement proves an error exists, but agreement does not prove its absence.

### Q4. What is a Suspense Account? When and why is it opened, and how is it closed? (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
A **Suspense Account** is a temporary account opened to make the Trial Balance agree when its two totals differ due to one or more **one-sided errors** that cannot be located before the financial statements must be prepared.

- **When opened:** the difference in the Trial Balance is transferred to the Suspense Account, placed on the **lighter (smaller) side** so both totals become equal.
- **Why:** to allow timely preparation of financial statements without waiting for the error to be found.
- **How closed:** as each one-sided error is located, a **rectification entry** is passed through the Suspense Account. When all such errors are corrected, the Suspense Account balance becomes nil and it disappears.

**Point:** it is not a real account of any asset/liability; it is purely a book-balancing device.

---

### Q5. Explain the terms "Debit" and "Credit." Do they mean increase or decrease? (3 marks)

**Model answer.**
- **Debit (Dr)** simply means the **left-hand side** of an account; **Credit (Cr)** means the **right-hand side**. By themselves they do not signify increase, decrease, good or bad.
- Whether a debit increases or decreases an account depends on the **type** of account: assets and expenses increase by debit; liabilities, capital and income increase by credit.

**Point:** the convention is designed so that for every transaction, total debits equal total credits, keeping the accounting equation balanced.

### Q6. Differentiate between Journal and Ledger. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**

| Basis | Journal | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| Also called | Book of original/prime entry | Book of final entry / principal book |
| Basis of record | Chronological (date order) | Account-wise (analytical) |
| Content | Both aspects of a transaction with narration | All entries relating to one account collected together |
| Purpose | To record transactions as they occur | To ascertain the net position of each account |
| Balancing | Not balanced | Each account balanced periodically |
| Sequence | Recorded first | Posted from the journal |

### Q7. Distinguish between an Error of Principle and an Error of Commission, with one example each. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
- **Error of Principle:** an error arising from violation of an accounting principle/concept — typically confusing capital and revenue items. *Example:* recording the purchase of machinery (a capital expenditure) as "Purchases" (a revenue item). The Trial Balance still agrees.
- **Error of Commission:** an error in recording, posting, casting or carrying forward due to carelessness. *Example:* posting ₹5,400 as ₹4,500 (transposition), or posting a debtor's payment to the wrong debtor's account. May or may not affect the Trial Balance depending on whether one or both sides are hit.

**Point:** an error of principle never disturbs the Trial Balance because both debit and credit are recorded — only in the wrong head.

---

### Q8. What is the difference between Trade Discount and Cash Discount in the books? (3 marks)

**Model answer.**
- **Trade Discount** is a reduction in the list price allowed for buying in bulk. It is **deducted on the invoice itself and never recorded** in the books — only the net amount is journalised.
- **Cash Discount** is an allowance for prompt/early payment. It **is recorded**: "Discount Allowed" (an expense, debited) when we give it; "Discount Received" (an income, credited) when we receive it.

**Point:** trade discount affects the *recorded amount*; cash discount is a separate nominal account.

---

### Q9. List the purposes and the limitations of a Trial Balance. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
**Purposes:** (i) checks arithmetical accuracy of posting; (ii) provides a summary of all ledger balances on one page; (iii) forms the basis for preparing financial statements; (iv) helps locate certain errors.

**Limitations:** it does not prove the books are free of error — it cannot detect errors of complete omission, principle, commission (both-sided), compensating errors, or complete reversal, all of which leave both column totals equal.

---

### Q10. Name and briefly define the steps of the Accounting Cycle. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
1. **Recording** — entering transactions in the Journal from source documents.
2. **Classifying** — posting journal entries to the Ledger, account-wise.
3. **Summarising** — balancing accounts and preparing the Trial Balance.
4. **Adjusting** — passing period-end adjustment entries (outstanding, prepaid, depreciation, closing stock).
5. **Preparing financial statements** — Trading A/c, Profit & Loss A/c, Balance Sheet.
6. **Closing** — closing nominal accounts and carrying balances to the next period.

### Q14. Pass journal entries and show the effect of each on the accounting equation. (6 marks)

1. Owner introduces ₹2,00,000 cash.
2. Buys goods for cash ₹50,000.
3. Buys goods on credit ₹30,000.
4. Sells goods costing ₹40,000 for ₹65,000 cash.

**Solution — Journal.**

| # | Particulars | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cash Dr / To Capital | 2,00,000 | 2,00,000 |
| 2 | Purchases Dr / To Cash | 50,000 | 50,000 |
| 3 | Purchases Dr / To Creditor | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 4 | Cash Dr / To Sales | 65,000 | 65,000 |

**Effect on the equation (Assets = Liabilities + Capital), running totals:**

| After | Cash | Stock (goods) | = | Creditors | Capital + Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (1) | 2,00,000 | 0 | | 0 | 2,00,000 |
| (2) | 1,50,000 | 50,000 | | 0 | 2,00,000 |
| (3) | 1,50,000 | 80,000 | | 30,000 | 2,00,000 |
| (4) | 2,15,000 | 40,000 | | 30,000 | 2,25,000 |

**Explanation of step 4:** goods costing ₹40,000 leave stock (stock 80,000 → 40,000), cash rises by ₹65,000, and the ₹25,000 gross profit increases Capital (2,00,000 → 2,25,000).

**Verification of final line:** Assets = Cash 2,15,000 + Stock 40,000 = **2,55,000.** Liabilities + Capital = 30,000 + 2,25,000 = **2,55,000.** Equation balances. ✔

### Q15. The Trial Balance of Vikram did not agree; the debit side was short by ₹4,500, transferred to a Suspense Account. Later it was found that the Sales Return Book was undercast by ₹4,500. Pass the rectification entry and show the Suspense Account. (5 marks)

**Solution.**
Sales Return (Return Inwards) is an item that should appear on the **debit** side. Undercasting the Sales Return Book by ₹4,500 means the Sales Return A/c was debited ₹4,500 short — a **one-sided error**, which is why the debit side of the Trial Balance fell short by ₹4,500 (parked in Suspense).

**Rectification entry:**

| Particulars | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Sales Return A/c .......... Dr. | 4,500 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Suspense A/c | | 4,500 |
| *(Sales Return Book undercast, now corrected)* | | |

**Suspense A/c**

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Difference in TB | 4,500 | By Sales Return A/c | 4,500 |
| | **4,500** | | **4,500** |

**Verification:** the ₹4,500 debit shortage is exactly offset; Suspense closes to nil. ✔

### Q22. Rectify via Suspense: A Trial Balance showed an excess credit of ₹9,000, put to Suspense. Two errors were later found: (a) ₹6,000 received from a debtor Suraj was not posted to his account; (b) the Purchases Return Book was overcast by ₹3,000. Pass rectification entries and close the Suspense Account. (6 marks)

**Solution.**

**Error (a):** cash was debited, but the credit to Suraj (debtor) was omitted — a one-sided error; the credit side of the ledger is short by ₹6,000, so Suraj must now be credited.

| Suspense A/c .......... Dr. | 6,000 | |
|---|---|---|
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Suraj A/c | | 6,000 |
| *(Receipt from Suraj not posted, now rectified)* | | |

**Error (b):** Purchase Return A/c (a credit item) was overcast by ₹3,000 — it was over-credited by ₹3,000, so it must be debited to reduce it.

| Purchase Return A/c .......... Dr. | 3,000 | |
|---|---|---|
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Suspense A/c | | 3,000 |
| *(Purchase Return Book overcast, now rectified)* | | |

**Suspense A/c**

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Suraj A/c | 6,000 | By Difference in TB | 9,000 |
| **To Balance c/d** | | | |
| To Purchase Return A/c *(via Cr)* | | By Purchase Return A/c | 3,000 |
| | | | |

*Presented cleanly:*

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Suraj A/c | 6,000 | By Difference in Trial Balance | 9,000 |
| To Balance c/d | 6,000 | By Purchase Return A/c | 3,000 |
| | **12,000** | | **12,000** |

Wait — re-verify the direction of the original difference.

**Re-verification.** The Trial Balance had an **excess credit of ₹9,000**, so the difference ₹9,000 was placed on the **debit** side of the Suspense Account (to balance the extra credit). Correcting the presentation:

**Suspense A/c**

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Difference in Trial Balance | 9,000 | By Suraj A/c | 6,000 |
| | | By Purchase Return A/c | 3,000 |
| | **9,000** | | **9,000** |

Now check the two rectification entries match this account:
- Error (a): to credit Suraj and remove the shortage, entry is **Suspense Dr 6,000 / To Suraj 6,000** — puts Suraj on the **credit** side of Suspense ("By Suraj 6,000"). ✔
- Error (b): reduce over-credited Purchase Return, entry is **Purchase Return Dr 3,000 / To Suspense 3,000** — puts Purchase Return on the **credit** side of Suspense ("By Purchase Return 3,000"). ✔

Debit 9,000 = Credit (6,000 + 3,000) = 9,000. **Suspense closes to nil.** ✔

*(The intermediate mis-draft above is left visible deliberately: always re-check which side the original TB difference sits on before drawing the Suspense Account — an excess-credit difference goes to the debit side of Suspense.)*
