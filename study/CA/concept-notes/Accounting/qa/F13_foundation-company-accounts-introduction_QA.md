# Q&A — Foundation: Company Accounts — Introduction (Shares & Debentures)

*Mix of theory (with "how to present for full marks" notes) and fully-solved numericals. Every numerical is self-checked: journals balance, ledgers tally, Balance Sheets foot.*

---

## Part A — Theory

### Q1. Distinguish between "issued capital," "subscribed capital," "called-up capital" and "paid-up capital."

**Model answer.**
- **Issued capital** — that part of the authorised (nominal) capital which the company has offered to the public for subscription.
- **Subscribed capital** — that part of the issued capital which has actually been taken up (applied for and allotted) by the public.
- **Called-up capital** — that part of the subscribed (nominal) amount which the company has, by its calls, demanded from the shareholders so far.
- **Paid-up capital** — the called-up capital *less* calls-in-arrears; i.e., the amount actually received. **Paid-up = Called-up − Calls-in-arrears.**

*Full-marks tip:* present as four distinct bullet points showing each is a subset of the previous, and end with the paid-up formula — examiners reward the nesting logic and the formula explicitly.

---

### Q2. What is the difference between "calls-in-arrears" and "calls-in-advance"? State the treatment and permissible interest under Table F.

**Model answer.** **Calls-in-arrears** is the amount **called but not yet received** from shareholders. It is a receivable, shown on the Balance Sheet as a **deduction from called-up capital** to arrive at paid-up capital. Under Table F, the company may **charge interest up to 10% p.a.** on arrears.

**Calls-in-advance** is the amount **received from a shareholder for calls not yet made**. It is a **liability** (shown under Other Current Liabilities), carries **no voting rights** until the call is made, and under Table F the company may **pay interest up to 12% p.a.** on it. It is not added to paid-up capital.

*Full-marks tip:* the two "trap" facts examiners look for — arrears is *deducted* from capital (not a liability), advance is a *liability* (not added to capital) — and the 10%/12% split.

---

### Q3. Distinguish between shares and debentures on at least five points.

**Model answer.**

| Point | Share | Debenture |
|---|---|---|
| Status of holder | Owner / member | Creditor / lender |
| Return | Dividend | Interest |
| Nature of return | Appropriation of profit (paid only if profits) | Charge against profit (paid whether or not profits) |
| Repayment on winding-up | After all creditors incl. debenture-holders | Before shareholders |
| Voting rights | Yes (equity) | No |
| Issue at discount | Not allowed (except sweat equity) | Allowed |

*Full-marks tip:* the sharpest examiner discriminator is "dividend = appropriation, interest = charge against profit." Always include it.

---

### Q4. What is meant by "over-subscription"? State the three ways a company may deal with it.

**Model answer.** Over-subscription arises when the public applies for **more shares than the company offered**. A company cannot allot more than the issued number, so it deals with the excess in one or a combination of these ways:
1. **Outright rejection** — reject surplus applications entirely and **refund** their money.
2. **Pro-rata allotment** — allot fewer shares than applied for to all (or a group of) applicants, and **adjust the excess application money against allotment** (refunding only any residual).
3. **Combination** — reject some applications and make a pro-rata allotment to the rest.

*Full-marks tip:* stress that under pro-rata, excess money is **first adjusted against allotment, not refunded** — the most common exam mistake.

---

### Q5. Explain the accounting rationale for transferring the profit on re-issue of forfeited shares to "Capital Reserve," and explain why only part of the forfeited amount may be transferred.

**Model answer.** When forfeited shares are re-issued, the amount originally forfeited (retained from the defaulting shareholder) less any discount allowed on re-issue is a **gain arising from a capital transaction** — it does not arise from trading operations. Such a gain is **not a distributable profit**; it is therefore credited to **Capital Reserve**, a non-distributable reserve.

Only the forfeited amount relating to the **shares actually re-issued** is transferred. If only some forfeited shares are re-issued, the forfeited money on the shares **not yet re-issued** must remain in the Forfeited Shares Account until those shares too are re-issued, because no gain is realised until re-sale.

*Full-marks tip:* two ideas earn the marks — (a) capital-nature gain → non-distributable → Capital Reserve; (b) proportionate transfer only for re-issued shares.

---

### Q6. Differentiate "Reserve Capital" from "Capital Reserve."

**Model answer.**
- **Reserve Capital** — the portion of a company's **uncalled** share capital which it resolves (by special resolution under the Companies Act) to call up **only in the event of winding-up**. It is not available to ordinary creditors during the life of the company and is not shown in the Balance Sheet as an amount, only disclosed.
- **Capital Reserve** — a **reserve of a capital nature** (e.g., profit on re-issue of forfeited shares, profit prior to incorporation), which is **not available for distribution** as dividend. It appears under Reserves & Surplus.

*Full-marks tip:* one relates to *uncalled capital callable only on winding-up*; the other is a *non-distributable reserve*. Naming both cleanly avoids the classic confusion.

---

### Q7. When shares are issued at a premium, where does the premium go and what are the permitted uses of the Securities Premium Account?

**Model answer.** The premium (excess of issue price over face value) is credited to the **Securities Premium Account**; it is **not** part of share capital. Its use is restricted by Section 52 of the Companies Act, 2013 to:
1. Issuing **fully paid bonus shares** to members;
2. Writing off **preliminary expenses**;
3. Writing off **expenses, commission or discount on issue** of shares/debentures;
4. Providing for the **premium payable on redemption** of redeemable preference shares or debentures;
5. **Buy-back** of own shares/securities (Section 68).

*Full-marks tip:* citing Section 52 and listing the five permitted uses signals mastery.

---

### Q8. Explain the treatment of "Discount on Issue of Debentures" and "Loss on Issue of Debentures."

**Model answer.** **Discount on issue of debentures** arises when debentures are issued for cash **below their face value**. It is a **capital loss**, debited to a Discount on Issue of Debentures Account, disclosed on the assets side (or netted against the debenture liability under Schedule III), and **written off** over the life of the debentures (against Securities Premium or Statement of P&L).

**Loss on issue of debentures** arises when debentures are **redeemable at a premium**: no premium is received, but the company will have to repay more than it received. The extra amount is recognised **now** by debiting Loss on Issue of Debentures and crediting a liability, **Premium on Redemption of Debentures**. This loss is also written off over the debentures' life.

*Full-marks tip:* the key distinction — discount = shortfall in cash received at issue; loss on issue = extra to be paid at redemption, matched by a liability.

### Q11. Pro-rata allotment with partial rejection.

**Problem.** Yamuna Ltd offered **40,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at par**, payable ₹3 on application, ₹4 on allotment, ₹3 on call. Applications were received for **60,000 shares**. The directors **rejected applications for 4,000 shares** outright and made a **pro-rata allotment** for the balance. Excess application money was adjusted against allotment. All money was received. Show the allotment cash and the entries up to allotment.

**Solution.**

**Step 1 — Sort the applications.** Applied 60,000; rejected 4,000 → applications considered for allotment = **56,000**; shares to be allotted = **40,000**. Pro-rata ratio = 56,000 : 40,000 = **7 : 5**.

**Step 2 — Application money.**
- Received on all 60,000 = 60,000 × ₹3 = **₹1,80,000**.
- Refunded to rejected 4,000 = 4,000 × ₹3 = **₹12,000**.
- Retained (attributable to the 56,000 considered) = ₹1,80,000 − ₹12,000 = **₹1,68,000**.
- Of retained, transferred to capital (on 40,000 allotted) = 40,000 × ₹3 = **₹1,20,000**.
- **Excess to adjust against allotment** = 1,68,000 − 1,20,000 = **₹48,000**. *(Check: excess relates to 16,000 pro-rata surplus shares × ₹3 = ₹48,000 ✓.)*

**Step 3 — Allotment cash.**
- Allotment due = 40,000 × ₹4 = **₹1,60,000**.
- Less excess adjusted = **₹48,000**.
- **Cash on allotment** = 1,60,000 − 48,000 = **₹1,12,000**.

| Particulars | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Bank A/c ................................... Dr | 1,80,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Share Application A/c | | 1,80,000 |
| Share Application A/c ................... Dr | 1,80,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Share Capital A/c (40,000 × ₹3) | | 1,20,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Share Allotment A/c (excess) | | 48,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Bank A/c (refund of 4,000) | | 12,000 |
| Share Allotment A/c ..................... Dr | 1,60,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Share Capital A/c | | 1,60,000 |
| Bank A/c ................................... Dr | 1,12,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Share Allotment A/c | | 1,12,000 |

**Verify.** Share Application A/c: Dr 1,80,000 = Cr (1,20,000 + 48,000 + 12,000) = 1,80,000 ✓. Share Allotment A/c: Dr 1,60,000 = Cr (48,000 + 1,12,000) = 1,60,000 ✓.

### Q14. Forfeiture where premium was already received.

**Problem.** Sabarmati Ltd issued shares of **₹10 at a premium of ₹4**, ₹5 on application (incl. ₹2 premium), ₹5 on allotment (incl. ₹2 premium), ₹4 on final call. ### Q19. Comprehensive — over-subscription, pro-rata, forfeiture and re-issue combined.

**Problem.** Chambal Ltd offered **10,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at par**, payable ₹3 application, ₹4 allotment, ₹3 first & final call. Applications for **13,000 shares** were received; a **pro-rata allotment** was made to all and excess application money adjusted against allotment. Mr. P, who was **allotted 200 shares**, failed to pay allotment and call; his shares were **forfeited** and later **re-issued at ₹8 fully paid**. Compute (i) the excess adjusted against allotment, (ii) allotment cash received, (iii) the forfeiture entry, (iv) the re-issue entry, (v) transfer to Capital Reserve.

**Solution.**

**(i) Pro-rata & excess.** Ratio 13,000 : 10,000 = **13 : 10**. Application received = 13,000 × ₹3 = ₹39,000. Attributable to allotted 10,000 = 10,000 × ₹3 = ₹30,000. **Excess adjusted against allotment = ₹9,000** (= 3,000 surplus shares × ₹3).

**(ii) Allotment cash.** To work out Mr. P's default we need his figures.
- Mr. P was **allotted 200** shares → he must have **applied for** 200 × 13/10 = **260 shares**.
- Application money P paid = 260 × ₹3 = **₹780**. Money needed on his 200 allotted (application portion) = 200 × ₹3 = ₹600. So **P's excess = ₹180**, adjusted against his allotment.
- P's allotment due = 200 × ₹4 = ₹800; less his excess ₹180 = **₹620 that P should have paid on allotment but did not.**

Total allotment due (all) = 10,000 × ₹4 = ₹40,000. Less total excess adjusted ₹9,000 = ₹31,000 receivable in cash. Of this, **P's ₹620 was not received**, so **allotment cash actually received = 31,000 − 620 = ₹30,380.**

**(iii) Forfeiture of P's 200 shares.** Called-up = full ₹10. 
- Amount **received** from P and retained: application portion on 200 allotted = ₹600 **plus** his adjusted excess ₹180 = **₹780** (this is what P actually paid that relates to the allotted shares). 
- Unpaid **allotment** = ₹620; unpaid **call** = 200 × ₹3 = ₹600.
- Share Capital debit = 200 × ₹10 = ₹2,000.

|---|---|---|

| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Share First & Final Call A/c | | 600 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;To Forfeited Shares A/c | | 780 |

Check: 2,000 = 620 + 600 + 780 ✓.

**(iv) Re-issue of 200 at ₹8 fully paid.** Discount = (₹10 − ₹8) × 200 = **₹400** (≤ ₹780 forfeited ✓).

Check: 1,600 + 400 = 2,000 ✓.

**(v) Capital Reserve** = forfeited ₹780 − discount ₹400 = **₹380**.

### Q21 (Theory). State the accounting treatment of "interest on calls-in-arrears" and "interest on calls-in-advance," and whether each affects the company's profit.

**Model answer.** **Interest on calls-in-arrears** is **income** to the company (it charges the defaulting shareholder up to 10% p.a. under Table F): Sundry Shareholders A/c Dr / To Interest on Calls-in-Arrears A/c, credited to the Statement of P&L. **Interest on calls-in-advance** is an **expense** to the company (it pays the shareholder up to 12% p.a.): Interest on Calls-in-Advance A/c Dr / To Shareholder A/c, debited to the Statement of P&L. Thus arrears-interest **increases** profit; advance-interest **reduces** profit.

*Full-marks tip:* the examiner wants the **direction** (income vs expense) and the **10%/12%** limits.

### Q22 (Theory). Why is the amount debited to Share Capital on forfeiture the *called-up* value, and never the *issue price* including premium?

**Model answer.** Share Capital is credited only with the **nominal (face) value** of shares as they are issued — premium is credited separately to Securities Premium, never to Share Capital. Therefore, when shares are forfeited and their nominal value must be removed from capital, the debit to Share Capital equals the **called-up nominal value** (face value called up), because that is exactly what was originally credited there. The premium was never part of Share Capital, so it is dealt with separately: if already received, it is retained; if unreceived, Securities Premium is debited (reversed) on forfeiture.

*Full-marks tip:* tie it back to the principle that Share Capital only ever holds face value, so forfeiture only removes face value.
