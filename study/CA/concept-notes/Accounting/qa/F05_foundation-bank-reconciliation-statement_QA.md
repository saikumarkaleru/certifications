# Q&A — Foundation: Bank Reconciliation Statement

*CA Foundation Paper 1 — Accounting | ICAI 2024 New Scheme | All amounts in Rupees (₹)*

## Section A — Theory / Concept-Check (test the WHY, with "how to present for full marks")

**A1. What is a Bank Reconciliation Statement, and why is it prepared?**
A Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS) is a **memorandum statement** — prepared outside the double-entry system — that reconciles the bank balance shown by the **Cash Book (bank column)** with the balance shown by the **Pass Book / bank statement** on a given date, by listing and adjusting for the specific causes of the difference. It is prepared because the two records of the *same* bank account almost always differ, and the business must satisfy itself (and its auditors) that the difference is fully explained by legitimate, identifiable reasons — and not by fraud, omission, or arithmetical error.
*Full-marks tip: state the three things it achieves — (i) locates errors/omissions in the Cash Book, (ii) detects delays/frauds, (iii) confirms the true bank balance for the Balance Sheet.*

**A2. Why do the Cash Book and Pass Book show different balances even when both are correct?**
Because they record the same transactions on **different dates**. The classic case is a cheque you issue: you credit your Cash Book *the day you write it*, but the bank debits your account only when the payee *presents* it days later. For that interval both books are correct yet disagree. Such **timing differences are self-correcting**. Differences also arise from items the bank has recorded but the business has **not yet** (charges, interest, direct credits) and from **errors**.

**A3. Explain the mirror-image relationship between the two books.**
The same bank balance is an **asset to the account-holder** but a **liability to the bank**. Hence a favourable balance is a **debit balance in the Cash Book** but a **credit balance in the Pass Book**; an overdraft is a **credit balance in the Cash Book** but a **debit balance in the Pass Book**. Likewise, a deposit is debited in the Cash Book but credited by the bank, and a cheque issued is credited in the Cash Book but debited by the bank. Every entry is reversed between the two books.

**A4. Distinguish between the "BRS-only" method and the "Adjusted Cash Book" method.**
Under the **BRS-only** method both books are left untouched; you start from one balance and add/subtract *every* cause of difference to reach the other. Under the **Adjusted Cash Book** method you first pass real entries in the Cash Book for items the business genuinely omitted (bank charges, interest, direct credits, standing orders, dishonours, own errors), strike a **true adjusted balance**, and *then* prepare a short BRS containing only the residual **timing differences**. The adjusted balance is the figure that goes into the Balance Sheet; hence this method is professionally preferred.

**A5. Which items are entered in the Adjusted Cash Book and which are not?**
**Entered:** items the bank has recorded but the business has not — bank charges, interest on overdraft, interest/dividend/bills collected, direct deposits by customers, standing-order payments, dishonour of a deposited cheque — **plus the business's own errors.** **Not entered:** pure timing differences (cheques issued not yet presented; cheques deposited not yet cleared) and any **error committed by the bank** — these belong only in the BRS.

**A6. A question says "debit balance as per Pass Book." What does it mean?**
Since the Pass Book is the *bank's* book, a **debit balance there is an overdraft** — the customer owes the bank. It is *not* a favourable balance. Misreading this reverses every sign in the answer, so it is a frequent exam trap.

**A7. How are cheques "issued but not presented" and "deposited but not cleared" different in their effect?**
A **cheque issued but not presented** means you have already reduced your Cash Book but the bank has not yet paid, so the **Pass Book balance is higher** than the Cash Book. A **cheque deposited but not cleared** means you have already increased your Cash Book but the bank has not yet credited, so the **Pass Book balance is lower**. They push the balances in **opposite** directions.

**A8. Why does a dishonoured cheque need careful sign treatment?**
It depends on *which* cheque bounced. A **deposited** cheque that is dishonoured makes the **Pass Book lower** (the bank reverses a credit it had given). An **issued** cheque that is dishonoured or stopped makes the **Pass Book higher** (the bank did not pay it after all). Always identify the direction before signing.

**A9. Is a BRS part of the double-entry system? Does it get posted to the ledger?**
No. A BRS is a **statement, not an account**. It posts nothing and forms no part of the double entry. Only the **Adjusted Cash Book** step alters the books; the BRS itself is purely a memorandum reconciliation.

**A10. How does an overdraft change the mechanics of a BRS?**
An overdraft is effectively a **negative balance**. The safest method: write the overdraft with a **minus sign**, apply the ordinary favourable-balance add/subtract rules, and read the sign of the result (negative = overdraft, positive = favourable). Because "more favourable" means a *smaller* overdraft, the add/subtract directions invert compared with a favourable balance.

**A11. State three practical benefits of preparing a BRS regularly.**
(i) It **detects errors and omissions** in the Cash Book early; (ii) it **reveals delays and possible frauds** — e.g., cheques deposited but never credited, or unauthorised withdrawals; (iii) it establishes the **true bank balance** to be reported, and discourages fraud by staff who know an independent check exists.

**A12. Where does the reconciliation feed into later CA studies?**
It underpins **AS 3 Cash Flow Statements** (closing cash must tie to a reconciled bank balance; on-demand overdrafts are cash equivalents) and the **Audit of Cash & Bank** in Auditing & Ethics (the bank reconciliation is a core substantive procedure, tested against an independent bank confirmation).

**A13. If the bank wrongly debits your account for another customer's cheque, where is it adjusted?**
It is the **bank's error**, so it is shown **only in the BRS** as a reconciling item and is **never** entered in your Cash Book. You correct your own book only for your own omissions and errors.

**A14. Why can two businesses with identical Cash Book balances report different "true" bank balances?**
Because the *uncorrected* Cash Book balance ignores items only the bank knows (charges, interest, direct credits, standing orders). The **true balance** emerges only after the Adjusted Cash Book incorporates those items — and different firms will have different such items — so the reconciled Balance-Sheet figure differs even from an identical starting point.

## Section B — Numerical Problems (fully solved, self-verified)

### B1. Cash Book balance → Pass Book balance (favourable)

**Problem.** On 31 March 20X1 the Cash Book of A Ltd. shows a **debit balance of ₹1,20,000**. (1) Cheques issued but not presented ₹15,000; (2) cheques deposited but not cleared ₹22,000; (3) bank charges ₹500; (4) interest credited by bank ₹800; (5) dividend collected by bank ₹6,000; (6) LIC premium paid by bank on standing order ₹3,500. Find the balance as per Pass Book.

**Solution.**

| Bank Reconciliation Statement as on 31 March 20X1 | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balance as per Cash Book (Dr.) | | 1,20,000 |
| **Add:** Cheques issued not presented | 15,000 | |
| **Add:** Interest credited by bank | 800 | |
| **Add:** Dividend collected by bank | 6,000 | 21,800 |
| | | 1,41,800 |
| **Less:** Cheques deposited not cleared | 22,000 | |
| **Less:** Bank charges | 500 | |
| **Less:** LIC premium paid by bank | 3,500 | (26,000) |
| **Balance as per Pass Book (Cr.)** | | **1,15,800** |

**Check.** 1,20,000 + 21,800 − 26,000 = **₹1,15,800.** ✓

### B2. Pass Book balance → Cash Book balance (favourable)

**Problem.** The bank statement of B & Co. shows a **credit balance of ₹80,000** on 31 March 20X1. (1) Cheques issued not presented ₹18,000; (2) cheques deposited not credited ₹11,000; (3) bank charges ₹250; (4) interest on deposits credited by bank ₹1,400; (5) a customer directly deposited ₹5,000. Ascertain the Cash Book balance.

**Solution.**

| Bank Reconciliation Statement as on 31 March 20X1 | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balance as per Pass Book (Cr.) | | 80,000 |
| **Add:** Cheques deposited not credited | 11,000 | |
| **Add:** Bank charges | 250 | 11,250 |
| | | 91,250 |
| **Less:** Cheques issued not presented | 18,000 | |
| **Less:** Interest on deposits credited | 1,400 | |
| **Less:** Customer's direct deposit | 5,000 | (24,400) |
| **Balance as per Cash Book (Dr.)** | | **66,850** |

**Check (reverse).** From Cash Book 66,850 → add unpresented 18,000, interest 1,400, deposit 5,000 (+24,400); less uncleared 11,000, charges 250 (−11,250): 66,850 + 24,400 − 11,250 = **₹80,000** = Pass Book. ✓

### B3. Overdraft as per Pass Book → overdraft as per Cash Book (BRS-only)

**Problem.** On 31 March 20X1 the Pass Book of C Enterprises shows an **overdraft (debit balance) of ₹25,000**. (1) Cheques issued not presented ₹9,000; (2) cheques deposited not cleared ₹4,500; (3) bank charges ₹350; (4) interest on overdraft ₹1,200; (5) bills collected by bank ₹7,000; (6) insurance paid by bank ₹2,000. Find the overdraft as per Cash Book.

**Reasoning.** Going from Pass Book overdraft to Cash Book overdraft: items that make the **Pass Book higher** (unpresented cheques, bills collected) are **added**; items that make it **lower** (uncleared deposits, charges, interest on O/D, insurance) are **subtracted**.

**Solution.**

| Bank Reconciliation Statement as on 31 March 20X1 | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Overdraft as per Pass Book (Dr.) | | 25,000 |
| **Add:** Cheques issued not presented | 9,000 | |
| **Add:** Bills collected by bank | 7,000 | 16,000 |
| | | 41,000 |
| **Less:** Cheques deposited not cleared | 4,500 | |
| **Less:** Bank charges | 350 | |
| **Less:** Interest on overdraft | 1,200 | |
| **Less:** Insurance paid by bank | 2,000 | (8,050) |
| **Overdraft as per Cash Book (Cr.)** | | **32,950** |

**Check (negative-number method).** Pass Book = −25,000. Cash = Pass − (items making pass higher) + (items making pass lower) = −25,000 − 16,000 + 8,050 = **−32,950**, i.e. a ₹32,950 overdraft as per Cash Book. ✓

### B4. Adjusted Cash Book + BRS (favourable)

**Problem.** On 31 March 20X1 the Cash Book of D Traders shows a **debit balance of ₹65,000**. (1) Bank charges ₹700; (2) interest on securities collected by bank ₹5,000; (3) insurance premium paid by bank ₹3,000; (4) a customer directly deposited ₹8,000; (5) a cheque of ₹2,500 earlier deposited was dishonoured; (6) cheques issued not presented ₹10,000; (7) cheques deposited not cleared ₹6,000. Prepare the Adjusted Cash Book and the BRS.

**Step 1 — Adjusted Cash Book (bank column).** Items 1–5 are business omissions → enter here; items 6–7 are timing → leave for BRS.

| Dr. | ₹ | Cr. | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Balance b/d | 65,000 | By Bank charges | 700 |
| To Interest on securities | 5,000 | By Insurance premium | 3,000 |
| To Customer (direct deposit) | 8,000 | By Cheque dishonoured | 2,500 |
| | | By Balance c/d | 71,800 |
| **Total** | **78,000** | **Total** | **78,000** |

True adjusted Cash Book balance = **₹71,800 (Dr.)**.

**Step 2 — BRS.**

| Bank Reconciliation Statement as on 31 March 20X1 | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balance as per Adjusted Cash Book (Dr.) | | 71,800 |
| **Add:** Cheques issued not presented | | 10,000 |
| | | 81,800 |
| **Less:** Cheques deposited not cleared | | (6,000) |
| **Balance as per Pass Book (Cr.)** | | **75,800** |

**Check.** 71,800 + 10,000 − 6,000 = **₹75,800.** ✓

### B5. BRS with errors in the Cash Book

**Problem.** On 31 March 20X1 the Cash Book of E Ltd. shows a **debit balance of ₹90,000**. On scrutiny: (1) a cheque of ₹4,600 issued to a supplier was recorded in the Cash Book (payments) as ₹6,400; (2) cheques deposited but not credited ₹12,000; (3) cheques issued but not presented ₹8,500; (4) bank charges ₹400 not recorded; (5) the receipts side of the bank column was **overcast by ₹1,000**. Compute the balance as per Pass Book (BRS-only).

**Reasoning on the errors.**
- Item 1: payment overstated by ₹6,400 − ₹4,600 = ₹1,800, so the Cash Book balance is **understated by ₹1,800** → **ADD 1,800** to reach the (correct) Pass Book.
- Item 5: receipts overcast by ₹1,000 means the Cash Book balance is **overstated by ₹1,000** → **LESS 1,000**.

**Solution.**

| Bank Reconciliation Statement as on 31 March 20X1 | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balance as per Cash Book (Dr.) | | 90,000 |
| **Add:** Cheque issued recorded ₹6,400 instead of ₹4,600 | 1,800 | |
| **Add:** Cheques issued not presented | 8,500 | 10,300 |
| | | 1,00,300 |
| **Less:** Cheques deposited not credited | 12,000 | |
| **Less:** Bank charges | 400 | |
| **Less:** Receipts side overcast | 1,000 | (13,400) |
| **Balance as per Pass Book (Cr.)** | | **86,900** |

**Check.** 90,000 + 10,300 − 13,400 = **₹86,900.** ✓

### B6. Overdraft as per Cash Book → Pass Book, with Adjusted Cash Book

**Problem.** On 31 March 20X1 the Cash Book of F & Sons shows an **overdraft of ₹30,000**. (1) Cheques issued not presented ₹12,000; (2) cheques deposited not cleared ₹7,000; (3) bank charges ₹600; (4) interest on overdraft ₹1,500; (5) customer's direct deposit ₹9,000; (6) bills collected by bank ₹4,000. Prepare the Adjusted Cash Book and BRS to find the Pass Book overdraft.

**Step 1 — Adjusted Cash Book (bank column).** Overdraft = opening credit balance ("By Balance b/d"). Enter items 3–6; leave 1–2 for the BRS.

| Dr. | ₹ | Cr. | ₹ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| To Customer (direct deposit) | 9,000 | By Balance b/d (overdraft) | 30,000 |
| To Bills Receivable (collected) | 4,000 | By Bank charges | 600 |
| To Balance c/d (overdraft) | 19,100 | By Interest on overdraft | 1,500 |
| **Total** | **32,100** | **Total** | **32,100** |

Adjusted overdraft = **₹19,100 (Cr.)**.

**Step 2 — BRS.**

| Bank Reconciliation Statement as on 31 March 20X1 | ₹ | ₹ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Overdraft as per Adjusted Cash Book (Cr.) | | 19,100 |
| **Less:** Cheques issued not presented | | (12,000) |
| | | 7,100 |
| **Add:** Cheques deposited not cleared | | 7,000 |
| **Overdraft as per Pass Book (Dr.)** | | **14,100** |

**Check.** 19,100 − 12,000 + 7,000 = **₹14,100 overdraft.** Cross-check by BRS-only from original overdraft: −30,000 + 12,000 − 7,000 − 600 − 1,500 + 9,000 + 4,000 = **−14,100**. Both agree. ✓
