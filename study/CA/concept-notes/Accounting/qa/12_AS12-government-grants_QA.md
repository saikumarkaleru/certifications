# Q&A — AS 12 — Accounting for Government Grants

> Companion question bank to the concept chapter. Every question is followed immediately by its complete model answer. All figures in Rupees (Rs.), ICAI conventions.

---

## Section A — Concept-check Questions (test the WHY)

**A1. What are "government grants" as defined in AS 12, and what is specifically excluded from its scope?**

**Answer.** Government grants are assistance by government in cash or kind to an enterprise for past or future compliance with certain conditions. "Government" includes government, government agencies and similar bodies (local, national or international). AS 12 **excludes**: (i) government assistance other than grants (e.g., free technical/marketing advice, guarantees) that cannot reasonably have a value placed on it; (ii) government participation in ownership of the enterprise; and (iii) grants covered under AS 22 (i.e., tax-related benefits such as tax holidays, investment allowances, concessional tax rates). Grants also exclude transactions with government that are indistinguishable from normal trading.

**A2. Explain the two broad approaches to accounting for grants. Which does AS 12 predominantly follow and why?**

**Answer.** The two approaches are: (a) **Capital approach** — the grant is treated as part of shareholders'/owners' funds (credited to capital reserve), on the logic that it is a financing incentive with no repayment expected and is not earned; and (b) **Income approach** — the grant is taken to the profit and loss statement over one or more periods, on the logic that it is a receipt from a source other than shareholders, is earned by complying with conditions/incurring obligations, and should be matched with the related costs. AS 12 predominantly follows the **income approach with a matching basis**: grants are recognised in P&L over the periods necessary to match them with the related costs they are intended to compensate. The capital approach is used only in limited cases (promoters' contribution nature).

**A3. Why is a government grant NOT recognised the moment it is sanctioned or received?**

**Answer.** Because recognition requires **reasonable assurance** of two things: (i) that the enterprise will **comply with the conditions** attached to the grant, and (ii) that the grant **will be received**. Mere receipt is not conclusive proof that conditions have been or will be met. Recognising on a prudent, matching basis prevents crediting income before the related obligation/cost is incurred.

**A4. Distinguish the accounting treatment of (i) a grant that is in the nature of promoters' contribution, and (ii) a grant received as compensation for expenses/losses already incurred.**

**Answer.** (i) A grant given as **total investment / promoters' contribution** (no repayment expected, given as part of the financing package with reference to total investment or by way of percentage of capital) is credited directly to **Capital Reserve** and treated as part of shareholders' funds — it is NOT routed through P&L. (ii) A grant received as **compensation for expenses or losses already incurred** in a previous accounting period, or for giving immediate financial support with no future related costs, is recognised in the **profit and loss statement of the period in which it becomes receivable**, as an extraordinary item if appropriate, with disclosure.

**A5. Can a grant related to a depreciable fixed asset ever be credited straight to capital reserve? Explain the treatment of grants for non-depreciable assets.**

**Answer.** A grant related to a **depreciable** fixed asset is NOT credited to capital reserve; it is either deducted from the asset's book value or treated as deferred income and recognised in P&L over the asset's useful life (matching depreciation). For a **non-depreciable** asset: if it carries **no conditions**, the grant is credited to **capital reserve** (since there is no charge to P&L to match against). If it requires the fulfilment of **obligations** (e.g., land granted on condition a building is erected), the grant is credited to income **over the same period over which the cost of meeting the obligation is charged** to P&L.

## Section B — Graded Computational Problems (with full solutions)

### B1 (Easy) — Revenue grant

An enterprise receives Rs. 5,00,000 from the State Government as reimbursement of training expenditure of Rs. 8,00,000 incurred and charged during the year. Show the treatment.

**Solution.** This is a **revenue grant** related to an expense of the current period. Under AS 12 it may be (a) credited to P&L as a separate income line, or (b) deducted from the related expense. Either is acceptable if disclosed.

Method (b) — net presentation:
```
Training expense (Rs. 8,00,000 − Rs. 5,00,000)  ...  Rs. 3,00,000 (net charge to P&L)
```
Journal (Method a, gross):
```
Bank A/c                          Dr. 5,00,000
   To Government Grant A/c                 5,00,000
(Grant received)
Government Grant A/c              Dr. 5,00,000
   To Profit & Loss A/c                    5,00,000
(Grant recognised as income)
```
Net effect on profit under both methods is identical: expense reduced by Rs. 5,00,000.

### B2 (Easy–Moderate) — Grant related to a depreciable asset (both methods)

On 1 April 2025 a company buys a machine for **Rs. 1,00,00,000** and receives a government grant of **Rs. 20,00,000** towards it. Useful life 10 years, SLM, nil residual value. Show both permitted methods for Year 1 and prove they give the same profit impact.

**Solution.**

**Method 1 — Deduction from cost of asset.**
- Net book value = 1,00,00,000 − 20,00,000 = **Rs. 80,00,000**
- Annual depreciation = 80,00,000 / 10 = **Rs. 8,00,000**
- Net charge to P&L (Year 1) = **Rs. 8,00,000**

```
Machine A/c            Dr. 1,00,00,000
   To Bank A/c                     1,00,00,000
Bank A/c               Dr.   20,00,000
   To Machine A/c                    20,00,000  (grant deducted)
Depreciation A/c       Dr.    8,00,000
   To Machine A/c                     8,00,000
```

**Method 2 — Deferred income.**
- Asset carried at full cost 1,00,00,000; annual depreciation = 1,00,00,000 / 10 = **Rs. 10,00,000**
- Grant Rs. 20,00,000 kept as deferred income, released over 10 years = **Rs. 2,00,000 per year**
- Net charge to P&L (Year 1) = Depreciation 10,00,000 − Grant income 2,00,000 = **Rs. 8,00,000**

```
Bank A/c                 Dr. 20,00,000
   To Deferred Govt Grant A/c        20,00,000
Depreciation A/c         Dr. 10,00,000
   To Machine A/c                    10,00,000
Deferred Govt Grant A/c  Dr.  2,00,000
   To Profit & Loss A/c               2,00,000
```
**Reconciliation:** Both methods hit P&L by Rs. 8,00,000 in Year 1 (and every year). Method 2 shows Deferred Grant balance of Rs. 18,00,000 (Rs. 20,00,000 − 2,00,000) in the Balance Sheet under a separate head after "Reserves & Surplus" / non-current liabilities.

### B3 (Moderate) — Refund of a grant on a depreciable asset

Using B2 data (Method 1, deduction), suppose the company **breaches a condition at the end of Year 3** and must **refund the full Rs. 20,00,000** grant. Show the accounting and revised depreciation.

**Solution.**
- Depreciation charged Years 1–3 (Method 1) = 3 × 8,00,000 = 24,00,000
- Book value end of Year 3 = 80,00,000 − 24,00,000 = **Rs. 56,00,000**
- On refund, the amount refunded is **added back to the book value** of the asset:
  Revised book value = 56,00,000 + 20,00,000 = **Rs. 76,00,000**
- Remaining useful life = 10 − 3 = 7 years
- Revised annual depreciation = 76,00,000 / 7 = **Rs. 10,85,714** (prospective; no restatement of prior years)

```
Machine A/c            Dr. 20,00,000
   To Bank A/c                     20,00,000
(Refund of grant added to carrying amount of machine)
```
**Note:** AS 12 requires that the refund be treated so that additional depreciation (the depreciation that would have been charged had there been no grant) is recognised prospectively. The Rs. 76,00,000 written off over 7 years achieves this.

### B4 (Moderate) — Refund under the deferred-income method

Same as B3, but the company had used **Method 2 (deferred income)**. Grant Rs. 20,00,000, released Rs. 2,00,000 p.a. Refund of full Rs. 20,00,000 at end of Year 3.

**Solution.**
- Grant released to P&L in Years 1–3 = 3 × 2,00,000 = 6,00,000
- Unamortised **Deferred Grant balance** at end of Year 3 = 20,00,000 − 6,00,000 = **Rs. 14,00,000**
- On refund of Rs. 20,00,000: first adjust against the deferred balance Rs. 14,00,000; the excess Rs. 6,00,000 is **charged immediately to P&L**.

```
Deferred Govt Grant A/c   Dr. 14,00,000
Profit & Loss A/c         Dr.  6,00,000
   To Bank A/c                        20,00,000
```
The asset's carrying amount and future depreciation (Rs. 10,00,000 p.a. on the full cost) are unaffected. **Cross-check with B3:** Total P&L pain of the refund is the same Rs. 6,00,000 hit now plus the loss of future grant credits — consistent with Method 1, where future depreciation rises from Rs. 8,00,000 to Rs. 10,85,714 (an extra Rs. 2,85,714 × 7 = Rs. 20,00,000 over the remaining life, versus foregone grant credits of Rs. 14,00,000 plus the Rs. 6,00,000 immediate charge = Rs. 20,00,000). Both routes ultimately expense the full grant.

### B5 (Exam-hard) — Mixed grant, non-monetary asset, and promoters' contribution

Sunrise Ltd. received the following during FY 2025-26:
1. A plot of land from the State Government **free of cost**, fair value Rs. 40,00,000, on condition it constructs a factory (construction spread over the useful benefit period; treat as non-depreciable land with an obligation).
2. A grant of Rs. 30,00,000 described in the sanction letter as **promoters' contribution** (a percentage of total investment, no repayment expected).
3. A plant costing Rs. 50,00,000 acquired at a **concessional price** of Rs. 35,00,000 under a government scheme (grant element embedded). Useful life 10 years, SLM, nil residual. Company uses the **deduction method** for asset grants.

Show the treatment of each item with amounts and Year-1 P&L / Balance Sheet effects.

**Solution.**

**Item 1 — Free land with obligation.** A non-monetary asset acquired free is recorded at a **nominal value** (AS 12). The obligation-linked grant element is credited to income over the period the cost of meeting the obligation is charged. Land recorded at nominal value (say Rs. 1). No depreciation on land. There is no automatic capital-reserve credit because an obligation (build the factory) attaches; the benefit is matched to obligation costs over their charge period.

**Item 2 — Promoters' contribution.** Credited directly to **Capital Reserve** Rs. 30,00,000. Not routed through P&L; treated as part of shareholders' funds; no repayment expected.
```
Bank A/c              Dr. 30,00,000
   To Capital Reserve A/c        30,00,000
```

**Item 3 — Concessional non-monetary acquisition (deduction method).** The asset is recorded at its **acquisition cost** to the enterprise, i.e., **Rs. 35,00,000** (the concession is captured by the lower cost, consistent with the deduction approach).
- Annual depreciation = 35,00,000 / 10 = **Rs. 3,50,000**
```
Plant A/c             Dr. 35,00,000
   To Bank A/c                   35,00,000
Depreciation A/c      Dr.  3,50,000
   To Plant A/c                   3,50,000
```

**Year-1 summary.**
| Item | Balance Sheet | P&L (Year 1) |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| 1. Free land | Land at nominal Rs. 1 | Nil (matched to obligation costs when incurred) |
| 2. Promoters' contribution | Capital Reserve Rs. 30,00,000 | Nil |
| 3. Concessional plant | Plant Rs. 35,00,000 (net of dep Rs. 31,50,000) | Depreciation Rs. 3,50,000 |

## Section C — Past-paper-style Full Questions (ICAI pattern)

**C1.** *A company received a government grant of Rs. 50,00,000 in 2024-25 for setting up a plant costing Rs. 2,00,00,000 (useful life 8 years, SLM, nil residual). It credited the entire grant to Profit & Loss in 2024-25. Comment on the correctness and show the correct treatment under both permitted methods for 2024-25.*

**Answer.** The treatment is **incorrect**. AS 12 prohibits recognising a grant related to a depreciable fixed asset entirely as income in the year of receipt; it must be matched to the asset over its useful life. Correct treatment:

*Method 1 (deduction):* Asset = 2,00,00,000 − 50,00,000 = 1,50,00,000; depreciation = 1,50,00,000 / 8 = **Rs. 18,75,000** p.a.

*Method 2 (deferred income):* Depreciation = 2,00,00,000 / 8 = 25,00,000; grant released = 50,00,000 / 8 = 6,25,000; net P&L charge = **Rs. 18,75,000** p.a. Deferred grant balance carried = Rs. 43,75,000.

Crediting Rs. 50,00,000 to P&L overstated 2024-25 profit by Rs. 43,75,000 (= 50,00,000 grant credited wrongly less Rs. 6,25,000 correctly attributable). The error must be rectified.

**C2.** *X Ltd. received a grant of Rs. 15,00,000 in 2023 to compensate it for losses incurred in an earlier flood-affected year. Additionally it received Rs. 10,00,000 to give immediate financial support with no related future costs. How should these be accounted?*

**Answer.** Both are grants that do **not relate to future costs**. Under AS 12, a grant receivable as **compensation for expenses or losses already incurred** in a previous period, or to give **immediate financial support** with no future related costs, is recognised in the **profit and loss statement of the period in which it becomes receivable**. Therefore both Rs. 15,00,000 and Rs. 10,00,000 are credited to P&L of 2023, disclosed separately (as an extraordinary item where the circumstances warrant, per AS 5), so that users understand the effect on results.

**C3.** *A grant of Rs. 25,00,000 related to a specific machinery was earlier presented as deferred income (balance Rs. 20,00,000 unamortised). The condition is breached and Rs. 25,00,000 becomes refundable. Machinery original cost Rs. 1,00,00,000, WDV now Rs. 80,00,000 (grant was NOT deducted from asset). Show the refund entry and explain the principle.*

**Answer.** Since the deferred-income method was used, the refund is **first set off against the unamortised deferred grant balance**, and any excess is charged to P&L.
- Unamortised deferred grant = Rs. 20,00,000
- Refund = Rs. 25,00,000 → excess = Rs. 5,00,000 to P&L
```
Deferred Govt Grant A/c   Dr. 20,00,000
Profit & Loss A/c         Dr.  5,00,000
   To Bank A/c                        25,00,000
```
The machinery's WDV (Rs. 80,00,000) and future depreciation are unaffected. **Principle:** a grant becoming refundable is treated as a **change in estimate** (AS 5) and accounted **prospectively**, not by restating earlier years.

## Section D — MCQs / Case Scenarios

**D1.** Government grants should be recognised only when there is reasonable assurance that (i) the enterprise will comply with the conditions, and (ii) the grant will be received.
a) Only (i) b) Only (ii) c) Both (i) and (ii) d) Neither
**Answer: (c)** — AS 12 requires reasonable assurance of both compliance and collection.

**D2.** A grant in the nature of promoters' contribution is credited to:
a) Profit & Loss b) Capital Reserve c) Deferred income d) The asset account
**Answer: (b)** — It forms part of shareholders' funds; no repayment expected, not earned through P&L.

**D3.** A non-monetary asset given by the government free of cost is recorded at:
a) Fair value b) Cost to government c) Nominal value d) Not recorded
**Answer: (c)** — AS 12 records free non-monetary assets at a nominal value.

**D4.** For a grant related to a depreciable asset presented under the deferred-income method, refund is first adjusted against:
a) The asset's book value b) Capital reserve c) Unamortised deferred grant balance, excess to P&L d) Directly to P&L in full
**Answer: (c)** — Set off against deferred credit; balance to P&L.

**D5.** A grant for a non-depreciable asset carrying **no future obligation** is credited to:
a) P&L over 10 years b) Capital reserve c) Deferred income d) The asset, reducing it to nil
**Answer: (b)** — With no matching charge, it goes to capital reserve.

**D6.** Which is OUTSIDE the scope of AS 12?
a) Cash subsidy for a plant b) Tax holiday under income-tax law c) Grant reimbursing training cost d) Land at concessional rate
**Answer: (b)** — Tax-related benefits are excluded (covered by AS 22/tax standards).

**D7.** *Case:* A company deducted a Rs. 12,00,000 grant from a Rs. 60,00,000 machine (life 6 yrs, SLM). Depreciation per year is:
a) Rs. 10,00,000 b) Rs. 8,00,000 c) Rs. 2,00,000 d) Rs. 12,00,000
**Answer: (b)** — (60,00,000 − 12,00,000)/6 = Rs. 8,00,000.

**D8.** *Case:* Grant received as compensation for a loss of an earlier period is recognised:
a) Over the asset's life b) In P&L when it becomes receivable c) In capital reserve d) As deferred income
**Answer: (b)** — No future costs to match; taken to P&L when receivable.

**D9.** Under the income approach, a revenue grant is recognised in P&L:
a) Immediately in full always b) Over the periods matching the related costs it compensates c) Only on cash receipt d) In capital reserve
**Answer: (b)** — Matching basis is the core of the income approach.

**D10.** A grant becomes refundable. This is treated as:
a) Prior-period item b) Change in accounting policy c) Change in accounting estimate — prospective d) Extraordinary item always
**Answer: (c)** — Refunds are accounted prospectively as a revision of estimate.
