## Section A — Theory

### Q1. Define a bill of exchange and state its essential characteristics. (5 marks)

**Model answer.**
Under **Section 5 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881**, a bill of exchange is *"an instrument in writing containing an unconditional order, signed by the maker, directing a certain person to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person, or to the bearer of the instrument."*

Essential characteristics (state each as a distinct point — examiners award a mark per feature):
1. It must be **in writing**.
2. It must contain an **order** to pay (not a request or a promise).
3. The order must be **unconditional**.
4. It must be **signed by the drawer**.
5. The **sum payable must be certain**, and payable in **money only**.
6. The **parties (drawer, drawee, payee) must be certain**.
7. It must comply with **stamp requirements** and be **accepted by the drawee** to bind him.

*Presentation tip: quote the section number, then list the features as numbered points — a bare definition without the characteristics loses half the marks.*

### Q2. Distinguish between a bill of exchange and a promissory note. (6 marks)

**Model answer.** Present as a table — fastest route to full marks.

| Basis | Bill of Exchange (Sec 5) | Promissory Note (Sec 4) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | An **order** to pay | A **promise** to pay |
| Drawn by | The **creditor** (drawer) | The **debtor** (maker) |
| Parties | **Three** — drawer, drawee, payee | **Two** — maker, payee |
| Acceptance | **Required** from the drawee | **Not required** |
| Primary liability | On the **acceptor** | On the **maker** |
| Maker & payee same? | Drawer & payee **may** be same | Maker & payee **cannot** be same |
| Notice of dishonour | Necessary to the drawer | Not necessary to the maker |

*Key sentence to include: "In a bill the creditor commands the debtor to pay, whereas in a promissory note the debtor himself promises to pay — which is why a note needs no acceptance."*

### Q3. Explain "days of grace" and the rule for fixing the due date of a bill. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
**Days of grace** are **three additional days** allowed by law beyond the nominal period of a time bill, as a customary concession to the acceptor. The **due date (date of maturity)** is therefore the date on which the stated period expires **plus three days**.

Rules:
- **Months bills** — count in calendar months to the corresponding date of the terminal month; if that date does not exist, take the **last day of the month**; then add 3 days.
- **Days bills** — count the exact number of days, **excluding** the date of drawing and **including** the date of payment; then add 3 days.
- **At-sight / on-demand** bills carry **no** days of grace.
- **Section 25:** if the due date is a **known public holiday**, the bill matures on the **preceding business day**; if it is a **sudden emergency holiday**, on the **next** business day.

### Q4. What is meant by "discounting a bill"? How is the discount treated in the drawer's books? (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
Discounting is the sale of a bill by its holder to a bank **before maturity**, in exchange for immediate cash equal to the face value **less the bank's discount**. The **discount** is interest for the unexpired period, computed as *Face × Rate × (unexpired months ÷ 12)*.

In the drawer's books:
- **Bank A/c** is debited with the **net proceeds**;
- **Discount A/c** is debited with the discount (a **finance expense** charged to the P&L);
- **Bills Receivable A/c** is credited with the **full face value**.

A discounted bill remains a **contingent liability** of the drawer until it matures, because he must make good if the acceptor dishonours it. This is disclosed, not journalised, unless dishonour actually occurs.

### Q5. Distinguish between retirement and renewal of a bill. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**

| Basis | Retirement | Renewal |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Acceptor pays **before** the due date | Old bill cancelled and a **fresh bill** drawn for more time |
| Time-value adjustment | Holder allows a **rebate** (discount) | Drawer charges **interest** |
| Effect on holder | Rebate is a **loss/expense** | Interest is an **income** |
| Effect on acceptor | Rebate is a **gain/income** | Interest is an **expense** |
| Trigger | Acceptor has surplus funds early | Acceptor cannot pay on time |

*Key line: "Retirement rewards early payment with a rebate; renewal compensates the creditor for extra time with interest — mirror applications of the time value of money."*

### Q6. What are noting charges? Who ultimately bears them and why? (3 marks)

**Model answer.**
When a bill is **dishonoured**, the holder gets the fact **officially recorded ("noted")** by a **Notary Public**, who certifies that the bill was duly presented and payment was refused. The small fee charged for this is called **noting charges**. Noting provides **independent legal evidence** of dishonour (the holder's own word is not proof).

The charges are ultimately **borne by the party responsible for the dishonour — the acceptor**, because it is his default that made the noting necessary. Hence, in every dishonour entry, the acceptor's account is debited for the **face value plus noting charges**.

### Q7. What is an accommodation bill and how does it differ from a trade bill? (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
A **trade bill** arises from a **genuine sale/purchase of goods or services** — it settles a real trade debt. An **accommodation bill** has **no underlying trade transaction**; it is drawn and accepted purely so that one or both parties can **raise short-term finance** by discounting it. The acceptor lends only his name/credit, "accommodating" the drawer.

Key differences:
- Trade bill = **consideration exists** (goods moved); accommodation bill = **no consideration**.
- Trade bill entries begin with **Sales/Purchases**; accommodation bill has **no Sales/Purchases** entry.
- In a **mutual** accommodation bill, the **net proceeds and the discount are shared in an agreed ratio**, and each party contributes his share of the face value at maturity.

### Q8. On dishonour of a discounted bill, why must the drawer (not the acceptor) first settle with the bank? (3 marks)

**Model answer.**
When a bill is discounted, the drawer effectively **guarantees** it to the bank by his endorsement. The bank's recourse on dishonour is against **the person who transferred the bill to it — the drawer** — not the acceptor, with whom the bank has no direct relationship. So the drawer must **repay the bank the face value plus any noting charges the bank paid**, and only then does he **revive the acceptor's personal account** to recover the amount from the defaulting acceptor. Hence the drawer's entry: *Acceptor's A/c Dr (face + noting) / To Bank A/c*.

### Q9. State any four parties to a bill of exchange other than drawer, drawee and payee. (4 marks)

**Model answer.**
1. **Acceptor** — the drawee after he signs his acceptance; he becomes primarily liable.
2. **Endorser** — a holder who signs the back of the bill to transfer it.
3. **Endorsee** — the person to whom the bill is transferred by endorsement.
4. **Holder in due course** — one who obtains the bill for value, in good faith, before maturity, without notice of any defect in title; he enjoys a protected title.
   *(Also acceptable: drawee in case of need; acceptor for honour.)*

### Q10. Why is a discounted bill shown as a contingent liability? (3 marks)

**Model answer.**
On discounting, the drawer removes the Bills Receivable from his books and receives cash, so the asset has left his balance sheet. **But his liability has not fully ended**: if the acceptor dishonours the bill at maturity, the bank will recover from the drawer. This liability is **contingent** — it will crystallise **only if** a future uncertain event (dishonour) occurs. Because it is neither certain nor measurable as a present obligation, it is **disclosed by way of a note** rather than journalised, until dishonour actually happens.

## Section B — Numerical Problems

### Q11. Compute the due dates. (4 marks)

Find the due date of each bill:
(a) A bill dated 5 March 2026, payable 3 months after date.
(b) A bill dated 30 November 2026, payable 2 months after date.
(c) A bill dated 18 May 2026, payable 90 days after date.
(d) A bill at sight dated 12 June 2026.

**Solution.**
(a) 5 March + 3 months = 5 June; + 3 grace = **8 June 2026**.
(b) 30 November + 2 months = 30 January 2027 (Jan has a 30th); + 3 = **2 February 2027**.
(c) 90 days from 18 May, excluding 18 May: May (19→31) = 13 days; June = 30 (running 43); July = 31 (running 74); need 16 more into August → 16 August; + 3 grace = **19 August 2026**.
(d) At-sight bill → **no grace days** → payable on presentation, **12 June 2026**.

### Q12. Basic drawing, acceptance and honour — both books. (6 marks)

On 1 January 2026, Amit sold goods worth Rs 30,000 to Bimal and drew a 3-month bill, which Bimal accepted. Amit retained the bill till maturity, and it was honoured. Pass journal entries in both books.

**Solution.** Due date: 1 Jan + 3 months = 1 April + 3 = **4 April 2026**.

**Books of Amit (Drawer):**

| Date | Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan | Bimal's A/c … Dr / To Sales A/c | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 1 Jan | Bills Receivable A/c … Dr / To Bimal's A/c | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 4 Apr | Cash/Bank A/c … Dr / To Bills Receivable A/c | 30,000 | 30,000 |

**Books of Bimal (Drawee):**

| Date | Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan | Purchases A/c … Dr / To Amit's A/c | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 1 Jan | Amit's A/c … Dr / To Bills Payable A/c | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 4 Apr | Bills Payable A/c … Dr / To Cash/Bank A/c | 30,000 | 30,000 |

*Check:* Each personal account opens and closes to nil; both maturity entries balance at Rs 30,000.

### Q13. Discounting of a bill. (5 marks)

On 1 February 2026, Ravi draws a 4-month bill on Sunil for Rs 60,000, accepted the same day. On 4 March 2026, Ravi discounts it with his bank at 15% p.a. The bill is honoured at maturity. Pass entries in Ravi's books.

**Solution.**
Due date: 1 Feb + 4 months = 1 June + 3 = **4 June 2026**.
Unexpired period from 4 March to 4 June = **3 months**.
Discount = 60,000 × 15% × 3/12 = **Rs 2,250**. Net proceeds = 60,000 − 2,250 = **Rs 57,750**.

**Books of Ravi:**

| Date | Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Feb | Sunil's A/c … Dr / To Sales A/c | 60,000 | 60,000 |
| 1 Feb | Bills Receivable A/c … Dr / To Sunil's A/c | 60,000 | 60,000 |
| 4 Mar | Bank A/c … Dr 57,750; Discount A/c … Dr 2,250 / To Bills Receivable A/c | 60,000 | 60,000 |
| 4 Jun | *No entry — bank collects from Sunil* | — | — |

*Check:* Discounting entry debits 57,750 + 2,250 = 60,000 = credit. Balances.

### Q15. Retirement under rebate — both books. (6 marks)

|---|---|---|
| Bills Payable A/c … Dr 48,000 / To Cash/Bank A/c 47,520; To Rebate on Bills A/c 480 | 48,000 | 48,000 |

*Check:* Latha's debits 47,520 + 480 = 48,000; Meena's credits 47,520 + 480 = 48,000. Both balance; rebate is Latha's loss and Meena's gain.

### Q17. Dishonour of a discounted bill. (5 marks)

Farhan drew a Rs 55,000 bill on Girish, discounted it with the bank, and on maturity the bill was dishonoured; the bank paid Rs 250 noting charges and recovered the total from Farhan. Pass entries in Farhan's books on dishonour.

**Solution.** Total the bank recovers from Farhan = 55,000 + 250 = **Rs 55,250**.

**Books of Farhan:**

| Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|
| Girish's A/c … Dr 55,250 / To Bank A/c 55,250 | 55,250 | 55,250 |

*Explanation for full marks:* Because Farhan discounted the bill, the **bank's recourse is against Farhan**; he repays the bank Rs 55,250 (face + noting) and simultaneously **revives Girish's account** to recover it from the defaulting acceptor. Girish, meanwhile, debits Bills Payable Rs 55,000 and Noting Charges Rs 250, crediting Farhan Rs 55,250.

### Q18. Renewal of a bill with interest — both books. (7 marks)

On 1 January 2026, Harish draws a 3-month bill on Ishan for Rs 36,000, accepted the same day. Before maturity Ishan asks for renewal. Harish agrees to draw a new 3-month bill including interest at 12% p.a. Pass entries in both books.

**Ishan's account in Harish's books (verification):** Dr 36,000 + 1,080 = 37,080; Cr 37,080 → **nil**. Correct.

**Books of Ishan (Drawee):**

| Particulars | Dr (Rs) | Cr (Rs) |
|---|---|---|

### Q21. Mutual accommodation bill with shared proceeds and discount. (8 marks)

On 1 July 2026, Om draws a 4-month bill on Pran for Rs 60,000 for their mutual accommodation, proceeds and discount to be shared Om : Pran = 2 : 1. Om discounts the bill at 12% p.a. on 1 July and remits Pran his share of the net proceeds. Before maturity Pran remits Om his share of the face value; Om meets the bill. Show the calculations and Pran's account in Om's books.

**Solution.**
Discount = 60,000 × 12% × 4/12 = **Rs 2,400**. Net proceeds = 60,000 − 2,400 = **Rs 57,600**.
Om's share of proceeds = 2/3 × 57,600 = **Rs 38,400** (kept); Pran's share = 1/3 × 57,600 = **Rs 19,200** (remitted to Pran).
Discount shares: Om 2/3 × 2,400 = Rs 1,600; Pran 1/3 × 2,400 = **Rs 800**.
Pran's share of face value = 1/3 × 60,000 = **Rs 20,000** (Pran remits this before maturity).

*Reconciliation:* Pran received Rs 19,200, pays Rs 20,000 → net cost Rs 800 = his discount share. Om kept Rs 38,400, receives Rs 20,000, pays bank Rs 60,000 → net cost Rs 1,600 = his discount share. Both tie.

**Pran's account in Om's books:**

| Dr | Rs | Cr | Rs |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Bank (proceeds remitted) | 19,200 | By Bills Receivable | 60,000 |
| To Discount (Pran's share) | 800 | By Bank (Pran's remittance) | 20,000 |
| To Bank (bill met, full face) | 60,000 | | |
| **Total** | **80,000** | **Total** | **80,000** |

*Check:* Account tallies at Rs 80,000, nil balance. Om's Discount A/c: debited 2,400, credited 800 (recovered from Pran) → net Rs 1,600 borne by Om. Correct.
