# Risk Assessment & Internal Control — Revision Note

## Snapshot
Auditor cannot check everything; misstatement is unevenly spread, sometimes hidden, and lives in estimates/disclosures/concealment, not just transactions. So audit is a sampling + judgement exercise: measure danger (risk assessment), lean on the entity's internal control, then target effort where danger is highest. Core standards: SA 315 (assess), SA 330 (respond), SA 265 (report control deficiencies).

## Core concepts

**Audit Risk model:** AR = IR × CR × DR (conceptual, NOT a calculator).
- Audit Risk (AR) = risk auditor expresses an inappropriate opinion (says statements fine when materially misstated). Kept low (conceptually ~5%); never zero → reasonable, not absolute, assurance.
- Inherent Risk (IR) = susceptibility to material misstatement before controls (raw danger).
- Control Risk (CR) = risk entity's controls fail to prevent/detect misstatement.
- Detection Risk (DR) = risk auditor's own procedures miss a misstatement. The ONLY component auditor controls directly (the lever).
- RMM = IR × CR = Risk of Material Misstatement (entity's; auditor can only assess, not change).
- DR = AR ÷ RMM → DR is INVERSE to RMM. High RMM → low DR → more/better/later work. Low RMM → less work.
- DR never zero: sampling risk + non-sampling risk always leave a floor → AR never zero.

**Two risk tiers:** Financial-statement level (pervasive, e.g. weak control environment, going concern, incentive to misstate → overall responses) vs Assertion level (specific transactions/balances/disclosures → specific procedures).

**Assertions targeted:** existence, completeness, valuation, rights & obligations, cut-off, classification, presentation & disclosure. Audit the assertion most at risk, not the balance by size.

**Five components of internal control (COSO/SA 315):**
1. Control Environment — tone at top; integrity, ethics, competence, governance. Foundation; weakness = FS-level risk.
2. Entity's Risk Assessment Process — how entity identifies/responds to its own business risks.
3. Information System & Communication — records that initiate, record, process, report transactions.
4. Control Activities — authorizations, reconciliations, segregation of duties (SoD), physical controls, IT application controls, performance reviews.
5. Monitoring of Controls — ongoing assessment of control effectiveness (incl. internal audit).

**SoD** splits three incompatible functions: custody, recording, authorization. One person holding two+ → SoD fails, CR rises. **Authorization**: general (standing policy, e.g. price list) vs specific (case-by-case sign-off).

**IT controls:** Application controls (inside a process — input validation, three-way match PO-GRN-invoice) rest on IT General Controls / ITGCs (access security, change management, IT operations). Reliance on an application control valid only if supporting ITGCs effective.

**Limitations of internal control (why CR ≠ 0, some substantive always needed):** management override (deadliest — sits above controls, selective), collusion, human error, cost-benefit limits, non-routine transactions, manual judgement lapses / IT program-change & access exposure.

## Key provisions / SAs

**SA 315 — Identifying & Assessing RMM through understanding entity & environment**
- SHALL perform risk assessment procedures to understand entity, environment, internal control → assess RMM at FS level & assertion level.
- Three risk assessment procedures: (1) Inquiry (weak alone), (2) Analytical procedures (flags anomalies), (3) Observation & inspection. Also draw on: client acceptance/continuance, prior-period experience (check for changes affecting current relevance), external info.
- TRAP: risk assessment procedures alone do NOT provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence for the opinion — they scope, not conclude.
- Mandatory engagement-team discussion on susceptibility to material misstatement, incl. fraud (overlaps SA 240). Must be documented.
- Understand: industry/regulatory/external factors (incl. reporting framework); nature of entity (ops, ownership, governance, related parties); accounting policies; objectives/strategies/business risks; measurement & review of performance; internal control.
- Evaluate control DESIGN & IMPLEMENTATION (D&I) for relevant controls — via walkthroughs (trace one transaction origin → FS). D&I confirms control exists and is used; it is NOT a test of operating effectiveness and NOT reliance. Inquiry alone never enough even for implementation.
- Inherent risk factors (revised SA 315, 2021, effective periods on/after 1 Apr 2023): complexity, subjectivity, change, uncertainty, susceptibility to management bias/fraud. Spectrum of inherent risk (likelihood × magnitude), not binary.
- Significant risks: require special audit consideration; = risk near upper end of inherent-risk spectrum. Consider whether involves fraud, recent economic/accounting developments, complexity, significant related-party transactions, high estimation/subjectivity, significant non-routine transactions. For significant risks, SHALL understand relevant controls; substantive procedures shall be specifically responsive. Revenue recognition = presumed fraud risk (SA 240) unless rebutted — must document reasons if rebutted.
- Significant risk judged on INHERENT risk (before controls); strong controls don't downgrade it, only affect response.
- Revise risk assessment when further-procedure evidence is inconsistent with it (assessment is ITERATIVE).

**SA 330 — Auditor's Responses to Assessed Risks**
- (A) Overall responses to FS-level risk: emphasize professional skepticism; assign more experienced staff / experts; more supervision; build in unpredictability; general changes to nature/timing/extent (shift to year-end). Weak control environment → more substantive, more at period-end, broader scope.
- (B) Further audit procedures at assertion level, two types: Tests of Controls (ToC) and Substantive Procedures (SP).
- Choose Nature / Timing / Extent (NTE) by risk:
  - Nature = kind/reliability of procedure (external confirmation > inquiry; inspection > observation). Higher risk → more reliable. MOST powerful lever, change nature first.
  - Timing = interim vs period-end. Higher risk → push to period-end.
  - Extent = sample size. Higher risk → larger.
- GOLDEN RULE: irrespective of assessed risk, perform substantive procedures for EACH material class of transactions, account balance, disclosure. Never rely on controls alone for material items.
- For significant risks, if approach is substantive-only, procedures SHALL include tests of details (not analytics alone).
- Must include: agreeing/reconciling FS to underlying records; examining material journal entries & other adjustments made in preparing statements.
- Evaluate sufficiency & appropriateness of evidence at end; if not enough, perform further procedures.

**Tests of Controls (ToC) — evidence a control worked**
- Purpose: support lower CR assessment → justify reliance → less substantive work.
- Mandatory when: (a) RMM assessment includes expectation controls operate effectively (auditor intends to rely); OR (b) substantive procedures alone cannot give sufficient appropriate evidence (common in highly automated/paperless, no audit trail).
- Test operating effectiveness (operated as designed, consistently, throughout period, by whom) — distinct from implementation.
- Methods ranked: re-performance (strongest) > inspection of documentary evidence > observation (only shows moment observed) > inquiry (never alone). Nature of control dictates nature of test; undocumented control can't be inspected.
- Timing/rotation: if tested at interim, get evidence for remaining period. Controls effective in prior audits may be tested less often but MUST be tested at least once every third audit. If control changed → test current period. Significant-risk controls → test EVERY (current) period, no prior-year reliance. More reliance planned → shorter acceptable gap.
- Deviations: investigate (isolated vs systemic; did it cause misstatement); consider compensating controls; if reliance undermined, increase substantive work. Deviation ≠ automatic misstatement, but always forces re-evaluation.

**Substantive Procedures (SP) — monetary accuracy**
- Tests of Details (ToD): vouching, tracing, external confirmation, physical verification, recalculation. Reliable, expensive.
- Substantive Analytical Procedures (SAP): plausible relationships (interest = balance × rate). Appropriate only where relationship plausible/predictable, data reliable, expectation precise enough. For a significant risk, SAP alone PROHIBITED — tests of details mandatory.
- Direction of testing: Vouching (records → source) tests occurrence/existence, catches OVERSTATEMENT. Tracing (source → records) tests completeness, catches UNDERSTATEMENT. Understated/unrecorded liabilities need a SEARCH FOR UNRECORDED LIABILITIES (trace post-year-end payments/GRN-not-invoiced), never vouching recorded payables.

**SA 265 — Communicating Deficiencies in Internal Control to TCWG & Management**
- Deficiency: control designed/implemented/operated so it cannot prevent-or-detect-and-correct misstatements timely; OR a necessary control is missing.
- Significant deficiency: deficiency (or combination) that, in professional judgement, merits attention of TCWG.
- Requirements: (1) SHALL determine whether deficiencies (individually/combined) are significant; (2) SHALL communicate significant deficiencies IN WRITING to TCWG, timely; (3) SHALL also communicate to management (written or oral): significant deficiencies + other deficiencies of sufficient importance meriting management attention.
- Written communication contents: description of deficiencies + explanation of potential effects; plus context — purpose of audit was to opine on FS; auditor considered internal control only to design procedures, NOT to opine on control effectiveness; matters limited to deficiencies auditor considered significant.
- Severity factors: susceptibility to loss/fraud; subjectivity of amounts; volume exposed; interaction with other deficiencies; importance of control to reporting; whether already caused a misstatement. Significant on POTENTIAL alone — no misstatement need have occurred. Listed indicators: ineffective control environment (e.g. senior-management fraud however small); absence of risk-assessment process where expected; restatement of prior FS.
- Deficiency (fault in control) ≠ misstatement (error in number); SA 265 = deficiencies, SA 450 = misstatements.

**Connections:** SA 200 (reasonable assurance made operational). SA 320 materiality (threshold; with risk sets extent). SA 240 fraud (revenue presumed fraud risk; management-override procedures mandatory regardless of control strength — journal-entry testing, estimate-bias review, unusual-transaction scrutiny). SA 500/530 (nature-timing-extent executed via reliability hierarchy & sampling). SA 520 (analytics: both risk-assessment and substantive tool). SA 402 (outsourced controls — understanding extends to service provider; use service-auditor report, often Type 2 for operating effectiveness, or test at service org). SA 450 (misstatements vs deficiencies). SA 260 (broader TCWG communication).
Companies Act: Sec 143(3)(i) — report on adequacy AND operating effectiveness of IFC (positive opinion, where applicable) — OPPOSITE scope to SA 265's no-opinion disclaimer. Sec 134(5)(e) — directors responsible for IFC. Sec 177 — audit committee (TCWG). CARO 2020 — internal-control comments. Sec 138 — internal audit for prescribed companies (feeds monitoring).

## Exam traps & must-remember
1. Risk assessment procedures do NOT give evidence for the opinion — they scope only.
2. Implementation (SA 315, exists/used, walkthrough) ≠ operating effectiveness (SA 330 ToC). Seeing a control exists is NOT reliance.
3. Strong controls never let you skip substantive work — SP mandatory for every material item.
4. DR is INVERSE to RMM (high RMM → low DR). Don't flip it.
5. SA 265 communication does NOT mean auditor opined on internal control — disclaimer required (unless separate Sec 143(3)(i) IFC report).
6. No prior-year reliance for significant-risk controls — test current period; any relied control tested at least every 3rd year; changed control → test this year.
7. For significant risks, substantive analytics alone insufficient — must include tests of details.
8. Deficiency can be significant on potential alone — actual misstatement not required.
9. Small company: components still exist (often informal); weak environment raises CR, pushes substantive — doesn't excuse assessment.
10. Don't test controls you won't rely on (unless substantive alone impossible) — efficiency trap.
11. Interim testing must be rolled forward to cover remaining period.
12. Inquiry alone never sufficient as a test of controls.
13. Audit risk model is conceptual, not a precise sample-size calculator.
14. "Significant risk" (SA 315, top-of-spectrum inherent risk) ≠ "significant deficiency" (SA 265, control weakness for TCWG).
15. Wrong direction of testing — vouching for understated liabilities is wrong; use search for unrecorded liabilities.
16. Management override sits above controls; strong design doesn't neutralise it — SA 240 override procedures mandatory regardless.
17. A control deviation is neither automatically a misstatement nor ignorable — investigate + re-evaluate reliance.
18. Reliance on automated application controls without ITGC evidence is unsupported.
19. Deficiency (fault in control) ≠ misstatement (error in number) — SA 265 vs SA 450.
20. Don't answer "increase the sample" to every high-risk case — nature/timing often stronger.

## One-line recall
- AR = IR × CR × DR (conceptual); RMM = IR × CR (entity's, only assessed); DR = AR ÷ RMM, INVERSE to RMM, never zero.
- SA 315 = assess (risk assessment procedures, team discussion, 5 control components, D&I via walkthrough, inherent-risk factors/spectrum, significant risks); SA 330 = respond (overall + ToC/SP, NTE by risk, substantive for every material item); SA 265 = report significant deficiencies in writing to TCWG.
- Five control components: Environment, Entity Risk Assessment, Information System, Control Activities, Monitoring; application controls rest on ITGCs.
- ToC when reliance planned OR no audit trail; methods re-performance > inspection > observation > inquiry (never alone); significant-risk controls tested current year, others at least every 3rd year.
- Vouching (records→source) = overstatement/occurrence; Tracing (source→records) = understatement/completeness; unrecorded liabilities → search, not vouching.
- Some substantive work always required; management override audited regardless of control strength (SA 240); assessment is iterative.
