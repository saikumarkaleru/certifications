# Chapter 04 — Audit Evidence (Revision Note)

## Snapshot
Owners cannot verify managers, so the auditor must gather evidence beyond management's own say-so to close the verification gap. The master rule (SA 500): obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to form a reasonable basis for the opinion. Evidence is persuasive, not conclusive. This chapter covers SA 500, 501, 505, 510, 530, 550, 570, 580, 620, plus assertions and reliability.

## Core concepts
- **Two flavours of misstatement:** Error (honest mistake) and Fraud (deliberate deception). Auditor reduces risk of undetected *material* misstatement to an acceptably low level.
- **Reasonable, not absolute, assurance** — four structural reasons: (a) selective testing (sampling); (b) inherent limitations of internal control (override/collusion); (c) evidence is persuasive not conclusive; (d) judgement is fallible.
- **Material, not every error:** immaterial error tolerated; misstatement is "material" if it would change a reasonable user's decision. Materiality (SA 320) sets the height of the bar evidence must clear.
- **Sufficient vs Appropriate (SA 500):**
  - Sufficient = *quantity*. Driven by (a) assessed risk of material misstatement (higher risk = more) and (b) quality of evidence (higher quality = less needed). No fixed sample size. Sufficiency judged only *after* appropriateness, against risk × materiality.
  - Appropriate = *quality* = Relevance + Reliability.
- **Relevance** has two dimensions: (a) relates to the *specific assertion* (subsequent receipts test existence/valuation of a receivable, not completeness); (b) depends on *direction of the test* (vouch vs trace).
- **Assertions = the auditor's coordinate system.** Every figure is management asserting several separable claims at once (e.g. inventory: it exists, we own it, it's complete, valued right, disclosed properly). Each can fail independently and needs its own evidence.
- **Direction of misstatement is directional:** assets/revenue tend to be *overstated* (test existence/occurrence), liabilities/expenses *understated* (test completeness).
- **Relevance and reliability are independent axes** — plot every piece of evidence on both; strong evidence needs both.

## Key provisions / SAs

### SA 500 — Audit Evidence (master standard)
Requirements:
- Obtain **sufficient appropriate** evidence.
- Consider **relevance and reliability**.
- When using **information produced by the entity**, evaluate its reliability — test its **accuracy and completeness** (e.g. an ageing report used to test receivables).
- When using a **management's expert**, evaluate competence, capability, objectivity; understand the work; assess appropriateness.
- If evidence is **inconsistent** or reliability doubtful, determine modifications/additions needed (chase contradictions).
- **Selecting items for testing:** (a) select all items (100%), (b) specific items, or (c) audit sampling.

**Audit procedures (memory "I ORREAI"):**
| Procedure | What auditor does | Primary assertions |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection | Examine records, documents, physical assets | Existence (physical), rights, valuation (documents) |
| Observation | Watch a process (e.g. inventory count) | Existence; whether controls operate |
| External confirmation | Direct written response from third party (SA 505) | Existence, rights, completeness of certain items |
| Recalculation | Check arithmetic | Accuracy/valuation |
| Reperformance | Independently execute a control/procedure | Whether control operates (accuracy, completeness) |
| Analytical procedures | Evaluate relationships; investigate deviations | Completeness, occurrence, reasonableness |
| Inquiry | Seek information from knowledgeable persons | Supports all but **weak alone** — must corroborate |

- **Inquiry alone is never sufficient** — it just repeats the assertion under test.
- Inspection of an *asset* proves existence, NOT automatically rights (may be leased/consigned) or valuation (may be obsolete). Inspection of a *document* depends on its nature/source.
- Observation only proves the control operated *at the moment watched*; reperformance is stronger (auditor re-executes himself).
- System-generated data is only as reliable as the IT controls behind it (link SA 315) — a printout is not independent.

### Assertions
- **Classes of transactions** (P&L, during period), **account balances** (B/S, at period end), **presentation & disclosure**.
- **Existence (balances)/Occurrence (transactions):** risk = overstatement (fictitious assets, fake sales). Evidence: physical inspection, confirmation, vouch to source.
- **Completeness:** everything that should be recorded is recorded; risk = understatement (hidden liabilities, unrecorded sales). Evidence: trace from source documents *into* books; cut-off tests; analytics.
- **Valuation/Accuracy & Allocation:** correct value/measurement. Evidence: recalculation, valuation review, ageing, expert reports.
- **Rights & Obligations:** entity owns assets/owes liabilities. Evidence: title deeds, charge registers, confirmations.
- **Cut-off (transactions):** recorded in correct period. Test last/first documents each side of year-end (GDNs, GRNs) vs recording date.
- **Classification (transactions):** proper accounts (capital vs revenue).
- **Presentation & Disclosure:** classification, occurrence and rights, completeness, accuracy and valuation, understandability.

**Direction (most-tested idea):**
- Test **overstatement (existence/occurrence)** → start from recorded figure, go OUT to reality = **vouching** (confirmation).
- Test **understatement (completeness)** → start from reality, come INTO the books = **tracing**.
- Cut-off lives *between* existence and completeness — profit easiest to manipulate at year-end boundary; touches two directional tests at one line.

### Reliability hierarchy (SA 500 generalisations, high → low)
1. External more reliable than internal (management doesn't control third parties).
2. Effective internal controls make internal evidence more reliable.
3. Evidence auditor obtains *directly* beats indirect/inferred.
4. Documentary beats oral.
5. Originals beat photocopies/faxes/scans.

- Reliability = **source × form** (two dimensions collapsed); **relevance** is a separate axis. A written confirmation beats an internal document; but an internal original document usually beats a fleeting oral outsider's remark.
- All are **generalisations subject to exceptions** — a colluding related party's confirmation or a forged "original" is unreliable. Scepticism overrides.

### SA 505 — External Confirmations
Counters: management fabricating balances only a third party can verify (receivables/existence, bank balances/loans).
- Auditor **controls the whole process** — selects, designs, sends, receives reply *directly* (not via management).
- **Positive** confirmation: reply whether agree or not (higher risk). **Negative:** reply only if disagree (weaker, silence ambiguous).
- **Four conditions for negative confirmations (all must hold):** (a) risk of material misstatement assessed low + evidence on operating effectiveness of relevant controls; (b) large number of small homogeneous balances; (c) very low expected exception rate; (d) auditor not aware of circumstances causing recipients to disregard. Fail any one → negatives not permissible.
- **Positive form with balance blanked out:** more reliable (respondent must retrieve figure) but lower response rate.
- **Management refuses to allow confirmation:** inquire reasons, evaluate reasonableness, perform alternative procedures; if unreasonable = **scope limitation** (qualification/disclaimer) and a fraud signal.
- **Non-responses:** alternative procedures (subsequent cash receipts, shipping docs, sales invoices).
- **Exceptions:** investigate (timing difference or genuine misstatement).
- **Doubtful reliability** (arrived via management, altered, electronic/oral): verify source.
- **Confirmations prove existence brilliantly, completeness poorly** — can't confirm a debtor not on your list. For completeness of liabilities, confirm *suppliers*/reconcile supplier statements (independent of entity's omissions).
- **Timing:** usually at/near year-end. If interim, perform roll-forward for intervening period (only where control risk low enough).

### SA 510 — Initial Audit Engagements: Opening Balances
Counters: first-year opening balances never audited by this auditor; if wrong, distort current figures/profit.
- Read most recent **prior-year financial statements** and **predecessor auditor's report**.
- Obtain sufficient appropriate evidence openings contain no material misstatement, by: (a) prior-period closing balances correctly brought forward; (b) **accounting policies consistently applied**; (c) evidence from predecessor's work, or current-period procedures giving *incidental* evidence (collection of opening receivables proves existence/valuation), or specific procedures.
- Material misstatement in openings not dealt with, or inconsistent policies → **modify opinion** (qualified/adverse).
- Unable to obtain sufficient evidence on openings → scope limitation → **qualified or disclaimer on results of operations**.
- **Current** items (receivables, payables, inventory) turn over → incidental evidence available. **Non-current** items (fixed assets, long-term loans, retained earnings) do NOT turn over → harder; inspect prior records or rely on predecessor.
- May review predecessor's working papers (depends on predecessor competence/independence) — does NOT reduce current auditor's responsibility. If prior report was modified, evaluate effect of the underlying matter on current period.

### SA 550 — Related Parties
Counters: transactions with connected parties used to siphon value, hide losses, manufacture profit on non-arm's-length terms; ordinary evidence tainted. Fraud hides here.
- **Understand** relationships/transactions; remain **alert** for undisclosed ones (bank confirmations revealing guarantees, unusual transactions).
- Where framework requires arm's-length disclosure, obtain evidence about management's assertion — don't accept label at face value.
- **Identified but previously undisclosed related parties/transactions** → communicate to team, treat as **significant risk** (fraud red flag).
- Obtain **written representations** that all related parties/transactions disclosed.
- Evaluate **business rationale** of significant transactions outside normal course.
- Related parties defeat the reliability hierarchy — external in *form* but not in *substance*; **substance over form** governs. Hunt undisclosed ones in: bank confirmations, register of contracts/charges, board minutes, statutory filings of directors' interests, large/unusual year-end journals, unusual-terms transactions (interest-free loans, odd prices, round-sum settlements).

### SA 570 — Going Concern
Counters: statements assume the entity continues; if false, entire basis misleading.
- Management makes going-concern assessment; auditor **evaluates** it and stays alert for events/conditions casting doubt.
- Where doubt exists, obtain evidence on whether a **material uncertainty** exists — evaluate plans (financing, asset sales, restructuring), test **cash-flow forecast** and assumptions, seek **written representations** on plans/feasibility.
- **Three buckets of warning signs:** (a) **financial** — net liabilities/negative net worth, recurring losses, adverse ratios, loan defaults, inability to pay creditors, withdrawal of supplier credit, dividend arrears; (b) **operating** — loss of key management/market/licence/supplier/customer, labour difficulties, supply shortages; (c) **other** — non-compliance with capital/statutory requirements, pending legal/regulatory proceedings, adverse law/policy changes. Significance may be mitigated by other factors (e.g. default offset by firm refinancing commitment).
- **Reporting decision tree:**
  - Basis appropriate + material uncertainty exists + **adequately disclosed** → **unmodified opinion with separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section**.
  - Material uncertainty + **not adequately disclosed** → **qualified or adverse**.
  - Going-concern basis used but **inappropriate** (won't continue) → **adverse**.
  - Management **unwilling to make/extend** assessment → scope limitation → **qualified/disclaimer**.
- Logic: disclosed uncertainty = statements not wrong (they truthfully warn) → unmodified. Modification triggered only by a *defect in the statements* (inadequate disclosure/wrong basis), never the mere existence of doubt.

### SA 580 — Written Representations
Counters: matters living only inside management's head (intentions, completeness of disclosures, knowledge of fraud) — no external evidence.
- Obtain representation that management **fulfilled its responsibility** for the statements and for providing **all relevant information and access**, and that **all transactions are recorded**.
- Obtain specific representations required by other SAs (SA 240 fraud, SA 550 related parties, SA 570 plans).
- **Dated** as near as practicable to, **but NOT after**, the auditor's report; addressed **to the auditor**; signed by those with appropriate responsibility (CEO/CFO); covers all statements/periods in the report.
- **Written representations are audit evidence but NOT sufficient appropriate evidence on their own** about the matters they concern — they complement, never substitute (still a management assertion).
- If management **refuses** a required representation, or auditor **doubts management's integrity** (reps unreliable) → casts doubt on all reps/evidence → potentially **disclaimer of opinion**. Refusal of *basic* responsibility representations ordinarily forces a **disclaimer**.

### Management's expert vs Auditor's expert (SA 500 / SA 620)
| Dimension | Management's expert | Auditor's expert |
|---|---|---|
| Engaged/paid by | Management | The auditor |
| Governing standard | SA 500 | SA 620 |
| Independence concern | High (paid by audited party) | Lower (still assess objectivity/competence) |
| Auditor does | Evaluate competence, capability, objectivity; understand work; assess appropriateness | Determine need; evaluate competence/objectivity; agree scope; evaluate adequacy of work |
| Effect on opinion | Auditor solely responsible | Auditor solely responsible; **no reference** to expert in unmodified report |

**Punchline: the opinion is the auditor's alone; expertise may be borrowed, responsibility never delegated.**

### Selecting items (SA 500)
- **100% examination:** small population with large items, single significant risk, or automated processing.
- **Specific items:** large/unusual/risky/key items chosen by judgement — NOT sampling, results **cannot be projected**.
- **Audit sampling (SA 530):** less than 100%, every unit a chance of selection, enabling a conclusion about the whole population; project sample results + misstatements.

## Exam traps & must-remember
- "Inquiry alone is enough" — NEVER; must corroborate.
- Written representations used as a *substitute* — they are evidence but **never sufficient alone**; "relied on the management letter" for collectibility/existence = error.
- Negatives are weak (silence ≠ agreement); need all four conditions; don't recommend for risky receivables.
- Confirmation received via — or *offered to be sent by* — management: both destroy reliability; auditor must control the *entire* process and receive replies directly.
- Sufficient ≠ appropriate; a huge file of weak internal vouchers is not enough; quantity cannot cure poor quality; sufficiency judged only after appropriateness.
- Existence ≠ valuation; evidence for one account ≠ evidence for another (clean receivables confirmation ≠ collectibility, ≠ inventory).
- First-year smugness: clean current-year work does NOT cover opening balances (SA 510); non-current openings are the hard ones.
- Going-concern mix-up: adequately-disclosed material uncertainty → unmodified + Material Uncertainty section, NOT a qualification. Trigger for modification is a defect in the statements.
- Reliability rules are generalisations; related parties are external in form but not substance; scepticism overrides.
- Direction: overstatement → vouch outward; understatement → trace inward. "Traced from sales register to dispatch notes to test completeness" is WRONG (that tests occurrence).
- Cut-off is its own assertion; test documents on both sides of the closing date.
- Cost/time as an excuse: "skipped confirmations because costly/time-consuming" is a **violation**, not judgement — difficulty alone never justifies omitting a procedure with no alternative (cost may steer *which* of several adequate procedures, never justify an inadequate one).
- 100% vs specific items vs sampling: a judgemental pick of large items is NOT a sample; cannot project.
- Management's expert ≠ auditor's expert; different standard (SA 500 vs SA 620); unmodified report never mentions the auditor's expert.
- Refusal to confirm / refusal to represent: both red flags — evaluate reasonableness, do alternative work, consider scope limitation/disclaimer.
- Written-representation dating: as near as practicable to, but NOT after, the report date.

## One-line recall
- SA 500: Sufficient (quantity, after appropriateness, vs risk × materiality) + Appropriate (relevance + reliability); persuasive not conclusive; inquiry alone never sufficient.
- Reliability = source × form; external/auditor-generated/documentary/original/direct beat internal/oral/copy/indirect — all generalisations with exceptions.
- Direction: existence/occurrence → vouch outward (overstatement); completeness → trace inward (understatement); cut-off at the year-end line.
- SA 505 auditor controls the process; positive vs negative (4 conditions); refusal = scope limit + alternatives. SA 510 opening balances (non-current = hard). SA 550 related parties = significant risk, substance over form.
- SA 570 disclosed material uncertainty → unmodified + MU section; else qualify/adverse. SA 580 written reps never sufficient alone, dated not after the report.
- Connections: SA 315/330 (risk/response), SA 240 (fraud), SA 320 (materiality), SA 230 (documentation), SA 501 (inventory/litigation/segments), SA 530 (sampling), SA 560 (subsequent events), SA 620 (auditor's expert), SA 700/705/706 (report).
