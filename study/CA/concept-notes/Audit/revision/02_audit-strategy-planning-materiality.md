# Chapter 02 — Audit Strategy, Planning & Materiality (Revision)

## Snapshot
Auditor cannot and should not check everything: finite resources (scarcity) plus not-every-error-matters (relevance). Solution: aim scarce effort at the intersection of LARGE amounts (materiality) and RISKY amounts (risk). Core quartet: SA 315 (find risks) → SA 320 (size threshold) → SA 300 (aim effort/plan) → SA 330 (respond); SA 450 measures found misstatements at completion.

## Core concepts
- **Reasonable, not absolute, assurance** — high but not absolute, due to three permanent inherent limitations: (a) nature of financial reporting (many figures are estimates — provisions, useful lives, fair values, ECL — inherently imprecise); (b) nature of audit procedures (evidence is persuasive not conclusive; relies on management info; cannot detect well-concealed collusive fraud with certainty); (c) audit must be completed in reasonable time at reasonable cost.
- **Materiality** = how big an error must be to matter. **Risk** = where errors hide. **Planning** = aiming effort at large + risky. Properly planned file is deliberately **lopsided** (thick where money and risk concentrate); an evenly-thick file signals a planning failure.
- **Planning vs "the plan":** Planning is the ongoing activity (continuous & iterative). Overall audit strategy and audit plan are its outputs. **Audit programme** = detailed list of procedures (operational heart of the plan) that assistants execute and initial.
- **Planning vs audit programme:** planning = broad forward thinking (scope, timing, direction, resources); programme = detailed procedure checklist.
- Materiality is set by the **auditor** as a planning tool (input to designing procedures and evaluating findings). NOT disclosed in financial statements, NOT negotiated with client, NOT the same as management's accounting materiality.
- Materiality is set for the FS **as a whole** (common information needs of users as a group), NOT per individual user.

## Key provisions / SAs

**SA 300 — Planning an Audit of Financial Statements**
- Purpose: conduct audit effectively — attention to important areas, problems identified/resolved timely, engagement properly organised, staffed, directed and supervised.
- **Benefits:** devote attention to important areas; identify/resolve problems timely; organise & manage efficiently; select team members with appropriate capabilities/competence and assign work; facilitate direction/supervision/review; coordinate component auditors and experts.
- **Preliminary engagement activities (before planning; done at start of CURRENT audit, every year):** (1) client/engagement **continuance** procedures; (2) evaluate compliance with **ethical requirements incl. independence** (SA 220/Code); (3) establish **understanding of engagement terms** (SA 210 — engagement letter). Act as a filter identifying events (loss of independence, integrity issues) that affect ability to plan/perform.
- **(a) Overall Audit Strategy** — sets **scope, timing and direction**; guides development of the plan. Auditor:
  - identifies characteristics defining **scope** (reporting framework, industry reporting, locations/components, reliance on internal audit);
  - ascertains **reporting objectives to plan timing** & communications (interim/final deadlines, TCWG meeting dates);
  - considers **factors significant in directing efforts** (materiality determination, preliminary high-risk areas, prior audit results, management's commitment to internal control);
  - considers results of preliminary engagement activities;
  - ascertains **nature, timing, extent of resources** (team size, experience, deployment, budget).
  - **Scope vs Direction:** Scope = boundaries/characteristics (framework, components, locations, use of internal audit). Direction = where effort is steered within boundaries (materiality, preliminary high-risk areas, prior-year results, management control attitude). Materiality is a **direction** factor, NOT scope.
- **(b) Audit Plan** — more detailed; converts strategy into procedures. Describes: **NTE of planned risk assessment procedures** (SA 315); **NTE of planned further audit procedures at assertion level** (SA 330 — tests of controls + substantive); **other procedures** required by SAs.
- **NTE triad:** Nature = kind/purpose of procedure (inspection, observation, external confirmation, recalculation, analytical) & control test vs substantive; Timing = when (interim vs at/after year-end) & period covered; Extent = how much (sample size, locations, quantity). Higher risk → more reliable nature, timing nearer/at year-end, larger extent.
- Strategy & plan are **interrelated, continuous, iterative** — a change in one may cause consequential change in the other; update as audit progresses.
- **Direction, supervision, review:** plan NTE of these; intensity rises with (i) assessed risk, (ii) complexity, and falls with (iii) staff competence/experience. Links to SA 220.
- **Documentation (SA 300):** overall strategy; audit plan; **significant changes** to strategy/plan **and reasons**.
- **Initial (first-year) audits need MORE planning** (no prior cumulative knowledge): arrangements with **predecessor auditor** (SA 210/ethics), procedures on **opening balances** (SA 510), confirm prior closing balances correctly carried forward.

**SA 320 — Materiality in Planning and Performing an Audit**
- Purpose: apply materiality in **planning and performing**. (SA 450 = evaluating misstatements found — measuring against the yardstick at end.)
- **Definition:** Misstatements (incl. omissions) are **material** if, individually or in aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to **influence the economic decisions of users** taken on the basis of the FS. Depends on **size and/or nature** (quantitative + qualitative). Judged from a reasonable user's standpoint.
- **No fixed formula** — professional judgement, framework-dependent (Ind AS/AS definition provides frame of reference where it exists).
- **Assumptions about users:** reasonable knowledge of business/accounting + willingness to study with reasonable diligence; understand FS prepared/audited to materiality levels (not perfection); recognise uncertainties in estimates/judgements; make reasonable economic decisions.
- **Three materiality figures (+ a fourth threshold):**
  - **(i) Overall materiality (FS as a whole):** set at strategy stage; % × chosen benchmark.
    - Benchmark factors: elements of FS (assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, expenses); items users focus on; nature of entity, life-cycle stage, industry/economic environment; ownership structure & financing (debt-financed → users focus on assets/claims); relative volatility of the benchmark.
    - Indicative benchmarks (confirm current ICAI figures; SA fixes no numbers): PBT ~5% (profit-focused); Revenue/turnover ~0.5–1% (PBT small/volatile/near-break-even, loss-making, growth); Total assets ~1–2% (asset-heavy/lenders); Net assets/equity ~1–5% (funds); Gross profit/total expenses varies (NPO/expenditure-focused).
    - **Normalise** a benchmark distorted by a one-off item (asset-sale gain, exceptional impairment, freak year) — use figure before exceptional item or multi-year average; document.
  - **(ii) Specific materiality** (particular classes/balances/disclosures, where applicable): lower figure where a misstatement below overall materiality would still influence users due to **nature**. Examples: related-party transactions; directors' remuneration; loan covenant / regulatory ratio compliance; amount crossing loss-to-profit; industry-sensitive disclosures. Flagging factors: law/regulation/framework drives expectations for an item; key industry disclosures; attention focused on a separately-disclosed aspect (e.g., newly acquired business). Set "where applicable," not always.
  - **(iii) Performance materiality:** amount(s) set at **less than** overall (and less than specific) materiality to **reduce to an appropriately low level the probability that aggregate of uncorrected + undetected misstatements exceeds overall materiality.** Two jobs: aggregation cushion + undetected (sampling) error cushion. Often ~50–75% of overall (lower for higher risk). Calibrated by: understanding of entity, prior-year misstatement history, expectation of current-period misstatements. **Can be set lower for a specific high-error class/balance** — not necessarily a single figure for the whole audit.
  - **(iv) Clearly-trivial threshold** (belongs to SA 450, set in planning): amount below which misstatements need NOT be accumulated. NOT a synonym for "immaterial" — a much lower bar. Misstatements above clearly-trivial but below materiality MUST still be accumulated and evaluated.
- **The ladder (largest→smallest):** Overall materiality > Performance materiality > Clearly-trivial threshold. Specific materiality is a parallel LOWER line for sensitive areas (with its own PM beneath it).
- **Materiality ↔ audit risk are inverse:** lower materiality → more items material → more evidence/work → detection risk down. Higher risk → lower performance materiality (bigger cushion).
- **Revision (SA 320 requirement):** revise materiality (and specific/performance) if new info would have caused a different initial determination — e.g., actuals diverge sharply from estimates used, decision to dispose of a major part of the business, change in understanding of entity. **Downward revision is the dangerous one:** must reconsider whether performance materiality and NTE of further procedures remain adequate; procedures done to a higher threshold may need extending/repeating.
- **Documentation (SA 320):** amounts AND **factors considered** for — overall materiality; specific materiality (if any); performance materiality; any revision.

**Other linked SAs**
- **SA 315** — Identifying/assessing risks of material misstatement; supplies the risk map planning aims at.
- **SA 330** — Auditor's responses to assessed risks; the plan's "further procedures."
- **SA 450** — Evaluation of misstatements; back-end partner, accumulate above clearly-trivial, judge aggregation at completion.
- **SA 510** — Initial audit engagements / opening balances.
- **SA 210 / SA 220 / SQC 1** — engagement terms; quality control (direction/supervision/review, competent staffing).

**Assertions (SA 315) — materiality lands on these:** Transactions: Occurrence, Completeness, Accuracy, Cut-off, Classification. Balances: Existence, Rights & obligations, Completeness, Valuation & allocation. Presentation/disclosure: Occurrence, Completeness, Classification, Accuracy, Valuation.
- Revenue → risky assertions Occurrence (fictitious sales) & Cut-off. Inventory → Existence & Valuation (obsolescence). Liabilities/provisions → Completeness (hunt unrecorded).
- **Directional testing:** Overstatement-prone items (assets, income) — test FROM recorded figure TO evidence. Understatement-prone (liabilities, expenses) — test FROM independent sources BACK to records (search for unrecorded liabilities: subsequent payments, unmatched supplier statements, post-year-end invoices).

## Exam traps & must-remember
1. Materiality is NOT a fixed rule/single %. It is judgement-based, entity-specific, benchmark-anchored. Never write "5% always."
2. Don't forget the **qualitative** dimension — a small amount in a sensitive area (director's pay, related parties, loss-to-profit flip, covenant breach) is material by **nature**. "Immaterial, ignore" is the trap answer.
3. Don't confuse Overall (top-line) vs Performance (lower working buffer) vs Specific (lower for sensitive areas). Performance is always BELOW overall.
4. Risk–materiality direction: higher risk → LOWER performance materiality; lower materiality → MORE work. Don't invert.
5. Planning is continuous/iterative, NOT a one-time event.
6. Strategy (scope/timing/direction/resources = map) vs Plan (detailed procedures = route) — don't list procedures under strategy or resources under plan.
7. Must **revise** materiality when projected vs actual profit diverges; downward revision → reconsider procedures already done.
8. Every decision (incl. reasons/factors and changes) must be **documented** — don't stop at "auditor decides X."
9. Planning is NOT all done before fieldwork; continues throughout; some substantive procedures may begin during planning.
10. Every audit is planned — scale varies with size/complexity; small audit needs less, not zero.
11. "Clearly trivial" ≠ "immaterial." Above clearly-trivial but below materiality MUST still be accumulated.
12. Not one performance materiality for the whole audit — a high-error class can carry its own lower PM.
13. Audit plan ≠ audit programme; programme = detailed procedure list within the plan.
14. Don't reflex to 5% of PBT — debt-financed/asset-heavy/loss-making/investment entities may need total assets/revenue/net assets. Justify from who the users are and what they watch.
15. Management does NOT set or get told exact materiality — it's the auditor's file judgement, not disclosed in FS.
16. Cross-check chosen benchmark against an alternative; a wildly different result flags an unstable primary benchmark.

## One-line recall
- Can't check everything (scarcity) and shouldn't (relevance) → aim at large × risky = planning; only reasonable (not absolute) assurance due to 3 limitations.
- SA 300 = machinery (preliminary activities → strategy [scope/timing/direction/resources] → plan [risk-assessment + further procedures via NTE]; continuous; documented).
- SA 320 = threshold (overall = %×benchmark; specific = lower for sensitive/nature; performance = below overall, ~50–75%, buffers aggregation + sampling; clearly-trivial below that).
- Ladder: Overall > Performance > Clearly-trivial; Specific runs lower and parallel. Materiality↓⇒work↑; Risk↑⇒PM↓.
- Materiality = size AND/OR nature; benchmark follows users' focus & financing structure; normalise one-offs; revise when actuals/facts change (downward = reconsider procedures).
- Chain: SA 315 (map) → SA 320 (size) → SA 300 (aim) → SA 330 (respond) → SA 450 (evaluate found misstatements).
