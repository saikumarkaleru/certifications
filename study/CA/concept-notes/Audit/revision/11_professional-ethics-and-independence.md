# Chapter 11 — Professional Ethics & Independence (Revision)

## Snapshot
An audit opinion is a **paid** opinion relied on by **outsiders who cannot verify it** (agency problem / information asymmetry). It has value only if the author is **independent** (in fact + appearance) and bound by **enforceable principles** stronger than self-interest. Credibility is a *shared reputational asset* — one bad audit poisons trust in all (tragedy of the commons), so the **profession**, not conscience, enforces ethics. Scaffolding: **principles → threats → safeguards → outright legal prohibitions.**

## Core concepts

**Governing instruments**
- **ICAI Code of Ethics** (based on IESBA Code) — principles, conceptual framework, threats & safeguards, independence for assurance. Organised in 3 parts: **Part A** (fundamental principles + framework — all members), **Part B** (members in practice), **Part C** (members in service).
- **CA Act, 1949 — Schedules I & II** — "professional misconduct"; gives the Code legal teeth.
- **SA 200** — requires compliance with relevant ethical requirements *including independence* (precondition of any audit).
- **SQC 1 / SA 220** — firm-level & engagement-level quality control incl. independence procedures.
- **Companies Act, 2013 — ss. 141, 144, 139(2)** — eligibility/disqualification, prohibited services, rotation.
- One line: *SA 200 says "comply"; the Code says "what"; the CA Act and Companies Act make some legally binding with penalties.*

**Independence — two forms** (assurance engagements only; NOT a fundamental principle — a separate additional requirement):
- **Independence of mind (in fact)** — actual unbiased state permitting a conclusion without bias/conflict/undue influence; integrity, objectivity, scepticism.
- **Independence in appearance** — avoiding circumstances so significant a **reasonable and informed third party** would conclude objectivity was compromised. *Reasonable* (sensible, not paranoid/naive), *informed* (knows facts + safeguards), *third party* (outsider). Appearance converts an unverifiable question ("is he objective?") into a verifiable one ("does he hold shares? is his relative a director?").
- **Period & scope:** must hold across BOTH the *engagement period* AND the *period covered by the FS* (a share sold day before signing does not cleanse a year of holding). Extends to the **firm, network firm, and engagement team members** (incl. their immediate family) — not just the signing partner.

**Objectivity vs Independence (exam axis):** objectivity = *state of mind*, required in **every** service (tax, consulting, audit); independence = *set of circumstances/relationships*, required only for **assurance** where an external user relies. A tax adviser must be objective, need not be independent; an auditor must be both.

**Five Fundamental Principles (I-O-C-C-P)** — bind *every* CA (practice or service):
- **Integrity** — straightforward & honest; fair dealing, truthfulness. Breached by *knowing association* with false/misleading/recklessly-made statements or omissions — reckless indifference = want of integrity. Cure: disassociate; insist on correction; if refused, withdraw.
- **Objectivity** — no bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence overriding judgement. Broadest principle; governs even where no assurance (e.g., advising two competing clients).
- **Professional Competence & Due Care** — two limbs: **attainment** + **maintenance** (CPE). Act diligently per standards. Make clients aware of limitations; decline/seek help beyond competence. Failure occurs at *acceptance*.
- **Confidentiality** — don't **disclose** *and* don't **use** client info (e.g., insider trading). Continues **after** relationship ends; extends to family/social settings; supervisory duty over staff/outside advisers. Permitted/required disclosures: (i) permitted by law + client-authorised; (ii) required by law (legal proceedings, disclosing infringements); (iii) professional duty/right (peer/quality review, ICAI investigation, self-defence in litigation, technical/ethical standards). Even permitted disclosure needs judgement (facts substantiated, audience, legal liability).
- **Professional Behaviour** — comply with laws; avoid conduct discrediting the profession. Covers advertising/solicitation restrictions (no soliciting by advertising; no disparaging/exaggerated claims). *Principle of no-solicitation endures even as boundaries liberalise.*

**Five Threats (classify by psychological mechanism — complete by construction):**
- **Self-interest** — auditor gains from an outcome. E.g., shares/loan in client, **fee dependence**, contingent fee, close business relationship, job offer from client.
- **Self-review** — judging own prior work. E.g., firm keeps books / prepared FS / did valuation, actuarial, IFC design, then audits it; former firm member now a director.
- **Advocacy** — promoting/championing client's position. E.g., representing client in litigation/tax dispute against a third party or authority; promoting/underwriting client shares.
- **Familiarity** — too close/trusting, dulling scepticism. E.g., relative in key position at client, long association (→ partner rotation), former partner now KMP, significant gifts/hospitality.
- **Intimidation** — deterred by actual/perceived pressure. E.g., threat of dismissal/non-reappointment over a disagreement, threat of litigation, pressure to cut work/fees, dominant client.
- *One fact can trigger multiple threats — always scan all five.*

**"No management responsibility"** — auditor must never assume management responsibility (authorising transactions, taking decisions, designing/implementing controls) for an audit client → purest self-review + loss of independence. Conceptual root beneath the s.144 list; use it to judge novel unlisted services.

**Safeguards** — action reducing a threat to an **acceptable level** (level at which a reasonable & informed third party would conclude principles not compromised). Two sources:
- **(A) Profession/legislation/regulation** — entry education/training/experience, CPE, professional standards, monitoring & discipline, external review by a legally empowered third party.
- **(B) Firm's own systems** — firm-wide (leadership tone, SQC 1 policies, documented independence policies, rotation of senior personnel, discipline) and engagement-specific (**EQCR** not on team, rotate engagement partner, separate partners/teams for non-assurance, consult ICAI).
- **NOT safeguards:** (i) **management's own assurances** (audited party can't certify itself); (ii) a **bigger fee** or the **client's importance** (worsens self-interest). Revised Code is sceptical of "client-side" safeguards — prefers *removing the circumstance*.
- Safeguards **scale with severity**: trivial → routine policies; moderate → EQCR/separate teams; severe (partner's spouse is CFO, 40%+ fee dependence) → often un-safeguardable → remove person / exit.
- **If no safeguard brings the threat to an acceptable level → remove circumstance / decline / resign.** No "manage it and hope." Revised Code verb: *threats must be addressed* (often by eliminating the circumstance), not merely "safeguards applied."

## Key provisions (CA Act Schedules + Companies Act sections)

**Companies Act, 2013 — the legal backstop (test FIRST):**

**s. 141 — Eligibility / disqualifications of auditor.** Not eligible if:
- a **body corporate** (except LLP); an **officer/employee** of the company; a **partner/employee of an officer/employee**;
- a person (or partner/relative) holding **any security or interest** — auditor & firm bar is **absolute (not even one share)**; relatives may hold up to prescribed face value (*historically ₹1,00,000 — verify*), with a corrective window (*historically 60 days*) if exceeded; "interest" read widely (not just equity) [self-interest];
- indebted beyond prescribed amount, or gave guarantee for a third party's debt beyond limit [self-interest/intimidation];
- a **business relationship** (beyond exceptions) [self-interest];
- a person whose **relative is a director or KMP** [familiarity — flat bar];
- in **full-time employment elsewhere**, or holding audits of **more than 20 companies** (ceiling; carve-outs: OPC, dormant, small, certain private cos — *verify*; count generally **per partner** for a firm);
- convicted of **fraud in last 10 years** [professional behaviour];
- directly/indirectly renders a **s.144 prohibited service**.

**s. 144 — Auditor not to render certain services** (to the company, its holding & subsidiary, **directly or indirectly** incl. via network/relative): accounting & book-keeping; internal audit; design & implementation of any **financial information system**; actuarial; investment advisory; investment banking; outsourced financial services; management services; any other prescribed service. Map: bookkeeping/internal audit/system design = self-review; investment banking/advisory = advocacy/self-interest. **NOT barred:** statutory audit, **tax representation**, and certification not on the list (so "can auditor file the tax return?" → generally *yes*).

**s. 139(2) — Rotation** (listed cos + prescribed classes; familiarity threat capped by statute): individual auditor **one term of 5 consecutive years**; audit firm **two terms of 5 (max 10)**, then **5-year cooling-off**. Anti-circumvention: cooling-off catches a **different firm with common partners** and firms under the **same network**; outgoing firm/associates barred for cooling period. Distinct from *partner* rotation (a Code safeguard, broader than statute).

**CA Act, 1949 — disciplinary consequence.** Conduct within the **First & Second Schedules** = **professional misconduct**, tried by ICAI's Board of Discipline / Disciplinary Committee; penalties up to **removal of name from the Register** + fines.
- **First Schedule = LESS grave → Board of Discipline** (reprimand, removal up to a limited period, capped fine).
  - Part I — professional misconduct, members **in practice** (clauses 1–12).
  - Part II — professional misconduct, members **in service**.
  - Part III — professional misconduct, members **generally**.
  - Part IV — **other misconduct**.
- **Second Schedule = MORE grave → Disciplinary Committee** (heaviest penalties incl. removal permanently or for a longer period + higher fine).
  - Part I — professional misconduct, members **in practice** (clauses 1–10).
  - Part II — professional misconduct, members **generally**.
  - Part III — **other misconduct**.
- Two classic Second-Schedule items tie to this chapter: **"grossly negligent in conduct of professional duties"** (= competence/due-care failure) and **"discloses information acquired in the course of professional engagement without consent"** (= confidentiality breach). *Name schedule + underlying principle for marks. Verify exact penalty amounts and clause allocations.*

**Fees (threat hiding in plain sight):**
- **Fee dependence / undue proportion** — one client too large a share → self-interest (keep it) + intimidation (fear of loss); heightened if fees *overdue* or client a *public interest entity*. *Verify if Code specifies a % trigger.*
- **Contingent fees** (dependent on outcome/result) — **prohibited for assurance**. Not "contingent" if fixed by court/authority; percentage-based fees traditionally restricted under CA Act misconduct schedules (*verify current permitted bases*).
- **Overdue fees** — function like a loan (unsecured creditor dependent on client) → self-interest; collect before issuing current report.
- **Lowballing / low fees** — a **professional competence & due care** threat (risk of cutting corners), not primarily self-interest; not misconduct per se but work must still meet standard.

**Standards linkage:** SA 200 (comply w/ ethics incl. independence); SQC 1 (firm-level policies & monitoring); SA 220 (engagement partner forms conclusion on independence); SQC 1/SA 220 (document threats, safeguards, conclusions); SA 220/firm policy (written independence confirmations from team). [SQC 1 being superseded globally by SQM standards — *verify ICAI timing*.]

## Exam traps & must-remember
- **"He's honest, so it's fine."** No — *appearance* is an independent requirement; reasonable third party's doubt fails independence regardless of actual integrity.
- **Don't call independence a fundamental principle** — it's a separate additional requirement, assurance only.
- **Only spotting one threat** — fee dependence + client pressure = self-interest AND intimidation; bookkeeping-then-audit = self-review (+ often self-interest). Scan all five.
- **Assuming safeguards fix anything** — s.141 disqualifications, s.144 prohibited services, contingent assurance fees admit **NO** safeguard → decline/resign.
- **Designing safeguards before checking the legal bar** — test permissibility (ss.141/144/139) FIRST; jumping to an EQCR reveals you missed a flat statutory bar.
- **Confidentiality = only "don't disclose"** — also forbids **using** info, continues **after** engagement, has **exceptions** (required by law, professional duty/right); a permitted disclosure still needs judgement (don't over-disclose).
- **Advocacy vs self-review** — decided by the *activity*: *argued the case before authority/tribunal* = advocacy; *computed/filed the return you now audit* = self-review. Same tax, different threat.
- **Self-interest vs intimidation (fees)** — wanting to *keep* client = self-interest; being *threatened* with loss = intimidation; one CFO sentence = both.
- **"Safeguard" that isn't** — management's assurances, bigger fee, client's importance are NOT safeguards.
- **Relative shareholding is not zero** — auditor/firm hold **none**; relatives up to prescribed face-value limit with corrective window if exceeded. A **relative as director/KMP** = flat disqualification.
- **Miscounting the audit ceiling** — exclude exempt classes (small/OPC/dormant/certain private — *verify*) BEFORE counting against 20; count generally per partner for a firm.
- **Ignoring the independence period** — must hold across the whole FS period audited, not just at signing.
- **Restricting perimeter to signing partner** — firm, network firm, team members' immediate family also count.
- **Assuming all tax work is banned** — s.144 does NOT bar tax representation or certification.
- **Forgetting enforcement** — breach = professional misconduct under CA Act Schedules; state the schedule (First = less grave/Board of Discipline; Second = grave/Disciplinary Committee).

**Answer skeleton:** Identify threat(s) → evaluate significance (+ reasonable & informed third-party test) → **check legal bar first (ss.141/144/139)** → apply safeguards → re-assess → remove/decline/resign if unacceptable → document + obtain written independence confirmations. *In writing, put the legal-bar step BEFORE the safeguards discussion whenever facts smell of s.141/144/139.*

## One-line recall
- Audit value = **independent (fact + appearance) + enforceable principles**; credibility is a shared asset the *profession* polices.
- Five principles (I-O-C-C-P) bind **every** CA; **independence** binds only **assurance** — objectivity is internal/universal, independence is external/assurance-specific.
- Five threats = **self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity, intimidation** — classify by mechanism; often more than one.
- Safeguards scale with severity; management assurances / bigger fee / client importance are NOT safeguards; if none suffices → **remove / decline / resign**.
- Test statute FIRST: **s.141** (disqualifications), **s.144** (prohibited services, direct/indirect, not tax), **s.139(2)** (rotation 5 / 2×5+10, 5-yr cool-off).
- Breach = **professional misconduct** — **First Schedule** (less grave/Board of Discipline) vs **Second Schedule** (grave incl. gross negligence & confidentiality breach/Disciplinary Committee) → removal from Register + fine.
- *Verify all monetary thresholds, limits, % triggers, ceiling exclusions, penalties, and Code paragraph numbers against the latest ICAI Code, CA Act Schedules, and Companies (Audit & Auditors) Rules.*
