# Nature, Objective & Scope of Audit

## Snapshot
Owners supply capital; managers control it and self-report performance — a trust gap born of the agency problem, information asymmetry and stewardship. Audit inserts an independent, competent third party to examine the financial statements and express an opinion, giving reasonable (not absolute) assurance that they are true and fair.

## Core concepts
- **Why audit exists:** Agency problem (agent acts in own interest) + Information asymmetry (manager knows more) + Stewardship (must prove faithful custody) → trust gap → need independent assurance.
- **Users served (public-interest activity):** existing shareholders, prospective investors, lenders/banks, trade creditors, employees/unions, government & tax authorities, regulators (SEBI, RBI), public/customers. Auditor appointed by members but effectively serves a wider unnamed body of users.
- **The payer paradox:** the audited entity proposes, pays and fixes fee — "accused hires the judge." Law breaks the link: appointment by members in general meeting (Sec 139); removal before term needs special resolution + Central Government approval (Sec 140); remuneration fixed by members (Sec 142); scope set by statute + SAs (management cannot shrink it).
- **Definition (ICAI / SA 200):** An audit is the independent examination of financial information of any entity — profit-oriented or not, irrespective of size or legal form — conducted with a view to expressing an opinion thereon.
- **Audit is NOT:** (1) preparation of accounts (management prepares; auditor who writes books faces self-review threat); (2) a guarantee of accuracy; (3) assurance of future viability or management efficiency; (4) an investigation; (5) accountancy ("accounting ends where auditing begins").

**Audit vs Investigation:** Audit = express opinion on true & fair, general scope, governed by SAs/statute, materiality applies, one year, persuasive/sampled evidence, recurring. Investigation = establish a specific fact, limited/deep scope, governed by terms of engagement, materiality often ignored, may span years, conclusive/often 100% checking, one-off. Mnemonic: audit is broad and shallow with an opinion; investigation is narrow and deep with a conclusion.

**Assurance engagement — 5 elements:** (1) three-party relationship (practitioner, responsible party, intended users); (2) appropriate subject matter; (3) suitable criteria (applicable financial reporting framework — AS/Ind AS + Companies Act); (4) sufficient appropriate evidence; (5) written assurance report. Missing any element → not an assurance engagement (agreed-upon procedures & compilation are non-assurance).

**Assurance grades:**
| Type | Level | Wording | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reasonable | High, not absolute | Positive ("in our opinion... true and fair") | Statutory audit |
| Limited | Moderate, lower | Negative ("nothing has come to our attention...") | Review (SRE 2400/2410) |

**Why not absolute — inherent limitations (SA 200):** (1) nature of financial reporting — estimates/judgement, no single true number; (2) nature of audit evidence — persuasive not conclusive, sampling not 100%; (3) fraud — collusion, forged documents, management override designed to defeat procedures; (4) timeliness & cost — forces sampling and materiality judgement; (5) other areas — related parties, non-compliance with laws hard to detect. Inherent limitations are NOT a defence for negligence — they shield only a properly conducted, skeptical, documented audit. Subsequent discovery of misstatement does not by itself prove the audit was deficient (SA 200).

**Audit risk model:** AR = IR × CR × DR. Inherent Risk (susceptibility before controls) and Control Risk (controls fail) are the entity's risks — auditor only assesses. Detection Risk (auditor's own procedures fail) is the only leg the auditor controls; set DR inversely to IR×CR → more/better evidence where risk is higher. Reasonable assurance = drive AR to acceptably low, not zero.

**Management assertions (SA 315):**
- Transactions/events (P&L): Occurrence, Completeness, Accuracy, Cut-off, Classification.
- Account balances (BS): Existence, Rights & obligations, Completeness, Valuation & allocation.
- Presentation & disclosure: occurrence & rights/obligations, completeness, classification & understandability, accuracy & valuation.
- **Directional testing:** assets/income tend to be OVERSTATED → test Existence/Occurrence (vouch: entry back to evidence). Liabilities/expenses tend to be UNDERSTATED → test Completeness (trace: source forward into records; search for unrecorded liabilities). Debtors → existence/valuation; Creditors → completeness.

**True and fair (Sec 143(2)):** True = factually accurate, conforms to reality and framework, no material misstatement. Fair = presented/disclosed without bias, clear, not misleading, substance over form. Chosen over "true and correct" because estimates make arithmetical exactness impossible. Auditor's anchors: books kept per law + framework followed/departures disclosed; no material misstatement, no window dressing or secret reserves; assets/liabilities correctly valued & classified; material/unusual items and post-balance-sheet events disclosed; statutory disclosures (Schedule III) complied. Polices BOTH overstatement (window dressing) and understatement (secret reserves).

Guidance Notes = recommendatory (explain departure). **Companies Act anchors:** Sec 138 (internal audit) · 139 (appointment/rotation) · 140 (removal/resignation) · 141 (eligibility/disqualification) · 142 (remuneration) · 143 (powers, duties, reporting — true & fair u/s 143(2), IFC u/s 143(3)(i)) · 144 (prohibited services). CARO reporting also part of company audit scope.

**Objective wording (SA 200):** "free from MATERIAL misstatement" (materiality SA 320 filters — influences users' economic decisions) · "whether due to fraud OR error" (both; SA 240) · "true and fair view."

**Error vs Fraud:** Error = unintentional (arithmetic mistake, wrong estimate in good faith), easier to detect, ordinary procedures. Fraud = intentional, harder (concealed), heightened skepticism + SA 240 duties. Fraud = (a) fraudulent financial reporting (usually management, cook statements) and (b) misappropriation of assets (usually employees, theft, smaller). **Responsibility split:** prevention & detection of fraud/error = MANAGEMENT and those charged with governance (via internal control); auditor = obtain reasonable assurance about MATERIAL misstatement whether from fraud or error. Auditor is NOT responsible for preventing fraud or detecting all fraud.

**Scope determined by:** applicable financial reporting framework + relevant SAs + terms of engagement/statutory requirements. Organised to cover all relevant aspects; assess reliability & sufficiency of information & disclosure; use judgement; NOT restrictable by management (limitation → modified opinion SA 705); not a guarantee of viability or efficiency.
- Management-imposed limitation (refuses debtor confirmations/stock count): try alternatives; if material & pervasive → withdraw if legally possible, else disclaim (treated most severely — hints at concealment).
- Circumstance-imposed (appointed after year-end, records destroyed): try alternatives; if unavailable → qualify or disclaim per materiality/pervasiveness; no bad faith implied.

**Opinion outcomes (material AND pervasive both decide):** clean/unmodified (no problem, SA 700) · qualified "except for" (material, not pervasive) · disclaimer (scope limitation, material + pervasive) · adverse (disagreement, material + pervasive). Logic: evidence problem + pervasive = disclaimer; disagreement + pervasive = adverse; either if only material = qualified; neither = clean.

**Types of audit:** By law — statutory (Companies Act, Banking Regulation Act, tax audit u/s 44AB; scope/removal not curtailable by agreement) vs voluntary/non-statutory (scope by contract). By subject — financial, cost, management/operational, internal, government (C&AG), tax, special/forensic. By coverage — continuous, periodic/final, interim, balance-sheet.
- **Continuous audit** — Advantages: early error/fraud detection, moral check, quick finalisation, deep familiarity, even workload. Disadvantages: figures checked may be altered afterwards (central risk), audit trail disturbed, staff dependence, mechanical approach, collusion risk. Safeguards: secret ticks/markings, note important balances in audit notebook, instruct that checked figures not be altered.

**Statutory (external) vs Internal audit:** External — opinion on true & fair to owners/third parties, appointed by shareholders, reports to members, governed by Companies Act (139–147) + SAs, independent, scope not restrictable, mandatory for every company. Internal — improve operations/controls/risk for management, appointed by management/board/audit committee, reports to management, governed by Sec 138 + SIA, part of entity, scope set by management, mandatory only for prescribed classes.
- External auditor may use internal audit's work under **SA 610** only after evaluating competence, objectivity, systematic approach; retains sole/undivided responsibility; makes all significant judgements himself; the higher the risk/judgement, the less he can rely; cannot use it at all if objectivity/competence inadequate. "Use, but never abdicate."

**Ethics (ICAI Code / IESBA):** Integrity · Objectivity · Professional competence & due care · Confidentiality · Professional behaviour. Crowned by **Independence** — of mind (actual unbiased state) + in appearance (reasonable third party's view). Protected by Sec 141 (disqualification), 144 (non-audit services), 139 (rotation), 140 (tenure/removal).
- **Five threats:** Self-interest (shares/loan/overdue fee), Self-review (audit own earlier work/valuation), Advocacy (promoting client's position/litigation), Familiarity (relative in finance role, long association), Intimidation (threat of removal/litigation). Method: identify → evaluate significance → apply safeguards → if none suffice, decline/withdraw.

**Two mandatory mindsets (SA 200):** Professional skepticism — questioning mind, neutral (neither assume honesty nor dishonesty), critical assessment of evidence; "watchdog, not a bloodhound" (Kingston Cotton Mills) now raised by SA 240's active fraud duties; weigh evidence reliability by source (external > internal), nature (documentary > oral, original > photocopy), circumstances. Professional judgement — applying training/knowledge/experience to materiality, risk, procedures, evidence.

## Exam traps & must-remember
1. Audit "certifies/guarantees" accounts — WRONG: expresses an opinion, reasonable assurance. Watch words certify/guarantee/absolute/correct.
2. Confusing reasonable (high, positive) with limited (moderate, negative) assurance — instant mark loss.
3. Absolute assurance is "expensive but possible" — WRONG: inherently unattainable (estimates, persuasive evidence, concealed fraud).
4. "Management can limit scope" — WRONG: set by law + SAs; limitation → modified opinion.
5. True and fair = arithmetically accurate — WRONG: fairly presented within framework; polices window dressing AND secret reserves.
6. Internal vs statutory audit — check who appoints, reports to, and scope control; SA 610 does not reduce statutory auditor's responsibility.
7. "Audit guarantees company won't fail / is well managed" — WRONG: going concern (SA 570) is about basis and disclosure, not viability; efficiency is management/operational audit.
8. Forgetting "whether due to fraud OR error" — objectives cover both.
9. Independence treated as optional/ethics-only — it is structural and legally enforced; analyse via the five threats.
10. Listing SAs with wrong numbers — state principle and flag "confirm exact SA number."
11. "Auditor responsible for detecting all fraud" — WRONG: prevention/detection = management; auditor = reasonable assurance re material misstatement.
12. Materiality is only about size — WRONG: a tiny amount is qualitatively material if it flips profit/loss, breaches a covenant, or involves fraud/related parties/statutory disclosure.
13. Jumping to "qualify" for every scope limitation — first ask source (management vs circumstance) and attempt alternative procedures; qualification/disclaimer is last resort.
14. Mixing pronouncements — SA = audit, SRE = review, SAE = other assurance, SRS = related/non-assurance, SQC 1 = firm-level quality.
15. Confusing audit with investigation — audit general/materiality-bounded/opinion vs investigation specific/100% check/conclusion.

## One-line recall
- Owners can't verify managers → independent expert gives reasonable (not absolute) assurance that FS are true and fair — an opinion, not a guarantee.
- SA 200: reasonable assurance FS free from material misstatement (fraud or error) + report; prevention/detection = management, reasonable assurance = auditor.
- AR = IR × CR × DR; auditor controls only DR, set inversely to IR×CR.
- Directional testing: assets/income → existence (overstatement); liabilities/expenses → completeness (understatement).
- Scope not restrictable by management; opinion = clean / qualified (material not pervasive) / disclaimer (scope limit, material+pervasive) / adverse (disagreement, material+pervasive).
- Five independence threats: self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity, intimidation → identify, evaluate, safeguard, else decline.
