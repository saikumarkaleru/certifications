# Chapter 11 — SM: Analysis of the External Environment

## Snapshot
Opportunities and threats arise ENTIRELY OUTSIDE the firm; strengths and weaknesses are inside. Strategy = matching the firm to an uncontrollable environment. Environmental scanning (map-making step) converts raw external facts into just two usable outputs: opportunities to exploit and threats to defend against. Anything that becomes neither is noise. Scanned in layers, far to near: macro (PESTLE) → industry (Porter's Five Forces) → strategic groups → individual competitor. Use a telescope for the far layer, a microscope for the near.

## Core concepts

**Environmental scanning** — process of monitoring, evaluating and disseminating information from the external (and internal) environment to key people. Purpose: EARLY WARNING of threats and EARLY SIGHTING of opportunities, so the firm responds before change fully arrives.

**Two failure modes (symmetrical):**
- Under-scanning (blind spot) → missed threat/opportunity, strategic surprise.
- Over-scanning (analysis paralysis) → cost, delay, inertia.
- Correct: scan SELECTIVELY — monitor strategically relevant environment intensely, the rest lightly.

**What a scan looks for (event → trend → issue → expectation ladder — a maturity sequence; read early):**
1. Events — important specific occurrences (new tax, product launch).
2. Trends — general direction events move in.
3. Issues — concern/debate arising from trends.
4. Expectations — demands of interested groups/stakeholders.

**Environment split:**
- Micro (task/operating) — actors close to firm: suppliers, customers, competitors, market intermediaries, financiers, public. Firm-specific, partly influenceable.
- Macro (general/remote) — broad forces on ALL firms: PESTLE. Uncontrollable, adapt only.

**Three tests to place a factor (macro vs micro):**
1. "Everyone" test — affects all firms = macro; specific to your sector/firm = micro.
2. "Controllability" test — can only adapt = macro; can influence = micro.
3. "Named actor" test — impersonal force (technology, demographics) = macro; named actor you transact with (supplier, buyer) = micro.

**Internal vs external is a separate (orthogonal) cut:** internal factors → Strengths/Weaknesses; external (macro+micro) → Opportunities/Threats. Trap: an internal factor ("skilled workforce") is a strength, NEVER an opportunity.

**Four scanning modes (broad-cheap → deep-costly):** undirected viewing → conditioned viewing → informal search → formal search. Escalate selectively.

## Key frameworks / models

### PESTLE (macro checklist — coverage, not calculation; evolved from Aguilar's ETPS → PEST → PESTLE; STEEPLE adds Ethical)

| Letter | Factor | Covers / signals |
|---|---|---|
| P | Political | Govt stability, ideology, centre-state relations, taxation, foreign-trade rules, "Make in India", govt attitude — the rules of the game / intent |
| E | Economic | GDP, inflation, interest & exchange rates, disposable income, credit, business cycle, fiscal & monetary policy — size/health of demand, cost of capital |
| S | Social/Socio-cultural | Demographics (age, urbanisation), lifestyle, education, attitudes, tastes, values, family structure — what people want and how many; slow, deadly if missed |
| T | Technological | Innovation rate, R&D, automation, new methods, obsolescence, digital disruption — obsolescence threat + new-product opportunity; fastest force |
| L | Legal | Company/competition/consumer/labour/environmental law, contracts, IPR, product safety — codified enforceable rules; compliance risk |
| E | Environmental/Ecological | Climate, pollution norms, carbon regulation, resources, sustainability, waste rules — green constraints and opportunities |

- Political vs Legal: Political = govt's STANCE/INTENT/discretion (what it wants/might do); Legal = CODIFIED enforceable rules already on statute book (what law already requires). "Govt is pro-manufacturing" = Political; "must comply with Factories Act" = Legal.
- How to use: list factors per head → assess direction & impact → classify O or T FOR THIS FIRM → judge timing/importance → feed into SWOT.
- Two-axis screen (importance × probability): High impact + high probability = act now; high impact + low probability = contingency plan; low impact + high probability = monitor; low + low = ignore.
- Same fact, opposite verdict (never state O/T without naming the firm): rising interest rates — threat to developer, opportunity to bank/NBFC; ageing population — threat to toymaker, opportunity to pharma; weak rupee — threat to importer, opportunity to IT exporter; drought — threat to rural FMCG, opportunity to irrigation-pump maker.

### Porter's Five Forces (industry attractiveness — Michael Porter, 1980, *Competitive Strategy*)
Industry profit is a value pool leaking in five structural directions: upstream (suppliers), downstream (buyers), sideways (rivals), capped (substitutes), diluted (new entrants). Four forces press in on the central rivalry.

- **Threat of new entrants** — bring capacity, want share, cut prices. Held back by BARRIERS TO ENTRY: economies of scale, capital requirements, product differentiation/brand loyalty, switching costs, access to distribution, government policy/licensing, cost advantages independent of scale (patents, tech, location, experience curve), expected incumbent retaliation. High barriers → low threat → high profit. It is the THREAT (contestability), not actual entry, that disciplines prices.
- **Bargaining power of suppliers** — powerful when: few/concentrated, input critical/no substitute, high switching costs, can threaten forward integration, industry is unimportant customer. Value leaks upstream. LABOUR (employees/unions) counts as a supplier.
- **Bargaining power of buyers** — powerful when: few/large-volume, product standardised, low switching costs, buyers earn low profits (price-sensitive), can threaten backward integration, product unimportant to their quality or large share of their cost. Needs BOTH price sensitivity AND bargaining leverage.
- **Threat of substitutes** — products from OTHER industries meeting the same need (tea vs coffee, video-conf vs air travel). Place a CEILING on price. High when better price-performance + low switching cost. Most-forgotten force; includes "do nothing" and "make in-house".
- **Competitive rivalry (central force)** — intense when: many/balanced competitors, slow growth, high fixed/storage costs, no differentiation/low switching costs, capacity added in large increments, diverse competitors, high strategic stakes, HIGH EXIT BARRIERS. Worst structural cell = HIGH ENTRY + HIGH EXIT barriers (chronic overcapacity, price wars — steel, airlines).

Golden rule: strong forces → LOW profit; weak forces → HIGH profit. Attractive industry = WEAK forces. Strategy: (a) position where forces weakest, (b) exploit change first, (c) reshape forces (raise switching costs, build scale). Read the TREND, not just the snapshot.

Limitations: static snapshot; understates cooperation/complementors; assumes clear industry boundaries; under-weights firm's unique resources (RBV critique). Sixth force = COMPLEMENTORS (Andrew Grove) — opposite of a substitute, raises your product's value; cooperative strategy. ICAI = classical FIVE forces; complementor is an extension only.

### Competitor analysis (Porter — four components; motivation × ability matrix)
- **Future goals** — what drives it (growth/cash/prestige); content or hungry.
- **Current strategy** — what it does now; baseline to out-manoeuvre.
- **Assumptions** — beliefs about itself/industry; wrong assumptions = exploitable BLIND SPOTS (highest-value, hardest to observe).
- **Capabilities** — genuine strengths/weaknesses; limits on response.

Goals + Assumptions = motivation/perception; Strategy + Capabilities = action/ability. Answers: Is rival satisfied? What moves will it make? Where vulnerable? What provokes fierce retaliation? Intelligence sources must be LEGAL/ETHICAL (financials, filings, teardowns, job postings, patents, statements — no espionage).

### Strategic groups
Clusters of firms following SIMILAR strategies (price/quality, product-line breadth, geographic reach, distribution, vertical integration). Strategic group MAP plots firms on two dimensions.
- Closest rivals = firms WITHIN your group; rivalry fiercest within a group.
- MOBILITY BARRIERS block movement between groups (like entry barriers inside an industry).
- Empty cells = POSSIBLE white space — but check WHY empty (may be structurally unprofitable).
- Five forces press on different groups with different intensity.
- Choose two axes that are strategically important AND uncorrelated (else firms line up on a diagonal, revealing nothing).

## Exam traps & must-remember
1. Describing instead of implying — always convert PESTLE factor / force into firm-specific O/T or profit effect.
2. Forgetting substitutes (most-missed force); include "not buying" and "in-house".
3. Rivalry vs substitutes: rivalry = SAME product (Pepsi vs Coke); substitutes = DIFFERENT product, same need (juice vs cola).
4. New entrants (potential/future, kept out by barriers) vs existing rivals (current competition).
5. Profitability logic: strong forces = LOW profit; weak = HIGH. Attractive = weak forces (students invert this).
6. PESTLE Political (stance/intent) vs Legal (enforceable laws); PESTLE is MACRO — suppliers/customers/competitors are micro (Five Forces, not PESTLE).
7. Environment has no fixed meaning — same factor is O for one firm, T for another ("recession threat — to whom?").
8. Authors: Five Forces, generic strategies, competitor 4-components, strategic groups = Michael Porter (1980). PESTLE ← Aguilar's ETPS. Do not credit Ansoff/Mintzberg.
9. High barriers PROTECT incumbents and DETER entrants (reduce the threat) — don't reverse direction.
10. Firm can RESHAPE forces — not a fatalistic verdict.
11. Never put an internal factor (workforce, brand, balance sheet) into the external scan — those are S/W.
12. Read dynamics/trend, not just a snapshot (deregulation, consolidation, new tech move the forces).
13. Weight forces unequally — identify the DOMINANT one (prioritisation = depth signal).
14. Empty strategic-group cell may be structurally unprofitable, not overlooked white space.
15. Substitute (caps your price) vs complementor (raises your value) — opposite directions.

Connections: External analysis = the O and T half of SWOT (internal = S and W). Scanning = stage two of the strategic-management process (after vision/mission/objectives, before strategy formulation). Five Forces → leads into Porter's generic strategies (cost leadership, differentiation, focus) as the RESPONSES. Strong forces justify a higher discount rate / conservative forecasts in FM capital budgeting.

## One-line recall
- O and T come from OUTSIDE; S and W from inside — never swap them.
- Layers, outer to inner: PESTLE → Five Forces → strategic groups → competitor analysis, each ending in an O/T or profit implication.
- PESTLE = completeness checklist (P-E-S-T-L-E); Political = intent, Legal = codified law.
- Five Forces (Porter 1980): new entrants, suppliers, buyers, substitutes, rivalry — STRONG forces = LOW profit; worst cell = high entry + high exit barriers.
- Competitor analysis = goals + strategy + assumptions + capabilities (motivation × ability); attack the wrong assumption.
- Analysis is worthless until converted into a strategic implication — an opportunity to seize or a threat to defend.
