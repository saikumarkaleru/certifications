# Types of Financing & Financial Markets

## Snapshot
The financing decision picks the mix of sources leaving shareholders richest and firm safest. Every source carries four attributes: **Cost, Risk, Control, Maturity**. Financing merely *slices* the pie — it changes firm value only through frictions: tax shield, distress/bankruptcy cost, flotation cost, information asymmetry, agency cost.

## Core concepts
- **Queue of claims:** Debt (first, lowest cost, tax-deductible) → Preference (middle) → Equity (residual, last, highest cost). Higher risk demands higher return.
- **Equity is the MOST expensive source**, not the cheapest (16-18% vs 9-11% debt); return arrives as expected dividends + capital gains, not a cheque.
- **3 reasons debt is cheaper:** (i) priority of claim (lower risk); (ii) security/charge on assets; (iii) **tax shield** (pure govt subsidy, not risk) → Kd = i × (1 − t). Shield worthless if no taxable profit.
- **Classification lenses:** by maturity (long/medium/short), ownership (owners' vs borrowed), origin (internal vs external), effort (negotiated vs spontaneous).

## Key provisions / rules
**Long-term owners' funds:** Equity (permanent, votes/control, dilutes, highest cost, no tax deduction, no charge) · Preference (fixed dividend before equity, priority in repayment, usually no vote — regain if unpaid 2 yrs; **redeemable within 20 yrs** under Companies Act 2013, infra excepted; cumulative default) · Retained earnings (internal; opportunity cost = shareholders' required return, NOT free; marginally cheaper than fresh equity only by saving flotation cost).
- Capital order: Authorised ≥ Issued ≥ Subscribed ≥ Called-up ≥ Paid-up. Dividends/bonus on **face** value. Securities premium = capital reserve, not distributable.
- **Bonus issue** raises NO new cash (capitalises reserves); **rights issue** does.

**Long-term borrowed:** Debentures (fixed interest paid regardless of profit, tax-deductible = cheapest; no vote; often secured; FCD/PCD/NCD, zero-coupon at discount) · Term loans (covenants, secured) · Venture capital (new high-risk ventures; stages: Seed → Start-up → Early → Expansion → Later/Mezzanine/Bridge; demands 30-50%+ for portfolio failures) · ADR (US equity) / GDR (non-US equity) / ECB (foreign-currency **debt**, currency risk) / FDI (control) vs FII/FPI (portfolio) / Securitisation (off-balance-sheet).
- Fixed charge (specific asset) ranks ahead of floating charge (changing pool, crystallises on default) on charged assets.

**Medium-term:** Lease (lessor owns & claims depreciation; lessee deducts rental only) — **Finance/Capital lease** (non-cancellable, transfers risks/rewards, on lessee's books) vs **Operating lease** (cancellable, lessor bears obsolescence). Sale-and-lease-back. Hire purchase (ownership transfers after last instalment; hirer claims depreciation, deducts interest part only). Public deposits (6mth-3yr).

**Short-term:** Trade credit (spontaneous, free unless discount forgone) · Cash credit (against drawing power on hypothecated stock) · Overdraft (fluctuating gaps) · Bills discounting · Letter of credit · Commercial Paper (unsecured PN, 7d-1yr, at discount, high-rated only) · Factoring (sell receivables; with/without recourse; Forfaiting = export, always without recourse) · Accruals (wages/tax payable — cheapest, zero cost, non-manoeuvrable).

| Purpose | Formula |
|---|---|
| After-tax cost of debt | Kd = Interest rate × (1 − t) |
| EPS (financing mix) | EPS = [(EBIT − Interest) × (1 − t) − Pref. Div] ÷ No. of equity shares |
| Favourable leverage | Return on capital (EBIT÷Total capital) > fixed-charge rate |
| Indifference EBIT | set EPS(Plan1) = EPS(Plan2), solve |
| Lease vs Buy | choose lower **PV of after-tax cash outflows** |
| Buy outflow/yr | after-tax interest + principal − depreciation tax shield (− salvage) |
| Depreciation tax shield | Depreciation × t |
| After-tax lease rental | Lease rent × (1 − t) |
| Cost of forgoing discount | [d ÷ (100 − d)] × [365 ÷ (Credit period − Discount period)] |
| Ex-rights price | (N × Cum-rights price + Issue price) ÷ (N + 1) |
| Value of a right | (Cum-rights price − Issue price) ÷ (N + 1) |

**Markets:** Money market (≤1 yr, RBI; T-Bills [Govt, zero-coupon], CP [companies], CD [banks], Call money [≤1 day]/Notice [2-14 days], Repos, Commercial Bills). Capital market (>1 yr, SEBI; shares/debentures/bonds). Primary = new issues, **company gets cash** (IPO/FPO/Rights/Private placement/Preferential). Secondary = existing securities, no cash to firm but provides **liquidity + price discovery** (which cheapens future primary fund-raising).

**Matching (hedging) principle:** match maturity of source to life of asset. Permanent (core) current assets → long-term funds; temporary (seasonal) → short-term. **Aggressive** (short funds also fund permanent needs: high return/high liquidity risk) · **Hedging** (balanced) · **Conservative** (long funds fund temporary: low return, idle cost/over-capitalisation).

## Worked mini-example
Trade credit "2/15, net 60", purchases ₹40,00,000/yr, bank rate 14%.
Cost = [2 ÷ 98] × [365 ÷ (60−15)] = 0.020408 × 8.111 = **16.55% p.a.** > 14% → **take the discount, borrow if needed**. Rupee gain ≈ discount 2%×40,00,000 = ₹80,000 − interest (≈39,20,000×14%×45/365 ≈ ₹67,660) ≈ **₹12,340/yr**.

## Exam traps & must-remember
1. "Equity is free/cheapest" — wrong, it's **most expensive** (no contractual cheque ≠ free).
2. Retained earnings have **opportunity cost** = Ke; only marginally cheaper via saved flotation.
3. Cost of debt = **after-tax**; preference dividend gets **no** shield; shield worthless without taxable profit.
4. Preference dividend deducted **after** tax (appropriation of PAT); interest before tax.
5. Company gets cash only in **primary** market; secondary still matters (liquidity/price discovery).
6. Money vs capital market cut-off = **1 year**. CP & CD are money-market (not capital); T-Bills = Govt/RBI.
7. Hire purchase → ownership transfers; lease → lessor keeps ownership. Depreciation claimed by owner.
8. Matching: long assets↔long funds. Short-fund-long-asset = liquidity risk; long-fund-short-asset = idle cost.
9. "More debt always raises EPS" — only above indifference EBIT (favourable leverage); below it EPS falls/negative.
10. Bonus issue = no new cash; rights issue = cash.
11. Redeemable preference within **20 years** (infra excepted); no irredeemable preference in India.
12. Trade credit "free" only until discount forgone (~16.5% for 2/15 net 60).
13. ADR/GDR = equity abroad; **ECB = debt + currency risk**.
14. Skipped cumulative preference dividend = **arrears**; skipped debenture interest = **default/insolvency**.
15. Without-recourse factoring: bad-debt risk moves to factor (bad-debt saving only here).
16. Ignoring a right forfeits its value; rights issue wealth-neutral only if you act.

## One-line recall
- Four attributes: cost, risk, control, maturity; financing only slices the pie (frictions give it teeth).
- Queue: debt < preference < equity in cost; debt cheapest via priority + security + tax shield.
- Kd = i(1−t); EPS = [(EBIT−I)(1−t) − Pref div] ÷ shares.
- Match source maturity to asset life; aggressive/hedging/conservative spectrum.
- Money market ≤1yr (RBI); capital market >1yr (SEBI); cash only in primary market.
- Factor/take-discount/lease decisions: compare cost vs benefit in PV terms.
