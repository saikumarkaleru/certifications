# Chapter 10 — SM: Introduction to Strategic Management

## Snapshot
Strategy is the child of scarcity, uncertainty, and rivalry (plus irreversibility of big commitments). FM asks "are we doing the thing right?" (efficiency); SM asks "are we doing the right thing?" (effectiveness). Strategy directs scarce resources toward a desirable competitive future, under uncertainty, while making a crowd of people pull one way — through commitments that are hard to reverse. Core chain: Vision → Mission → Objectives → Strategy → Tactics, with Values guarding every rung; three nested levels (corporate/business/functional); the prize is competitive advantage; the will is strategic intent; the machine is a four-stage loop.

## Core concepts

**Strategy (Chandler, 1962):** determination of the basic long-term goals and objectives + adoption of courses of action + allocation of resources to carry them out. Bundles goals + action + resources.
- **Strategy is NOT:** forecasting (predicts vs decides/commits); a to-do list/budget (operational); efficiency. It IS effectiveness and **trade-offs** — choosing to be different means choosing what NOT to do.
- Two lenses (Mintzberg): **intended/deliberate** (planned) vs **emergent/realised** (pattern that actually shows up).

**Strategic Management:** set of managerial decisions and actions determining an organisation's long-run performance. Four activities: environmental scanning, formulation, implementation, evaluation & control.
- **Three enduring questions:** Where are we now? / Where do we want to go? / How do we get there?
- **Benefits:** direction; prepares for future/reduces surprise; rational allocation of scarce resources; builds competitive advantage; coordination & control; makes firm proactive not reactive.
- **Limitations:** turbulent/unpredictable environment; time- and cost-intensive; rigidity can blind to change; hard to forecast accurately; implementation harder than formulation; false certainty/over-commitment; demands top-management time and skill.
- **SM vs Strategic Planning:** planning = formulation only (analyse, forecast, produce the plan); management = formulation + implementation + evaluation & control, continuous and firm-wide. Planning stops at the plan; management carries it through.

**The hierarchy of purpose (top-down for meaning; bottom-up for feasibility via the means–ends chain — each level is an end for the one below, a means for the one above). Horizons telescope/nest.**

| Rung | Question | Horizon | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vision | What to become? | Very long (aspirational) | Inspirational, future |
| Mission | What business now / why exist? | Present, ongoing | Identity |
| Objectives/Goals | What results, by when? | Medium (1–5 yrs) | Measurable targets |
| Strategy | How deploy resources vs rivals? | Medium–long | Method |
| Tactics/Plans/Policies | What daily actions? | Short | Operational |

- **Values** = ethical guard-rails constraining every rung simultaneously; a real value costs a profit to uphold (else it is a slogan).
- **Vision:** desired future state — clear, inspiring, forward-looking, memorable, stretching; NOT measurable; sets a boundary that rejects off-course ventures. A pure forecast is not a vision.
- **Mission (ICAI content):** covers basic product/service & markets; customers; principal technology; philosophy/self-concept/public image. Feasible, precise, clear, motivating, distinctive. Broad enough to grow, specific enough for identity. Define by **need served, not product made** (Levitt's marketing myopia — railroads vs "transportation") — but don't over-broaden (Goldilocks band).
- **Objectives vs Goals:** goals = broad, qualitative, open-ended direction; objectives = specific, measurable, time-bound milestones. Objective characteristics: specific & measurable; realistic yet challenging; time-bound; consistent & hierarchical; understandable & acceptable. Dark side: tunnel vision, gaming/short-termism, conflicting objectives — fix via balance & consistency.

## Key frameworks / models

**Mintzberg's full vocabulary:** Intended (planned) · Deliberate (intended AND implemented) · Emergent (arose unplanned) · Realised = deliberate + emergent · Unrealised (intended but abandoned). Line: "Realised strategy is rarely the same as intended; the difference = unrealised intentions dropped + emergent patterns picked up."

**Mintzberg's 5 Ps:** Plan (intended course) · Ploy (manoeuvre to outwit a rival) · Pattern (consistency over time = emergent) · Position (locating firm in market) · Perspective (firm's ingrained way of seeing).

**Levels of strategy (classify by SCOPE, not department keyword):**

| Level | Decided by | Question | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate | Top mgmt/board | Which businesses to be in? | Portfolio, scope, diversification, resource split across units (parenting) |
| Business (SBU) | SBU heads | How to win in THIS market? | Competitive advantage in one industry |
| Functional | Dept heads | How does each function support? | Resource productivity + alignment within a function |

- **SBU** = distinct division with its own mission, own competitors, own market/P&L, plannable independently. Levels must nest (lower serves higher). **Single-business firm → corporate and business levels coincide.**

**Competitive advantage:** superior value to customers, or comparable value at lower cost, in a way rivals find hard to imitate → above-average returns.
- **Porter's generic routes:** cost leadership · differentiation · focus (either route in a niche). Must choose one — a firm being both cheapest and most differentiated is **"stuck in the middle"** and earns below-average returns.
- **Sustainable** advantage = protected by barriers to imitation. **VRIN/VRIO:** Valuable (helps exploit opportunity/neutralise threat) · Rare (else only parity) · Inimitable & non-substitutable (makes it last) · Organised to exploit / Non-substitutable (fourth condition, both formulations exist). Insight: Valuable + Rare gives advantage; Inimitable makes it last; Organised lets you capture it. Fast-moving industries: sustainable asset shifts from product to a renewal capability (string of temporary advantages).

**Strategic intent (Hamel & Prahalad, 1989):** obsessive, long-term ambition to achieve a leadership position, beyond current resources, driving innovation and capability-building. Expressed THROUGH vision + mission + objectives. Characteristics: captures essence of winning; stable over time; sets a stretch target; engages emotions/energy of all employees. Examples: Canon vs Xerox, Komatsu vs Caterpillar.
- **Fit vs Stretch:** Fit (traditional) = start from current resources, choose strategies they support (efficient within limits; self-limiting). Stretch/intent = start from ambition beyond means, leverage resources to close the gap (expands limits). Fit suits resource-rich mature firms; stretch suits resource-poor challengers.
- **Resource leverage (closes the gap):** Concentrate · Accumulate · Complement · Conserve · Recover. Intent without a leverage plan is fantasy.

**Strategic management process (a LOOP, not a line):**
1. **Environmental/Strategic Appraisal (Scanning)** — external O/T + internal S/W (SWOT, PESTLE live here). "Where are we now?"
2. **Strategy Formulation** — set vision/mission/objectives + design corporate/business/functional strategies. "Where do we go?" *Effectiveness.*
3. **Strategy Implementation** — resources, structure, systems, policies, leadership, culture, change. **Where most strategies fail.** *Efficiency.*
4. **Evaluation & Control** — set standards, measure, correct → **feeds back into Stage 1**.

**Formulation vs Implementation:**

| | Formulation | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Positioning before action | Managing during action |
| Focus | Effectiveness (right things) | Efficiency (things right) |
| Activity | Intellectual, analytical | Operational, motivational |
| Skills | Analysis, judgement | Leadership, coordination |
| Requires | Few strategists at top | Many people across firm |

Deep point: a mediocre strategy well implemented usually beats a brilliant strategy poorly implemented.

**Strategy vs Tactics:** strategy = overall, long-horizon, top-level, hard to reverse ("win the war"); tactics = short-horizon, specific, lower-level, adjustable ("win each battle").

**Strategic vs Operational decision:** strategic = long-horizon, resource-committing & largely irreversible, top-management, directional/holistic (sets the frame), environment-facing, complex/non-routine. Operational = short, reversible, low-level, repetitive, works within the frame. Test: does it commit big resources over a long horizon in a way that shapes the arena for smaller choices?

## Exam traps & must-remember
- **Vision ↔ Mission swap:** Vision = future "become"; Mission = present "business/why." Anything measurable/dated = **objective** (never a vision, e.g. "₹800 cr by 2028").
- **Strategy ≠ long-term planning** (planning assumes knowable future; strategy handles uncertainty + emergent). And SM ≠ strategic planning.
- **Effectiveness vs efficiency:** strategy/formulation = right things; operations/implementation = things right. Both can appear in one correct answer.
- **Levels: classify by SCOPE, not the department name.** "Marketing" can be corporate, business, or functional.
- **Always show the feedback loop** in the process (line diagram loses marks).
- **Strategic intent ≠ vision** — intent is the stretching will to win, expressed through vision/mission/objectives; attribute to Hamel & Prahalad; stress stretch + resource leverage.
- **Name the author** — ICAI rewards attribution.
- **Give benefits AND limitations** when asked to "discuss."
- **Values are not decoration** — name them; a real value would cost a profit.
- **Don't over-apply Levitt** — too-broad mission loses identity (Goldilocks band).
- **Don't force three levels on a single-business firm** (top two coincide).
- **Spot emergent strategy** — divergence from plan due to market opportunity is emergent, not a planning failure; realised = deliberate + emergent.
- **Not every big-spend decision is strategic** — test horizon + irreversibility + frame-setting.
- **Author roster:** Chandler (strategy definition; structure follows strategy) · Mintzberg (deliberate vs emergent; 5 Ps) · Porter (competitive advantage/generic strategies/five forces/stuck-in-the-middle) · Hamel & Prahalad (strategic intent, resource leverage, core competence) · Levitt (marketing myopia).
- **Answer format (4–6 marks):** name concept + author + why it exists + one-line example.

## One-line recall
- Strategy = scarcity + uncertainty + rivalry + irreversibility → one deliberate, adjustable direction; it is trade-offs (what NOT to do).
- Hierarchy: Vision (become) → Mission (present business) → Objectives (measurable/dated) → Strategy → Tactics; Values guard every rung.
- Three nested levels — corporate (which businesses), business/SBU (how to win here), functional (how each dept supports); classify by scope.
- Competitive advantage = valuable + hard-to-imitate → above-average returns; sustainable via VRIN; routes = cost leadership/differentiation/focus, avoid stuck-in-the-middle.
- Strategic intent (Hamel & Prahalad) = stretch ambition beyond resources, closed by resource leverage; Fit (match to means) vs Stretch (grow the means); realised = deliberate + emergent (Mintzberg).
- Process = appraise → formulate → implement → evaluate, feeding back; formulation chooses right things, implementation (where most fail) does them right.
