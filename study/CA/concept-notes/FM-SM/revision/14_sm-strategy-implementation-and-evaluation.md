# SM Chapter 14 — Strategy Implementation & Evaluation

## Snapshot
- A brilliant strategy that is not executed is worth less than a mediocre one that is. Most strategies fail in execution, not choice — the **strategy-execution gap**. Failure occurs at the *weakest link*, not the average.
- Four managerial questions → four tool families: resistance → change management (Lewin); wrong org chart → structure follows strategy (Chandler); culture/skills pull old way → McKinsey 7S; flying blind → strategic control + Balanced Scorecard.
- Formulation = judgement under uncertainty (once); implementation = coordination under interdependence (continuous). Implementation, control and evaluation run **simultaneously** as overlapping loops — strategy is a cycle, not a line. Strategic management is NOT a one-time exercise.

## Core concepts

**Formulation vs Implementation (memorise the contrast):**
| Dimension | Formulation | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Positioning forces *before* action | Managing forces *during* action |
| Focus | Effectiveness (right things) | Efficiency (things right) |
| Process | Intellectual / analytical | Operational / administrative |
| Skills | Analytical, conceptual | Motivational, leadership, integrating |
| People | Few, at the top | Many, across all levels |

- They are *interrelated but not identical* — analytically separable, operationally intertwined. Implementation reality can force reformulation.
- **Forward linkage:** strategy design shapes execution (bold strategy → more change). **Backward linkage:** existing structure/systems/resources constrain which strategies are feasible.
- **Implementation activities:** institutionalising the strategy; resource allocation; structuring; behavioural implementation (leadership, culture, change); functional/operational implementation (plans, policies, procedures per function).
- **Six barriers to implementation:** (i) vague communication; (ii) resources still fund old priorities; (iii) rewards tied to old behaviour; (iv) structure misfits strategy; (v) leadership talks but doesn't model change; (vi) culture contradicts strategy.

## Key frameworks / models

### 1. Lewin's Three-Stage Change Model
1. **Unfreeze** — create motivation; challenge status quo ("burning platform"); reduce forces holding old behaviour.
2. **Move / Change** — implement new behaviours, structures, systems, skills; training, quick wins, support.
3. **Refreeze** — lock in via revised rewards, SOPs, culture, structure. Change not refrozen decays.

- **Force-field logic:** behaviour held by *driving forces* (push change) vs *restraining forces* (hold status quo). Leverage = *reduce restraints*, not crank up driving force (that only raises tension and rebounds).
- **Sources of resistance:** fear of unknown; loss of self-interest (pay/status/power/security); distrust of management; different perceptions; social/peer pressure; low tolerance for change. Diagnose source before choosing tactic.
- **Kotter & Schlesinger — six methods (slow/durable → fast/resented):** (1) Education & communication (misinformation); (2) Participation & involvement (initiators lack info, others can resist — builds ownership); (3) Facilitation & support (fear/adjustment); (4) Negotiation & agreement (someone clearly loses & has power); (5) Manipulation & co-optation (covert, symbolic role — backfire risk); (6) Coercion (force/threat — last resort). Move *down* the list only as speed pressure and power imbalance rise.
- **Kotter 8-step = operationalises Lewin:** steps 1-4 (urgency, coalition, vision, communicate) = Unfreeze; steps 5-7 (empower/remove obstacles, short-term wins, consolidate) = Move; step 8 (anchor in culture) = Refreeze. Step 6 short-term wins keep momentum.
- Resistance reduced most by **participation** — people support what they help create.

### 2. Structure Follows Strategy (Chandler)
- Studied DuPont, GM, Sears, Standard Oil: change strategy → old structure misfits → inefficiency/administrative problems → performance declines → redesign structure → performance restored.
- **Two-way truth:** dominant proposition = "structure follows strategy" (state cleanly first); nuance = structure can subsequently *constrain* strategy (feedback, not one-way). Never lead with the nuance.
- **Structural types:**
| Structure | Best fit | Key trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Simple/Entrepreneurial | Small, single owner, one product | Fast/flexible; overloads owner; fails on growth |
| Functional | Single/dominant product, stable | Efficient/specialised; silos, poor coordination |
| Divisional (product/geo/market) | Diversified products/regions/customers | Accountability/focus; resource duplication |
| SBU | Large, highly diversified, many divisions | Groups related divisions; adds a layer |
| Matrix | Multiple projects needing shared specialists | Dual expertise; dual-reporting conflict |
| Network/Virtual/Hourglass | Outsource non-core; flat, IT-enabled | Lean/adaptive; partner dependence, less control |
- **Trade-off lens:** *specialisation* (functional — experts together) vs *coordination* (divisional — everything a customer needs under one boss). Matrix chases both → two-boss problem. Derive: single product + stable → functional; many products → divisional.
- **Trigger to leave functional:** diversification-driven overload at the apex (CEO becomes bottleneck).
- **SBU characteristics:** distinct business; own set of competitors; manager accountable for strategic planning & profit. Restores span-of-control; cost = extra layer, lost cross-SBU economies.
- **Hourglass:** IT + BPR thin the middle-management layer; broad top + broad bottom, narrow middle. Benefits: lower cost, faster communication, empowered lower levels. Cost: fewer promotions, overloaded surviving middle.
- **Network/Virtual:** keep only core competence, outsource everything else via IT web. Max lean/flexible; surrenders control, partner-dependent. (Hourglass *thins* middle in-house; network *externalises* activities.)

### 3. Leadership & Culture
- **Strategic leadership:** anticipate, envision, stay flexible, think strategically, initiate change for a viable future.
- **Transformational** (charisma, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration) → changes hearts → powers *Unfreeze/Move*.
- **Transactional** (contingent reward, management-by-exception) → steady execution → powers *Refreeze*.
- Leader's two roles: **change agent** (visible sponsor — vision, resources, removes obstacles) and **culture-builder** (shapes values via what is rewarded/modelled). CEO = "chief implementation officer"; missing leader-sponsor is the top predictor of failure.
- **Culture** = shared values/beliefs/assumptions/norms — "culture eats strategy for breakfast." Active variable, not backdrop. Shaped via rewarded behaviours, stories/heroes, rituals/symbols, recruitment/socialisation, reward system.
- **Strategy-culture compatibility matrix** (importance of change × compatibility with culture): (1) **Manage around** — important but mildly incompatible (reroute via structure/procedures); (2) **Reinforce** — important + compatible (easiest; ride culture); (3) **Change culture** — vital + strongly opposed (hardest, slowest, last resort); (4) **Tolerate/reformulate** — minor + incompatible.

### 4. McKinsey 7S (Peters, Waterman, + Pascale, Athos)
Alignment of seven interdependent elements = successful implementation; change one, realign the rest.
- **Hard (tangible, changeable by decree, fast):** Strategy (build competitive advantage); Structure (who reports to whom); Systems (daily procedures, processes, info flows — budgeting, IT, appraisal).
- **Soft (intangible, live in habits, slow, decisive):** Shared Values (originally Superordinate Goals — core beliefs/culture at the *centre*, the glue); Style (leadership/management behaviour); Staff (people — numbers, recruitment, development, motivation); Skills (organisation's collective distinctive competencies).
- **Core trap:** managers change the easy *hard* S's, declare victory, and stall because the decisive *soft* S's never moved. Excellent companies win on the soft S's.
- **Staff ≠ Skills:** Staff = people as resources; Skills = what the firm *as a whole* is distinctively good at (survives turnover). **Skills ≠ Systems:** Systems = formal procedures; Skills = competencies.
- **Diagnostic:** list current vs desired state of each S → find gaps/conflicts; start with Shared Values, check hard then soft elements against strategy, map interconnections, realign iteratively. It is an alignment loop, not a one-time checklist.

### 5. Strategic Evaluation & Control
Determine effectiveness of a strategy and take corrective action. Checks: (i) strategy implemented as planned; (ii) results as intended; (iii) premises still hold.
- **Difficulties of strategic control:** measurement problems (long horizons, hard attribution); environmental turbulence (moving yardstick); timing problem (results visible too late). → relies on *leading* indicators and *premise* checks.
- **Control process (6 steps):** determine what to control → set standards/benchmarks → measure actual → compare & analyse variance → judge whether deviation is *significant* (within tolerance?) → corrective action (adjust performance, revise standards, or reformulate strategy). Act only on significant deviations — over-correcting on noise destabilises.

**Strategic vs Operational control:**
| Basis | Strategic | Operational |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Direction; "right things?" | Efficiency; "things right?" |
| Horizon | Long term | Short term |
| Concerned with | Environment, assumptions, strategy | Resource use, day-to-day |
| Nature | External, adaptive | Internal, corrective |
| Data | Mostly external, qualitative | Mostly internal, quantitative |
| Corrective action | May reformulate strategy | Adjusts execution |

**Four types of strategic control (Schendel & Hofer):**
1. **Premise Control** — checks assumptions/predictions (economy, industry, competitors, technology). *Focused* — each premise pre-identified and assigned.
2. **Implementation Control** — should strategy continue given events as roll-out proceeds? Tools: monitoring strategic thrusts + milestone reviews.
3. **Strategic Surveillance** — broad, *unfocused* monitoring of a wide range of events; catches the threat nobody listed as a premise.
4. **Special Alert Control** — rapid reconsideration triggered by sudden, unexpected event (crisis, competitor acquisition, disaster, regulatory shock). Uses contingency plans / crisis teams.
- **Sort by 2 axes:** trigger specific/broad × monitoring continuous/event-driven. Premise = specific + continuous; Surveillance = broad + continuous; Implementation = specific + continuous (internal roll-out); Special Alert = broad + event-driven. Premise vs Surveillance discriminator = *focus* (targeted vs undirected).

### 6. Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton, early 1990s)
**Why financial measures alone fail:** lagging indicators (report past outcomes); ignore drivers of future performance (customer loyalty, process quality, capability, innovation); encourage short-termism & can be manipulated; ignore intangible assets (brand, knowledge, relationships).

| Perspective | Core question | Example measures |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | How do shareholders see us? | Revenue growth, ROCE, margin, cash flow |
| Customer | How do customers see us? | Satisfaction, retention, market share, on-time delivery |
| Internal Business Process | What must we excel at? | Cycle time, defect/quality rates, cost, innovation cycle |
| Learning & Growth (Innovation & Learning) | Can we keep improving/creating value? | Training, skills, IT capability, staff retention, new ideas |

- **Causal chain (bottom-up):** Learning & Growth → Internal Process → Customer → Financial. Financials are the *last* link.
- **Four "balances":** financial/non-financial; lagging/leading; internal/external; short-term/long-term.
- Each perspective pairs a lagging *outcome* with a leading *driver* (e.g. market share vs on-time delivery).
- **Strategy map** = one-page cause-effect diagram; visual articulation of the causal chain; makes BSC a communication device.
- BSC = measurement system + strategic management & communication system.
- **Limitations:** mechanical if causal logic ignored; too many measures dilute focus; cause-effect links assumed not proven; non-financial data costly & gameable; needs continual updating.
- **Numeric BSC:** score = weight × achievement; cap achievement at 100%. *Inverse* measures (defect rate, cost, cycle time, attrition — lower is better) use **target ÷ actual**, not actual ÷ target.

## Exam traps & must-remember
1. **"Structure follows strategy"** — never reverse it. State proposition first, nuance (structure later constrains strategy) second.
2. **7S** = seven elements, **Shared Values at centre**; don't list only six. Hard = Strategy/Structure/Systems; Soft = Shared Values/Style/Staff/Skills.
3. **Author-framework pairs:** Lewin = change (+ force-field); Kotter = 8-step; Kotter & Schlesinger = overcoming-resistance methods; Chandler = structure follows strategy; Peters & Waterman = 7S; Kaplan & Norton = BSC (+ strategy map); Schendel & Hofer = types of strategic control.
4. **Staff ≠ Skills ≠ Systems** in 7S — keep the three apart.
5. **BSC perspectives:** Financial, Customer, Internal Business Process, Learning & Growth (don't drop L&G or rename Internal Process). Causal direction L&G → Financial.
6. **Why financials insufficient** — give reasons (lagging, ignore drivers, short-termism, miss intangibles), not adjectives.
7. **BSC "lower is better" reversal** — inverse measures use target ÷ actual.
8. **Name all four BSC balances**, not one.
9. **Strategic vs Operational control** — practise the distinction table.
10. **Four control types** — Surveillance is *unfocused/broad*; Premise is *targeted at pre-identified assumptions*.
11. Implementation is a *set* of activities (institutionalising, resource allocation, structuring, behavioural, functional) — not one step.
12. **Lewin order:** Unfreeze → Move → Refreeze; most-forgotten = **Refreeze**.
13. **"Just change the culture"** = last resort only (vital + strongly incompatible); prefer reinforce or manage-around.
14. **Over-correcting on noise** — act only on *significant* deviations outside tolerance.

## One-line recall
- Lewin: Unfreeze → Move → Refreeze; weaken *restraining* forces, don't push harder; participation is the strongest anti-resistance tactic.
- Chandler: change strategy → old structure misfits → redesign; functional (specialisation) vs divisional (coordination); diversification triggers the shift.
- 7S: align all seven around Shared Values; managers change easy hard S's and neglect the decisive soft S's.
- Control: 6-step loop acting only on significant variance; four types = Premise, Implementation, Surveillance, Special Alert (Schendel & Hofer).
- BSC: L&G → Internal Process → Customer → Financial; financials are lagging; four balances; inverse measures use target ÷ actual.
- Whole chapter = one loop: make people willing to change, give a fitting structure, align every internal element, and continuously check leading + lagging signals to correct in time.
