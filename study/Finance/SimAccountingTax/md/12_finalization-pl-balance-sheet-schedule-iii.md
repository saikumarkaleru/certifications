# Finalization: Trial Balance to P&L and Balance Sheet (Schedule III)

## The situation

It's **12-Apr-2027**. The books for FY 2026-27 are closed, all twelve monthly closes are locked, and the audit kicks off next week. This just landed on your desk: an email from the CFO — *"Draft the statutory financials. Schedule III, Division I format. I want the Statement of Profit and Loss, the Balance Sheet as at 31-Mar-2027, and the three notes the auditor always opens with — revenue, PPE, and borrowings. PBT must come to Rs 1,50,60,000. Show me the PAT-to-reserves tie-out."*

You now convert a 400-line trial balance into the two statements every stakeholder reads: the **Statement of Profit and Loss** (did we make money?) and the **Balance Sheet** (what do we own and owe?). For a company, the format is not optional — **Schedule III, Division I** of the Companies Act 2013 dictates the line items, the order, and the current/non-current split.

## Do it — step by step

### Step 1 — Build the Statement of Profit and Loss (Schedule III, Division I)

Schedule III wants revenue, then other income, then expenses *by nature* (not by function), then PBT, tax, and PAT.

**NTSPL — Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ended 31-Mar-2027**

| Particulars | Note | FY2026-27 (Rs) |
|-------------|:----:|---------------:|
| **I. Revenue from operations** | 1 | 12,00,00,000 |
| II. Other income | | — |
| **III. Total income (I+II)** | | 12,00,00,000 |
| **IV. Expenses:** | | |
| Cost of materials / purchases of stock-in-trade | | 8,40,00,000 |
| Changes in inventories | | (included above) |
| Employee benefits expense | | 1,08,00,000 |
| Finance costs | | 9,00,000 |
| Depreciation and amortisation | 2 | 14,40,000 |
| Other expenses | | 78,00,000 |
| **Total expenses** | | 10,49,40,000 |
| **V. Profit before tax (III−IV)** | | **1,50,60,000** |
| VI. Tax expense — current tax | | 39,60,000 |
| VII. **Profit for the year (V−VI)** | | **1,11,00,000** |

Total expenses = 8,40,00,000 + 1,08,00,000 + 9,00,000 + 14,40,000 + 78,00,000 = **10,49,40,000**. Total income 12,00,00,000 − 10,49,40,000 = **PBT 1,50,60,000**. ✔ ties to the anchor.

Current tax at the 25% + 4% cess route (domestic company, turnover ≤ Rs 400 cr) ≈ Rs 39,60,000 on taxable income, giving **PAT ≈ 1,11,00,000** (the exact tax computation with add-backs is done in the year-end tax chapter; here we plug the provision).

### Step 2 — Classify every balance as current or non-current

Schedule III splits assets and liabilities on the **operating-cycle / 12-month** test. An item is **current** if it is expected to be settled/realised within 12 months (or the operating cycle) of the reporting date; else **non-current**.

| Item | Classification | Why |
|------|----------------|-----|
| PPE, term-loan (>12m portion) | Non-current | Used/settled beyond 12 months |
| Inventories, trade receivables, cash, GST ITC | Current | Realised within the cycle |
| Trade payables, statutory dues, CC/OD, current maturities of term loan | Current | Due within 12 months |
| Reserves & surplus, share capital | Equity | — |

## How it's checked

- **P&L to anchor:** Total income 12,00,00,000 − total expenses 10,49,40,000 = PBT 1,50,60,000. Any deviation means an expense is mis-grouped or omitted.
- **Balance Sheet balances:** Equity + liabilities (4,75,00,000) = assets (4,75,00,000). If it doesn't tally, a trial-balance line is on the wrong side or unclassified.
- **PAT → reserves:** opening surplus + PAT − dividend = closing surplus. Reviewer confirms Rs 1,09,00,000 + 1,11,00,000 = 2,20,00,000.
- **PPE note ties to FAR and to P&L depreciation:** note-2 depreciation column total = P&L depreciation line = Rs 14,40,000.
- **Current/non-current:** term-loan current maturity (Rs 12,00,000 due within 12 months) sits in current liabilities, not non-current — a classic auditor check.

## Common mistakes & red flags

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Grouping expenses by *function* (COGS/S&A) | Not Schedule III Division I compliant | Present by *nature* (materials, employee, finance, dep, other) |
| Leaving current maturity of long-term debt in non-current | Understated current liabilities, distorted ratios | Split the next-12-month portion into current |
| PAT not carried to reserves | Balance Sheet won't tally | Post the surplus appropriation to Note 4 |
| Netting off ITC receivable against GST payable | Overstated/understated current items | Show gross under Schedule III |
| Forgetting cess in tax provision | Tax expense understated | Current tax = base rate + 4% health & education cess |

## On the job & in the interview

Finalization is the moment a heap of ledgers becomes a *story an outsider can read*. Schedule III exists so every company's financials look the same — a banker or investor can compare NTSPL to any other private company without re-learning the layout. The **articulation** between the two statements is the concept interviewers probe: the P&L's bottom line is not the end of the road — it flows into the Balance Sheet through reserves.

**Q: How does the P&L connect to the Balance Sheet?**
A: Through retained earnings. The Profit for the year (PAT Rs 1,11,00,000) is appropriated to Reserves & surplus, taking it from Rs 1,09,00,000 to Rs 2,20,00,000. That single link is why a Balance Sheet with a wrong reserve figure won't tally — the error is almost always a mis-posted PAT or a missed appropriation.

**Q: What's the current vs non-current test?**
A: An asset/liability is current if it's expected to be realised or settled within 12 months of the reporting date or within the operating cycle. So inventories, receivables, and the CC/OD are current; the term loan's beyond-12-month portion is non-current, but its next-year instalment (Rs 12,00,000) is reclassified as current.

**Q: Why present expenses by nature under Division I?**
A: Schedule III Division I (for companies still on AS, not Ind AS) mandates the nature-of-expense format — employee benefits, finance costs, depreciation, other expenses — rather than the function format. It gives a consistent, comparable P&L and makes the depreciation and finance-cost lines explicit for lenders and tax reviewers.
