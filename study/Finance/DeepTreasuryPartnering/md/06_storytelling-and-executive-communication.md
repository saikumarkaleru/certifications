# Data Storytelling & Executive Communication

## What you'll be able to do

Take a messy variance and turn it into a message a CFO reads in 20 seconds and acts on. By the end you can structure any communication with SCQA and the Minto pyramid, pass the "so-what" test, write board narrative that says something, build an executive one-pager, design a single board slide that survives scrutiny, and hold your own in a Q&A or balance-sheet review call without getting flustered. This is the skill that decides whether your analysis ever reaches a decision — brilliant work communicated badly is invisible.

## The essentials

**The core failure.** Analysts write *bottom-up* (here's everything I did, conclusion at the end). Executives read *top-down* (answer first, support on demand). You must invert.

**SCQA (Minto).** The setup for any message:
- **Situation** — the stable context everyone agrees on.
- **Complication** — what changed / the tension.
- **Question** — the question the complication raises.
- **Answer** — your recommendation. *This goes first in delivery.*

**The Minto Pyramid.** One governing thought at the top, supported by 3-4 grouped arguments, each backed by data. Two rules: arguments must be **MECE** (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive) and each group must genuinely *support* the thought above it. You present the top; you drill down only if challenged.

**The so-what test.** After every sentence, ask "so what?" If the sentence doesn't change what the reader thinks or does, cut it or push it further. "Revenue fell 8%" → so what? → "Revenue fell 8%, all of it in the North region, all of it one churned account — which is recoverable." Keep asking until you hit an action.

**Signal vs. noise.** Executives have ~30 seconds. One message per artefact. Numbers rounded to the precision that matters (₹4.2 cr, not ₹4,23,71,559). Colour used to mean something, never for decoration.

**Executive presence.** Calm, brief, owns the number (good or bad), says "I don't know — I'll have it by 3pm" rather than bluffing. Presence is mostly *not* panicking and *not* over-explaining.

## Hands-on — step by step

**The raw material.** You're closing the month. Gross margin came in at 61% vs. 64% budget — a 3-point miss. Digging in: freight costs spiked (new supplier), one product line (SKU-7) was over-discounted to clear inventory, and FX on imported components moved against you. Freight is the biggest chunk and it's structural; the discount was one-off; FX is ~30 bps.

**Step 1 — Find the one message (so-what).**
"GM missed budget by 3 pts" → so what? → the *composition* matters: half is a fixable supplier problem, a quarter is one-off, a quarter is FX. So what? → **"The margin miss is mostly a fixable freight issue — action already in motion — not a demand problem."** That's your governing thought.

**Step 2 — Build the pyramid (MECE).** Governing thought supported by three drivers that sum to the 3-point miss:

| Driver | Impact | Nature | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freight (new supplier) | −1.6 pts | Structural, fixable | Re-tender in progress, back to plan by Q3 |
| SKU-7 clearance discount | −0.8 pt | One-off | Inventory cleared, won't repeat |
| FX on components | −0.6 pt | Market | Hedge 60% forward from next month |
| **Total** | **−3.0 pts** | | |

They're MECE and they tie exactly to the variance — that's what makes it credible.

**Step 3 — Draft the commentary top-down.** Answer first, then the three drivers, then the action.

**Step 4 — Write it once badly (before), then fix it (after).**

**BEFORE (analyst-style, bottom-up, no so-what):**
> During the month we onboarded a new freight vendor as the previous contract expired, and rates were higher than anticipated. Additionally, the SKU-7 product line required promotional activity to reduce ageing inventory as previously discussed in the S&OP meeting. There were also foreign exchange movements on our imported components due to rupee depreciation. As a result of these factors, gross margin for the month came in at 61.0% against a budget of 64.0%, a variance of 3.0 percentage points, which we are continuing to analyse.

**AFTER (exec-style, top-down, so-what):**
> **Gross margin missed budget by 3.0 pts (61% vs 64%) — but it's mostly a fixable freight problem, not a demand issue.** Breakdown: freight from the new supplier cost 1.6 pts (re-tender under way, back to plan by Q3); a one-off SKU-7 clearance cost 0.8 pt (won't repeat); FX on components cost 0.6 pt (hedging 60% forward from next month). Underlying demand and pricing are on plan. **Ask:** approve the forward hedge; no action needed on demand.

The *after* leads with the answer, is MECE, ties to the variance, and ends with a decision.

**Step 5 — Design the board slide.** One slide, one message.
- **Title = the message, not the topic.** "Margin miss is a fixable freight issue, back to plan by Q3" — never "Gross Margin Analysis."
- **One chart:** a waterfall from 64% budget → −1.6 freight → −0.8 SKU-7 → −0.6 FX → 61% actual. The waterfall *is* the argument.
- **Three annotation callouts**, colour-coded: red (structural/freight), grey (one-off), amber (FX).
- **Footer:** the ask, in one line.
- No logos, no clip-art, no 8-point footnotes.

**Step 6 — Prep the Q&A / BS-review.** List the three questions a director will actually ask and pre-write the answer: (a) "Is freight permanent?" — "No, re-tender by Q3, expect 1.2 pts back." (b) "Why over-discount SKU-7?" — "Ageing inventory; the write-down avoided was larger than the margin hit." (c) "What's the FX exposure going forward?" — "₹X cr/quarter; hedging 60%." Rehearse the number you'll get pushed on.

## The output

The finished artefacts:

**Board one-pager (top half):**
> **Margin — 3-pt miss is mostly fixable freight, back to plan by Q3**
> GM 61% vs 64% budget. Freight (new supplier) −1.6 pts, re-tender under way. SKU-7 clearance −0.8 pt, one-off. FX −0.6 pt, hedging 60% fwd. Demand and pricing on plan.
> **Decision requested:** approve forward FX hedge (60% of component exposure).

**Board slide:** title = the message; a single budget-to-actual GM waterfall with three colour-coded callouts; footer = the ask. Backup slides (not shown) hold the SKU-level detail for the Q&A.

## Checks, gotchas & red flags

- **Ties out:** the drivers in your narrative must sum exactly to the headline variance (1.6 + 0.8 + 0.6 = 3.0). If they don't, a director will find the gap and you lose the room.
- **Red flag — topic titles.** "Q3 Revenue Review" tells the reader nothing. The title must be the takeaway.
- **Red flag — burying the answer.** If your recommendation is in the last paragraph, executives never reach it.
- **Gotcha — false precision.** ₹4,23,71,559 signals you don't know what matters. Round to the decision-relevant digit.
- **Gotcha — one slide, many messages.** If a slide needs a second message, it needs to be a second slide.
- **Gotcha — bluffing in Q&A.** "I'll confirm by 3pm" beats a confident wrong number every time; the wrong number costs you the next ten meetings.

## Interview drill

**Q1. "Walk me through how you'd present a bad number to the board."**
"Answer first, framed by so-what. Lead with the message — say, the margin miss is mostly a fixable freight issue, not demand. Then a MECE breakdown of the drivers that ties exactly to the variance, each with its nature and the action. End with the specific decision I need. I own the bad number calmly and I bring the fix, not just the diagnosis. The slide title is the takeaway, and there's backup detail behind it for the questions I know are coming."

**Q2. "What's the so-what test?"**
"After each sentence I ask 'so what?' until I reach something that changes what the reader thinks or does. 'Revenue fell 8%' isn't a message; 'revenue fell 8%, all one churned account, and it's recoverable' is. It forces every line to earn its place and pushes analysis toward an action."

**Q3. "SCQA — use it on a real example."**
"Situation: we budgeted 64% gross margin. Complication: we came in at 61%. Question: is this a demand problem? Answer: no — it's mostly a fixable freight cost, one-off discount, and FX, and the freight fix is already under way. In delivery I say the answer first, then walk the SCQA only if they want the build-up."

## Learn/practise (free)

- **Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*** — the source; free summaries and lecture notes cover SCQA and MECE.
- **Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*** — free blog and monthly makeover challenges; do the makeovers.
- **McKinsey / BCG communication guides** — free articles on the pyramid and "action titles."
- **Rehearse free:** take any chart from a company's annual report, write a topic title, then rewrite it as an action title (the takeaway). Do 20.
- **Before/after reps:** grab your own past emails or a public earnings-call transcript, rewrite one paragraph top-down with a so-what lead. Read both aloud — the difference is audible.
- **Waterfall practice:** in Excel or Power BI, build a budget-to-actual bridge for any metric and give it an action title. That single chart is the most-used exec-comms artefact in finance.
