# Finance Business Partnering & Influence

## What you'll be able to do

Walk into a room where you own none of the P&L levers — sales, ops, pricing, headcount — and change the decision anyway. By the end of this chapter you can map stakeholders, land a "credible challenge" that a commercial head actually accepts, translate a number into a decision, and run a partnering conversation that ends in a committed action, not a note-taking exercise. This is the difference between an FP&A analyst who emails a variance pack and a finance business partner (FBP) whom the sales VP calls *before* signing the deal.

## The essentials

**Scorekeeper vs. business partner.** The scorekeeper reports what happened; the partner changes what happens next. Same data, different job.

| Dimension | Scorekeeper | Business partner |
|---|---|---|
| Tense | Past ("margin fell 180 bps") | Future ("here's how to claw back 120 bps in Q3") |
| Trigger | Month-end close | The decision, before it's made |
| Output | Variance pack | A recommendation with a number attached |
| Relationship | Sends reports | Sits in the deal review |
| Success metric | Accurate & on time | Decision changed, value captured |

**Influence without authority.** You can't *tell* the sales head to reprice. Six levers (Cialdini, adapted): reciprocity (do their analysis first), consistency (tie the ask to a goal they already committed to), social proof ("the other region already did this"), authority (your credibility + data), liking (relationship), scarcity ("this window closes at quarter-end"). In finance, **authority through competence + reciprocity** carry the most weight.

**Credible challenge.** A regulated concept (RBI/Basel use it for risk functions) that generalises: challenge that is *specific, evidenced, and offered as a partner*, not a gotcha. Formula: **Acknowledge the goal → surface the number that threatens it → offer a path.** "You want to hit ₹50 cr bookings — this discount structure gets you the logo but at 9% margin vs. our 22% floor; here are two ways to keep the logo *and* the margin." You challenge the *decision*, never the person.

**Commercial finance.** Owning the economics of a decision: deal pricing, discount governance, customer/product profitability, unit economics (CAC, LTV, contribution margin per unit), make-vs-buy. It's FP&A pointed at the front line rather than at the board pack.

**Stakeholder mapping (power/interest grid).** Plot each stakeholder on *power to affect the decision* × *interest in it*. Manage closely (high/high), keep satisfied (high power/low interest), keep informed (low power/high interest), monitor (low/low). Re-map per decision — the CFO is high-power on capex, low-interest on a single SMB discount.

## Hands-on — step by step

**Scenario.** You're the FBP for the North sales region of a SaaS firm. The regional head, Priya, wants to close a ₹2 cr annual contract with a large retailer by giving a **35% discount** plus 90-day payment terms. Q-end is 12 days away and it would put her at 104% of target. Your job: partner, not police.

**Step 1 — Do the analysis before the meeting (reciprocity).** Build the deal economics.

- List price ARR: ₹2.00 cr
- Discount 35% → Net ARR: ₹1.30 cr
- Gross margin at 78% → Gross profit: ₹1.014 cr
- CAC (sales commission + onboarding): ₹22 lakh
- Payment terms 90 days → carrying cost at 11% WACC on ₹1.30 cr for 90 days ≈ ₹3.6 lakh
- Discount floor policy: 20% without CFO sign-off

**Step 2 — Find the "so what."** At 35% the deal breaches the 20% self-approval floor and drags regional blended discount from 18% to 23%, which resets the anchor for every renewal in that segment. The *real* cost isn't this deal's ₹70 lakh of forgone ARR — it's the precedent.

**Step 3 — Build two paths (never arrive with only "no").**
- **Path A:** 35% for **2-year** commitment (locks ₹2.6 cr, amortises the discount).
- **Path B:** 25% discount + standard 30-day terms + a phased seat ramp; nets ₹1.50 cr ARR, stays defensible, still closes this quarter.

**Step 4 — Map stakeholders for this decision.**

| Stakeholder | Power | Interest | Play |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priya (regional head) | High | High | Manage closely — co-own the recommendation |
| CFO | High | Medium | Keep satisfied — she signs the exception |
| Deal-desk lead | Medium | High | Keep informed — enforces the floor |
| Customer procurement | High | High | Priya's to manage; arm her |

**Step 5 — Run the conversation (credible challenge).**
1. *Acknowledge the goal:* "You're at 104% with this — I want it to close."
2. *Surface the number:* "At 35% we're below the 20% floor, so it needs CFO sign-off anyway, and it moves regional blended discount to 23% — that's the anchor every renewal negotiates from next year."
3. *Offer the path:* "Two ways to still close in 12 days and protect that anchor — a 2-year lock at your 35%, or 25% with normal terms. I'll build whichever you pick into the deal-desk request tonight."

**Step 6 — Close with a committed action.** Not "let me know." Instead: "Can you sound out procurement on the 2-year lock by Thursday? If they bite, I'll have CFO approval ready Friday."

## The output

The artefact is a **one-page deal recommendation** you leave behind:

> **Deal: North / BigRetailer — ₹2 cr ARR**
> **Recommendation:** Pursue Path A (35% @ 2-yr lock). Fallback Path B (25% @ std terms).
> **Why:** 35% single-year breaches 20% floor (needs CFO sign-off) and lifts regional blended discount 18%→23%, resetting the renewal anchor. 2-yr lock captures ₹2.6 cr and amortises the concession.
> **Economics (Path A):** Net ARR ₹1.30 cr/yr; GM 78%; CAC ₹22 L; payback ~4.3 mo.
> **Ask:** Priya to test 2-yr with procurement by Thu; FBP to secure CFO exception by Fri.
> **Owner / date:** Priya + FBP / this quarter-end.

## Checks, gotchas & red flags

- **Ties out:** net ARR, discount %, and blended-discount impact must reconcile to the deal-desk model and the pricing policy — one wrong % and your credibility is gone.
- **Red flag — the "abominable no-man."** If your only contribution is blocking deals, you'll be routed around. Every "no" needs a costed alternative.
- **Red flag — analysis with no recommendation.** Handing over a spreadsheet is scorekeeping. Partners take a position.
- **Gotcha — challenging the person, not the decision.** "Your discounting is reckless" loses the room; "this discount resets the anchor" keeps it.
- **Gotcha — skipping reciprocity.** Bring their analysis done. People accept challenge from someone who's already saved them work.
- **Independence:** partnering ≠ becoming an advocate. You still own the floor; you help them win *within* it.

## Interview drill

**Q1. "A sales head wants a discount that breaks policy and it's quarter-end. What do you do?"**
"I don't lead with the policy. I build the deal economics first, find the real cost — usually the precedent it sets on renewal anchors, not the one deal — then acknowledge their target, surface that number, and bring two costed paths that still close on time. I close with a committed action and an owner. I protect the floor by making the defensible option the easy option, not by saying no."

**Q2. "How do you influence someone who doesn't report to you and outranks you?"**
"Competence and reciprocity. I do their analysis before they ask, so the ask arrives as help. I tie my recommendation to a goal they already own publicly, and I give social proof from peers who did it. Authority comes from being right on the numbers, repeatedly — that's the only durable currency."

**Q3. "What's a 'credible challenge' and how is it different from just disagreeing?"**
"Disagreeing is a position; credible challenge is specific, evidenced, and offered as a partner. Acknowledge the goal, show the number that threatens it, offer a path. It challenges the decision, never the person, and it always comes with an alternative — otherwise it's just an obstacle."

## Learn/practise (free)

- **Cialdini, *Influence*** — the six levers; free summaries and his TED talks.
- **CIMA / ICAEW "finance business partnering"** frameworks — free articles define the scorekeeper-to-partner shift with rubrics.
- **Rehearse free:** take any decision in your own life (a purchase, a project) and write the one-page recommendation above — goal, so-what number, two paths, committed action. Do ten and the structure becomes automatic.
- **Deal-economics reps:** rebuild a public SaaS company's unit economics (CAC, LTV, contribution margin) from its investor deck in Excel — free filings, real numbers, exactly the muscle commercial finance uses.
- **Power/interest grid:** map the stakeholders of any real project you're on; predict who blocks it. Check your prediction against what actually happens.
